## Source classifications: structure and coverage

The author first identifies four dimensions for an attributed gain/loss: the nature of the significator planet, its zodiac sign, its house, and its partile aspects. He then provides **planetary 'Matters or Things' versus 'Persons' tables for all seven planets**, followed by sign groups classified by four elements and six planetary natures, then all twelve houses. Each original two-sided planet row and house remains separately addressable rather than being blended into invented modern vocational stereotypes.

Source-first structured data retain **316 normalized source-list items**: 185 planet matters/person categories, 9 elemental items, 17 planetary-nature items, and 105 house items. These terms are semantic readings of printed 1647 category cells, *not* full diplomatic transcriptions, direct verbatim text, or 316 separately tested claims.

The source includes historical social-class, criminality, religion, gender and moral character stereotypes. They are descriptions of Lilly’s classifications, not evidence about people. The long planetary and house lists must never become person-level diagnosis, wealth prediction, criminal-dangerousness inference or financial advice.

## Explicit rule for combining testimony, and source flexibility

After the tables Lilly says to count which significators are strong versus weak, then attribute gain through favorable indicators and negative episodes through unfavorable indicators and their sign/house classifications. Even under strong overall wealth promise, a particular weak planet may signal local loss; the example ties a third-house malefic to a brother or kinsman. Conversely, a generally weak wealth profile may have profitable periods. When weak and strong counts are equal, the author suggests fluctuation rather than unbroken wealth or want. Crucially, he asks about the **condition in which ancestors left the native**, implying an inherited socioeconomic baseline. Neither timing windows nor a calibrated probability, exact astrological degree thresholds or untouched economic comparison cohort is given.

These conditions explain why post-hoc chart/life matching would be unreliable as evidence. The present work extracts conditional source history and does not implement or endorse any deterministic outcome algorithm. It also excludes the next printed CHAP CXVI on direct/lawful versus indirect/unlawful acquisition.
