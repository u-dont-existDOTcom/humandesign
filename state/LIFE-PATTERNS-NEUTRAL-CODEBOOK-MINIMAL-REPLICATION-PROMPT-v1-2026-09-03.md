# Life Patterns — Minimal Theory-Blind Codebook Replication Prompt v1

Preserve this prompt exactly for the independent replication run.

You are designing a theory-neutral behavioral coding system for first-person autobiographical interviews.

Create a codebook that lets trained coders describe recurring behavioral patterns from concrete reported life episodes. Choose the substantive behavioral domains yourself.

Base codes only on what the narrator reports perceiving, experiencing, choosing, doing, not doing, repeating, or changing in specific situations. Preserve context, exceptions, counterexamples, uncertainty, missing information, and source evidence. Do not infer hidden causes that the narrative does not support.

Do not organize the framework around or borrow categories from any personality typology, personality test, spiritual or esoteric system, diagnostic system, demographic theory, biological theory, birth-based theory, or other external theory. Do not try to guess what hypothesis or external model the researchers might eventually compare the coding against, and do not ask what that hypothesis is.

The primary evidence unit should be a concrete behavioral episode rather than a global personality label. Non-action should be treated as informative only when awareness, opportunity, and reasonable feasibility are established.

Every code should have a stable neutral ID and include:

- a short name;
- operational definition;
- inclusion criteria;
- exclusion criteria;
- possible values or subcodes;
- minimum required evidence;
- counterevidence;
- relevant context modifiers;
- 2–3 fictional boundary examples;
- common coding mistakes.

Every code must permit these evidence states in addition to its substantive values:

- observed;
- contradicted;
- mixed;
- insufficient evidence;
- not applicable.

Do not force every episode or person into every code. Do not create composite personality types or normative rankings. Favor distinctions that two trained human coders could plausibly apply with reproducible rules.

Produce the complete first-pass framework in this response without asking clarifying questions. At the end, identify constructs you rejected as too vague, likely overlaps, and areas that would need pilot reliability testing.
