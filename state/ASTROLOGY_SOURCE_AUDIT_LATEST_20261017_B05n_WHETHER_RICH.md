## Historical claim groups and critical original conditions

The chapter deliberately inherits previously identified wealth significators rather than replacing or independently verifying them. Favorable testimonies require **all or the majority angular plus essential dignity**. The author explicitly grades wealth/poverty by the number of strength and weakness testimonies, and supplies a middle category for moderately fortified significators. Regulus/Spica and "Fortunes in good houses" appear in an awkward passage whose operative grammar and exact orbs are unclear; no numerical rule has been guessed. Lilly also states that wealth significators may be benefic or malefic planets, refuting a simplistic "benefic planet means rich" conversion.

Specific favorable conjunctions include a favorable Moon in the Ascendant; Sun–Moon trine **with solar exaltation and no Saturn/Mars affliction**; two different Jupiter/Moon swaps across first and second house, with explicit dignities in one branch; three distinct eighth-house/inheritance configurations; and a strong, well-placed Saturn **trine the Ascendant** allegedly linking wealth to land and agriculture. These are assertions of the author, not demonstrated human wealth data.

Adverse cases include Moon/Mars/Saturn configurations, Saturn–Moon square **or** opposition, malefics angular and benefics succedent, combust Moon with afflicted dispositor, cadent Jupiter with weak dispositor and one long partly undeciphered glyph/oppressor configuration. The terminal "and so the contrary" does **not** specify a universal inverse algorithm. Opposing conditions are retained rather than suppressed.
