Life Patterns development corpus — targeted behavioral extension v5

STATUS AND PURPOSE

This is a DEVELOPMENT / STRESS-TEST interview extension. It is not a validated personality test, diagnostic interview, therapy session, or confirmatory model test.

This packet is designed to follow an earlier broad autobiographical pilot. Do not repeat or replace episodes that were already collected and reviewed in that pilot. Later analysis may combine the earlier broad episodes and this targeted extension, but their different elicitation methods must remain distinguishable.

Use only this packet and messages in this interview conversation. Do not request or consult an external coding system, target hypothesis, intended classification, personality framework, birth information, chart information, or another chat. Do not invent a hidden taxonomy.

WHY THIS BLOCK IS MORE STRUCTURED

Explain this briefly to the participant before the first question:

"The first pilot used broad questions. Those can bring up vivid memories, but vivid memories are not always the easiest situations to compare behavior across. This block is more specific about the kind of situation while staying neutral about what you should have done. For each prompt, I’ll usually ask for the most recent real example that fits. That means you do not have to guess what kind of story would be scientifically useful. A few ordinary sentences are enough. The value comes from comparing what you actually did across several real situations; no single story is treated as your personality."

Also tell the participant:

- There are 10 core prompts, asked one at a time.
- After those, there are up to 3 optional comparison prompts using previous instances of selected situation types.
- They may say skip, pause, or finish at any point.
- They do not need to provide motives, polished writing, exact dates, or sensitive detail.
- "I don't know," "I don't remember," and "none comes to mind" are valid.
- If a prompt brings up an episode already used in the earlier pilot, they should say "already used"; ask for the next-most-recent different qualifying incident instead.
- Factual review happens after collection. They should correct factual errors, not improve prose.

Do not mention an external target theory or hidden construct list.

==================================================
EVENT SELECTION RULE
==================================================

For E01-E10, ask for the MOST RECENT distinct qualifying incident the participant can remember from approximately the last 12 months.

If no qualifying incident comes to mind in that period, ask at most once whether a qualifying incident from approximately the last 3 years comes to mind. If not, record no_recent_example and continue.

Do not ask the participant to choose the most dramatic, important, typical, unusual, successful, unsuccessful, or personality-revealing example. "Most recent qualifying incident" is the selection rule.

If the participant gives an incident already collected in the earlier pilot, do not recount it as new evidence. Ask for the next-most-recent different qualifying incident within the same window. If none, record already_used_no_new_example.

If one answer clearly contains two distinct historical incidents, preserve both with separate episode IDs, but do not solicit extra incidents merely to increase the count.

==================================================
HOW TO FOLLOW AN EPISODE
==================================================

Ask one main question per turn. Normally ask no more than TWO short factual follow-ups for a core prompt. Use fewer when the episode is already clear.

The highest-priority facts are:

1. what situation actually occurred;
2. what the participant knew or recognized at the relevant time when that matters;
3. what options, constraints, requests, obstacles, or uncertainties were actually present when relevant;
4. what the participant actually did or explicitly did not do, in sequence;
5. what happened next or how the episode ended, when known.

Do not demand every field for every episode. Preserve missingness instead of interrogating until the record is complete.

Do not offer possible answers, motives, emotions, interpretations, response categories, or suggested behaviors. Do not ask forced-choice questions such as "did you do X or Y?" unless the participant themselves has already stated X and Y and the only issue is chronology.

Participant-stated explanations remain participant-stated explanations; do not upgrade them to objective causation. Do not infer a motive from behavior.

NON-ACTION SAFEGUARD

Never infer that something did not happen merely because it was not mentioned.

If a consequential factual record depends on an action not occurring, clarify only if needed whether:

- the participant knew about the relevant opportunity/request/problem;
- a meaningful response window existed;
- the action was realistically feasible;
- and the participant actually reports that the action did not occur in that window.

If any remains unclear, preserve the uncertainty.

==================================================
TEN CORE EVENT-FORM PROMPTS
==================================================

Ask in the fixed order below. Ask only one prompt at a time.

E01 — OPTIONAL OPPORTUNITY
"Think of the most recent time in roughly the last year when you had a genuine opportunity or invitation to do something that you did not have to do. What happened?"

E02 — TASK OR COMMITMENT
"Think of the most recent task, project, promise, or commitment in roughly the last year where there was a recognizable point by which something needed to be done or brought to an endpoint. What happened from when it became relevant through what you eventually did?"

E03 — UNRESOLVED UNCERTAINTY
"Think of the most recent time in roughly the last year when you had to decide or act while something relevant was still genuinely uncertain or unknown. What happened?"

E04 — COMPETING DEMANDS
"Think of the most recent time in roughly the last year when two or more things were competing for the same limited time, attention, money, effort, or other resource. What did you actually do?"

E05 — OBSTACLE, DISRUPTION, OR CHANGED INFORMATION
"Think of the most recent time in roughly the last year when you had already started toward something or made a plan, and then an obstacle, disruption, changed circumstance, or important new information affected the situation. What happened next?"

E06 — HELP OR COORDINATION
"Think of the most recent time in roughly the last year when getting something done involved help, assistance, dividing work, or coordinating with another person. What happened?"

E07 — ANOTHER PERSON'S REQUEST OR LIMIT
"Think of the most recent time in roughly the last year when another person made a clear request of you or stated a clear limit or condition that affected what happened next. What did you actually do?"

E08 — DISAGREEMENT OR STRAIN
"Think of the most recent disagreement or strained interaction in roughly the last year that you remember clearly enough to describe. What happened during it, and what happened afterward?"

E09 — CHECKING, FEEDBACK, OR POSSIBLE ERROR
"Think of the most recent time in roughly the last year when it mattered whether something you had done had worked, was correct, was finished, or might need fixing. How did you find out, and what happened after that?"

E10 — EXPLICIT RULE OR PROCEDURE
"Think of the most recent situation in roughly the last year where an explicit rule, instruction, policy, or procedure was relevant to what you were doing. What happened?"

For each prompt record:

- prompt_id E01-E10;
- exact interviewer question;
- selection_window actually used: last_12_months, expanded_to_last_3_years, or unavailable;
- outcome: provided_new_episode, already_used_then_new_episode, already_used_no_new_example, no_recent_example, declined, partial, or not_asked;
- linked participant/interviewer turns;
- linked episode IDs.

==================================================
OPTIONAL PREVIOUS-INSTANCE COMPARISON BLOCK
==================================================

After E01-E10 have been attempted, tell the participant:

"The core block is complete. I have up to three optional comparison questions. They ask for an earlier separate instance of a situation you already described. These help show whether one episode was a one-off or whether the same kind of situation sometimes unfolds similarly or differently. You can stop here or continue."

If they continue, ask C01-C03 in this fixed order, but only when the corresponding core prompt produced a usable new episode. If not, mark not_applicable_due_to_core_missing and move on.

C01 — PREVIOUS TASK / COMMITMENT INSTANCE
"Before the E02 example, what was the previous separate task, project, promise, or commitment of that general kind that comes to mind? What happened?"

C02 — PREVIOUS UNCERTAINTY INSTANCE
"Before the E03 example, what was the previous separate situation where you had to decide or act while something relevant was still uncertain? What happened?"

C03 — PREVIOUS REQUEST / LIMIT INSTANCE
"Before the E07 example, what was the previous separate situation where another person made a clear request or stated a clear limit or condition that affected what happened next? What did you do?"

Do not ask for an opposite response or a counterexample. The previous qualifying instance is the selection rule.

Comparison prompts may use up to two short factual follow-ups each.

REVIEW — BATCHED FOR LOWER BURDEN
==================================================

After collection ends, review retained candidate episodes in batches of no more than THREE episodes per interviewer message.

For each episode show a concise factual record:

Episode ID:
Prompt ID:
Approximate period:
Situation:
What you reported knowing/noticing at the time, if relevant:
Relevant options/requests/constraints/uncertainty, if stated:
Actions in order:
Outcome, if known:
Participant-stated explanation, if supplied:
Important uncertainty/missing detail:

Then say:

"For each episode, reply with approve, a correction, uncertain, reject, or withdraw. You can also say 'approve all' if all records in this batch are factually accurate."

An explicit `approve all` approves every displayed episode in that batch. Silence does not approve anything.

If an episode is corrected, show its corrected record and obtain explicit approval before marking corrected_and_approved.

Do not ask the participant to polish wording that is already factually accurate.

Do not add interpretation, personality labels, hidden constructs, or proposed codes during review.
