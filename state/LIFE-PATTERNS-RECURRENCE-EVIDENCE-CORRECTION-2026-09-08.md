# Life Patterns recurrence-evidence correction — 2026-09-08

Status: owner-directed development-method correction. This does **not** overwrite any frozen artifact. It identifies a conceptual defect in the current development measurement and blocks collection of the independent human calibration pass until a versioned theory-blind revision resolves it.

## Owner correction

The project must not treat elicited concrete incidents as automatic additional support for a generalized recurrence statement such as `I always X`.

A concrete example selected after a person has already said `I always X` is not an independent sample of their opportunities and does not, merely by existing, increase evidence for the frequency proposition. It can add information about what `X` means, how the behavior unfolds, context, sequence, exceptions, prerequisites, consequences, or ambiguity. It can also provide counterevidence if it is a genuine exception. But asking for an incident solely to make the recurrence claim look better supported is pointless and can add burden plus selection bias.

## Correct evidential distinctions

### Direct recurrence self-report

A statement such as `I always checked the door before leaving` is direct self-report evidence that the narrator reports the behavior as recurrent at the stated strength. Preserve the wording/quantifier rather than demoting it to zero evidence.

It is not the same thing as independently verified universal frequency. If the scientific target is the true opportunity-level rate, self-report uncertainty remains. But the right way to improve frequency estimation is not to collect one or several self-selected confirming anecdotes.

### Concrete episode

A concrete episode can establish that the behavior occurred in at least that bounded instance and can clarify process/context. It does **not** automatically strengthen the recurrence-frequency claim when it was elicited as an example of a pattern the participant already asserted.

Selected confirming examples are conditionally sampled on the claim and should not be counted as independent frequency evidence.

### Counterexample / exception

A concrete counterexample can be highly informative because it can falsify a literal universal claim and, more generally, reveal context boundaries or nuance. Asking for exceptions, failure cases, or conditions where the pattern does not hold can therefore have high information value.

### Informative episode sampling

Episodes can inform frequency only when the sampling design makes them informative about the opportunity distribution: for example, prospective diary sampling, random/structured opportunity sampling, or a bounded exhaustive set of opportunities. Ordinary volunteered or prompted examples are not that.

### Trait labels versus behavioral recurrence

`I always checked the door before leaving` directly reports recurring behavior.

`I am always cautious` is primarily a trait/interpretive label unless behavioral content is supplied.

These should not be collapsed.

## Interview-design rule

Do **not** ask for concrete incidents as ritual corroboration.

Ask for an incident only when it has expected information gain, such as:

- clarifying what the participant means by a behavioral claim;
- distinguishing two plausible behavioral/process interpretations;
- resolving sequence, prerequisites, opportunity, feasibility, or non-action;
- identifying context dependence;
- eliciting exceptions/counterexamples or boundary conditions;
- characterizing consequences or mechanisms relevant to the construct;
- obtaining a genuinely sampled/prospective observation under a defined sampling scheme.

If a generalized behavioral self-report already answers the construct at the required level and no such uncertainty remains, do not demand an anecdote merely to satisfy an episode quota.

## Consequence for the current frozen development measurement

The current reconciled codebook's person-level recurrence rule is episode-centric: it requires either multiple qualifying episodes or an episode plus an anchored series report for provisional recurrence. That rule is now recognized as a development-model defect for generalized behavioral recurrence self-report.

Because no human first pass and no automated coding pass have yet been collected, the scientifically preferable action is **not** to run a calibration pass on a known-defective recurrence rule merely to preserve chronology. Instead:

1. preserve the existing codebook, package, calibration selection and regenerated handoff unchanged as historical development artifacts;
2. create a new versioned, theory-blind recurrence-evidence policy/measurement revision that implements the distinctions above;
3. ensure the revised interview/coding logic does not require concrete incidents unless they add discriminating information;
4. regenerate a new development package/calibration handoff under the revised frozen version;
5. only then build/use the human-facing calibration UI and collect the independent first pass.

The revision must remain theory-blind and must not use target-model fit, Human Design labels, chart/birth information, automated labels, or desired outcomes to decide the recurrence policy.
