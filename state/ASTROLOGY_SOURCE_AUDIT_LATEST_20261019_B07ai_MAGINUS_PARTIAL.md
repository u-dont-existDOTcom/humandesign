## Source table-use instructions

The instruction paragraph at the bottom of the preceding printed folio711 gives the lookup threshold: if the natal Sun's zodiac-degree fractional minutes **exceed 30**, move to the next table degree. The worked example Sun **6°37′ Libra** therefore selects **Libra 7°**, whose first-edition annual-rate table cell reads **54′25″**. Lilly explicitly permits omitting the 25 arcseconds and forming a simplified **54 arcminutes per nominal year** accumulation table. This paragraph was *not* represented in B07ah's fourteen table/column annotations and is preserved here rather than silently skipped.

First-edition printed712/PDF746 includes **24 cumulative rows** for whole years1–24 (separately stored as historical annual 54′ accumulation). Examples: year1 =0°54′, year10=9°00′, year13=11°42′, year22=19°48′, year24=21°36′. The source has three distinct quantities: right-ascension distance from a significator to a target; accumulated annual equatorial arc; and the actual remaining arc between target and the table row. They must not be conflated.

## Original worked arithmetic discrepancy and temporal convention

Both actual printed editions reproduce two Maginus examples:
- Terms of Mars: target **11°55′**, 13-year accumulated rate **11°42′**, so exact residual = **13′**, but the text uses the attained-arc minutes **42′**, claiming **284 days**.
- Opposition to Mars: target **19°52′**, 22-year accumulated rate **19°48′**, so exact residual = **4′**, but the text uses attained-arc minutes **48′**, claiming **324 days**.

The source explicitly equates **1 arcminute with 6 days 18 hours** (27/4 nominal days). Thus the printed fractions are 42×27/4=**283.5 days** (rounded 284), 48×27/4=**324 days**. Applied instead to the mathematical *target residuals*, the same historical daily factor yields 13×27/4=**87.75 days** and 4×27/4=**27 days**, differing from the printed fractions by **195.75** and **297** nominal days. This is a *mathematical diagnostic*, not a validated historical corrected natal event prediction. At 54 arcminutes a nominal year would equal **364.5 days** under the literal printed 6d18h/min factor, which is an approximate annual conversion, not an exact modern calendar constant. Prior B07ae separately showed the difference using a **365-day** annual fraction and reached slightly different exact nominal-day differences; neither analytical convention rewrites original text.

Lilly introduces a third time measure attributed to **Valentine Naibod**, and states he personally uses it when sufficient time is available, otherwise Ptolemy's simpler method. His assertion that Naibod is most exact is a historical evaluation **without independent modern ephemeris or actual human validation**. Its original printed degree/minute tables begin **PDF747/printed713**, outside this batch.
