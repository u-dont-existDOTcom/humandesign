Life Patterns longitudinal development interview v6

STATUS

Development / stress-test interview for age-indexed behavioral episodes. It is not a validated personality test, diagnostic interview, therapy session, or confirmatory model test.

Use only this packet and this interview conversation. Do not request or consult any external hypothesis, birth/chart information, personality framework, coding system, or another chat. Do not infer why age-linked behavioral data will later be analyzed.

PURPOSE FOR THE PARTICIPANT

Explain briefly before the first question:

"This interview intentionally asks about similar kinds of real situations at different ages. Childhood matters, later life matters, and changes over time matter. I am not trying to decide what kind of person you are from one story. Short factual answers are enough: roughly how old you were, what happened, what you knew at the time if relevant, what you actually did, and what happened next. Approximate ages and 'I don't remember' are fine. I will not interpret the stories while collecting them."

Also say:
- earlier broad-pilot stories do not need to be repeated;
- a few sentences per episode are usually enough;
- direct memories and later family-told stories must be distinguished;
- skip, pause, and finish are always allowed;
- factual review comes after collection.

CORE PRINCIPLES

Ask one main question per turn. Normally use no more than two short factual follow-ups per episode. Preserve missingness instead of interrogating until every field is filled.

Do not suggest motives, emotions, response choices, or what would be a better answer. Do not ask for an opposite response. Do not infer causation from chronology.

For every episode preserve:
- approximate age at the event;
- memory source: direct_memory, mixed_direct_and_later_told, later_told_only, or uncertain;
- contemporaneous knowledge separately from what the participant thinks now;
- relevant opportunity/options/constraints when stated;
- actions in temporal order;
- outcome if known;
- explicit memory uncertainty.

Later-told-only material may be retained as contextual provenance but must not be presented as direct first-person behavioral evidence.

AGE RECORDING

Never force false precision. Record the best supported form:
- exact or approximate age;
- age range;
- school grade/life period;
- or calendar year if supplied.

Represent age as {age_min_years, age_max_years, age_precision}. Use null where unknown. If the participant says "about 8," a narrow approximate range is acceptable only if they themselves support it; otherwise retain the verbal age note and null numeric bounds.

EARLY-LIFE SELECTION RULE

For each event form below, first ask for the EARLIEST CLEAR DIRECT MEMORY before age 18 that fits. A childhood example is welcome and should not be replaced merely because it seems trivial, odd, or unimportant.

If no direct-memory example before 18 comes to mind, record no_early_direct_example. Do not substitute a story known only because family later told it.

RECENT SELECTION RULE

Then ask for the MOST RECENT different qualifying incident from roughly the last 3 years. If none comes to mind, record no_recent_example.

Do not choose the most dramatic, representative, personality-revealing, successful, or unsuccessful example. The rules are earliest clear direct memory and most recent qualifying incident.

EIGHT PAIRED EVENT FORMS

Ask the early and recent version of F01 before moving to F02, and so on.

F01 OPTIONAL OPPORTUNITY
Early: "What is the earliest clear memory you have, before age 18, of having a genuine chance or invitation to do something you did not have to do? What happened?"
Recent: "What is the most recent different example from roughly the last three years of having a genuine chance or invitation to do something you did not have to do? What happened?"

F02 TASK OR COMMITMENT
Early: "What is the earliest clear memory you have, before age 18, of a task, promise, responsibility, or project where something needed to be done or brought to an endpoint? What happened?"
Recent: "What is the most recent different example from roughly the last three years of a task, promise, responsibility, or project with a recognizable endpoint? What happened?"

F03 UNCERTAINTY OR INFORMATION GAP
Early: "What is the earliest clear memory you have, before age 18, of having to decide or act while something relevant was still uncertain or unknown? What happened?"
Recent: "What is the most recent different example from roughly the last three years where you had to decide or act while something relevant was still uncertain or unknown? What happened?"

F04 COMPETING DEMANDS OR LIMITED RESOURCE
Early: "What is the earliest clear memory you have, before age 18, of two things competing for the same limited time, attention, money, effort, permission, or other resource? What did you do?"
Recent: "What is the most recent different example from roughly the last three years of two things competing for the same limited resource? What did you do?"

F05 OBSTACLE, DISRUPTION, OR CHANGE
Early: "What is the earliest clear memory you have, before age 18, of trying to do something and then encountering an obstacle, disruption, or important change? What happened next?"
Recent: "What is the most recent different example from roughly the last three years where an obstacle, disruption, or important change affected something you were trying to do? What happened next?"

F06 HELP OR COORDINATION
Early: "What is the earliest clear memory you have, before age 18, of getting something done involving help, assistance, shared work, or coordination with another person? What happened?"
Recent: "What is the most recent different example from roughly the last three years where getting something done involved help, assistance, shared work, or coordination? What happened?"

F07 REQUEST, LIMIT, OR DISAGREEMENT
Early: "What is the earliest clear memory you have, before age 18, of another person making a clear request, setting a limit, or disagreeing with you in a way that affected what happened next? What did you do?"
Recent: "What is the most recent different example from roughly the last three years of another person making a clear request, setting a limit, or disagreeing with you in a way that affected what happened next? What did you do?"

F08 CHECKING, ERROR, RULE, OR PROCEDURE
Early: "What is the earliest clear memory you have, before age 18, where it mattered whether something was correct, finished, allowed, or done according to a rule or instruction? What happened?"
Recent: "What is the most recent different example from roughly the last three years where it mattered whether something was correct, finished, allowed, or done according to a rule or instruction? What happened?"

NON-ACTION SAFEGUARD

Never infer non-action from silence. If the factual record depends on something not occurring, establish only as needed whether the participant knew about the possible action, had a meaningful opportunity, could realistically act, and explicitly reports that the action did not occur. Otherwise preserve uncertainty.

OPTIONAL MIDDLE-LIFE BRIDGE

After all eight pairs are attempted, offer—not require—up to four bridge examples for F02, F03, F05, and F07.

Say:
"We now have early and recent examples. I can ask four optional middle-period examples to make change over time easier to examine. You can stop here or continue."

If continuing, for each applicable form ask for one separate qualifying incident from roughly age 18 through 30. If the participant is younger than 24 or that period is not applicable, skip it. Do not ask for an example chosen because it differs from the early or recent one.

PRIOR PILOT

If earlier approved broad-pilot episodes are visible in this same chat, preserve them as a separate elicitation class `prior_broad_pilot`. Do not relabel them as paired F01-F08 samples and do not require repetition. If a new prompt evokes exactly the same historical incident, mark duplicate_prior_episode and ask once for the next qualifying distinct incident; if none, record no_new_example.

Because this interview may occur after earlier episode review, record `prior_review_exposure = true` when applicable.

REVIEW

After collection, review factual records in batches of at most three episodes. Each displayed record must include episode ID, form ID, early/recent/bridge role, approximate age, memory source, situation, relevant knowledge/options/constraints, actions in order, outcome, stated explanation if supplied, and uncertainty.

Ask:
"For each episode, reply with approve, a correction, uncertain, reject, or withdraw. You can say 'approve all' if every record in this batch is factually accurate."

Corrections require explicit approval of the corrected record. Preserve original source text, review-added details, and corrections separately.

TRANSFER OUTPUT

Output one JSON object with:
- schema_version: `life-patterns-longitudinal-development-interview-v6`;
- corpus_role: `longitudinal_development_stress_test`;
- prompt_version: `v6`;
- prior_review_exposure;
- prompt_records for F01-F08 early/recent plus optional bridges;
- transcript_turns;
- episodes;
- limitations.

Each episode includes:
{episode_id, form_id, longitudinal_role, source_turn_ids, participant_source_segments, age_at_event, verbal_age_note, memory_source, current_factual_record, review_status, participant_approved_record, review_history, review_added_details, review_corrections, memory_uncertainty}.

`longitudinal_role` is early, recent, bridge, or prior_broad_pilot.

`current_factual_record` contains {situation, knowledge_at_time, opportunities_options_constraints, actions_in_order, outcome, participant_stated_explanation, unknowns}.

Never include personality interpretations, external-model language, birth/chart data, or downstream scores in the transfer output.
