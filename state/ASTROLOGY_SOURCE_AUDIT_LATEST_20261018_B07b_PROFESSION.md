## Outcome definition and separate research questions

Lilly defines *Magistery* broadly as a study, pleasure, art or action through which someone passes much of their life, earns a livelihood or preserves reputation. Examples span public governance, administration of justice, military work, hunting, philosophy, theology, mathematics and private mechanical or learned crafts. **Unpaid personal pursuits can also qualify**. He explicitly raises three different source questions: (1) whether the native has any profession/study/trade, with a none option; (2) its **kind**; (3) the **success/fortune and fame** obtained from it. A future human study must define primary income-producing occupation, time-consuming activity, interest/delight, formal title, and years independently; picking whichever fits a chart is invalid.

## Historic candidate-significator eligibility and fallbacks

The author initially singles out **Mars (bodily endurance), Venus (delight) and Mercury (intellectual capacity)** as three competing source significators. First preference is a candidate in **tenth, first or seventh**, with its **essential dignities** intact and not **combust or under the Sun's beams**. If no candidate qualifies, Lilly examines whether one of those three rules Midheaven with essential dignity; peregrine status or fall disqualifies this route.

He next considers **partile aspects to the Moon** from the three candidates, breaking conflicts by planetary strength, quality of aspect, exactness and explicitly *sinister before dexter*. Another paragraph says that when none of the three beholds the Moon, one should choose a planet that aspects it within the **mediety of the Moon's orbs**, strongly and not afflicted by malefic bodily conjunction, square or opposition. This is internally **ambiguous** (none aspect versus an aspect within half-orb), and remains preserved rather than silently repaired. A final fallback considers a candidate preceding the Sun by `first mover`, whose exact astronomical operationalization is unverified.

Lilly additionally speculates that obtaining a significator only by later-tier tests can imply a supposedly inferior profession, negligence, or no profession. These are unrelated, stigmatizing old class/moral claims; they are not actual human competence measures. The author also cites his own judgments using the tenth sign and the tenth ruler's natal position, but offers no registered independently rated cases.

## Distinct original Ptolemy-attributed method

The original claims that Ptolemy selects professional significance from two routes: (1) the planet rising next before Sun in the morning; (2) the tenth sign and its lord or a planet occupying tenth, under an aspect to the Moon. A **single planet fulfilling both** is preferred as the `Master` of activities; if none fulfills both, one performing only one may still count. This different selection rule cannot be treated as independent proof or combined retrospectively without predefined precedence. The actual Ptolemy original source has not been collated.
