RECURRENCE PRINCIPLE

A generalized behavioral statement such as “I always X”, “I usually X”, “I often X”, “sometimes X”, or another exact recurrence statement is itself evidence of REPORTED recurrence. It does not need a confirming anecdote or invented minimum count to become usable recurrence self-report.

Specificity is not evidential independence:
- do not count a confirming concrete episode as independent support for frequency merely because it illustrates the same claim;
- do not increase recurrence strength based on the number of self-selected confirming examples;
- preserve genuine exceptions/counterevidence when supplied;
- only sampled opportunities, a bounded exhaustive opportunity set, or external records/observations can support inference beyond reported-recurrence semantics.

EXCEPTIONS

- exceptions_explicitly_denied: exact source explicitly denies exceptions in the stated scope;
- exceptions_reported: exact source reports an exception/boundary;

When supported use exception_frequency:

If exception status is unknown, exception_frequency must be null or unknown. If exceptions are explicitly denied, use none_reported.
