## Historical ideas and their exceptions

Lilly suggests a favorable aspect between first- and third-house rulers indicates sibling concord, a square or opposition disagreement, and no aspect little affection. He expands this method to kin and neighbors, opening multiple potentially interchangeable sources of apparent fit.

He also asserts that he has not recalled failing in cases with Saturn peregrine, Mars or South Node in the third house leading to family disturbance. This is an **unverified personal recollection** with no case count, documented negative examples or blinded accuracy measurement. It is not independently supported prediction.

The source has explicit mitigating or contradictory conditions. Mars acting as third-house ruler is considered favorable if trine to the Ascendant lord **and** in reception, even though Mars otherwise appears in unfavorable classifications. First/third lord mutual reception, the third ruler at Ascendant, or the same planet ruling both are additional alleged ways to obtain sibling harmony. The author permits concord between the native and siblings while the siblings themselves disagree.

Financial gain/loss narratives involve third ruler joined to Part of Fortune or its ruler in good house, or South Node third combined with a combust second ruler. Lilly also assigns several alleged outcomes to Aries/Libra, Cancer/Scorpio, Leo/Aquarius rising, and Mercury opposition Mars. These are 17th-century stereotypes, not evidence regarding a real person's family ties, character, wealth or legal conduct.
