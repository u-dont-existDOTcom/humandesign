# Life Patterns development interview v4 — first pilot audit

Date: 2026-09-05

Status: development-only methodological audit.

## Privacy / provenance note

This audit is based on an owner-supplied transcript of the first completed v4 pilot. The participant's autobiographical episode text is intentionally **not reproduced in this public repository**. Only aggregate methodological findings are recorded here.

The pilot remains development evidence. It is not untouched validation data and does not establish construct validity, target-model validity, or a personality profile.

## What v4 demonstrated successfully

1. The short orientation was understandable enough for the participant to begin without a setup questionnaire.
2. The participant could provide concise autobiographical material without essay-length answers.
3. Short factual follow-ups could recover important action details without requiring a long interrogation.
4. Individual factual review/approval was operationally feasible.
5. The participant supplied process feedback after review.
6. The prompt correctly avoided turning pilot feedback into another autobiographical episode.

## Empirical failure exposed by the pilot

The pilot showed that **broad autobiographical invitations and generic recent-day anchors do not reliably produce behaviorally information-dense episodes**.

The broad invitations preferentially elicited memorable autobiographical incidents. Memorable incidents can be scientifically useful, but they need not contain the opportunity structure, competing alternatives, sequence, endpoint, feedback, coordination, or other factual conditions needed to stress-test a behavioral measurement system.

A recent-day anchor can likewise produce a perfectly valid ordinary interaction with little action structure beyond participation in the interaction. Recency improves temporal anchoring but does not by itself guarantee measurement-relevant behavioral opportunity density.

The participant's explicit process feedback was that the questions felt vague and that the usefulness of the material for understanding behavior/personality was unclear.

This is not a participant failure. It is evidence that v4's elicitation design was underspecified for the next development objective.

## Interpretation

V4 is useful as a **collection-pipeline pilot** and as a small broad-recall baseline. It should not be used as the sole development corpus for the 22-observable measurement system.

The next data collection block should improve behavioral opportunity density without telling the participant which response category is desired.

A better design therefore separates:

- *situation-form prompting* — specifying the kind of real-world situation to recall;
- from *response prompting* — which remains prohibited.

For example, asking for the most recent real situation involving a genuine optional opportunity is acceptable development elicitation. Asking whether the participant accepted immediately, waited, researched first, or declined would be response-leading and remains prohibited.

## Why a most-recent-event rule

Free choice among all autobiographical memories gives the respondent enormous discretion over which incident becomes evidence. A fixed `most recent qualifying incident` rule reduces cherry-picking by both participant and interviewer while retaining natural behavioral response variation.

The initial window is approximately 12 months to favor more recent recall. If no example is available, the prompt permits one standardized expansion to approximately 3 years and records the expanded window explicitly.

This is a development sampling rule, not a claim that one year or three years is an optimal psychometric period.

## Why targeted event forms

The v5 extension uses ten fixed theory-neutral event forms:

1. optional opportunity;
2. task/commitment;
3. unresolved uncertainty;
4. competing demands;
5. obstacle/disruption/changed information;
6. help/coordination;
7. another person's request/limit;
8. disagreement/strain;
9. checking/feedback/possible error;
10. explicit rule/procedure.

These prompts specify situations, not preferred responses.

They are intended to increase the probability that the resulting episodes contain concrete action opportunities, temporal sequences, constraints, outcomes, and other codable evidence while preserving response neutrality.

Because this event-form bank was designed in a target-theory-exposed project context, it is **development-only substantive elicitation**. It must not be represented as an independently theory-blind confirmatory instrument. A later validation elicitation protocol requires its own frozen provenance and applicable theory-blind review/replication safeguards.

## Why optional previous-instance comparators

A single episode cannot establish recurrence or context-conditioned consistency. V5 therefore offers three optional, prespecified previous-instance prompts after the core block. They ask for the previous qualifying instance, not an opposite response or a counterexample.

This supplies a limited repeated-opportunity sample without forcing the participant to invent contrast.

## Why batched review

V4's episode-by-episode review is accurate but turn-intensive. V5 permits review batches of up to three episodes while still requiring explicit disposition for each episode. `approve all` is allowed only for the episodes displayed in that batch.

This is a participant-burden optimization for development. If pilot evidence shows batching increases missed corrections, the review procedure should return to one-at-a-time review.

## Supporting methodology

The redesign is consistent with two established methodological observations:

- Retrospective autobiographical reporting benefits from temporal/contextual retrieval cues and structured event anchoring rather than relying exclusively on unrestricted free recall. See Belli (1998), *Memory*, PMID 9829098, and reviews of event/life-history calendar methods.
- Critical-incident interviewing is most informative when a specific situation, the actor's concrete actions, and the outcome are recovered, rather than relying on global self-description. See Schluter et al. (2008), PMID 18173737.

These literatures support the general elicitation strategy; they do **not** validate the Life Patterns instrument or its specific event-form bank.

## Disposition

- Preserve v4 unchanged as historical pilot protocol.
- Preserve the completed v4 pilot as development evidence outside the public repository according to participant-data policy.
- Do not ask the participant to repeat the six v4 episodes merely because the protocol changed.
- Use `state/LIFE-PATTERNS-DEVELOPMENT-INTERVIEW-TARGETED-EXTENSION-v5-2026-09-05.txt` for the next development block.
- Record v5 as `prior_review_exposure = true` when it follows this completed v4 pilot.
- After v5, assess codable applicability, missingness, episode/action density, participant burden, and LLM/human coding stability before adding further autobiographical collection.

No merge, deployment, target-model execution, model scoring, or validation promotion is authorized by this audit.
