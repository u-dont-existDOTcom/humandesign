**Trutine method and precision admission.** Lilly says birth-time reporting could err by one or two hours or place the rising degree near a sign boundary, making his proposed natal judgments invalid. He describes an ancient method often attributed to Hermes and the Ptolemaic Centiloquium: the sign of Moon at birth should be the rising sign at conception; the conception Moon's sign (or opposite) relates to natal Ascendant; in the stronger version precise Moon degree at conception should be ascendant degree at birth. Yet **he explicitly reports Trutine often fails within one, two or three rising degrees**, although in some cases it helped find the *rising sign* where estimated birth hours were wrong. He refers to later authors and event-based rectification as supporting cases, which are not independent blind assessments.

**Formal lunar/gestation construction.** First erect an estimated horoscope. If natal Moon is *below* Earth (houses 1–6), measure its zodiacal distance from eastern Ascendant; if *above* Earth (houses 12–7), measure from the western Descendant. Use zodiacal 12-sign wraparound where needed, then consult the two-column **historical table of months/days in utero**, which claims different gestation durations for the same angular distance depending on lunar hemisphere. A separate table provides cumulative day-of-year under common or bissextile leap years. Subtract table gestation-days from natal day-of-year, add 365/366 if necessary, derive a proposed conception calendar day, then inspect Moon near *noon* on that date. Lilly's acceptance window is **within 13°** of estimated natal rising point; if outside, he allows aspects of benefics or malefics to advance/retard actual birth.

**Merchant dated calculation.** Original natal chart shows Moon around Gemini **1°44** below horizon, and the table computation gives ~**285 days** gestation. The purported birth year **1616** is leap; cumulative day count through Aug=**244**, add Sept19→**263**, add 366 and subtract285→**344**, look up common previous year to end Nov **334** then add ten→proposed **10 December 1615** conception. This integer calendrical sequence is arithmetically consistent. Lilly puts Moon at ~28°13 Sagittarius at noon of the proposed conception day, within ~8° of the natal ascendant, and infers the Moon might reach the required Capricorn degree around 1 AM that Sunday night. These are **unverified retroactive conception hypotheses**, not actual gestation records or independently verified birth-time correction.

**Preference and explicit source rejection.** At the chapter end Lilly says the merchant nativity was precisely rectified **by Accidents (life events)** using Midheaven and Ascendant directions. He then describes a separate procedure for computing the exact conception *hour*, but explicitly says it is too fine and **he gives it no credit**. These are distinct epistemic dispositions: Trutine sign-level usefulness but poor rising-degree precision, favored accident-based rectification but no independent holdout, and a rejected alternative conception-clock formula.
