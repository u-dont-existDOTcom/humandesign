## Exact historical-method disagreement

Lilly's second sibling-number method uses prolific third-house sign **and** its lord; additionally he suggests counting planets aspecting the third house or its ruler and dividing them by masculine and feminine zodiac signs to infer brothers versus sisters. Beneficial or adverse mutual aspects among significators allegedly indicate surviving or dying siblings, and combinations involving combust Mars, combust lord of third, Sun in third or a possibly ambiguous Jupiter opposition allegedly indicate few siblings or short lives. Other configurations of Saturn/Jupiter in the first, superior/inferior lords and angular dignities produce an ambiguous **firstborn OR best-off sibling** result rather than one precise outcome.

The chapter explicitly disputes the method Lilly attributes to Ptolemy. In Lilly's telling, Ptolemy instead uses the Midheaven as the mother's place, **Venus in diurnal and Moon in nocturnal nativities**, and the eleventh house, with favorably aspecting benefics to signify many siblings. Lilly suggests Ptolemy's approach might apply particularly to **siblings sharing a mother**, while promoting his own third-house approach generally. He claims limited ancient support for Ptolemy and closes with a Latin maxim. All these are **claims and interpretations by Lilly**. Original Ptolemy passages have **not** been independently collated in this batch.

It would be methodologically invalid to count this chapter and the earlier sibling-number chapter as two independent confirmations: both are literary assertions from the same astrologer. The supposed sibling sex, survival, birth order and longevity indicators are also *not* reliable demographic or clinical evidence.
