## Extracted historical claims and source-negating exceptions

Lilly directs the astrologer to consider Venus/Moon, seventh house/lord and planets there when discussing **men's** marriage, with further speculative judgments about a wife's personal qualities and dowry. He names Virgo, Gemini and Leo as allegedly barren signs and includes the sixth, ninth, twelfth, and **eighth house despite the eighth not being cadent** among the conditions supposed to predict remaining unmarried or disinclination toward marriage.

The author also claims lack of marriage under strong-Saturn conjunction or adverse aspects to weak Venus/Moon, but adds two important safeguards against overly simple readings: **(1) when Venus and Moon are stronger than Saturn, the negative prediction does not hold**, and **(2) Moon square or opposed to Saturn does not by itself prove singleness without many other supporting indicators, even when Venus occupies a barren sign**. These counterconditions are preserved as source records, not discarded to enable post-hoc matching.

Other speculative text interprets Saturn elevation, monastic/single life, affairs, concubines, chastity, lust and sexual ability. These are seventeenth-century sex/gender norms and moral stereotypes, *not actual psychological or private sexual-life evidence*. They do not validate any assertion about a real person.

A separate positive *Signes of Marriage* segment lists a fertile seventh-house sign, fortunate seventh ruler, benefic occupant, Venus/Moon fecund signs and first/fifth/tenth/eleventh-house placements, applying first lord to seventh ruler and mutual reception. Lilly distinguishes **desiring marriage** from **eventually marrying**, and allows a majority rather than always every testimony. Birth records, legal marriage histories, independent chart/family comparisons and prenoted negative events are entirely absent from the source.
