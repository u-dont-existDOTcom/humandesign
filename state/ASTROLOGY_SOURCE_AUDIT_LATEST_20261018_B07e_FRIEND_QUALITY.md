## Social categories by selected planets, not evidence about humans

The original proposes selecting planets occupying first or eleventh house, if any, or their rulers, and references an intercepted sign where relevant. Its claims then assign **Saturn** friends who are older men, farmers, metal workers, stonecutters, lenders or supposedly covetous; **Jupiter** clergy, lawyers, provincial rulers, nobles, judges and people described as sober/honest; **Mars** military officers, soldiers, surgeons, traders, supposedly bold or red-haired people; **Sun** kings, princes, advisors, magistrates and powerful officials; **Venus** artists, poets, musicians, apothecaries, gamers, women and other gendered descriptions; **Mercury** intellectually engaged, learned and commercial/administrative people; **Moon** matrons, widows, noblewomen, couriers, sailors, fishermen, ordinary people and supposedly inconstant persons. The occupational, status, appearance, sex/gender and moral labels are **multiple alternatives for a given source position**, not precise categories unique enough to support real-person predictions. These are old class/appearance/gender stereotypes and are not validated claims about any person's friends.

The internal `Constancy of Friends` subsection changes outcome to relationship durability. Lilly says a **fixed sign** on the eleventh cusp supposedly predicts fixed or steadfast friends; **movable sign** unstable friends; **common/bicorporeal sign** an intermediate or mixed degree of constancy. He also considers zodiac signs containing the supposed friendship significators. The final sentence proposes a source limit involving eleventh ruler not aspecting its own house or Venus in its own house and predicts little comfort from friends; the precise original **OR** logical scope is not completely clear, so it remains non-executable instead of being repaired or treated as a definitive sign of poor friendships.
