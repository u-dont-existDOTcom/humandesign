## Historical conceptual distinctions preserved

The chapter's bulk/leanness judgment concerns **mature-age natural body composition**, approximately around age thirty or after—not children's weight, temporary changes or medically defined obesity. Five groups distribute twelve zodiac signs into early/later-section or more general middle-build categories. The later portion of Aquarius is an explicit exception within a group otherwise treated as moderate.

The decision procedure is not a simple zodiac lookup. It first considers the ascending sign, ruler and planet with most essential dignities; differentiates the ruler's partile aspect to the ascending degree from alternative sign-based judgment; prescribes a fallback based on the *mediety of the sign not occupied by the Almuten* when no planet aspects the ruler partile; and adds a specially qualified Sun conjunction requiring closeness within the orb moiety, Mars absent, and concurrence of rising sign and ruler. A tie for planetary dominion is resolved by more partile aspect and a preference for domicile rulership over exaltation.

Lilly also explicitly declines to make predictions about a historical category of atypical bodies he calls “Monsters”; the terminology is archaic and stigmatizing and must not become a modern disability classification.
