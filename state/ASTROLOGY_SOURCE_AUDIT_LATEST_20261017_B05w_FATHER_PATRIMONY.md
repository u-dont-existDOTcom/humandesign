## First-edition source conditions and uncertainties

Lilly names fourth house, its lord, **Sun for day nativity or Saturn for night**, and any planets occupying fourth as father significators. A majority strong/fortunate plus supporting aspects of Jupiter or Venus supposedly indicates favorable paternal condition after the child's birth. Afflicted or cadent indicators form adverse branches. Mixed strong/weak sources are said to imply moderate fortunes, and the most dignified fourth-house occupant takes priority among several. Critically, Lilly states the **father's own nativity** is the preferred source for such judgments, an input caveat incompatible with asserting certainty from a child's chart alone.

Additional social-status categories involve fourth lord's reception, trine or sextile with first lord; Sun/Jupiter in fourth; and father significator in angles/succedents with domicile or exaltation dignity. The section includes relative Sun/Moon dignity claims comparing the parents' ancestry. These are period social classifications, not measured socioeconomic outcomes.

The latter half introduces several **historical claims about paternal/maternal lifespan and death cause** involving benefic configurations, Sun/Saturn/Mars/Mercury, lunar nodes, Sun/Moon dignities, and twelfth-house conditions. Several glyphs and conjunction scopes on PDF604 are ambiguous, and claims about short life or death by melancholy are not actual medical diagnoses, causal evidence, or reliable mortality predictions. Some clauses concern the **child** or **mother**, not father, and must not be merged with the paternal outcome category.
