## Dual actor essential/accidental strength hierarchy

Lilly asserts that the **Significator and Promittor** must both be judged at the natal radix, in their **essential dignities and angles**. If both are strong he alleges that a direction's success is evident and fully realized; he illustrates with the **Sun directed to the sextile of the Moon**, both essentially dignified. If one is strong and the other weak, outcome is claimed to be moderate. Crucially, Lilly distinguishes **which one is stronger**: stronger Significator supposedly produces happiness without a large initiating cause, whereas stronger Promittor supposedly makes happiness apparent but not in proportion to a triggering cause. These are source-specific contrasts, not an observed causal phenomenon.

If **both are weak**, effects and the event's initiating causes allegedly diminish with weakness; if wholly weak/unfortunate, the direction **may come to nothing** or only a shadow of an effect may appear. The original thus supplies a negative/no-event case and subjectively graded strength, which is not a fixed forecast unless a future protocol predefines numeric event magnitudes and abstentions.

The section explicitly warns that traditionally unfortunate planets (**Infortunes**) acting as Promittors can allegedly produce **less** harm if strong in their natal radical chart or occupying their own domiciles. That is an original countercondition against reading every Saturn/Mars contact as necessarily severe.

## Contradictory directions in the same year and additional source flexibility

Lilly explicitly considers **positive and negative directions occurring in the same year** from different Significators. He says both may operate simultaneously without contradiction unless they refer to the **same domain**, when the practitioner may seek corroboration from **annual Profections and Revolutions**, and, if still uncertain, choose the natal **strongest Promittor**.

His hypothetical example gives an adverse Ascendant square Mars in a **fiery sign** and a favorable Moon sextile/trine Venus in a **watery sign**, both regarding health. The author asks whether the native will suffer a violent fever or avoid sickness. Profection/Revolution leaning Mars supposedly favors illness; leaning Venus allegedly permits escape or reduced effects. This example has no clinical validity; its ability to invoke additional annual rules AFTER conflicting directions poses a substantial historical flexibility/overfitting concern.
