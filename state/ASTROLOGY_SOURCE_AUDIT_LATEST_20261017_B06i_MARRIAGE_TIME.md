## Two distinct research outcomes, with many historical alternatives

The first source passage predicts how much effort it allegedly takes a man to obtain marriage: most marriage indicators fortunate or dignified versus weak or malefic; all weak signifiers supposedly lead to failed pursuits; Venus not in favorable aspect with Mars allegedly brings love troubles. Lilly cites unverified personal cases with Saturn and Mars opposed across first/seventh, Saturn close to seventh cusp, Venus in Leo/Scorpio without Mars aspect, or Mars in Virgo/Capricorn/Taurus seventh. He asserts unusually difficult but eventually sudden marriage and sexual behavior in these examples. His reported anecdote has no independently archived cases or failures.

In the *time of Marriage* passage, he distinguishes early marriage from **a later marriage to a younger partner**, and similarly late marriage from **a young marriage to an older or widowed spouse**. He assigns source-defined east/oriental houses (10, 11, 12, 4, 5, 6) and west/occidental houses (9, 8, 7, 1, 2, 3), plus Moon orientality to Sun, direct/swift planets versus retrograde/slow/stationary, and an ambiguous priority to those indicators closest to Moon or Venus. Neither calendar ages nor threshold definitions are provided.

The more specific alleged timing uses historical *directions*: seventh cusp/ruler or Moon/Venus to Ascendant/cusp ruler; Midheaven to Venus or Moon's conjunction/sextile/square/trine with a well-dignified radix Venus; Sun directed both direct and converse to Moon/Venus; Moon to Sun or Mars with a historical fortunate-term mitigation on hostile aspects; and Midheaven, Sun or Moon trine/sextile seventh lord or a benefic occupying seventh. Multiple configurations can nominate different times with no priority or explicit no-forecast path.
