## Findings

They do not reduce the three counted errors of the better single indicators, and they flag more unknown years when made more inclusive. A trivial always-flag control actually scores better on the selected labels, showing that those labels are inadequate for an overall accuracy ranking.

**There is one concrete development improvement to the old2008-versus2018 comparison.** An exact Javane/Bunker annual/Triangle compound concurrence keeps2018 while excluding2008. Adding it as a condition to Cheiro's recurrence therefore distinguishes your major2018 from your moderate2008. A different filter using Decoz's numerical5/5 alignment does exactly the same thing. Moreover, the Javane/Bunker condition alone already selects2018, so this history does not show that adding Cheiro improves it.

The most defensible result is therefore **a promising new source of relationship detail, plus explicit testable combination hypotheses—not a demonstrated best system or permission to select whichever system explains each event afterward**.

There is no improvement in the counted error total when the familiar indicators are combined. The union appears more successful only if one counts the extra hit while neglecting the extra false importance claim and extra unassessed years. Intersection reduces false importance claims but loses a major year. No candidate among the36 has fewer than three counted errors.

The always-flag control's lower error is an important warning: eight of the ten selected year labels are positive. This is not an unbiased sample of your whole life. Consequently, the figures are **not70% versus80% predictive accuracy** and cannot establish sensitivity, specificity or a reliable ranking of complete systems. They are a useful failure check on the claim that adding systems necessarily improves predictions.
