# Source-edition reliability checkpoint: Zi Wei Quan Shu (2026-10-21)

**Impact:** The pinned Wikitext oldid1963110 is a reproducible *modern transmitted textual reference*, not independently authenticated classical authority. All 979 current Chinese source records remain historical documentation; no person-level rule/engine should be promoted from uncertain wording.

## Newly identified edition comparison target

The publisher Xin Yi Tang / Sunyata's 2017 `紫微斗數全書（明末清初木刻真本）`, ISBN **9789888266944**, describes a facsimile assembled from **two surviving impressions of late-Ming/early-Qing Wenguangtang woodblocks** (Dunhuatang 敦化堂 and Jishutang 繼述堂) from a private collection. The publisher explicitly warns that popular Republican-era lithographic reproductions contain **many character errors**, including important chart-mechanics differences in year-stem Four Transformations `安四化` and a Sun/Moon placement example. This is the publisher's provenance/comparison claim, **not our independent physical collation**.

A 2017 first-person blog by a separate Zi Wei teacher says an additionally encountered Wenshengtang 文盛堂 impression appears nearly identical in layout/typeface/defects to Jishutang, while Dunhuatang differs. These are unverified observational assertions and possible common-block dependence: two book impressions do not automatically provide independent textual lineages. The blog references variant Four Transformations traditions; we have not authenticated those historical cells.

**Free access status:** The publisher's full facsimile is commercial; Google Play provides a sample and a retailer page shows sample images, but neither currently proves the needed `二兄弟` folios are available free or that we inspected them. No legitimate freely accessible, verified relevant-page scan of both original impressions was secured in this pass. A 52.6-MB third-party free-listing advertises a *different modern edited* edition (梁湘潤), not an authenticated matching woodblock witness; do not substitute it as such or treat availability as authorization to reproduce it.

## Consequences for Chinese research

1. Treat direct Unicode/UTF8/Wikitext hash checks as **transcription fidelity to one online source**, not reliable ancient glyph verification.
2. Flag the suspected modern sentence in Brothers, the `羊玲` doubtful compound, and star-condition punctuation as **source unresolved**; do not resolve using an online reproduction likely copied from the same source.
3. Independently collate source-critical rules that would materially alter actual computed charts first (e.g. year-stem Four Transformations `庚` cases), using both early printed witnesses if lawfully accessible. Preserve competing variants rather than choosing favorable human matches.
4. Mark older synthetic Zi Wei parity benchmarks as *algorithm-parity only* until the edition and calculation conventions are pinned. None is empirical human predictive validation.
5. Continue source-order annotation provisionally, but do not allow increased annotation count to substitute for an exact calendrical chart engine or preregistered real-human tests.
