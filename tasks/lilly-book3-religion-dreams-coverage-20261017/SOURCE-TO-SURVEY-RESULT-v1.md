# Lilly Book III: the questionnaire's overlooked religion and dream domains

**Source:** William Lilly, *Christian Astrology* (1647), Book III (Nativities), Chapter CXLII “Of the Religion of the Native,” printed pp.611–613; Chapter CXLIII “Of Dreames,” pp.613–614. Historical text is cross-checked using the original 1647 volume's archived OCR and a separately retyped version retaining printed page numbers. **Original page images, especially the planetary glyphs, have not been manually adjudicated**, so the clauses below remain a source-language ledger, not executable astrology predictions.

## The important correction

These are **natal, individual-chart passages**. They provide much stronger evidence that religion, conversion, divination and dream experience belong in Lilly's traditional *chart subject matter* than the earlier citation to generic ninth-house significations. The material does not demonstrate that Lilly's judgments are statistically reliable.

The source ledger contains **31 narrowly classified units**: 20 from religion (including one explicit interpretive restriction), and 11 from dreams. The records preserve printed pages, the historically asserted planetary/house/sign conditions, the described target, caveats and source-witness classification. Some religion aphorisms restate earlier conditions; count **source units**, not 31 statistically independent hypotheses.

Lilly offers examples of allegedly predicting *attachment to religion, piety, changes of religious affiliation, variability of belief, interest in divination and the kind of speech considered oracular*. For dreams, the text differentiates historical claims of dreams' supposed truthfulness from **themes of actual dream content**: dark or frightening situations, religious/preferment themes, quarrels, status and valuables, beauty and pleasure, learning/travel, and water/flying.

The text itself contains a crucial brake on deterministic interpretation. Lilly cautions that stellar influences do **not compel or cause** someone's actual religion; he says his rules indicate natural inclination. That is a historical qualification within his account, not an independent modern finding about astrology.

## What the existing V7 interview actually asks

The frozen V7 bank contains 79 question records in 29 scenario families. A complete question-text-only lexical search returns **zero direct instances** of the words religion, faith, dreams, visions, divination, prophecy, awe, wonder, spirituality, revelation or synchronicity. This **does not prove every related experience is absent semantically**: V7 includes broader questions on feelings, values, opinions, meaning of an activity and gradual life-stage change. But its scenario prompts do not directly measure the source domains under audit.

| Historical chart claim/topic | Possible neutral human observation | V7 coverage and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Piety and religious practice | Self-described belief, experience, and practice over time | No direct V7 prompt; the historical moral rankings are inappropriate as psychometric labels |
| Conversion/change of religion | Actual worldview or affiliation changes, sudden versus gradual | Development/life-phase scenes only indirectly relevant |
| Divination interest | Whether someone considers omens, dream messages, or divination, distinct from their conviction | No direct V7 prompt; interest is not prophetic ability |
| Dream content and emotional character | Recall frequency, recurring themes and emotional tone | No direct V7 dream prompt; old planetary dream categories should not be suggested to participants |
| Dreams taken as predictions | How often a participant retrospectively interprets a dream that way, with later appraisal | Measurable as an experience/report, not proof the dream foretold the event |
| Transcendent awe and sudden realization | Direct frequency and subjective intensity | **Neither chapter gives a quantified rule for these modern dimensions** |

## Six direct caveats about source interpretation

1. The 1647 OCR corrupts many astrological symbols and long-s spellings. The retyped witness supplies planet names but is not a critical edition. In particular, the aphorism about Jupiter/Venus *cazimi* and prophecy, and the reference to Spica, need comparison with the printed facsimile before any mathematical chart-predicate implementation.
2. Lilly's religion chapter uses historically normative Christian terms and derogatory categories for disapproved beliefs. Those are **his own judgments**, never neutral labels the interviewer should apply to someone today.
3. A historical assertion that a configuration predicts a person's **opinion or practice** is not the same claim as being physically guided by a higher power. Source tradition and subjective causal interpretation must remain separate.
4. An assertion about dreams having “corresponding effects” is a claimed predictive effect, not a method of verifying the accuracy of dreams or measuring how often they are recalled.
5. Specific religion and dream passages are useful **construct-discovery evidence**; they are not proof of a person's chart, nor can they be counted as human prediction tests.
6. The respondent whose life suggested these dimensions is a **development** participant for this research. Turning their answers into new chart scoring features and reranking their birth information would not be an untouched validation result.

## Candidate future instrument, not a live interview change

`OPTIONAL-RELIGION-DREAM-QUESTIONS-v0.json` contains **five entirely optional, nonleading prompts** about personal religious/spiritual orientation, substantial worldview change, interest in divination/omens/dream interpretation, dream recall/themes, and experiences of seemingly predictive dreams with later reassessment.

They complement—but do not replace—the separate optional awe, surprise, insight and meaning module. They are not integrated into the V7 bank, the current Custom GPT or the live Railway participant interview. **No completed participant should be asked them again merely to fill a chart topic**, and no answers map automatically to a planetary feature.

## Verified boundary and next source work

The original public OCR witness downloaded from the 1647 item matches its frozen SHA-256. Its normalized text confirms 12/12 short source anchor checks across the relevant chapters. The separate retyped source was accessible to the web index, but the laptop's direct HTTP request returned 403; it remains an independent *textual*, not exact-facsimile, witness. Source glyphs and ambiguous original spellings require manual page-image comparison for rule-level promotion.

This is a deliberately narrow, accepted **subject-coverage study**, not the complete Lilly Book III corpus, nor full Hellenistic/Jyotish coverage, nor a newly fitted natal model. The next priority is original facsimile adjudication of the few ambiguous conditions, plus extending the domain ledger to other natal Book III sections without conflating that with the parallel complete Lilly textual-extraction lane.
