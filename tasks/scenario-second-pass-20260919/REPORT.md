# Scenario-first survey — second review

19 September 2026. Design revision; pilot paused at the owner's request.

## Result and scope

This pass reviewed all **55 incoming question nodes** and reconciled all **73 inherited information-planning facets**, rather than sampling another three categories. Fifteen existing question texts were rewritten. Twenty optional probes were added for information that a short answer may omit. The available bank now contains 75 nodes, **not a required 75-question survey**. No claim of a shorter interview or measured respondent burden is made.

This is a same-context semantic/design review. It is not independent evaluation, a new 730-cell re-score of the fictional profiles, a real respondent study, or a test of the deployed interviewer. Source archives and historical answers remain unchanged. Old answers cannot validate revised questions. The earlier 73/73 result remains an inventory of author-assigned labels, not a certificate of semantic sufficiency or AstroHD recovery.

## 1. An action does not supply its reason

For the bare answer “Yes, I'd go,” the next useful question is **“What would make you say yes?”** That asks for the reason without suggesting guilt, sympathy, duty or enjoyment. It also avoids presuming the person wants to go: someone can agree reluctantly.

“Is that because you feel bad for them, or why?” names a candidate explanation before the person has supplied one. In this design, begin open. If the person later says they feel guilty, the next question may be “What would you feel guilty about?” If they have already explained that they enjoy seeing the friend, do not ask them to explain again or probe for a hidden guilt explanation. Mixed motives can coexist.

Store action, reported reason, reported feeling and stated conditions separately. None needs to become a global trait. An action-only answer is useful but cannot complete a motive field. A reason is also a self-description, not independent proof of its psychological cause.

The owner's conditional example about saying yes is **design feedback, not an actual answer to the opening scene**.

## 2. Missing questions were hidden by unusually complete fictional answers

The incoming bank targeted only 71 of the 73 named planning facets. There was no explicit target route for **adaptation to external roles** or **physical affection/sensuality**. The earlier authored people volunteered adaptation after a question about what they would not give up, and volunteered affectionate touch after a question about closeness. That made the study appear more complete than its question design warranted.

The revision supplies conditional probes for adaptation and physical closeness. Physical affection does not automatically establish sexual desire; that part remains unknown unless explicitly discussed. These are optional, role-appropriate topics, not compulsory intimate disclosure. No hypothetical question can make a person supply information they do not know or do not wish to disclose.

Other targets were too generous even where a node existed. Asking which room condition matters does not by itself establish its functional effect. Asking when to stop working does not describe recovery. Naming a next decision step does not describe how clarity changes over time. Questions about practical resources do not automatically answer status and ownership motives. Each now has a narrower interpretation and, where useful, a separate missing-information probe.

## 3. Several questions supplied the answer's premise

The bank still included questions equivalent to “what have you learned that lets you respond differently?” and “what do you need before relying on the friend again?” These presuppose change, learning, reduced reliance, or an intention to restore trust.

The revised development question asks what contributed **after a change has actually been reported**, without assuming improvement. The trust question permits unchanged trust, limited access, a decision not to restore the relationship, or an unknown future. Reaction-specific probes about recurrence, bodily signals, pressure, withdrawal and recovery require the matching reaction to have been reported first.

A deliberately imagined contrast is different. “For this version, suppose you still intend to deliver...” can introduce a counterfactual condition, provided it is identified as a new scene and is not stored as the person's previous behavior. A blanket rule that suppresses all imagined contrasts unless they really happened would defeat scenario-first elicitation; the revision separates contrasts from reaction-dependent follow-ups.

## 4. Some situations remained too abstract

The practical-change and value-exchange questions still required the respondent to invent their own audience, stakes and proposal. They now use a group-trip departure time and a shared-meal arrangement. The familiar-risk prompt no longer asserts that a warning arrived before reasoning; it permits deliberate checking or no warning. The room prompt no longer assumes a defect or forces a modification.

These are improved candidates, not demonstrated universal scenes. A participant can dispute applicability, supply a relevant assumption or choose another setting. Changes to stakes, role, familiarity or time perspective must remain visible in the record. A clarification request is not evidence of indecision or a personality trait.

## 5. Coverage must remain smaller than the source supports

The coverage plan retains the 73 existing identifiers for traceability but spells out compound parts, including interruption versus depth of focus, stopping versus recovery, consistency versus usefulness of a bodily reaction, and status versus ownership. These are planning locators, not 73 mandatory answers or a new scoring ontology.

There is no automatic semantic PASS from question IDs, keywords, source tags, or matching quotations. A quotation can prove that words were said without proving the associated interpretation. Parts absent from the answer stay missing. Explicit absence, unknown, declined, inapplicable and not yet asked are distinct. Ordinary reactions such as depletion after prolonged overload are not automatically distinctive or predictive.

## Private Chat answer preservation

The companion capture helper saves immutable event records outside both Git and Railway. It records the exact question/version, exact response, evidence mode, corrections and source relationships. Original records are preserved; corrections are appended. Exports are portable JSON, **not a tested Railway import format**. The helper does not monitor Chat or run in the background: the assistant must explicitly save and verify each received answer during its turn.

Raw owner answers, private record locators and content-derived hashes must not enter the public repository. The repository holds the design, capture protocol and non-sensitive verification receipt. A saved answer can be reused without asking the person to repeat it, but reuse must preserve that it came from this development Chat and the actual scenario version, rather than an untouched validation or live-app session.

At the start of this pass there were no actual pilot answers. The opening question, the owner's current design-feedback message and the pause can be preserved without inventing an answer. See the final verification receipt for the actually executed save and readback, rather than treating this description as proof of persistence.

## Verification interpretation

The included checks test full-node/facet inventory, references, question punctuation, explicit typed routing states, and private-write behavior. They do not parse natural language or certify semantic adequacy. The case review shows intended conversation behavior for short, mixed, missing, corrected and meta answers. All are constructed cases, not new respondent observations.

The full 55-row audit and revised bank are delivered with this report. The pilot remains paused; no new live pilot question is being asked in this response. No inference VM, app code, deployment, birth data or chart scoring was used.

## Method sources and limits

This pass reuses the existing question-evaluation approach rather than inventing a new psychometric method. CDC/NCHS cognitive-interviewing guidance supports open probes into how an answer was reached, with findings grounded in responses. GOV.UK question-page guidance supports one response task at a time, reuse of prior information and appropriate unknown responses. Neither source validates the wording here or requires importing government-form navigation controls into a conversational interview.

## Authority and design diagnosis

Current owner authority is another design review plus durable Chat-answer preservation, not app deployment or waking inference. The parent outcome remains open. No new mandatory validation campaign, model tournament, fixed question quota or compulsory memory exercise is introduced.

L0: inferred motives and compound targets add cognitive burden; require one missing distinction at a time. L1: concrete everyday scenes must not imply a correct personality. L2: preserve understandable, role-appropriate wording and explicit changes of premise. L3: separate stimulus, self-report and interpretation rather than manufacture evidence from fluent answers. L4: preserve pause, uncertainty, privacy and reversible corrections.
