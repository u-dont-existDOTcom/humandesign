Life Patterns · What each question can actually test

Research proposal, NOT a live questionnaire or validated birth decoder. Human Design links are hypotheses from theory publishers, not empirical proof. The interviewed participant never sees chart targets.

25 active questions audited · 12 theory hypotheses · 10 potential but not scoring-ready question/chart links · 15 questions with no direct chart link · 4 new draft questions.

Main finding: all 25 live questions lack explicit target links, which is appropriate inside a blinded interviewer. The missing component is a separate, source-anchored construct-to-chart scoring protocol. Most plausible links fail the question-to-claim match without better questions.

Current questions and detected limitations

TF1-A0 broad context

When your time is your own, do you usually protect uninterrupted time, look for company, or vary depending on who and what is involved?

What it can measure / what it does NOT prove

Observable dimension: company preference conditioned on closeness and available solitude

Real distinction needed: frequency of company-seeking vs protected solo time at comparable opportunity

Confounds: recent contact, obligations, money and energy

Chart hypothesis status: no chart claim — No defensible direct source link

No replacement proposed; may be useful qualitative context.

Four new supplemental question drafts (NOT LIVE)

NEW-DECISION-TIMING

On a consequential choice with no urgent deadline, what first tells you yes or no, and how—if at all—does that sense change over the next day or two?

Separate quick bodily reaction from emotional evolution and deliberate analysis; allow none/variable.

NEW-RECOGNIZED-ENTRY

Think of times you had useful input for a group. What determines whether you volunteer it immediately or wait until someone asks for your contribution?

Evidence must distinguish invitation/recognition from courtesy, role hierarchy and being asked to help.

NEW-EMOTIONAL-TIMING

When you care about a decision, does the answer usually feel stable from the beginning, or change with your feelings over time? Describe exceptions.

Do not count deliberate research or indecisiveness as an emotional wave.

NEW-IDENTITY-DIRECTION

Across major changes of work, home or social circles, which parts of your sense of direction have stayed stable and which have changed?

No identity label and no assumption that stable direction is better.

Preliminary chart hypotheses and strict limits

Type: Projector

Theory claim: Recognition for a specific contribution followed by an invitation matters for entering interpersonal roles; not simply liking praise.

Evidence, counterevidence and unknowns

Potential evidence: Repeated real role entry becomes easier or more effective when invited on the basis of specific recognized contribution, contrasted with matched uninvited advice.

Possible counterexample: Matched self-initiated/solicited contributions show no invitation-related difference across occasions.

Do not code from: Praise without invitation; ordinary politeness; role hierarchy; one anecdote without counterfactual.

Theory-publisher source · Proposed only; not scored or validated.

Authority: Splenic

Theory claim: A quick and non-repeating felt safety/correctness signal may precede reasoning, versus clarity emerging after time.

Evidence, counterevidence and unknowns

Potential evidence: A subtle bodily signal usually precedes explicit reasoning on real consequential decisions, is immediate and does not recur with deliberation.

Possible counterexample: Matched real choices are repeatedly decided only after delayed emotional shifts, with no reported early bodily signal.

Do not code from: Learned bodily vocabulary; obvious danger cues; general anxious feelings; theoretical authority labels.

Theory-publisher source · Proposed only; not scored or validated.

Authority: Emotional

Theory claim: For personally consequential decisions, clarity may change over time with an emotional wave rather than being immediate.

Evidence, counterevidence and unknowns

Potential evidence: The reported yes/no about real important choices predictably changes as emotions settle over time rather than feeling decisively clear at the outset.

Possible counterexample: Decisions across matched important choices have immediate stable clarity despite emotional changes.

Do not code from: More information arriving over time; avoidance of accountability; minor preferences.

Theory-publisher source · Proposed only; not scored or validated.

Solar Plexus: undefined

Theory claim: A close person’s mood may be felt or amplified, with relief or return toward baseline after separation; distinct from empathy.

Evidence, counterevidence and unknowns

Potential evidence: Person reports mood intensification in proximity to an upset other, then return toward baseline after separation in comparable situations.

Possible counterexample: No detectable affective shifts across repeated exposure to others’ strong moods, with context and relationship controlled.

Do not code from: General compassion, interpersonal conflict directed at participant, preexisting mood, acute mental-health changes.

Theory-publisher source · Proposed only; not scored or validated.

Root: undefined

Theory claim: Interpersonal urgency can create pressure to hurry even when the objective deadline has not changed.

Evidence, counterevidence and unknowns

Potential evidence: Imposed urgency changes felt pressure/pace despite constant objective deadline, and pressure drops when interpersonal urgency is removed.

Possible counterexample: Equal objective deadlines and workload produce similar pace despite different external urgency signals across cases.

Do not code from: Urgent requests with genuine penalties; practical prioritization; medical anxiety.

Theory-publisher source · Proposed only; not scored or validated.

Sacral: defined

Theory claim: Sustained access to work energy for suitably engaging activity; effects of prolonged extreme load are a separate question.

Evidence, counterevidence and unknowns

Potential evidence: Reports sustainable interest-matched ordinary work compared with similarly engaged peers, with clear duration and recovery qualifiers.

Possible counterexample: Repeating large depletion during ordinary feasible workloads despite sleep, health, motivation and workload matching.

Do not code from: Extraordinary all-night work, health or sleep disorders, noncomparable work intensity, success/self-image.

Evidence, counterevidence and unknowns

Potential evidence: Freely adopted important feasible commitments are repeatedly delivered after enthusiasm wanes, with separately reported selection threshold.

Possible counterexample: Repeated failures of deeply chosen feasible important promises without changed conditions or external obstacles.

Do not code from: Casual favors, imposed duties, coercion, inaccessible resources and moral judgments.

How to interpret a test correctly

No source or chart feature has been empirically validated here. Never code general politeness, ordinary prudent decisions or question critiques as support. Abstain for ambiguity. Chart absence never implies the opposite personality. A new independent birth-data cohort must be scored against a fixed baseline and matched decoys after frozen mapping, coding and candidate-universe hashes.
