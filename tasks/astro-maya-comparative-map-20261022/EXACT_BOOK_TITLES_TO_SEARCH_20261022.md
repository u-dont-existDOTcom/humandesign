## Already owned: useful immediately

1. **Barbara Tedlock, *Time and the Highland Maya*, revised edition (1992).** Owner EPUB readable by individual `content/page_N.xml` files despite broken embedded `title=test` metadata. Priority living K'iche'/Momostenango daykeeper traditions; read pp89–103 first.
2. **Gabrielle Vail & Anthony F. Aveni (eds.), *The Madrid Codex: New Approaches to Understanding an Ancient Maya Manuscript* (2004).** PDF **461 pages**. Priority Ch5 calendar and divinatory almanacs, Ch6 comparative codices, Ch7 Haab dates, original PDF pp170, 186 and 210.
3. **Edward M. Reingold & Nachum Dershowitz, *Calendrical Calculations: The Ultimate Edition* (2018).** PDF **663 pages**. Chapter 11, printed pp169–184/actual PDF pp214+, exact Tzolk'in, Haab and Long Count algorithms; distinguish mathematical GMT correlation **584283** from alternative 584285 and living daykeeper continuity.
4. **Anthony F. Aveni, *Skywatchers: A Revised and Updated Version of Skywatchers of Ancient Mexico* (2001).** PDF **428 pages**. Historical astronomy/calendar/codex interpretation. No assumption of natal personality traits.
5. **Jeff Wheeler, *Doomsday Match (The Dresden Codex)* (2023)** exists on owner machine but is a **modern fictional thriller**. **Exclude** from historical manuscript/source counts. To read the actual Dresden Codex use the original digitized manuscript at the Saxon State and University Library Dresden: https://www.slub-dresden.de/en/explore/manuscripts/the-dresden-maya-codex .
