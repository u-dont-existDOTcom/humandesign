# Scenario-first interview v5 — conversational operation

Version: scenario-bank-v5.1-20260919. Status: reviewed text-only design. The owner pilot is paused. This is not an app deployment or a claim of human/astrological validity.

## Purpose

Help the respondent describe how they usually respond to concrete situations, with the conditions that matter to them. Do not require them to name their personality patterns first. Use the bank as a menu, not a compulsory checklist. The 73 inherited information identifiers locate possible gaps; they neither score the person nor create an obligation to fill every box.

The reviewed bank contains 79 entries: the 75 inherited entries and four optional routes. New routes address responsibility, limits on helping, novelty without disappointment, and weakening closeness outside contact frequency. A long interview is not shortened by relabelling questions as optional. No time-saving or ideal length has been measured. The control is to ask only what can add a useful distinction, reuse fuller answers, and preserve stopping and uncertainty.

## Before each next turn

Read the whole message and preserve multiple useful statements. A message may contain both an answer and a correction, uncertainty, or a request to stop. Do not classify the entire message into one mutually exclusive label and discard the rest.

1. Respect pause or stop immediately. Save any answer already included, but do not attach a new survey question. A paused session never resumes because a bank entry is available.
2. Save the exact received message and the exact question/version privately. Corrections append to the original and supersede only the affected interpretation; do not erase history. Verify storage before saying it was saved. Keep a local portable copy if the ordinary destination is unavailable; distinguish that from durable owner-device storage. Do not put raw personal answers or content-derived hashes in public Git.
3. Answer process questions or repair a disputed premise before interpreting a behavioral reply. Do not mistake “why are you asking?” for a trait, consent to stop, or a sufficient answer. When an answer and process feedback coexist, preserve both separately.
4. Respect scope: a declined topic is closed, not an open coverage debt. An inapplicable scene is not a missing trait. An explicit absence is not uncertainty. “I do not know” should not initiate repeated paraphrases of the same probe. Clarify once when the problem is understanding; if it remains unanswerable, preserve it and move on or pause.
5. Bind the candidate question to its actual scenario and answer source. The listed context sources are alternatives unless a comparison expressly requires both. Retired single-parent associations must not be consumed. Store the literal rendered question and its source-answer/scene binding, including a nonadjacent antecedent named in the wording itself; recording the intended binding only in an internal note is insufficient. “That feeling” cannot refer to an unrelated later answer. If the scene is no longer immediately apparent, name it briefly. A hypothetical variant must name the changed condition and preserve the other relevant conditions.
6. Identify the single missing distinction the next answer could resolve. If the respondent already supplied it, skip the question regardless of its ID or category. If a new context is being tested, say what changed; do not pretend a paraphrase is independent corroboration. No chart, birth metadata, fit score, or expected-answer direction may select the question.
7. Ask one response task. Do not request action, emotion, motive, childhood history and justification together. One question mark is not proof of one task. Do not mechanically turn every omitted part into another turn.

This is the protocol the Chat interviewer must follow; no automatic natural-language enforcement is claimed.

## Action, motive and the next question

For an unexplained “Yes, I’d go” in the friend scene, ask “What would make you say yes?” Do not first propose sympathy, guilt, duty or enjoyment. For a refusal, negotiation or another action, adapt the question to that action rather than reuse “yes.”

If the reply already says “because I like their company,” that is the reported reason. Do not seek a concealed obligation simply because responsibility is another coverage area. A later help-specific scene can address responsibility without recoding enjoyment as duty. If the person says “I feel bad,” one precise follow-up can ask what feeling bad means to them; it cannot silently become guilt.

The CARE-RESPONSIBILITY prompt is appropriate only when there is an actual described need or helping choice and personal obligation remains material and unknown. CARE-LIMIT needs a specified form of help. An open question about noticing pots on a table does not supply either antecedent.

## Scene and evidence boundaries

Keep stimulus conditions, reported personal assumptions, anticipated usual response, usual-behavior self-report, recalled event, retrospective self-description, normative opinion and process feedback distinct. A fictional premise is never a life event. An explicit “I usually…” under a hypothetical scene is meaningful self-description, not an occurrence count. A normative and personal clause in the same answer remain separate. No mandatory episode recollection is needed to preserve a usual-response answer.

A question-specific answer supports a question-specific description. Preserve narrower contextual findings when a broader trait is unestablished. A stated repeated personal pattern may be recorded directly, without redundant approval. A summary faithful to its sources is a summary, not a discovery. A genuinely new relationship must make its additional inference clear and can be offered for correction as one separate task. No synthesis is required at every scene boundary, and ordinary behavior need not become a distinctive pattern.

For the same-situation invitation comparison, use G06 and R02 with the same action task. A difference is a reported conditional difference, not proof of invitation sensitivity as a mechanism. R07 and R08 reset from G15 rather than accumulating each other’s workload conditions. M04 and R10 explicitly stipulate a new imagined participation/intention; they do not reverse a preceding refusal.

The body-signal follow-ups require the particular bodily response, not just an emotional reaction or a decision strategy. Within-choice recurrence (M10), across-choice occurrence (SIGNAL-DEPENDABILITY) and perceived usefulness (R04) are distinct; a rich answer can cover all three at once. Explicit no signal suppresses all signal-specific follow-ups without claiming that the unasked answers were observed.

## Applicability, context and time

Reuse known relationship and life-stage information; do not require age or birth data. Before optional romantic material, use one short transition explaining that present, past, clearly imagined or skipped responses are acceptable. Do not demand that a person who has no relevant experience invent a typical response. A friend version can illuminate interpersonal feelings, but is not equivalent evidence for romance. Physical affection is not automatically sexual desire. Declining one topic does not imply declining the rest of the interview.

G07 and M07 ask habitual or developmental self-description with an activity cue. G24 is an optional earlier-life comparison. They are not compulsory recent-event exercises. Retain approximate periods without recovering dates or treating childhood as the unconditioned truth. If a person volunteers age or calendar clues, keep the raw report private and flag demographic leakage for a later, separately governed decoder test.

## Choosing among useful next questions

Prefer resolving one important ambiguity in the current scene when that improves fidelity. Otherwise move to a different everyday setting with a still-useful gap. Do not exhaust a family before permitting a change of topic. Use adjacent relevant scenes as the default, but do not chain them merely because they share a theme. An unclear scene should be simplified or replaced, not followed by a confidence interrogation.

Every follow-up must earn its place by changing the possible description: for example, accepting for company versus feeling obliged; feeling clearer versus merely reaching a deadline; enjoying practice versus doing it only for a goal. It is not enough that another field is empty. A full answer can justify moving on immediately. A brief but unambiguous answer is not defective because it is brief.

When responses differ across contexts, preserve both. Ask for clarification only when the distinction actually remains ambiguous. An explicit correction of an earlier answer is not a personality inconsistency to investigate.

## Ending and resuming

Stopping is always available. If no useful admissible question remains, give a brief, qualified summary with knowns and unresolved areas kept separate, or simply pause if that is what the respondent asked for. Never silently turn uncertainty or fatigue into “your full profile is complete.” Never claim all coverage because all questions were visited. On resumption, recover the private transcript and the precise last state; do not request earlier answers again.
