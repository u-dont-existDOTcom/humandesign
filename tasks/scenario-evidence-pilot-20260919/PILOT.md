# First real-answer pilot

Status: ready for the first owner answer. No actual owner answers have been collected in this packet.

## Participant-facing opening

Imagine the ordinary version of yourself now, not the version giving the ideal answer. A remembered example is welcome but not required. You can say that a scene does not fit, explain a missing detail, skip, or stop.

You have an hour free and are about to read a book you have been enjoying. A close friend messages: "I'm bored. Want to hang out?" They just feel like company; nothing urgent has happened.

**What would you usually reply?**

## Interviewer's next move

Do not show this section as instructions to the participant.

Read the actual answer before selecting a continuation. Preserve the concrete reply and whatever motivation, feeling, condition, or uncertainty the person independently supplies. Accepting an invitation is not proof of duty. Declining is not proof of autonomy. Guilt is not proof of its cause.

If the person supplies the decisive conditions, do not ask them to supply them again. When only an action is supplied and a motive would materially clarify the description, a possible follow-up is "What would matter most in that choice?" Do not force this follow-up if the motive is already clear or the topic is unproductive.

A brief answer is not itself a defect. For example, "I'd go; I enjoy seeing them" establishes an enjoyable invitation response, not an obligation to regulate the friend's boredom. It is legitimate to move on and leave responsibility unassessed rather than interrogating that answer until it fits a category.

When a participant says "it depends," distinguish a scene omission from a condition they are already explaining. Repair an omitted decisive fact explicitly and return to the same response task. Do not code the clarification as indecision, personality, assent, or consent to end.

When moving topics, a natural next candidate is G01: helping arrange a trip with conflicting practical details. The available bank supplies other options. The actual next question depends on the answer; none is queued as though that answer were already known.

## Coverage discipline

Question served, answer captured, and information supported are different states. Keep a source-linked narrow description before considering broader relationships. Reuse volunteered information across relevant areas without pretending repeated paraphrases are independent observations.

This pilot uses the 73-check inventory as a planning aid, not a quota the participant must satisfy. An honest partial profile is preferable to invented completeness. An absent reaction can make a reaction-specific follow-up inapplicable without proving the entire domain or an HD feature.

No mandatory recent-event retrieval, no birth data, no chart cues, no forced percentages, no routine approval of paraphrases, and no mandatory synthesis at scene boundaries. Separate current usual predictions from retrospective reports and genuinely hypothetical guesses.

## Stop and evaluation

The participant can stop at any point. Otherwise continue with the next useful question, without asking for a routine Continue confirmation. There is no fixed question-count completion rule.

Observe whether the participant can answer, whether important context was missing, and whether a follow-up adds rather than repeats information. Invite process feedback when a problem appears rather than adding a feedback questionnaire after each answer.

Do not claim success before this interaction actually occurs. Do not use this development pilot as evidence of independent chart recovery or as permission to wake inference or deploy.
