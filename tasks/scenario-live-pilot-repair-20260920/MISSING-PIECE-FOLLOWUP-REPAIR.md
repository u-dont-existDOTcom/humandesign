# Live pilot repair — ask only the missing piece — 2026-09-22

## Finding

In the B0 live scene, the respondent first supplied what they would be wondering and what they might do, while the target mood remained unspecified. The interviewer then replayed the whole scene instead of acknowledging the supplied material and asking only for the missing mood.

This violated the existing protocol principle that a follow-up must identify the single missing distinction and must not mechanically re-ask material already supplied.

## Repair rule

When a response supplies one or more useful parts of a scene but one target remains missing:

1. Explicitly acknowledge the supplied part in ordinary language.
2. Name the single missing distinction.
3. Ask only that missing piece.
4. Do not restate the full scenario unless needed to repair referential ambiguity.
5. Do not interpret the supplied action/process as if it answered a different target such as mood.

Example:

> Okay, you would be wondering what kind of support they want. And what happens to your mood while you are wondering that?

Not:

> [Replay the entire original scene.] What happens to your mood?

## Evidence handling

If process feedback and a substantive answer arrive in the same message, preserve both separately. The process feedback is not trait evidence; the substantive clause may still answer the target.

## Generalization

Apply this across all families: action vs motive, mood vs behavior, bodily signal vs decision rule, stopping cue vs recovery, and any other multi-part scene where the respondent already supplied some but not all useful information.
