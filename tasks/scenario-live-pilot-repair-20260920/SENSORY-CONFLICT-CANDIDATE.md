# Candidate item — cross-person sensory conflict — 2026-09-20

## Origin

Live pilot owner suggestion. Do not treat the owner's comparative claim that this behavior is rare as established population evidence.

## Construct

Response to **conflicting sensory evidence from another person** when one's own perception does not detect the reported environmental hazard.

This is not identical to:
- mold concern itself;
- generic environmental sensitivity;
- generic interpersonal trust;
- intuition;
- novelty seeking.

Current partial overlaps:
- `D11.conditions`: environmental/sensory conditions attended to;
- `X04.context_and_limits`: what counts as a risk cue and how far a cue is relied upon.

No automatic AstroHD credit is authorized from this item until a later mapping review confirms the fit.

## Candidate question

> You walk into a room that smells normal to you. A friend you generally trust says, “This smells moldy.” You still cannot smell anything unusual. What would you do next?

## Interpretation boundary

Admissible answers include:
- treating the other person's report as meaningful evidence;
- checking with another person or instrument;
- inspecting or leaving the room;
- discounting the report;
- remaining uncertain.

Do **not** infer:
- objective mold presence;
- superior or inferior sensory ability;
- gullibility or skepticism;
- a global tendency to trust or distrust people;
- health anxiety;
- population rarity.

The useful evidence is the respondent's **rule for resolving a sensory disagreement in this specific hazard context**.

## Current participant evidence

The owner volunteered that if another person smells mold that they do not smell, they find it concerning, would trust the report, and would consider sensory adaptation as an explanation for their own non-detection.

Retain this only as the participant's self-report. Do not treat the claim that most other people behave differently as established without external data.
