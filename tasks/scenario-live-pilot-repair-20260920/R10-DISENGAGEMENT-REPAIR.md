# Live pilot repair — R10 disengagement variant — 2026-09-20

## Finding

The canonical R10 wording:

> For this version, suppose you chose to join the project and have put a month into it, but progress is poor. What would make you stop investing in it?

is under-specified. “Progress is poor” does not identify:
- the expected baseline;
- whether the cause is controllable;
- whether the obstacle is temporary (for example weather);
- whether the obstacle is social/organizational (for example group conflict);
- whether attempts to repair the problem have already failed.

A respondent therefore has to invent the main determinant before answering the intended `D15.continue_disengage` distinction.

## Disposition

Treat the owner’s live response (“poor compared to what?... group politics or too much rain?”) as design feedback, not as a substantive answer to R10.

## Replacement live variant

Keep the same important-work project from G16, but specify the obstacle:

> Suppose you joined that important project. After a month, very little work is getting done because the group keeps getting stuck in internal disagreements, and attempts to sort out the disagreements have not helped much. What would you usually do at that point?

Interpretation boundary:
- continuing, changing role/strategy, taking a break, or leaving are all admissible;
- this is a response to persistent organizational blockage, not to weather or an uncontrollable temporary delay;
- do not generalize one response into a universal persistence trait;
- do not infer aversion to conflict unless the respondent says so.

## Design implication

If different causes of slow progress are scientifically important, they must be separate matched variants rather than hidden inside the phrase “progress is poor.”
