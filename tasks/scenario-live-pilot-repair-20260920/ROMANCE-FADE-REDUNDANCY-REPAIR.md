# Live pilot repair — ROMANCE-FADE inverse redundancy — 2026-09-22

## Finding

The canonical `ROMANCE-FADE` prompt:

> In the romantic connection you are considering, what, if anything, would usually make you feel less close over time?

was asked after the participant had already answered G13:

> more safety, more freedom and attraction over time

In this live case, the weakening prompt mostly elicited the inverse of already-supplied dimensions: less desire, less love, less respect, less safety. The participant explicitly experienced the question as obvious/repetitive.

## Disposition

- Preserve genuinely new additions from the later answer, here `love` and `respect`.
- Do not treat mirrored opposites as independent corroboration.
- Mark the later prompt as largely redundant for this participant.
- Do not ask a positive/negative mirror pair merely to fill both sides of a facet.

## Admission repair

Before asking `ROMANCE-FADE`, compare the actual G13 answer with the missing distinction.

Skip the prompt when the earlier answer already names dimensions whose ordinary weakening is sufficient for the intended description, unless there is a specific scientifically useful asymmetry to test.

Examples:
- `more safety helps closeness` usually already supports that reduced safety can weaken closeness at the scoped descriptive level;
- do **not** assume every positive condition has a symmetric negative effect when that asymmetry itself matters;
- ask only when a genuinely additional weakening pathway remains useful, such as contempt, repeated boundary violations, incompatible goals, or another respondent-supplied dimension not recoverable from the positive answer.

## General rule

Inverse wording is not independent evidence. A follow-up must add a distinction, not simply negate an earlier answer.
