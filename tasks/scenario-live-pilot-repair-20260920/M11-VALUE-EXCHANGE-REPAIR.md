# Live pilot repair — M11 value-exchange premise — 2026-09-21

## Finding

The canonical M11 prompt:

> You offer to organise a shared meal if someone else buys the ingredients. They are interested but unsure about the arrangement. How would you explain your proposal?

does not state why the respondent made that offer.

In live use, the participant immediately identified at least two materially different motives:
- they may not have enough money for the ingredients;
- they may be contributing organising/hosting labour and therefore see the other person's ingredient purchase as the fair counterpart.

Those motives change the explanation. The respondent should not have to invent the hidden rationale before answering the intended `X08.offer_and_leverage` construct.

## Disposition

- Treat the participant's “why did I propose that?” as design feedback.
- Preserve the two volunteered conditional motives as scoped possibilities, not as evidence that either is their usual motive.
- Do not infer persuasion style from that response.

## Replacement scene

Use one explicit rationale:

> You offer to organise and cook a shared meal, which will take several hours of your time. You can afford the ingredients, but you think a fair split would be for the other person to buy them while you do the organising and cooking. They are interested but unsure about that arrangement. How would you explain it?

Interpretation boundary:
- this measures how the participant presents a proposed exchange and responds to uncertainty;
- the fairness premise is supplied by the scene and is not evidence about the participant's broader values;
- asking what the other person is unsure about is a valid response;
- do not score pitch quality, sales ability, manipulation, or likely success.

## Design implication

If affordability itself is worth studying, use it as a separate matched variant. Do not leave the motive latent.
