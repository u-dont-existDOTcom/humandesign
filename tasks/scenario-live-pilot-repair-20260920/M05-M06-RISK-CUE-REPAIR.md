# Live pilot repair — M05/M06 familiar-risk-cue transfer — 2026-09-21

## Finding

The M05/M06 pair is only informative when the cue named in M05 is one whose meaning plausibly depends on prior familiarity with that kind of situation.

In live use, the participant named:
- inability to obtain a ticket;
- a ticket price that is too high;
- a driver who appears intoxicated.

These are not all the same kind of evidence:
- ticket availability and price are direct practical constraints;
- an apparently intoxicated driver is a direct safety cue;
- none of these requires familiarity with the journey type in order to matter.

The live M06 follow-up using the expensive-ticket cue therefore did not validly test `X04.context_and_limits`.

## Disposition

- Treat the live response “obviously it’s wrong ... why fly if it’s too expensive” as design feedback, not as evidence about transfer of a familiar risk cue.
- Retain M05 only narrowly:
  - high price / no ticket = practical constraints;
  - apparently intoxicated driver = direct safety cue.
- Leave `X04.context_and_limits` unresolved for this participant.
- Do not ask M06 unless the M05 cue is genuinely familiarity-dependent.

## Admission repair

Before M06, classify the M05 cue:

1. **Direct constraint** — e.g. unavailable, unaffordable, prohibited.
   - Do not ask M06.
2. **Direct hazard cue** — e.g. visibly intoxicated driver, active fire.
   - Do not use familiarity transfer as the main distinction.
3. **Familiar-pattern cue** — e.g. a deviation from how this route/process normally works whose significance comes from prior experience.
   - M06 may be useful.

## Better example for future testing

> On a route you know well, the driver suddenly takes a turn that is normally never used for that destination. What would you make of that?

Only after the respondent gives a familiarity-based interpretation should an unfamiliar-context transfer probe be considered.
