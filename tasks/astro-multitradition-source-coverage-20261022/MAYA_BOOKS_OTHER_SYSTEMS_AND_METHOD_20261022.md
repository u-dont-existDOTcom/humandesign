# Which Maya astrology books we need, and which other systems materially matter

22 October 2026 — source-discovery audit only. **None of these Maya source books was comprehensively read during this pass**. Ranked source URLs are verified institutional/publisher research leads, not file acquisitions. Original source bytes, Maya actual source-derived personal divination rules, natal calendar conversion parity, and human validation remain unverified. *Astrology* here is a modern umbrella term for distinct astronomic calendars, daykeeping, birth-day divination and independent ritual/electional advice; it does **not** imply every old Maya codex supplies a modern personality horoscope.

### Additional institutional original material and computation checks (free sites)

- **[Other Maya codices index](https://www.slub-dresden.de/en/explore/manuscripts/the-dresden-maya-codex/other-maya-codices)**: Paris Codex (including calendric cycles and a zodiac), Madrid Codex, and Maya Codex of Mexico (Venus). Each manuscript should have its **own** source ID and folio/citation witnesses; this page is an institutional guide, not an automatic text transcription.
- **[FAMSI original codices facsimile portal](https://www.famsi.org/mayawriting/codices/)**: additional manuscript facsimiles with commentary; preserve ancient manuscript, modern folio reproduction and contemporary edited translation distinctly.
- **[FAMSI Mayan Long Count date converter](https://research.famsi.org/date_mayaLC.php)**: explicit selectable correlation constant, usually default **GMT 584283**. Freeze Julian-day-number and midnight/noon convention, Gregorian/Julian style, day-offset and regional tradition **before** any birth-day feature is computed; test actual dated input/output witness pairs against independent algorithms.
- **[Smithsonian Living Maya Time calendar](https://maya.nmai.si.edu/calendar)** and associated [indigenous daykeeper material](https://maya.nmai.si.edu/corn-and-maya-time): institutional original contemporary Indigenous participant statements and culture-based 260-day practice. **Automated access returned 403** from the authorized laptop (and one external fetch failed), so human open access requires fresh verification; do not claim this site was programmatically read or indexed in full. The Smithsonian 260-day day names are cross-referenced with K’iche’ Chol Q’ij and Yucatec Tzolk’in, but translating the **name alone** does not transmit an ethnographically attested personal description.
- **[Dennis Tedlock, *Popol Vuh*](https://www.si.edu/object/popol-vuh-mayan-book-dawn-life-translated-dennis-tedlock-commentary-based-ancient-knowledge-modern%3Asiris_sil_724998)** (revised translation 1996): worth reading for K’iche’ origin cosmology; **not** a direct natal fate/personality manual and low priority for a first blinded behavioral test.

### Three historical identities that cannot be combined under the label “Mayan astrology”

1. **Ancient Maya codices and Classic/Postclassic calendar**: Tzolk’in 260-day cycle, Haab 365-day vague year, Calendar Round, Long Count, eclipse and Venus tables; event/ritual/collective interpretations may dominate rather than independent natal individual traits.
2. **Living K’iche’ Chol Q’ij daykeeping**: contemporary Indigenous community interpretation, 20 named days, 13 numerical day coefficients, contextual divination and ceremonial responsibilities. Study direct participants and ethnography; distinguish modern developments from earliest extant Maya historical texts.
3. **Modern Dreamspell**: modern late twentieth-century system. It may be a separately testable set of *modern* propositions, but must **never** silently supply alleged ancient codex rules.

**Maya calendar conversion is not astrology validation**: mathematically recovering a 260-day sign or Long Count date is engineering parity. To test a traditional divinatory claim, find the **actual original/living source statement**, stable date/input, outcome definition, alternative/negative contrast, and freeze prediction before seeing participant answers. No such original-source-to-human questionnaire mapping is accepted yet in the project.

| **4 — history/method origin** | **Babylonian horoscopy and celestial omens** | Francesca Rochberg, [*The Heavenly Writing*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/the-heavenly-writing/9A24735C23CA63EF8E1819B283B39F47), 2004 | Actual earliest recorded birth horoscopes and celestial omen historical distinction; foundational for comparing lineage and source rule selection, but direct modern behavioral utility may be lower. |
| **5 — different question type** | **Chinese Qi Men Dun Jia and related electional/question divination** | First identify a verifiable older Chinese manual/edition, not an unauthenticated modern manual | Tests might address question/event/action time rather than natal personality. Do **not** make it a missing mandatory personality question domain. |
| **6 — optional breadth** | **Burmese/Thai weekday natal astrology** | First verified local-language ethnographic or original practice editions | Potentially distinct simple day/week birth predicates. Unknown source transmission and weak evidential integration; only after high-priority methods. |

Extra systems with *divination but not necessarily celestial natal horoscope* methods (e.g., Ifá, raml/geomancy, Tarot, runes) belong outside the natal astrology scope unless the owner wants a separate parallel prediction track with their own original cultural sources, ethical context and independent controls. Modern “Celtic tree zodiac” and one-click “Maya Kin personality” websites should NOT substitute for original documentation.

## C. Are we working correctly? The material correction

**Partially.** The original text/documentation work is extensive and useful, but its numerical volume is a *proxy metric*. The central empirical outcome—whether an independently calculated chart makes accurate out-of-sample behavioral predictions—has **zero actual validated human results**.

The repository already has a substantial **Human Design-specific** `ARCHITECTURE.md`, source mapping evidence protocol, Survey-v2 domains/dependencies, and a forward-blind protocol. Separate Chinese/Jyotish/Tibetan/Western source lanes and synthetic chart parity studies exist. What was missing was a **living cross-tradition ontology and graph** that connects source passages to their actual chart predicates, independent calendar computation, the observable question, overlaps with existing questionnaire domains, pre-response freeze, controls and actual results.

That is now explicitly drafted in [the cross-tradition living map](MULTITRADITION_SOURCE_TO_TEST_MAP.md), with [15 ranked Maya source records](MAYA_RANKED_SOURCE_REGISTER_V1.json), [7 comparative other-tradition classes](ADDITIONAL_TRADITIONS_PRIORITY_REGISTER_V1.json) and [20 source-domain/survey crosswalk rows](TRADITION_CONSTRUCT_AND_SURVEY_CROSSWALK_V1.json). These are **research-only** and not integrated into the main chart-to-question scorer or the user interview. The six existing Survey-v2 domains remain HD-specific. Human personality data should not be asked blindly just because a traditional book contains a metaphysical word like Mars, Venus, wealth or fate.

### More useful than reading every book before testing

The efficient workflow is **coverage inventory → exact source-critical rules → certified calculation policy for those features → observable with explicit negative contrast → dependency/overlap audit → freeze model/decoy/null → untouched participant**. Continue reading only sections that can change credible candidate claims or resolve actual calculation ambiguities; keep separate traditional claims and historical cosmology as background. Multiple traditions can share zodiac source ancestry, so correlated identical claims cannot be counted as independent successes.
