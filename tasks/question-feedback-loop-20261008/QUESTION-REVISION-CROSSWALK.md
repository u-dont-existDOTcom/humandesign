# Prospective question wording revision — development draft, NOT live

This edits **9 current tendency-first wordings**, in a separately versioned proposed bank. It is not the frozen v7 survey or the current TF1 v1 runtime policy. The retirement list also adds three historically weak optional routes: ROMANCE-FADE, ROOM-EFFECT and CORRECTION-REASON.

The source is one development participant. No accuracy or population discrimination claim is made. The next methodological step is an independently administered cognitive interview comparing the existing and revised versions, followed by a new prospective freeze.

## TF1-M11

Old: When sharing work or costs with someone, how do you usually work out an arrangement?

Revised: When negotiating how to share work or costs, what considerations usually carry the most weight for you?

Why: Ask for the deciding considerations, not an obviously fair split under forced practical assumptions.

## TF1-D0

Old: When practising a skill, how do you usually feel about repeating and refining a small detail?

Revised: When you can already perform a skill adequately, what, if anything, makes refining a small detail worth repeating?

Why: Separate the intrinsic value of repetition from rational practice toward a specific result.

## TF1-G10

Old: When joining a group with a different way of doing things, how much of your own usual approach tends to change?

Revised: When you join a group with routines different from yours, how do you decide what to adapt and what to keep?

Why: Elicit an actual adaptation boundary without presuming fixed resistance or demanding a repeat of a settled answer.

## TF1-M03

Old: How do small voluntary commitments usually play out for you after the initial enthusiasm fades?

Revised: When a small voluntary commitment becomes less appealing, what normally determines whether you complete it, renegotiate, or drop it?

Why: Elicit the boundary behind follow-through rather than treating waning enthusiasm as dishonesty.

## TF1-G15

Old: What kinds of activity tend to leave you energized or drained?

Revised: How does an ordinary amount of work you find worthwhile affect your energy, and what changes when the workload becomes unusually long or stressful?

Why: Distinguish baseline work-energy response from fatigue under excessive load.

## TF1-G17

Old: When you notice a minor factual error that has no practical consequence, how much do you usually feel pulled to correct it? Important misinformation can be different; describe that difference only if it helps explain your pattern.

Revised: When you notice a harmless factual mistake, what normally determines whether you feel like correcting it or letting it pass?

Why: Capture the individual's low-stakes correction threshold beyond the obvious consequence calculation.

## TF1-STATUS

Old: How important is recognition from other people to you, apart from its practical benefits?

Revised: Apart from practical benefits, how, if at all, does recognition from people close to you affect you differently from recognition from others?

Why: Preserve relationship-qualified recognition instead of collapsing all praise into one trait.

## TF1-ROUTINE-CHANGE

Old: When you genuinely have a choice, do you usually seek new experiences, prefer familiar ones, or have different patterns in different areas?

Revised: In which parts of life do you usually prefer familiar routines, and in which do you tend to seek something new when both are available?

Why: Capture domain-specific novelty preference rather than a forced binary.

## TF1-G04

Old: When a method or set of instructions fails, how do you usually approach working out what to try next?

Revised: When a familiar approach stops working, what do you usually try first to figure out why?

Why: Avoid coding the obvious fact of troubleshooting as a personality finding.
