### Method

Three rules cover the main risks: "read existing answers first", "not a mandatory checklist" and "do not infer a broad trait from one example". The risks are an example being coded as a trait, and overlap between items (G15/G18 on time with people; ROUTINE-CHANGE and the familiarity candidate on familiar options). No extra skip logic is needed. No lead-in forces a self-label. Each is either open, asks for degree, or offers a "differs by area/activity/source" option.

### Errors needing repair

**1. Calendar familiarity candidate: a dominated choice remains.**
- "Meet your needs equally well" equalizes outcomes, not cost. The known approach still saves learning and switching effort, so preferring it is sensible whatever one's temperament.
- A high answer therefore can't separate familiarity itself from switching cost.
- This is the mirror image of turning "the new features are worth the effort" into adventurousness, which the same section rules out.

Repair (same structure as STATUS): "When two approaches meet your needs equally well, how much does already knowing one of them matter to you, apart from the time or effort it saves?"

**2. F0: politeness already prescribes the example's contrast.**
- Convention already calls for different phrasing with close friends than with people just met.
- Saying nothing to strangers can simply reflect having no standing to ask.
- So a reported difference can't be separated from following that norm.

Repair: "Beyond what ordinary politeness calls for, how much do you usually change the way you express the same preference to different people?" Using "to" also removes the misreading "a preference for different people".

**3. G18: the example varies enjoyment and demand together.**
- "A relaxed enjoyable visit and a demanding social day" can be answered from the descriptions alone: more of the pleasant one, rest after the demanding one.
- The informative cases are the ones the descriptions don't settle. An example is wanting time alone even after company one enjoyed.

Repair: "After time with people, including company you enjoyed, what usually makes you want more company versus time on your own? For example, whether length, group size or how demanding it was makes a difference."

**4. D0: the options mix a feeling with an appraisal.**
- "Enjoyable, mainly useful, tedious" are offered as alternatives.
- Refinement is useful almost by definition of practice, and "useful" fits with either enjoyment or tedium.
- Someone who finds it tedious but worthwhile has two applicable options. If they pick "useful", the tedium is never recorded.

Repair: "For example, repeating a pronunciation: the refining itself may feel enjoyable, neutral or tedious (whether or not you find it worthwhile), or differ across activities."
