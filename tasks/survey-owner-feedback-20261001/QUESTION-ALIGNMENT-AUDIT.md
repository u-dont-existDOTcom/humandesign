# Life Patterns: question-to-objective alignment audit

**Status: development candidate, not a validated instrument and not silently activated.** Frozen v7 source files, mappings and historical records are unchanged. All 79 routes were individually reviewed. A disposition is an engineering/method judgment, not a population result.

## Main finding

The bank often measures a narrow, defensible response to one situation while the product is expected to reveal a recurring pattern. Correctly forbidding a broad inference does not make that narrow answer useful for the broader objective. Several prompts are also economical descriptions of sensible cost-benefit decisions; their answer variation may reflect the supplied constraint rather than the intended preference.

Some items already ask general recurring behavior and need no rewrite. Many others should remain conditional probes, not a questionnaire to administer in full. The hybrid candidate starts with the usual pattern, adds an example only to clarify its meaning, then asks a boundary only when it can change the interpretation. It does not force a new anecdote after an adequate direct answer.

## Why the earlier checks were insufficient

The attribution/quotation/absence fix lane tests source fidelity, not whether an item identifies a general tendency. The independent service reviews a supplied route under the bank's existing narrow authority; it does not independently redesign that authority on every turn. Earlier calibration did flag tautology and construct mismatch, but contract-presence checks and later implementation tests did not establish that those failures were absent from actual participant use. Exact-wording restrictions can preserve a weak question as faithfully as a good one.

No claim is made that most people empirically give the same answer: that requires response data. The audit identifies concrete logical alternatives and likely low-information cases for targeted participant pretesting. Approval by another model is not a substitute for those data.

## How to test the replacement

Use a small, versioned cognitive pretest, not another complete interview for somebody whose answers already exist. For each trial, check what the participant thought the question meant, whether the example changed that meaning, whether the answer changes the intended reading versus an ordinary rational baseline, and whether an existing answer already settled it. Preserve both direct and example answers when they diverge; do not let the example silently overrule the general report. Measure actual burden, redundancy, and answer usefulness before switching the production instrument.

Disposition counts: replace: 18, retain: 15, probe: 41, demote: 5.

## A0 — replace

**Current question:** You have an hour free and are about to read a book you've been enjoying. A close friend messages that they're bored and asks if you want to hang out. They just feel like company; nothing urgent has happened. What would you probably reply?

**Current permitted scope:** Retain the owner-preferred opening. Yes establishes an anticipated reply, not guilt, generosity, obligation or actually owning an enjoyable book.

**Finding:** A single invitation decision confounds recent contact, desire for company and current activity. It is not a social-orientation measure.

**Hybrid candidate (HYB0-A0):** When your time is your own, do you usually protect uninterrupted time, look for company, or vary depending on who and what is involved? For example, a friend invites you out while you are enjoying something alone.

This is a new development item. Its answer is not automatically equivalent to the old scenario's facet contract.

## G23 — retain

**Current question:** You arrive at a casual shared meal with a few friends or acquaintances you know. People are getting things ready, and nobody has asked you to take on any particular job. What tends to catch your attention first?

**Current permitted scope:** Retain only what the person says catches their attention in this familiar social-meal setting. Omitted needs are not unnoticed needs. A responsibility probe still requires a particular need or helping choice.

**Finding:** Open description of what attracts attention can be useful; do not turn one dinner observation into a global noticing rank.

## G19 — replace

**Current question:** You promised to help a friend sort things for a move, but it is taking longer than expected and using up time you had set aside for yourself. Your friend is open to changing the arrangement. What would matter most in deciding what to do?

**Current permitted scope:** Replace the unspecified shared project with a friend’s move; ask what matters rather than assume two enduring values. A stipulated time conflict is not the person’s chronic dilemma.

**Finding:** Need, burden and negotiability explain a reasonable boundary choice. Ask the recurring way of balancing own plans and requests first.

**Hybrid candidate (HYB0-G19):** When someone wants more of your time than you had planned to give, how do you usually balance helping them with your own plans? A non-urgent request that you could renegotiate is one example.

This is a new development item. Its answer is not automatically equivalent to the old scenario's facet contract.

## F0 — replace

**Current question:** The same noisy meeting place is suggested in two otherwise identical situations, and nothing has been booked. In one, you are deciding with three friends you have known for years; in the other, with three friends you have known for only a few weeks. You would prefer somewhere quieter in both. What, if anything, would you say differently between the two groups?

**Current permitted scope:** Credit only an answer-originated difference or stated lack of difference in how the same preference is framed to long-known versus newly-known friends. Do not infer persuasive success, manipulation, global social skill, or adaptation outside this familiarity comparison.

**Finding:** Matched audience contrast can clarify adaptation, but is burdensome as an opening and partly elicits communication skill. First ask the usual audience-dependent pattern.

**Hybrid candidate (HYB0-F0):** Beyond what ordinary politeness calls for, how much do you usually change the way you express the same preference to different people? For example, asking for less noise with close friends versus people you have just met.

This is a new development item. Its answer is not automatically equivalent to the old scenario's facet contract.

## G05 — replace

**Current question:** You suggest leaving an hour earlier for a group trip to avoid traffic, but the others would rather have more time in the morning. When you try to persuade people about a plan like this, how does it usually go?

**Current permitted scope:** Add the actual disagreement: traffic versus morning time. The answer supplies self-rated outcomes, not objectively demonstrated persuasive ability.

**Finding:** One transport argument mixes stakes, persuasion ability, desire to persuade and the partner response. Keep capacity and preferred use separate.

**Hybrid candidate (HYB0-G05):** When you actually try to persuade someone, how does it usually go? For example, explaining why you prefer an earlier departure. This is about your experience of doing it, not whether you enjoy persuading people.

This is a new development item. Its answer is not automatically equivalent to the old scenario's facet contract.

## M11 — replace

**Current question:** You and a friend are planning a meal for just the two of you. You offer to organise and cook it, which will take several hours, and ask your friend to buy the ingredients. They say, “I'd like to do the meal, but paying for all the ingredients feels like too much for me.” What would you say next?

**Current permitted scope:** The friendship, two-person meal, proposal, cooking time, and listener objection are stimulus premises, not respondent evidence. Credit only the respondent's answer-originated response: clarifying, reframing contributions, asking about budget, renegotiating, changing the split, or withdrawing the proposal. Do not score sales ability, manipulation, fairness ideology, or likely success.

**Finding:** A meal bargain may elicit an ordinary fair division rather than a stable bargaining preference. Start with how negotiation normally unfolds.

**Hybrid candidate (HYB0-M11):** When sharing work or costs with someone, how do you usually work out an arrangement you both accept? Preparing a meal together is one example; another familiar situation is fine.

This is a new development item. Its answer is not automatically equivalent to the old scenario's facet contract.

## G06 — replace

**Current question:** You have joined a group organising a shared meal. People are deciding how to divide the work, and nobody has asked you to take a role. What would you usually do?

**Current permitted scope:** Specify a shared-meal role context. No role assignment is not the same as exclusion. Initiation, observation and offering bounded help remain possible.

**Finding:** A shared meal can illustrate entry into group work; spontaneous initiative and awaiting role clarity should be asked as a usual pattern, not inferred from this event alone.

**Hybrid candidate (HYB0-G06):** In group work without assigned roles, what do you usually do: step in, offer a particular contribution, wait for a clearer role, or something else? Preparing a shared meal is one example.

This is a new development item. Its answer is not automatically equivalent to the old scenario's facet contract.

## R02 — probe

**Current question:** Consider the same shared-meal scene, except someone asks you to coordinate the work. What would you usually do?

**Current permitted scope:** Use the same response task as G06 and name the one changed condition. Compare two scoped answers, never credit a contrast from this answer alone.

**Finding:** Use only to clarify a real difference between assigned and unassigned roles. An instruction to coordinate creates role expectations, not a leadership trait.

## G07 — retain

**Current question:** You find yourself starting an activity with someone you did not know before. In your own life, how do connections like that usually begin?

**Current permitted scope:** Label as habitual recollection with an activity cue, not a fictional future event. No compulsory recent example or claim that an imagined connection proves past pathways.

**Finding:** Already asks a recurring actual pattern in how connections begin. Preserve life-stage and opportunity differences.

## G08 — retain

**Current question:** You offer to help with one clearly defined task, but people begin treating you as though you can take care of the whole situation. How familiar is that experience in your own life?

**Current permitted scope:** Retain familiarity as the task. An unfamiliar scene may still be imagined, but cannot supply recurrence evidence.

**Finding:** Can reveal recurring expectations placed on the person. Keep others expectations distinct from verified competence or the persons responsibility.

## R03 — probe

**Current question:** In the scene where you offered one task and people expected you to handle the whole situation, what would you usually say when that became clear?

**Current permitted scope:** Bind to the overextended-help scene, even after a topic switch. A hypothetical response stays hypothetical when familiarity was denied.

**Finding:** Useful boundary probe only after mismatched expectations were established; do not manufacture the antecedent.

## M07 — retain

**Current question:** Choose a familiar activity that you do reasonably well. Looking back, what parts, if any, came fairly easily from the start, and what parts did you have to learn or practise?

**Current permitted scope:** Treat this as one acquisition-history contrast. Preserve easy-from-start, learned-through-practice, mixed, or cannot-separate answers. Do not infer innate talent independently of the respondent's recollection.

**Finding:** Explicitly separates ease at the start from improvement through practice. Self-report does not objectively establish exceptional ability.

## M08 — probe

**Current question:** What, if anything, do other people tend to ask you to do because of that ability?

**Current permitted scope:** Only after an actual named ability; repeat its name if nonadjacent. No request from others is not absence of ability.

**Finding:** Others requests may corroborate recognition only at the named skill and source scope. Requests are not objective ability measurements.

## G01 — replace

**Current question:** You are helping arrange a weekend trip. Several people have sent overlapping messages about travel, costs, and timing, and some details disagree. How would you start making sense of it?

**Current permitted scope:** Retain conflicting travel messages as a concrete organization task. Organization and checking accuracy are distinct; a rich reply can satisfy both.

**Finding:** Organizing a contrived message thread is not the only competent approach. Delegation, asking another person or using a tool must be usable answers, not failures to answer.

**Hybrid candidate (HYB0-G01):** When information is messy or conflicting, what is your usual first move? For example, sorting it yourself, asking someone to explain, or using a tool. Describe what you actually tend to do.

This is a new development item. Its answer is not automatically equivalent to the old scenario's facet contract.

## G02 — replace

**Current question:** You have worked out why two sets of plans for a trip do not agree. Someone else is trying to make sense of the same messages. What would you usually do next?

**Current permitted scope:** Use the trip-message puzzle as the concrete problem. Helping another is offered, not compelled. An explanation is not automatically a reusable protocol.

**Finding:** A trip explanation can illustrate information selection, but may test compliance with the task. Ask what the person usually does with an idea for different listeners.

**Hybrid candidate (HYB0-G02):** When explaining something you understand, how do you usually decide what to include for the listener? A short practical explanation is enough as an example; you do not need to perform one now.

This is a new development item. Its answer is not automatically equivalent to the old scenario's facet contract.

## G03 — retain

**Current question:** There is a disagreement about how a shared plan went wrong, and you care about understanding it. You have to put the question aside to do something else. What usually happens to your attention in a situation like this?

**Current permitted scope:** Use a shared-plan puzzle as the default. If too inconsequential or unfamiliar, adapt to one question the respondent already regards as important, without demanding autobiographical proof.

**Finding:** Directly asks the return of a personally important unresolved issue. Importance must be the persons, not supplied as proof of persistence.

## R01 — probe

**Current question:** Return to the important question you had to put aside. What would usually let you stop returning to it?

**Current permitted scope:** Requires a reported return to an unresolved question. If they never return, suppress it; if they want to drop the topic, do not reopen it to obtain closure data.

**Finding:** Ask what closes a returning issue only when closure remains unknown. Do not conflate injustice, curiosity and inefficiency.

## G04 — retain

**Current question:** You are following instructions to set up a shared calendar on your phone. Most steps work, but the step for inviting another person keeps failing. What would you usually do next?

**Current permitted scope:** Make the troublesome step concrete enough to answer. Seeking clarification, retrying and abandoning are as legitimate as inventing a method.

**Finding:** A failed instruction is a concrete way to observe the first troubleshooting step. Scope to troubleshooting, not global originality.

## D0 — replace

**Current question:** You are practising the pronunciation of one short phrase in a language you want to learn. You can already say it well enough to be understood, but repeating the same ten-second phrase lets you notice small improvements. You have ten minutes left, with no test or deadline. What would you probably do?

**Current permitted scope:** Retain this exact pronunciation-practice context. Continuing, switching activity, or stopping do not by themselves establish discipline or novelty seeking. Any value of repetition must be tied to this activity.

**Finding:** Repeating while improvement is visible is instrumentally reasonable for many preferences. Ask whether refinement itself is rewarding, with activity-specific exceptions.

**Hybrid candidate (HYB0-D0):** When practising a skill, how do you usually feel about repeating and refining a small detail? For example, repeating a pronunciation: the refining itself may feel enjoyable, neutral or tedious, whether or not you find it worthwhile, or differ across activities.

This is a new development item. Its answer is not automatically equivalent to the old scenario's facet contract.

## G22 — retain

**Current question:** You are reading something that holds your interest. Someone interrupts briefly, then leaves you free to continue. What usually happens to your concentration?

**Current permitted scope:** Use an explicit reading context. Resumption after interruption is not the same as depth, duration or stillness; do not infer those.

**Finding:** A useful interruption-response question, but not by itself evidence of uninterrupted focus depth or a general attention trait.

## E0 — probe

**Current question:** A nearby group is offering a free cooking class, one evening a week for a month. You like the sound of it, and the time would work for you, but you haven't tried a class with this group before. You need to sign up by tomorrow. What would you probably do next?

**Current permitted scope:** Retain the cooking-class choice. It asks the next step, not every stage of decision-making. A direct no or no uncertainty is valid.

**Finding:** A choice context for subsequent questions, not an independently scored trait item. Prefer an already described choice rather than creating another scene.

## G09 — probe

**Current question:** Return to the cooking-class offer. Before you have weighed it up, what, if anything, would you usually notice in your body?

**Current permitted scope:** Bind body question to the existing cooking-class scene instead of silently introducing another commitment. Ask only if the initial bodily response is genuinely missing.

**Finding:** Only asks noticed bodily signals in the established choice. Do not imply everyone has one or that noticing makes it trustworthy.

## R04 — probe

**Current question:** How useful is that reaction when you are deciding whether a choice suits you?

**Current permitted scope:** Use only a reported bodily reaction, with source context. Perceived usefulness is not objective accuracy; do not ask after explicit no signal.

**Finding:** Useful only if a bodily signal was reported and its repeatability or perceived usefulness is genuinely unresolved.

## M10 — probe

**Current question:** While you consider that same choice, what usually happens to the bodily reaction you described?

**Current permitted scope:** Distinguish within-choice duration/recurrence from consistency across choices. A passing feeling and later reasoning may coexist.

**Finding:** Within-choice change should not be mistaken for across-choice consistency. Suppress if the respondent already described the trajectory.

## M05 — retain

**Current question:** On a bus or car route you know well, the driver suddenly turns onto a road that route normally never uses for your destination. What would you make of that?

**Current permitted scope:** The cue is familiarity-dependent by construction: a deviation from the route the respondent knows. Retain what meaning they give it. Do not label it intuition or assume danger.

**Finding:** Can elicit the cues underlying a feeling that something is off in a familiar setting. It cannot validate intuition accuracy.

## M06 — demote

**Current question:** Thinking about the familiar-route turn you just considered: what would you need to already know about a route before a turn away from its usual path would carry any meaning for you?

**Current permitted scope:** Credit only the respondent's answer-originated familiarity threshold or conditions under which a route deviation would or would not carry meaning. Do not infer danger, concern, actual unfamiliar-route behavior, accuracy, or transfer from any cue other than knowledge of the route's usual pattern.

**Finding:** Knowing a familiar route helps detect a deviation almost by definition. Use only if a nontrivial familiarity threshold matters; never mandatory evidence.

## G10 — replace

**Current question:** You join a group whose work interests you. They ask everyone to use a fixed schedule rather than choose their own working times. How would you usually respond?

**Current permitted scope:** Specify fixed scheduling rather than an undefined way of working. A boundary here is not global self-direction across life.

**Finding:** Accepting a groups fixed schedule often reflects feasibility or interest, not stable direction or a malleable identity. Ask a usual adaptation boundary directly.

**Hybrid candidate (HYB0-G10):** When joining a group, which parts of your usual way of doing things tend to adapt, and which remain important to keep? A group with a different schedule or working style is one example.

This is a new development item. Its answer is not automatically equivalent to the old scenario's facet contract.

## G20 — retain

**Current question:** Your basic needs are covered, and you unexpectedly receive extra money equal to about one month of your ordinary living costs, with no debt, tax, or repayment obligation attached. What would you most want that money to do for you?

**Current permitted scope:** The relative amount and absence of repayment obligations are premises. Credit the respondent's intended function for this specified amount only; do not infer actual wealth, financial security, or a complete resource motive.

**Finding:** The normalized resource amount helps make purposes concrete. It yields reported priorities at that amount, not a population-level materialism score.

## M03 — replace

**Current question:** You agreed to slice vegetables and make one simple dressing for a shared meal. **Hybrid candidate (HYB0-M03):** How do small voluntary commitments usually play out for you after the initial enthusiasm fades? For example, a manageable promise to help with a task. Describe your actual pattern, including exceptions, not what someone ought to do.

## M04 — probe

**Current question:** Return to that same thirty-minute meal preparation, with the same available hour and no competing obligation. In this version you are unusually tired and start making mistakes. **Current permitted scope:** Use the same bounded meal-preparation task as M03 and vary available energy only. Credit only the respondent's answer-originated change or non-change in intention. Do not infer execution, capacity, or actual follow-through unless separately reported.

## G17 — replace

**Current question:** While planning a shared meal, you are fairly sure of two corrections. In one case, someone tells the group the meal starts at 7:00 instead of 7:30, so several people could arrive half an hour early.
