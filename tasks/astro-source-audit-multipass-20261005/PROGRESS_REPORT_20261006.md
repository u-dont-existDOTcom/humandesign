## What the first reading changes methodologically

### Conditional synthesis, not isolated keywords
Ptolemy I.2 treats judgement as a synthesis of positions, timing and combined qualities. I.4 and I.8 distinguish a planet’s intrinsic qualities from changes arising through configurations. Our extraction retains both layers instead of converting either into an unconditional personal trait. Support: English PDF pp. 33–37, 59–63, 69.

### Sect has different rationales for different planets
I.7 gives the Sun, Jupiter and Saturn a diurnal affiliation, and the Moon, Venus and Mars a nocturnal one. Mercury depends on morning/evening status. The chapter explains reinforcement by similar qualities for beneficent temperaments but moderation by opposing qualities for Saturn and Mars. This is not a single universal numerical bonus. Support: English PDF p. 67 / printed p. 43.

### Mercury and lunar phases need explicit unresolved states
Unspecified Mercury phase is not silently converted to a fixed sect. I.8’s four lunar-phase qualities are retained separately from our modern longitude formula. Exact new/quarter/full boundaries remain unresolved in the isolated reference because this passage does not specify inclusive endpoint handling. Support: English PDF pp. 67–69.

### Solar phase is more than an ordinary degree aspect
I.8’s planetary sequence refers to rising, stations, evening rising and setting; Robbins identifies the three superior planets in a note. It is not licensed here as a generic phase rule for Venus or Mercury, nor does the text supply a universal visibility threshold. That calculation remains unresolved rather than improvised. Support: English PDF p. 69 / printed p. 45 and its translator notes.

### The book’s arguments are not validation
I.1 distinguishes the certainty of astronomical calculation from interpretation. I.2 acknowledges limitations beyond practitioner error, and I.3 distinguishes conditional outcomes and larger collective circumstances. These are historical methodological positions, not proof of astrological effectiveness. Historical medical analogies are recorded as rationale, not recommendations or event predictors. Support: English PDF pp. 27–29, 37–57.
