**Authorial rule groups:** The first house and its lord represent the clergyman asking, with the Moon a supplementary candidate indicator; the ninth lord represents the ecclesiastical benefice. Direct or translated favorable contacts, placement in ninth and reception are separately said to favor appointment. Even favorable appointment conditions sometimes imply effort or solicitation. A square with reception is distinguished from trine or sextile with reception. The source speculates about possible payment to a patron in the square case; it does not document an actual bribe. Contact with a malefic without reception is negative, but transfer of influence to a strong benefic may reverse the alleged outcome. Translation to another planet can also imply a different applicant obtains the post. Multiple planets can mean competitors OR difficulties, not an unambiguous count of applicants. Angular placement supposedly hastens, succedent delays, cadent hinders, with an explicit rare trick exception.

The closing chapter distinguishes **obtaining the appointment** from **whether it is lucrative or worth accepting**, represented by the tenth house and dignities. The last author's criticism of clergy, opponents of astrology, and simony is political/religious opinion and self-description, not observed data about actual wrongdoing.

**Evidence category:** The chapter supplies conditional historical astrology and polemic rather than a named, independently documented ecclesiastical vacancy or appointment result. No prospective frozen question, blinded candidate panel, measured job outcome, or verified corruption is supplied. None of these historical statements is treated as predictive evidence, a modern employment assessment or individual-character evidence.
