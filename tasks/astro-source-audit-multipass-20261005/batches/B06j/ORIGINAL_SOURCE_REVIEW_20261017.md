## Source contents and methodological incompatibilities

Lilly proposes an overlapping set of algorithms for alleged wife count. These draw on watery/fruitful marriage-indicator signs versus barren Virgo/Leo/Gemini, Moon occupying seventh, Moon conjoined one planet, Moon applying by partile aspect to planets (direct, not combust/fallen/detrimental), an alleged doubling in fruitful signs, an original method Lilly attributes to Ptolemy involving one-form or bicorporeal zodiac signs, planets located in the arc between Midheaven and Venus, counting partile contacts to the seventh-house ruler, the author's personally preferred Moon same-sign applications, and further two- or three-wife combinations of Moon/planet/seventh-house sign classifications.

These are **ten alternative, overlapping and sometimes contradictory counting methods**, preserved separately in `COMPETING_WIFE_COUNT_HISTORICAL_METHODS_V1.json`. Among the conflicts: one source assigns one wife to Moon joined to one planet, while another assigns multiple women or wives to Moon in seventh; retrograde/combust planets are disqualified in one method but later count if dignified; planets in fruitful signs double count; Moon application versus separation matters; and some counting methods are fallback-only but lack a rigorous priority tree. The original Ptolemy attribution has not been independently verified against Ptolemy's exact passage.

Especially important, Lilly admits that his Moon-seventh observation does **not always yield two legal wives**, but can instead indicate multiple female friends or one/two concubines; he describes this as personally never failing while supplying no case series, denominator or negative examples. A later passage similarly allows weak indicators to mean sweethearts or unsuccessful wooing rather than marriage. This is an explicit **change of outcome variable**, not an independent success. Legal spouses, unions, affairs, attempted relationships, and the survival order of husband/wife are all fundamentally separate targets.

The source also alleges that relative Moon versus companion-planet strength determines which spouse dies first. That is a period mortality hypothesis without medical or genealogical verification and cannot be treated as a health, safety or lifespan forecast.
