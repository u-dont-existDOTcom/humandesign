## Important source exceptions, counterexamples and scientific limits

The early appearance subsection refers to seventh-cusp sign and wife signifier plus planets to which the Moon applies, with **all or majority** of matching sign categories. Original categories of 'fairness', 'mediocrity' and 'deformity' and sign portions are retained as period taxonomy only. Several glyphs and sign fractions remain unresolved; no executable classifier or real person's body/beauty/disability judgments may be issued.

The source specifically notes supposed effects of **Saturn affliction, solar combustion and Venus with Saturn** on spouse physical appearance or age. It also offers positive Jupiter/Venus/Moon conditions with **an exception:** Jupiter in seventh or Jupiter conjunct Moon is not automatically a favorable appearance verdict if other supporting circumstances are absent. The author's retrospective 'proved true' anecdotes contain no enumerated failures or independent observer.

In the internal **Conditions of Wife** passage, Lilly attributes temperament material to Ptolemy and later authors. Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Venus and Mercury receive *opposite* personality interpretations depending on being favorably or unfavorably dignified; many implications are old social and moral stereotypes. The author expressly limits one rule by saying the Sun and Moon are not the direct indicators of temperament **within that subsection**, not that luminaries never matter in natal marriage doctrine.

The separate **Aphorisms concerning the posture of Venus** associate assorted Venus signs/houses and aspects with wealth, partner status, gender roles, children, timing, marital history and private conduct. Allegations about witchcraft, poisoning, infertility, adultery, prostitution, physical disability or spouse moral character are historical period claims—not evidence or appropriate inferences about real people.

The **Riches of Wife** section is unusually important to accurate scientific testing because Lilly explicitly rejects an absolute promise of noble/aristocratic birth from a favorable spouse configuration. His worked hypothetical **beggar** case counts marriage into a farmer's or tradesman's family, and possible dowry or improved subsistence, as socially favorable *relative to native's initial class*. This dramatically changes the target variable. Any future validation must freeze the outcome measure, denominator, social context, expected base rate and negative cases in advance, rather than count either absolute nobility or a merely somewhat better-off spouse as interchangeable hits.

Likewise, powerful malefics in seventh are said sometimes to indicate a **wealthy wife with scandal or trouble** rather than no wealth. The remaining source sections distinguish several reciprocal house-ruler alternatives, spouse inheritance and planetary dignity exceptions. The author's Saturn/Mars assertions of sorrow or a spouse's death are unsupported historical mortality predictions with explicit benefic aspect qualifications, not present-day health or relationship safety judgments.
