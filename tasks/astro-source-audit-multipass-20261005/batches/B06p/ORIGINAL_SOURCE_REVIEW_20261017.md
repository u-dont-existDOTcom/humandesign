## Important source distinctions

Lilly prioritizes the **fifth house**, then the eleventh and first, and refers to occupant planets, zodiac sign and first-hand ruler. Critically, he explicitly says if an allegedly fruitful planet occupies fifth but a barren planet occupies eleventh, the fifth-house evidence wins; the reverse (barren fifth, fruitful eleventh) also prioritizes fifth. When relevant houses have no planets, he examines the **fifth-house ruler**; when signs and planets conflict, he claims to use a majority of testimonies without declaring weights or a reproducible counting scheme. The original expressly recognizes fruitful, barren and *middling* categories and two counterexamples where the planet and sign classifications disagree. These prevent a valid extraction from simply counting how many planets are classed fruitful.

The taxonomy assigns Jupiter, Venus, Moon and North Node to allegedly fruitful categories and Saturn, Mars, Sun and South Node to barren ones. **Mercury is expressly neutral or convertible**, depending on conjunction, aspect, essential dignity and sometimes its oriental or occidental status and favorable versus malefic aspects. No such planetary labels are independent scientific measures of fertility.

The following original aphorisms compare the fifth-house ruler and the first- and fifth-house signs, Moon/Mercury in fifth, Jupiter/Moon favorable aspects, benefics angular, and planets in allegedly fruitful signs, with outcome claims ranging from unspecified childbearing to many children or supposed benefits from offspring. It cites **Alubater, Schoner and Origanus (page 640)**; those historical original sources have not yet been collated, so an attribution does not supply independent replication.

The source also includes period claims concerning lack of issue, few children, welfare/morality of children, alleged child death, infant death, birth complications and supposed abortion. Mixed and unusual original planetary dignities and glyphs are kept unresolved where warranted. An isolated retrospective anecdote of a Jupiter-retrograde fifth-house chart with no children is recorded as a claim by Lilly, not a validated case series. These historical assertions are not appropriate as individual pregnancy, fertility, miscarriage, child disability or mortality predictions, nor medical advice.

Especially important, the source ambiguously moves among **no children, few children, difficulty having children, failure to survive, and infant or parent death**. These are different outcomes, not independent confirmations. Any subsequent scientific test must preregister endpoint, risk window, age, reproductive intent and baseline before looking at a person's chart or record.
