## 1. Ptolemy explicitly describes dynamic connections between two birth charts

In IV.7 he moves from lasting friendship/enmity to occasional relations, then examines when the prorogations of planets in one nativity reach places in the other. Robbins's note expressly connects this to the method in III.10, with a point of departure in one nativity and an arrival point in the other (PDF441-443/printed417-419).

The ten possible pairs among Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Venus and Mercury have separately listed descriptions. Examples include Jupiter-Mercury for learned discussion and philosophical association, Saturn-Venus for connections through kin that quickly cool, and Mars-Venus for brief, insecure amorous associations. These are not descriptions of any bare natal conjunction. The table now carries the cross-chart directional prerequisite, and the helper rejects attempts to retrieve it as a same-chart conjunction, ordinary transit or secondary progression.

The chapter also describes a shorter endpoint at completion of the prorogation and a longer endpoint when another approaching planet reaches the place. The exact onset, directional key, handling of different birth epochs/latitudes and competing encounters remain implementation questions. The table does not calculate dates, and none of the existing empirical pair-timing results is upgraded by discovering this passage.

## 2. Affection, practical need, authority and benefit are different dimensions

For important relationships the source examines the Sun, Moon, Ascendant and Lot of Fortune in both charts. It distinguishes relations based on choice, need, and pleasure/pain. Luminary familiarity is linked to choice; Fortune familiarity to need; horoscopic familiarity to pleasure/pain; all or most relevant places in familiarity combine the bases (PDF437-441/printed413-417).

A separate passage assigns greater authority over the relationship according to elevation of the configuration, but greater benefit from friendship or success in enmity according to favorable planetary regard. These can be distinct outputs. A useful association is not necessarily affectionate, and the person directing a relationship is not automatically the person benefiting most. That final sentence is our methodological implication, not an additional quoted doctrine.

The text further separates conspicuousness/intensity from benefit or injury. Neither a particularly intense relationship nor a strong astrological configuration can simply be renamed good compatibility (PDF445/printed421).

## 3. The apparently precise 17-degree condition is not ready for a hard cutoff

The main text says the horoscopic regions are **about 17 degrees apart**. Robbins's later explanatory note says **within 17 degrees** and observes that the horoscopes could therefore fall in successive signs (PDF439 and441/printed415 and417). Both readings are visible in the page images; this is not an OCR discrepancy.

Those are different predicates. A tolerance around17, an upper limit of17, and an exact17 contact are not interchangeable. The surrounding all/most-place and exchange conditions also remain necessary to investigate. We preserved the main text and note separately. The geometric probe can compute a declared ecliptic separation, but even a result of17 does not certify the whole source condition. No new orb was inserted into the engine.

## 4. Travel is not reduced to a ninth-house reading

IV.8 begins with both luminaries relative to the angles, especially the Moon. Moon setting or declining supports journeys or changes of place. Mars has an additional conditional route: setting or declining from the Midheaven **and** a square/opposition to the luminaries. Fortune in the travel-causing signs extends the topic to life, personal relations and business abroad (PDF447/printed423).

Robbins explains the relevant places as the seventh and the cadent third, sixth, ninth and twelfth. That expansion is retained as his note, not a universal house-system definition. The helper preserves the Moon OR and Mars AND/OR structure, and treats unknown qualifications as unknown. A non-triggered branch is not a forecast that the person will not travel.

Residence abroad, frequency, direction, activity quality and return are separate source outputs. The lifetime-abroad clause and quick-return clause can overlap in the text; no retrospective priority has been invented to make a known life fit them.

## 5. Inherited conditions change Mercury's travel meaning

Jupiter and Venus qualified as rulers of the travel places and luminaries produce favorable travel in this source, with help through influential people or friends, supplies and weather. Adding Mercury often adds gain, gifts and honor. In the separate adverse Saturn-Mars sequence, Mercury adds weather-related difficulties, accusations and poisonous-creature hazards (PDF449-451/printed425-427).

The source explicitly requires mixture and dominance in every case, referring back to the earlier method. It does not support one unconditional Mercury travel rule or an automatic flat count of benefics minus malefics.

The adversity list also contains an awkward transition from watery voyages/shipwreck to hard going and desert places without assigning the latter a new sign category. That alternative remains unallocated rather than being silently relocated to the later terrestrial-sign branch.

Finally, the chapter calls for timing from the ingresses of the five planets. Robbins says their destination is **presumably** the signs causing travel. The main-text timing instruction and that tentative supplied target are distinct records. No real travel safety or health recommendation is derived from these historical claims.

## 6. Rhetorius preserves materially different qualifications

Rhetorius16 describes sympathy between particular disjunct signs through equal rising, equal power and like zone, and expressly connects the treatment to relations among people. Rhetorius17 identifies six sympathetic square pairs. These differ from Ptolemy IV.7's qualified association/enmity scheme. They should be retained in author-specific profiles, not inserted silently into Ptolemy or counted as independent observational confirmation (Rhetorius PDF19-20/printed16-17).

Rhetorius57's opening ninth-house paragraphs offer a different topical entry: good planets there signify favorable foreign circumstances, especially when the ruler is suitably placed and unregarded by malefics. This is not equivalent to Ptolemy's angular/cadent selection method (Rhetorius PDF84/printed87).
