## Main original source logic

The chapter starts by explaining that a single selected planet Mars, Venus or Mercury may determine a putative profession, but a **strong combination with another significator can override it**: Lilly even says Mercury relinquishes its occupational authority to strongly aspecting Mars. Other planets, houses and signs can augment or diminish its interpretation. This means the historic account is a branching, many-alternative method rather than a simple three-planet classification.

Under **Mercury alone**, old examples range across reading, writing, numbers, mathematics, astrology, philosophy, poetry, oratory, secretaries, engineering/instruments and commercial exchange. The source nevertheless adds a separate claim that financial success in these endeavors depends on a favorable Mercury–Jupiter aspect. It then differentiates Mercury combined with **Saturn** (service, church work, poor oratory when adversely aspected), **Mars** (farm, seafaring, craftsmen, occult or dream interpretation), **Jupiter** (law, judgment, banking and rhetoric), **Sun** (public accounting, secretarial and treasury work; with exceptions around retrogradation, combustion and **cazimi**), **Moon** (interest in future events, astrology, divination or magical pretenses by zodiac condition) and Venus (pleasant speech, rhetoric and performing arts). These are source-attributed old categories, not actual measured intelligence or supernatural skill.

Under **Venus alone**, the author proposes food, apothecaries, grocery, fragrance, decorative arts, jewelry and music. An explicit assertion that Venus dislikes academic learning is qualified by Saturn, so it is not even a universal historical Venus rule. Venus mixed with **Saturn** gets either pleasure-related commerce or degraded/stigmatized professions depending on planetary condition. With **Jupiter** it shifts to clergy, medicine, textiles and supposedly favorable social advancement through women or religious institutions. Solar Venus **combust** is sometimes said to lose authority as a profession significator, whereas Venus at the **heart of the Sun** (cazimi) is assigned stronger artistry and wealth. One cannot treat all solar contacts identically.

Under **Mars alone**, Lilly relates bodily strength and sometimes fire to laborious/manual occupations or military leadership, with different outcomes for **night births**, strong, intermediate or weak Mars. Those categories include soldiers, captains, engineers, ironworkers, stonemasons, medical surgeons, cooks and ordinary laborers. Weak Mars also receives unsupported accusations of theft/piracy, not valid criminality prediction. Mars mixed with **Saturn** can allegedly lose leadership and instead yield labor, chimney/coal and agricultural jobs; with **Jupiter** it may allegedly support military command and prestige; with **Sun** it may favor magistracy or coinage; with **Moon** it may include meat processing or water/fishing/trade. The same planet mix admits many dissimilar possible careers.

The sections **Mercury and Venus commixed**, **Mercury with Mars**, and **Mars and Venus significators of Art** provide long lists of rhetoric, music, dancing, arts, teaching, health trades, legal professions, and alternative pejorative labels. The crucial qualifiers are which planets actually govern the vocational significator, their aspects and strength, signs/houses and additional Jupiter/Saturn/Moon participation. Several close variants give both lawful professional roles and alleged serious crimes or immoral behavior. No one can be classified as a fraudster, murderer, immoral person or occult adept from this unverified text; those are original prejudicial labels.

## Success and zodiac-sign category caveats

The internal **`With what successe`** subsection changes question from profession **type** to reputation and prosperity. Lilly alleges professional renown when the selected significator is essentially dignified, angular, oriental and unafflicted by close malefic aspects; adverse dignity, cadency, occidentality and malefics are alleged to indicate poor work, low earnings, sloth or disgrace. **Skill, income, formal occupation, honor, pleasure and long-term stability are separate empirical outcomes**. The old moral accusations cannot be scored as facts.

The **`Nature of the Signes`** subsection adds movable, fixed (**except Scorpio** for the learning claim), humane, water/earth and quadruped classifications. A portion is explicitly attributed to **Albubater**, who associated the zodiac elements with historically familiar occupations: fire with foundries, baking and glass; earth with pottery, digging and construction; air with singers, jesters, fowlers, mills and shooting; water with fishing, laundering, fulling and water transport. Albubater's original text is not independently collated here. The closing **`Quality of the Profession`** revisits ranking the strongest vocational planet and best aspect, points back to original printed pages **57–83**, and gives further dignity-dependent alternatives. It then transitions into the separately headed eleventh-house source, not another professional aphorism.
