## Source-defined procedures

**1. Significators and roles are context-dependent.** The first four aphorisms distinguish the ascendant, its lord and the figure lord from the sixth-house sign, ruler and occupants, the Moon's sign, seventh-house physician, tenth-house medicine, and fourth-house illness ending. Neither seventh- nor tenth-house affliction is automatically a claim about the patient. The fourth-house rule gives moveable signs a quick ending, common signs variation and fixed signs duration. Aphorism30 instead gives moveable signs variation, without naming a house: only the *movable* class conflicts if applied to the same location; fixed claims agree and common-sign interpretations are broadly compatible. [PDF316,318–319; aphorisms1–4,30; R1703–R1706,R1732; U19]

**2. The opening physician-hour predictions address different alleged endpoints.** Saturn's hour concerns the patient's duration and suffering (a later pronoun remains ambiguous), Mars's hour concerns reception of the physician and unrewarded work, and Jupiter/Venus hours concern good words, esteem and remuneration; if a cure fails, only the doctor's **estimation/reputation** is expressly protected. The text uses different phrases — **first visit** for Saturn/Jupiter/Venus and **first entering upon a cure** for Mars — without establishing that these are different real-world events. These are historical claims about professional interaction, not known outcomes. [PDF316; aphorisms5–7, R1707–R1709]

**3. Consent and derived-house calculation are not a single universal rule.** Aphorism 8 makes the patient's urine specimen a condition for treating the ascendant as the sick person's significator, whether or not the querent comes with the sick party's consent. Aphorism 9, which crosses PDF316–317, changes the ascendant to the querent when it states absence of urine **or** consent; an example uses the sixth for a servant and the *sixth from the sixth* (the eleventh) for that person's illness. Aphorism9 closes with 'where no consent is'. The original *no Urine or consent* may mean either missing item (in which case urine-present/no-consent overlaps #8 and #9) **or** both missing (no such overlap). These alternatives are not resolved. Nothing in this passage supplies modern ethical authority for diagnosis or treatment without consent. [PDF316–317; aphorisms8–9, R1710–R1711; U02,U15,U26]

**4. Lunar conditions are not interchangeable.** Aphorisms 10–13 concern the Moon as general significator, both lights below Earth in #11, ambiguous either-versus-both lights obscured in #12, and affliction as a further danger, and the source's two parallel categorical negatives: benefic square/opposition **destroyeth not**, and favourable aspects of malefics **do not profit unless with reception**. Aphorism 20 additionally requires a Moon both swift and waxing, applying sextile or trine to the ascendant lord, with reception separately making the cure easier and above-earth application separately making it sooner. Treating each clause as a separate favourable vote would double-count dependencies. [PDF317–318; aphorisms10–13,20; R1712–R1715,R1722]

## Conditional prognosis and unresolved printed polarity

Aphorisms 14–19 combine freedom from affliction, eighth-house relations, planetary weakness and reception with much stronger claims of recovery or death. The print contains real interpretation hazards, retained rather than silently resolved:

- **#14:** The referent of “two of these Significators be so affected” is unclear alongside the earlier favourable conditions and source's “otherwise he dyes.” Do not generate a new certain two-planet threshold.
- **#15:** The original “it's doubtfull the sick party will dye” is not rewritten as certain death; ruler-alternative scope is uncertain.
- **#17:** Reception by the eighth-house lord is expressly allowed without reciprocal reception.
- **#18:** The enumeration of houses *above Earth* reads **twelfth, eleventh, ninth, eighth seventh** (no printed conjunction between the last two), omitting the tenth; the worker must not insert an unprinted item into the list.
- **#19:** The original visibly reads **“there's great fear of recovery”**, counterintuitive in context. It is preserved as source wording, not repaired to “great fear of death,” and no determinate medical interpretation is licensed.

[PDF317; aphorisms14–19, R1716–R1721; U03–U06]

Later conditions likewise must remain local: fourth-house ruler **applying to conjunction** of eighth lord (#23), disease significator *retrograde* (#24) versus *stationary*, with *combustion* as an **additional** fatality condition (#25), and *fall or detriment* (#26). Neither a generic afflicted-planet flag nor retrograde alone is sufficient to reproduce all of these doctrines. [PDF318; aphorisms21–26, R1723–R1728]

## Other noninterchangeable families

Aphorisms 27–28 distinguish source-described disease in **body**, **spirits** and **animals**, with different patterns of ascendant, Moon, their rulers and a malefic's aspect. These early-modern categories are not modern psychiatric diagnoses. Aphorism 29 gives explicit *manifest* and *occult* house sets; it does not categorize unlisted houses. Aphorism 30, continued onto PDF319, revisits movable/fixed/common signs and specifies several different ways a complaint may vary or recur. [PDF318–319; aphorisms27–30, R1729–R1732; U07–U09]

**Cross-chart natal comparisons in aphorisms 32–34 are not available from a question chart alone.** The printed source makes the natal comparison conditional on the patient's nativity **being obtainable**, then compares illness Moon or illness ascendant to natal infortunes, natal houses and an ambiguous “Revolution” ascendant. In #33 the printed erratum changes there to therein, limiting the infortune to the named place, with rescuing rays cast thither. No natal inputs, exact orbs, true date conversions or validated epidemiology are supplied in this batch; these maxims cannot be applied to people without a separately specified chart and frozen methodology. [PDF319; aphorisms32–34, R1734–R1736; U10–U11,U18,U24]

The later entries distinguish treatment costs (#35), solar placements and combustion (#36), a separately numbered solar description (#37), a Jupiter–Sun 'fear not the death' qualification (#38), illness growth until lunar opposition to Sun (#39), the author's unverified attribution of delayed recovery to patient carelessness (#40), orientation and lunar separations (#41), planetary humoral origins (#42), and waxing/waning Moon, a warning at sickness onset, and ambiguous joint-versus-separate Mars modifiers (#43). A categorical claim should be preserved *as a historical source claim*, not used for medical treatment, relationship characterization or clinical risk. [PDF319–320; aphorisms35–43, R1737–R1745; U12,U16,U17]

## Original errata and provenance

The volume's original errata page (original PDF888) includes **four printed corrections within this section: page 284 line 14, read “if”; page 284 line 37, delete a semicolon; page 285 line 4, read “her”; page 285 line 11, read “therein.”** The printed correction tokens have been recorded separately, but the exact body-word target and the line-counting convention have **not** yet been proved for all four. The original body reads “there”, while the corresponding erratum directs “therein”. The source-reader matched the erratum to aphorism33's printed line: the corrected locality restricts the infortune to the stated place at both times, but the replacement-word target remains inferred from the line. None of these entries licenses silently replacing the original body image or inventing a resolved interpretation. [PDF888, PDF318–319; ERRATA_COLLATION.json; U18]
