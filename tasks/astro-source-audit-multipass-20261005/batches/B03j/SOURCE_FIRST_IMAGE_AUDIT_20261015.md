**Numbering anomaly:** The heading at the top of original PDF431 literally prints **CHAP. LIV. Fish Stolen.** It comes after LXIII and immediately before printed LXV, so its sequential chapter identity is **LXIV**, not the earlier correctly printed LIV on buying and selling. Both the literal original numeral and the logical chapter index are preserved. Never repair one by conflating the chapters.

**Epistemic status:** The author describes fish bought for Lent, shipment to Walton, a report of loss, a horoscope cast upon learning of the loss, astrologically guided attention to waterside occupations, a failed initial scan of boatmen, selection of a fisherman, a warrant and later search. He reports finding part of the fish, part eaten, and some remaining; also reports loss of the bag and Portuguese onions. The leftover fish is explicitly relinquished. This is a **partial fish recovery in the author's own account**, NOT full restitution or independent proof astrology identified a thief. A claim that a housewife washed a bag or made pottage without recognizing onions, as well as the author's unflattering account of the local priest, remains disputed period testimony rather than an established modern fact.

**Unheaded concluding source rules:** Before Chapter LXV, the source distinguishes Moon applying to second-house lord as an argument for recovery, full recovery when both have essential dignities, partial recovery with accidental fortification, and *discovery but no recovery* if both are peregrine. The negative/no-recovery branch is deliberately retained. The diagram's sign/planetary labels and cross-page conditions have not been verified by an independent ephemeris or blind second diplomatic reader.
