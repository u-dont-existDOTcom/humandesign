## Opening: Moon and alternative rulers for things missing

The initial printed material treats the **Moon as general significatrix**, including a marginal note that the adjacent indications **principally concern strayed cattle**. It turns to Moon's application, ascendant and second-house lords, her **domicile ruler**, and later the sixth/eighth/twelfth. Moon's own sign is not the same subject as **Moon's house ruler** when selecting the original human signs or animal-sign glyphs. Negative instructions where Moon does not apply to the listed relevant bodies and is in none of the specified houses must not be turned into favourable promises merely because one planet happens to be angular. Elsewhere Moon’s domicile lord’s contact with the owner’s or goods’ significator is the operative relationship, a narrower requirement than “Moon helpful.” [Original PDF353–354; R2139–R2143]

The author proposes directional and proximity judgments from the Moon's **quarter/sign and element**, first lord and lunar house lord. The original states **within ONE SIGN** rather than only 'roughly thirty degrees' for one close-home clause, and another says a thing's significator or Moon in the same quadrant is in the **same Town or Hundred**. Some rules place a good in the owner's home or close neighborhood; another describes distance out in town/country. Their angular geometry is not a road distance or verified geographic coordinate. The independent reader should check whether the distance thresholds and quadrant conditions have been preserved precisely: an apparent threshold or spatial mapping may depend on *both* relative house placements and degree difference. [PDF354; R2144–R2148]

## How a thing came to be lost

The distinct subsection *How the thing or Goods was lost* assigns possible circumstances to the **planet from which the ascendant lord has separated**. Lilly lists, among other illustrations, Saturn with forgetting, age, illness or cold; Jupiter with duties of state, magistrates or great business; Mars with fright, fire, quarrel or anger; Sun with hunt, recreation or great persons; Venus with drinking, games or music; Mercury with writing, letters and messages; and Moon with ordinary daily use or other household interactions. Some printed planet alternatives also apply when the **ascendant lord is IN the planet's own house**, rather than merely separating from that planet; Jupiter additionally has a printed fast/abstinency example. These are **competing conditional source attributions** depending on the specified prior separation or placement. They must not be mistaken for a set of observed reasons explaining a particular loss or for a psychological assessment of any owner. [PDF355; R2149–R2156]

The next original passage turns to **lost beasts/cattle**, requiring separate scrutiny of whether the animal strayed itself, was driven off by another person, is alive, has been confined, or is recoverable. This category is *not* automatically interchangeable with an inanimate lost object, a missing human servant or a stolen good. The earlier *Whether it be stolen or no* is embedded in the cattle/strays discussion and should not be confused with the later unnumbered, standalone *Of a Thief and Theft* heading on PDF364. [PDF355–356; R2157–R2169]

## Livestock: self-departure, theft, death and recoverability

A key alleged discriminator in the cattle passage depends on **which planet separates from which**: Moon’s house-lord separating from another planet is treated differently from the *other planet* separating from Moon’s house-lord. The draft records opposite separating directions separately. **The second-house lord must be checked whenever the Moon's house lord shows no separation; only if neither lord shows it is the animal said to remain in place.** The source's initial alleged death indicator invokes **the eighth counted FROM the Moon**, which is not the radical eighth; the latter appears as a separate last fallback. The domicile lord's counterpart can mean **dead OR dying shortly**. Later PDF358–359 introduces other, explicitly separate radical-eighth/house claims. Those clauses do **not** permit veterinary prognosis or an independently established claim that the animal died. [PDF356; R2159–R2165]

Further clauses propose theft from contact among a presumed thief’s significator, ascendant lord, Moon and the lords of the Moon’s **house or term** and second house. The positive allegation, a separate **planet occupying first and transferring power to a thief significator**, the **lord of the Part-of-Fortune glyph**, a repeated 'Not stolne' branch for beasts, and negative/contrary circumstances must remain separate. An infortune in ascendant or second is a location-specific loss exception. The source assumes a thief significator before demonstrating an actual thief exists; it should not be presented as real-world evidence of wrongdoing. [PDF356–357; R2166–R2176]

The next pages distinguish location by Moon's **house** (first, fourth, seventh or tenth), **element** (fiery/watery/earthy/airy), **sign** (including readable **Libra/Aquarius exceptions**, and **Capricorn's last FACE on p323** versus **last MOIETY on p324**), the lunar dispositor and angular distance. Moon **IN ascendant** is the case for **return with trouble or pain**, whereas different helpful-finder clauses distinguish a person who *would fain* restore from an asserted *will restore*. For a lost thing these can suggest a field, ground, house, wells, shops or ships; for a beast they can describe water meadows, pens, rushes, plains and gates. These are historical classification systems with potentially differing premises, **not** individually checked map or GPS predictions. [PDF357–359; R2177–R2199]

## “Another Judgment”: animal size, impounding, field type and rescue

On **original PDF358/printed324**, *Of Beasts or Strayes* introduces sixth/twelfth ruler distinctions traditionally interpreted as small versus larger animals, with further **pound/enclosure** claims when those rulers occupy specified houses. In this context “pound” means a place for seized/impounded cattle, **not money or animal weight**. The author distinguishes sixth ruler in fiery signs from Moon in watery/common or other sign modalities, which may mark different pieces of a hypothetical environment. [PDF358; R2187–R2195]

Original **PDF359/printed325** opens *Another Judgment*, offering different readings for inanimate things versus beasts. Its printed *Cattle shall be found againe* clause uses the **second, FIFTH or eleventh house**, **not sixth**; and its lost-beast clause says **chiefly if** cadent, not that cadency is mandatory. The draft does not force these into one deterministic single-chart rule. Later original PDF360–361 separates **terrain/direction** by sixth lord's sign and angularity: watery **angular north/close**, **succedent north/right**, and **cadent left/wet-meadow** are different source outcomes. For airy signs the **cadent** clause is **left and straying farther**, not westward; airy west appears in angular/succedent clauses. Fiery/angular **BUT** replaces the east/woods location with confinement/enclosure. It then discusses **cattle to pound** and alleged risk of starvation, death, custody or later escape. The source's Mars aspect to Saturn **or Moon in twelfth** is not an invented Moon–Saturn aspect. One Saturn four-house long-impoundment clause has different prerequisites from an ascendant-lord eighth-house probable-death claim. These are source-attributed outcomes, neither veterinary evidence nor instructions for animal retrieval. [PDF359–362; R2196–R2232]

An important issue for source review is maintaining **relative placement of sixth/twelfth rulers** and the stated contrast between houses, signs, planets and degrees. Converting all variants into unconditional “beast east/west” loses the operative chart/animal-class conditions and may produce apparent contradictions by deleting their contexts. ## Human fugitives, capture, return and distance

A separate heading **“Whether the Fugitive shall be taken”** occurs on original PDF362/printed328. The draft switches to **ascendant/its lord/SUN for querent** and **seventh/its lord for fugitive OR missing thing**, then explicitly identifies **Moon as natural significatrix of the fugitive**, not an additional initial querent significator, rather than using the beast’s sixth-house significator by inertia. Source clauses distinguish **news from Moon separating FIRST-lord and joining SEVENTH**, versus **regret/entreaty from the reverse Moon direction**, a voluntary return, being found/captured against one's will, **detained but NOT imprisoned when both planets lack angularity**, a capture only **upon good search**, news coming through another, **being pressed for money before release**, and impediments signified by an infortune. A forced return is not the same as willing return or a successful rescue. These historical fugitive or servant categories are not modern criminal-law guidance or evidence of wrongdoing. [PDF362–364; R2233–R2253]

Original **PDF364/printed330** contrasts **two proposed distance methods**: seventh lord and hour lord’s angular separation, followed by the Moon’s distance from the party’s significator, which the author says he considers more accurate. The source assigns **seventeen houses/furlongs per movable-sign degree**, **five per common-sign degree** and **one per fixed-sign degree**, with the movable measure qualified **'at discretion'**, while asking the reader to consider town versus fields and **fugitive versus thing lost**. These old units are textual claims. They cannot be presented as contemporary calibrated geographic predictions, modern miles/kilometers or the actual distance to a missing person. [PDF364; R2254–R2259]

Immediately before the thief-and-theft heading, **“Of a Woman flying from her Husband”** gives a distinct Sun/Venus plus first/Moon/seventh-lord set, including Sun below horizon, Venus oriental/occidental and retrograde, trine/sextile versus square/opposition **without reception**, and Moon/Mars circumstances. A claim of **returning unwillingly** is a different source proposition from a voluntary return, and the author’s prediction of permanent separation is not a modern assessment of relationship safety, agency or consent. [PDF364 above the separate heading; R2260–R2265]
