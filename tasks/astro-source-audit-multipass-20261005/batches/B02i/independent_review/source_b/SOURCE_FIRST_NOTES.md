## Source-order reconstruction

Anchors below are short locating transcriptions with long-s expanded. Planet names in explanatory prose are visual glyph readings. The prose is source interpretation, not verbatim quotation or an assertion that astrology is empirically effective.

### PDF 246 / printed 212: Chapter XXXV

Heading: *If good to remove from one house or place to another, or to stay or abide in any place or not?*

1. Opening `SEE to the Lords of the ascendant`: examine lords 1, 4 and 7. If lord 4 is in 7 and a good planet, and lords 1/7 are good planets or strong where placed or in the whole figure, direct, swift and in aspect with good planets, remaining is good. The conjunctions and the alternatives in this long sentence must survive extraction; the exact reach of the final plural `they` is not formally defined.
2. `but if the Lord of the seventh be with a good Planet`: lord 7 with a good planet and lord 4 with an evil one means staying is not good and would bring much damage. `With` has no quantitative contact threshold here.
3. `That which I have observed`: Lilly introduces his own generalized reported practice. Removal advice follows an OR-list. Its first branch is recent separation of lord 1 from square/opposition of lord 6, 8 or 12 AND a concurring Moon: she separated from an evil aspect of infortunes who were lords 7 or 4 etc., and were not friends or significators of the querent. Other branches are an infortune in ascendant, peregrine or retrograde; a peregrine or unfortunate planet in 4; or weak/ill-placed lord 2. This is not one named historical enquiry.
4. `for if I found the Lord of the sixt house`: lord 6 in ascendant OR afflicting lord 1 supported a judgement of poor health/sickness OR troublesome servants through whom vocation failed to thrive. These are attributed diagnostic reasons given for removal, not separately reported confirmations.
5. `If the Lord of the twelfth afflicted`: affliction of lord 1 OR Moon by lord 12 supported backbiting/evil/slanderous neighbours or nearby people scandalizing the querent.
6. `if the Lord of the second was unfortunate`: unfortunate lord 2 OR its square/opposition to lord 1 OR Part of Fortune in 12/8/6 supported worldly decline and estate consumption.
7. The unfinished `If his Significator` continues at 247; it must not be dropped at the page break.

### PDF 247 / printed 213, continuing XXXV

8. `afflicted by the Lord of the tenth`: lord 1 afflicted by lord 10 supported reputation lost, trade decayed, or no trading.
9. `if the Lord of the fourth was unfortunate, or the fourth house it selfe`: the judgement is unlucky house/few occupants thriving OR repairs weakening the querent. The starred margin adds the alternative that the house stood inconveniently for his trade. Preserve that physical-location alternative.
10. `If the Lord of the seventh afflicted`: affliction of lord 1 OR lord 2 by lord 7 supported rival/overthwart neighbours having the trade and better provision of commodities.
11. `Now in giving direction which way`: select the most fortunate and strongest planet, with the friendliest aspect to lord 1 OR lord 2; take the quarter of heaven indicated by its SIGN, not merely its house or the planet's name. Lilly says he advised relocation there, recalls no regret, and received later thanks/rewards from many. This is retrospective self-report with no denominator, dates, charts, prior records or case-level verifications in this span.
12. `And whereas I mention these words`: an extended digression preserves the author's opinion on unlucky houses. He sets out an objection (God's blessing alike everywhere; an inanimate house cannot be unsuccessful/unfortunate), leaves objectors their opinion, and attributes his own view to experience. He thinks angels curse a house where execrable acts occur, until expiation by a godly person OR a time limited by the angel expires; he extends the claim to families. The marginal gloss is `Or unfortunate`. He alludes to seals/laminae in Latin, with no operational remedy supplied. Preserve as theological/speculative explanatory belief, not a horoscope calculation and not established causation.
13. `But some for resolution of this Question, say`: visibly Jupiter or Venus after Moon separation, followed by `then stay`; sentence continues 248.

### PDF 248 / printed 214: finish XXXV; XXXVI

14. Moon separates from an infortune => remove. Fortune in ascendant => stay; infortune there => remove. This is still the doctrine attributed to `some`; Lilly says it should be considered with the preceding judgement, rather than declared a replacement or an absolute precedence rule.

Heading: *Of turning the course of Rivers, or bringing Water into ones Ground or House, either by Conduit or Pipes.*

15. Opening `principally consider`: consider positions/strengths of Saturn and Moon and aspects of either/both to Jupiter or Venus. Saturn direct + swift + oriental, Moon in 3/11/5, and no good OR evil aspect to Mars argue good/easy completion, prosperity and credit. Grammar most directly connects no aspect to the Moon, but does not repeat the subject: retain possible scope ambiguity instead of inventing a double-planet requirement.
16. `and this the rather`: the previous favourable judgement is strengthened if Moon applies to the ruler of her current sign and he receives her in any of his dignities. Direction is Moon -> own sign ruler; reception is ruler receives Moon.
17. `if that Planet who is receiver`: if that receiving planet is a fortune + ascending in latitude + in a fixed sign, plentiful water and long continuation. No conversion of `ascending in latitude` into an invented numerical threshold or motion convention.
18. Jupiter OR Venus in 10, especially Jupiter, is called a sure argument that the waterwork will remain many years. The duration has no number.
19. `In further consideration`: Saturn in 11, very strong/potent; Moon sextile/trine him; Moon's dispositor in fixed/common sign OR Moon herself in a rain-producing sign. The printed rain glyphs are Cancer, Leo, Aquarius, Pisces, including the seemingly unusual Leo. Do not silently substitute Scorpio or a modern watery-triplicity list. `All these are arguments` does not specify a scoring weight or formal precedence.
20. `but if you find an infortunate Planet in the tenth`: adverse clause continues at 249.

### PDF 249 / printed 215: finish XXXVI; XXXVII

21. Under an unfortunate planet in 10, pipes probably break, banks/watercourse rupture, water fails to run currently, plot is ill laid, and no success is promised to the undertaker(s). Preserve `probable` and no-success-promised modalities rather than making certainty uniform.

Heading: *Of Treasure lying hid in the Ground, or to be digged out of the Earth.*

22. The preamble makes resolution depend on proposal and nature/quality of object: money/plate/jewels/movables, long-hidden unknown treasure, or mine/minerals. It requires knowing who hid/owned it and the hider's relation to the querent, versus asking generally without knowing when/where/whose/what. These prior-known inputs govern which branch is admitted; a generic treasure classifier would omit source content.
23. If querent hid own plate/money/jewels in own ground or house but forgot whereabouts: observe sign/lord of 2, lord's sign and quarter, and sign/lord/quarter of 4. Lords 2 AND 4 angular => still in house/ground, not removed. If these planets are not angular AND an infortune without dignities is in 4 OR 7 => some or all removed. The text gives no rule to decide `some` versus `all`.
24. If the figure promises goods unremoved, find location by the first chapter of this house concerning things hid. This is a cross-reference, not permission for this reader to inspect earlier pages; root may link it to its corresponding span.
25. `If the Question be concerning Treasure absolutely, without` continues at 250.

### PDF 250 / printed 216: XXXVII presence and quality

26. General treasure without knowing whose/what: Jupiter OR Venus OR North Node in 4 => probability of treasure. If there and in own houses => source's stronger `without dispute`/sure assertion of treasure or value. The North Node's inclusion makes the later plural own-house clause semantically awkward; do not invent node domicile rules.
27. Saturn OR Mars in own houses + direct + unimpeded + in 4 => treasure. Venus in Taurus + in 4 + without misfortune => probable treasure. The source says no planet is unfortunate when in own house OR essentially dignified AND a significator; grouping and breadth need retaining as a reservation against mechanical malefic labels.
28. `If you are ignorant of the nature and quality`: examine treasure significator; if lord 7, use his nature/property; otherwise combine him with lord 7 for a mixture.
29. Immediately `But if that Planet ... be not Lord of the 7th or have affinity with him`: then take lord 7 absolutely for nature/species. The printed syntax does not cleanly explain whether it means neither ruler nor affinity; it can conflict with the previous mixture instruction. Preserve both and flag the grammatical/precedence gap.
30. Sun as lord 7, in domicile/exaltation => gold or stones/jewels matching/near solar colour. If mine question, place considered, probably a good mine. Sun less dignified but still treasure significator => something precious and near gold in goodness. These are graded correspondences, not assayed composition.
31. Moon as lady 7 in domicile/exaltation => silver, plate, crystal, or lunar-colour jewels.
32. Mars as lord 7, `so dignified` => possible brass/glass, curiosities or iron engines; weak => perhaps rusty iron, candlesticks, kettles. If iron-stone enquiry => probable good iron-stone, sentence continues 251. Its attachment to dignity branch is not unambiguously restated.

### PDF 251 / printed 217: XXXVII quality; acquisition subsection

33. Completion: iron-stone makes good iron. Saturn as lord 7 fortified as before => valued antiquities/ancient monuments/urn(s), or things in old black cloth/wooden boxes. In corresponding mine/quarry question => probably rich coal mine or good stone. Weak/ill-dignified Saturn => mine not rich and cannot be worked without great expense. Derive water/other impediment from Saturn's sign, considering earlier chapter content. No cost, yield or hydrology measure supplied.
34. Jupiter lord 7 essentially fortified => silver OR much rich cloth OR tin. Venus lady 7 => fine household goods/costly jewels/fine linen. Mercury as significator => pictures, medals, writings/books/pretty toys. No identical dignity prerequisite is restated for Venus/Mercury; do not copy Jupiter's into them.

Unnumbered heading: *If the Querent shall obtaine the Treasure hid.*

35. Treasure significator applies TO ascendant lord OR mutual reception OR translation OR `collation of light and nature` between them => probable obtaining. Preserve original `collation` rather than silently imposing a modern technical term.
36. Square/opposition entails difficulty/much labour. Conjunction best performs business, more assuredly when they are in a fixed sign AND in 2 OR ascendant. The second-house/ascendant alternative applies to placement, not a logical alternative to fixed-sign condition.
37. Either luminary in ascendant and not unfortunate => great ease. Neither in ascendant nor beholding it AND both cadent => little hope. The relationship of these subsidiary arguments to the prior acquisition promise is not formalized.
38. Part of Fortune in ascendant AND its lord/disposer there => acquisition promised. Adverse clause begins: lord of Fortune cadent, both lights especially Moon, with no aspect to Fortune; continues 252.

### PDF 252 / printed 218: XXXVII completion and Alkindus

39. Adverse acquisition continuation: lord of Fortune cadent + both lights (especially Moon) + no aspect to Fortune, OR ascendant lord not beholding ascendant => no hope of obtaining. Cadency is expressed first of Fortune's lord and then followed by `and both the Lights`; whether cadency is elided onto both lights is a real grammatical scope question. No automatic graph should erase it.
40. `Alkindus giveth this generall rule`: explicitly attributed doctrine begins here. Correctly erect figure and consider planetary aspects. Fortune in ascendant OR any angle => treasure present and still in ground; quantity/price/esteem according to potency/virtue/debility of that fortune. Preserve against earlier lord-4-based alternatives without inventing a hierarchy.
41. If unremoved: consider ascendant lord and Moon, a good aspect between them and the fortune that indicated treasure, clarified as good aspect AND reception => querent obtains it. The multi-actor wiring of `betwixt them, and that Fortune` is not fully formalized; preserve words and uncertainty.
42. Fixed signs => earth; common signs => in/near wall; movable => high/up in covering of houses. The passage does not re-name which significator's sign for this first mapping; nearest context is treasure significator.
43. Depth: treasure significator newly entered sign => shallow/upper earth. Further into sign => deeper; beginning/middle/end distinguished without degree cutoffs, units, linear conversion or maximum depth. This is ordinal guidance only.
44. Digging: infortunes should not be angular. `if possible`, significators apply by sextile/trine to lord 2 OR Moon separates FROM treasure significator THEN applies TO ascendant lord. This is action/election advice and preserves the temporal chain; it is not the same as the acquisition paragraph's application direction.
