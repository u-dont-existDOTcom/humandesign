## PDF236 / printed202 — fourth-house scope; XXXII begins

The heading is “Of the fourth House, and the Judgment depending thereupon.” Its preamble assigns parents; lands; tenements; hereditaments; cities, towns, villages; farms; manors; castles; treasure-trove; and things hidden in the ground to this house. This is a scope and significator assignment, not a forecast.

XXXII is “To find a thing hid or mislaid.” It first requires an exact Ascendant and identification of whose goods are missing. For the querent’s own commodity use lord of the second; for a brother’s or sister’s goods lord of the fourth; for the father’s goods lord of the fifth; for the mother’s goods lord of the eleventh; continue in order according to the party. The subsequent prose reverts to “Lord of the second”; it does not explicitly rewrite every ensuing condition for every derived owner.

If lord of the second is in any angle, the item may be judged within the questioner’s house. If it is in the Ascendant, or in the sign containing lord of the Ascendant, or in one of “his houses,” the item may be judged in the part the questioner most frequents, inhabits, uses, stores commodities in, or keeps favored things in. The antecedent of “his” in “his houses” is naturally lord of the Ascendant, but the original wording should accompany the paraphrase.

Lord of the second in the tenth gives locations by occupation/status: a mechanic’s shop; a gentleman’s hall or dining room; a husbandman’s ordinary common room or first room after entering. Lord of the second in the seventh places it where the wife or maidservants have most to do (sentence finishes at top of PDF237). These are alternatives within each role and not claims that every listed place is simultaneously occupied.

## PDF237 / printed203 — XXXII locations and explicit method disagreement

Lord of the second in the fourth places the item where the oldest member lodges or formerly frequented, in the middle of the house, or in its oldest part where a father or ancient man lodged. The signs occupied by significators identify the place’s nature/quality.

An airy sign of the second, or concurrence of the greater number of significators and the sign containing Part of Fortune, places the item in eaves/top/upper part of the house or room, or high above ground. If already located in a field, garden, or orchard, it is above ordinary ground, on its highest hill/part, or hanging on a plant stalk or tree. The airy nature is the quality being sought in the concurrence; no weighting, tie-breaking, or exact threshold beyond “greater number” is supplied.

The former significators being strong and in watery signs places it in a buttery, dairy, wash house, or near water. Fiery signs give chimney, iron, or in/near house walls. Earthy signs give on ground/earth, under/near pavement/floor; if mislaid outside in ground, near the bridge or stile by which people enter. “Strong” is explicit in the watery sentence and is not separately repeated for the next two elements; preserve that textual distinction rather than invent a universal strength test.

A significator leaving one sign and entering another gives alternatives: behind something; carelessly fallen between rooms; near a threshold; or at the joining of two rooms. Height is higher/lower according to sign nature. No degree tolerance for “going out” or “entering” is given.

Under marginal heading “What part of the house or ground,” Lilly reports the Ancients’ planetary-hour method: lord of the hour in the tenth or eleventh → south part toward east; between fourth and Ascendant → northeast; between fourth and seventh → northwest; between tenth and seventh → southwest. Keep the first branch’s explicit houses (10 or 11), rather than silently rewriting it to a complete quadrant. Boundary handling is not specified.

Lilly immediately identifies that as former astrologers’ opinion, says he has not found that judgment very exact, and introduces what he considered a more certain/exact way. The end of this sentence continues onto PDF238. Do not merge the reported ancient method with his later method, or portray both as unqualified Lilly endorsements.

## PDF238 / printed204 — Lilly’s alternative; anecdote; XXXIII begins

Lilly’s alternative is initially scoped to something mislaid/missing in a house and not stolen. He considers eight sign positions, in this order: rising sign (including its nature and direction); sign of Ascendant lord; fourth-house sign; sign of fourth lord; Moon sign; second-house sign; sign of second lord; Part of Fortune sign. Direction is chosen according to the greater number of testimonies. This is a tally procedure but supplies neither weights nor tie handling. The subsequent directional table explicitly extends to things lost, mislaid, or fugitives; retain both the opening house/not-stolen scope and the table’s stated extension.

The twelve signs and directions are: Aries east; Leo east by north; Sagittarius east by south; Libra west; Gemini west by south; Aquarius west by north; Cancer north; Scorpio north by east; Pisces north by west; Capricorn south; Taurus south by east; Virgo south by west. These are named directions, not numerically specified azimuths. Do not substitute modern angle values without marking an added convention.

Once the quarter is found, sign nature specifies place: airy above ground; fiery near a wall/partition; earthy on floor; watery near a moist place in the room. Lilly says a few experiments may improve the judgment. He reports sometimes, “in merriment,” setting a present figure and thereby finding the house-part in which a glove, book, or another object was hidden, and says he found the rule very true. This is an aggregate first-person historical experience claim. It gives no dates, charts, counts, predeclared directions, individual outcomes, failures, denominators, independent observers, or prospective evaluation details. It must not become a fabricated fully specified case or empirical accuracy estimate.

XXXIII begins “Of Buying and Selling Lands, Houses, Farmes, &c.” Assign Ascendant, its lord, and planet from which Moon separates to querent/buyer; seventh, its lord, and planet to which Moon applies to seller. The remaining assignments continue onto PDF239.

## PDF239 / printed205 — XXXIII purchase agreement; quality begins

Assign fourth house, planet therein, Moon, and fourth lord to house/ground/manor to be bought. Assign tenth, its occupant(s), and its lord to price (cheap/dear). These purchase assignments differ from the later lease subsection’s tenth = profit and fourth = end; keep their contexts.

Ascendant lord and seventh lord in an amicable aspect, with seventh lord applying to Ascendant lord, indicates seller’s goodwill to sell/deal. If the significators have essential dignities at their application or translation of light, or their application is conjunction, agreement and purchase with little labor is probable. Application/translation by square or opposition eventually gives a bargain, but with many words, prospects of breaking off, and much time. The source does not give a numeric delay or an override ranking against other negative testimonies.

For actual acquisition, consider application from Ascendant lord or Moon to fourth lord, or fourth lord or Moon to Ascendant lord; also fourth lord applying to Ascendant lord with the latter receiving him in any dignity. An occupation alternative follows: “or if the Lord of the ascendant be in the fourth, or the [Moon], or the Lord of the fourth in the ascendant.” This is grammatically compressed. It should be quoted/flagged rather than silently assign the isolated Moon either fourth or Ascendant. The stated conclusion is that the querent buys the house/inheritance in question.

If this occupation of houses does not obtain, but Moon transfers fourth lord’s light/virtue to Ascendant lord, the bargain concludes through messengers/brokers rather than the principals’ personal treaty. With no application, translation, or light transfer between planets, a concluded bargain is unlikely.

The unnumbered heading “Of the goodnesse or badnesse of the Land or House” begins at the page bottom. Its first condition runs across PDF239–240: two infortunes in the fourth, “very potent, or peregrine,” or fourth lord retrograde, unfortunate, in fall, or in detriment → the holding will not continue long with the purchaser’s posterity. The phrase about both infortunes being potent/peregrine is awkward but must be preserved, not modernized into a different dignity condition.

## PDF240 / printed206 — continuity, ground roles, tenants, timber

Jupiter, Venus, or North Node in the fourth, or fourth lord “in his owne house, viz. in the fourth” gives expected good success in the purchase, possible long continuance with posterity, and an argument for increase of the purchaser’s money. Preserve the explicit gloss “in the fourth”; do not silently convert it to zodiacal domicile.

For arable land and its nature, Ascendant represents tenants/husbandmen/farmers; fourth soil condition, nature, form (or the house(s) if asked about them); western angle quality and quantity of herbage; Midheaven wood, trees, and plants. These assignments govern the following subrules.

An infortune in Ascendant describes occupiers as ill/deceitful and unwilling that the land’s goodness be discovered. A fortune there gives the contrary: honest tenants, contentment and affection toward landlord, willingness to keep their holdings/occupation. An unfortunate planet there direct predicts tenants purloining woods or wearing out land’s virtue; retrograde predicts throwing land back on landlord, running away, or surrendering leases. Preserve the different outputs and direction condition.

For wood on the ground: a fortune in Midheaven direct → good timber and much of it; the fortune retrograde → many trees but little timber, lopped trees, recent seller sales/spoliation, or much decay. An infortune there direct → few trees; retrograde → country people have stolen/wasted it. These alternatives mix present condition with retrospective explanatory claims and must not be represented as newly observed facts.

If no planet is in Midheaven, use tenth lord: a good aspect to that house plus any own dignity gives some wood. This sentence continues on PDF241. Keep the empty-house prerequisite attached to this fallback.

## PDF241 / printed207 — timber fallback; herbage; land form and affliction

Under that no-planet-in-Midheaven fallback, tenth lord not aspecting Midheaven → little/no wood or worthless wood. Tenth lord oriental and aspecting its own house → young/small trees or coppices; occidental “in the condition aforesaid” → more-grown/ancient wood. Then direct → sound trees continuing a long time; retrograde → many trunks/hollow trees. The referent/scope of “then” and “condition aforesaid” should remain visible; no unmentioned wood-health condition is to be substituted.

For herbage/smaller plants, Jupiter, Venus, or seventh lord in seventh argues plentiful grass, corn, or the seasonally required yield. An infortune there gives contrary judgment. Again, no precedence is supplied if a planet qualifies both as seventh lord and infortune.

For terrain use fourth sign/cusp: Aries/Leo/Sagittarius → hilly, mountainous, dry, hard ground or largely so; Taurus/Virgo/Capricorn → level/open country and excellent pasture, grazing, or tillage; Gemini/Libra/Aquarius → mixed hills/plains and mixed good/bad ground; Cancer/Scorpio/Pisces → river/rivulet or abundant water.

General qualification: an infortune in fourth’s sign, retrograde or in fall/detriment, makes the land partake strongly in that planet’s misfortune. The punctuation leaves some possible compression, but its condition cannot be reduced to sign alone. The worked illustration is hypothetical: Scorpio on fourth cusp, Saturn therein, and Saturn either retrograde or otherwise afflicted → excess water or boggy/unwholesome, long rushy grass. If the land is near sea, fear sea incursion, defective sea banks, or overflow by river/water (last phrase crosses PDF242). This is contingent on the affliction example, not a unconditional coastal forecast.

## PDF242 / printed208 — further Saturn examples; price; leasing begins

Saturn afflicting a fiery sign in fourth → barren, stony, hungry, mountainous land; little/no profit without immense labor; lack of water; little grass. Saturn afflicting Gemini by presence, or the named humane signs Libra/Aquarius by retrogradation, with the relevant sign on fourth cusp → defective land whose former husbandmen managed unthriftily. Preserve the source’s syntactic distinction between presence and retrogradation; the sentence can also be read as a retrogradation condition applying to the whole humane-sign group, so retain uncertainty instead of fabricating a sole executable parse.

Saturn unfortunate in an earthy fourth sign → intrinsically good but heavy clay land, currently under-tilled/mismanaged by occupiers described as idle, lazy, slothful, penurious, unwilling to spend, and ignorant of the soil. Keep “land good” beside poor management rather than collapsing this into uniformly bad soil.

Under “Cheap or dear”: tenth lord angular, direct, and strong in essential dignities → high price/dear sale. Tenth lord “cadent, combust, retrograde, slow of motion, afflicted” → price not rising high. The second list has no written and/or between its members; retain its enumeration and mark Boolean grouping unspecified. Numeric thresholds for strong, slow, and price are absent.

The unnumbered lease/hire heading asks whether good to hire/take the desired farm, house, or land. Ascendant/lord = prospective hirer; seventh/lord = person letting or selling; tenth/lord = profit that may arise; fourth and occupants = end of taking or not taking the property.

Positive hirer testimony (crosses PDF243): Ascendant lord in Ascendant or rising sign; or that lord sextile/trine to rising sign, more properly rising degree, within the moiety of its own orb; or a fortune in Ascendant whether essentially dignified or not; or unimpeded Part of Fortune there. The output says the farmer shall take it, has hopes of benefit/a good bargain/profit, likes it and is pleased. Keep hopes and liking distinct from an independently observed financial result. The orb statement permits only a parameterized half-orb comparison; no numerical orb table is supplied here.

## PDF243 / printed209 — lease conditions and outcome

With either infortune in Ascendant, timing matters: already taken before consultation → regrets it now; not yet taken → no wish to take it; if nevertheless taken → soon transfers it to someone else, caring nothing for the bargain. These are separate case branches, not contradictory universal results.

Seventh lord in seventh, or benevolent aspect to its cusp, or fortune there → lessor keeps word and tenant gets what was bargained for, while lessor profits. Infortune in seventh and not lord of seventh → heed covenants/conditions, because landlord will overreach and pursue own ends. Preserve the explicit exception for an infortune that is seventh lord.

Fortune in tenth or aspecting tenth → despite obstacles parties proceed and property is let to querent. Unfortunate planet in tenth, or aspecting it by opposition/square → no hiring/taking. With land, probable disagreement concerns timber/wood or new building; with a house, repairs. These object-specific probable explanations should not become separate observed historical cases.

For ending: fortune in fourth, or fourth lord there, or fourth lord sextile/trine to that house → good conclusion pleasing both parties. Infortune there → bargain/demise pleases neither. Source does not settle cases where fourth lord is itself an infortune or mixed testimonies coexist.

## PDF244 / printed210 — XXXIV paternal estate

XXXIV is “If the Querent shall enjoy the Estate of his Father.” Ascendant/lord signifies querent; fourth/lord/occupant father; fifth/lord/occupant father’s personal estate or movable goods. This explicitly differs from treating all “estate” as landed property.

Second and fifth lords in reception, with fifth lord in second and second lord in fifth → querent obtains a competent fortune from father’s estate. Fifth lord retrograde or in a bad aspect with any malevolent planet → part intended for querent is wasted or otherwise disposed of by father. The latter is a modifying adverse testimony, not an independently proven event.

To identify cause/person of diminution, find planet impeding fifth lord by square/opposition, or Sun by combustion, and that planet’s house rulership. If sixth lord: probably father’s siblings, tenants, or neighbors persuade him to reduce intended provision. If seventh lord: woman/sweetheart or someone who has been at variance with querent withdraws parental intention. If twelfth lord: parson/parish priest (source uses pejorative wording) or maternal kin. Keep probability stated for the sixth branch and alternate persons rather than selecting one.

Conditional reconciliation: only if the described party is identified by the querent and the querent wishes to obtain favor/reduce malice, observe impeding planet and Ascendant lord approaching sextile, trine, or conjunction; on the day the ephemeris shows Moon separating from one and applying to the other, attempt reconciliation at/about that time. Lilly says the desires may be obtained and reports having found this many times by experience (sentence completes at PDF245). This combines doctrinal timing advice and an unspecific experience claim; it is not a charted observed case or deterministic psychological causation.

## PDF245 / printed211 — paternal benefit alternatives; timing limitation; preference

Fifth lord disposing Part of Fortune and in Ascendant or second → querent obtains expected desires from father. Jupiter or Venus from fifth giving benevolent aspects to any planet in querent’s second argues the same. Moon separating from fifth lord and presently thereafter sextile/trine to second lord or Ascendant lord → strong/assured hopes of acquiring what was demanded of father. Preserve “hopes” modality.

Infortune in fourth without dignity there → father reluctant to part with money; do not press him much until that planet leaves the sign. If waiting is impossible, observe that planet direct, swift, oriental, and sextile/trine to Jupiter, Venus, or Ascendant lord, then approach father. This is specifically for a querent wanting present means who cannot conveniently await father’s leisure. Crucial explicit limitation: Lilly does not say observing the times by themselves forces father’s mind/will; he claims more benevolent inclinations at those times. This limitation governs these timing suggestions and must not disappear when transformed into a rule.

Second and fifth lords applying by retrogradation to any good aspect → some estate received suddenly/unexpectedly. The source does not say whether both must be retrograde; retain “applying by Retrogradation” rather than inventing planet-by-planet motion prerequisites or a numeric horizon.

To judge whether father prefers querent or siblings, compare third lord/third occupants to Ascendant lord in relation to fourth lord. If a sibling significator is nearer or in better aspect to fourth lord, or has reception/translation with it while Ascendant lord and fourth lord have none, father’s affection is judged greater toward another. The nearest planet in aspect to fourth lord and the most powerful reception show the beloved party. “Better,” “nearest,” and “most powerful” are not metrically specified here, and the grouping of “and none betwixt” should be retained rather than exported as a decided Boolean scope. Stop at the page’s end, before XXXV.
