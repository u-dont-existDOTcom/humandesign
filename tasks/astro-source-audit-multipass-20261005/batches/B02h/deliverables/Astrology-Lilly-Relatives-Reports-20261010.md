## 1. The reference frame depends on the question and relationship

For concord with a brother, sister or neighbour, Lilly assigns the asker to the Ascendant lord and the other party to the third lord. For an absent brother, the third cusp becomes the brother's Ascendant and the fourth his substance house. He extends the same inclusive counting to an absent father, child and servant. These are relationship-specific source conventions. (PDF222–226 / printed188–192; R898, R906, R934–R936.)

| Person asked about | Their Ascendant in the original figure | Their substance house |
|---|---:|---:|
| Absent brother | 3 | 4 |
| Absent father | 4 | 5, by inclusive extension |
| Absent child, son or daughter | 5 | 6 |
| Absent servant | 6 | 7 |

Immediately after these instructions, Lilly says that although each house has its own sixth, eighth and twelfth, the **sixth of the figure** signifies the absent person's infirmity, the **eighth** death and the **twelfth** imprisonment. He then requires the practitioner to vary the rules without giving a complete precedence algorithm. Both statements have to survive extraction. (PDF226 / printed192; R937–R938.)

The worked absent-brother example makes this more concrete. Lilly examines Venus's freedom from affliction by **Mercury, ruler of the original eighth**, and **Mars, ruler of the eighth counted from the brother's Ascendant**. The eighth counted inclusively from the third is the original tenth. He therefore actually uses both eighth-house references in one judgement. (PDF231 / printed197; R976.)

The new reference function returns all twelve inclusive house mappings and the original-figure6/8/12 references separately. It leaves interpretive precedence unspecified and requires a named absent-relative context. Earlier records retain the different seventh-house frame for visiting an unrelated familiar person and the first-house frame for a general absent unrelated person. A future implementation must select its question frame before evaluating the conditions.

## 2. The outcome and degree of assertion matter

The absent-brother rules distinguish health, distress, fear, a feared past death, a likely future death and inability to leave a place. These cannot share a single adverse-outcome label without losing the source's meaning.

For example, malignant square/opposition without reception in the opening third-house context can mean that the brother **lives and is healthy while deeply distressed**. Reception in the corresponding adverse-contact branch permits escape from distress; it does not remove the distress premise. A later rule concerning likely death from an infirmity requires that the brother has **already been judged ill**. Significator in the eighth can instead signify **his fear that he will die**. The adverse tenth-house clause says that it **may be feared he is dead**. (PDF223–225 / printed189–191; R909–R912, R921, R923, R926.)

Other distinctions are equally concrete: trying to obtain an estate is not having acquired it; making strenuous efforts to escape when opportunity arises is not a successful escape; advice intended for someone's good is not advice whose usefulness has been established. The inventory preserves the source's alternatives, reception qualifications, house context and stated modality.

The general comparison found an instructive error in the first draft. Lilly's adverse third-house paragraph says present unity **will not continue**, then says hatred or grumbling **usually** follows. The draft had weakened the first part into lack of a promise. The corrected record preserves the stronger historical statement and attaches “usually” only to the following outcome. The source's moral and medical characterizations remain historical assertions, with no current-person inference performed.

## 3. Report truth, harm and political benefit are separate targets

Lilly introduces several ancient methods for reports, then gives his own account of judging news during the Civil War. His favourable parliamentary pattern requires Moon in one of four houses, a specified prior separation, and application by **sextile, trine or conjunction** to the Ascendant lord. The sextile glyph is visible in the original but dropped from the text extraction. (PDF226 / printed192; R939–R940.)

In that wartime account, a report can be true and tend to Parliament's good whether its surface content sounds good or bad. Application to seventh lord concerns which side benefits. The void-of-course passage reports that news proved inconsequential and was **usually** vain or false. A later question asks whether news will harm the enquirer. Its favourable opening pair is **Jupiter or Venus in the Ascendant**, followed by a different Moon/Mercury dignity-and-aspect alternative. (PDF226–227 / printed192–193; R940–R947.)

The ancient rules also include a secrecy outcome: with Ascendant lord under the Sun's beams or combust, few will know the truth. That is an epistemic limitation, not itself a verdict of true or false. Two source readings remain materially uncertain: the cancelled word before “naturally ill” on PDF224 and the visible spelling **“ufortunate”** in the Moon alternative on PDF228. Neither has been silently repaired into a clean machine condition. (R917, R952, R954; unresolved U07 andU14.)

## 5. Two historical enquiries contain different levels of evidence

### Absent London brother,1645

The asker already knew that his brother had gone west and that no news had arrived for weeks. Lilly's requested judgements concern life, a conditional question about killing by soldiers, news, location and homecoming. Once he judges the brother alive, he stops only the conditional killing enquiry. His statement that the Moon has little part in the question is expressly limited to **describing the parties**; he uses her in the later reasoning. (PDF230–231 / printed196–197; R972–R977.)

The narrative includes two reported operational episodes. The querent later says that about four a carrier arrived and reported the brother alive and healthy. Lilly separately says the brother returned the following Tuesday. The carrier's arrival, time and report of health are components of one episode in one enquiry, not independent cases. The account also contains Lilly's descriptions of the asker's physique and the brothers' existing concord. There is no original prediction freeze or independent external verification in this source packet. (PDF232–233 / printed198–199; R982, R987–R988.)

Lilly's southeast location is **within the county already visited**, not an unconditional southeast bearing from London. He estimates distance as one or two days' travel. These specific location and distance judgements do not receive separate confirmation in the passage. Returning home is not enough to certify them. (R983–R985.)

### Cambridge rumour,1643

This example states that Cambridge had not been taken and that it should not be taken by the king or his forces. Those are two different temporal claims. The later discussion repeats Lilly's judgement and rationale; it does not supply a separate confirming account. The future claim also lacks an explicit forecast horizon. (PDF233–235 / printed199–201; R990–R999.)

Lilly then asks what would follow **had** the question concerned siblings. The same chart becomes a hypothetical teaching example for sibling abundance, more sisters than brothers, indications of brothers as well, and concord. There is no new actual enquiry or reported family outcome. He again declines an exact sibling count. (PDF235 / printed201; R1000–R1008.)

This refusal agrees with the earlier section: the planet-count doctrine is attributed to “some,” and Lilly considers demanding such particulars from a horary question too scrupulous. A reported doctrine plus explicit rejection must not enter the inventory as his endorsed counting method. The original also says masculine sign or **house**, where the text extraction says “hour.” (PDF229 / printed195; R961–R963.)
