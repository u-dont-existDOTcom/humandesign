## PDF231 / folio197: first narrative and ChapterXXX

### Setup above chapter heading

In November1645 a citizen of London had gone into the West of England. His brother had no news for many weeks and pressed Lilly for judgment. Numbered questions: (1) living or dead, and, only if dead, killed by soldiers; the text associates this concern with the kingdom then being full of soldiery; (2) if living, when the querent should hear from/about him and where he was; (3) when he would come home. These are inputs/questions, not evidence that the requested conditions occurred.

### Description and roles

The ascendant represents the querent's shape and form, with consideration of Saturn, lord of its sign. Both ascending sign and lord are called dry. Lilly says the querent was lean, spare of body, and Saturnine. This combines a stated interpretive method with a retrospective description of this querent.

Taurus is the ascendant of the third house. Venus, lady of that sign, represents the absent brother / person inquired after. The Moon, because she applied to neither significator, had little to do **in the description of the parties**. The limiting words matter: Lilly uses the Moon later in the same question, so this does not exclude her from the entire judgment.

### Living/dead judgment

Venus, significatrix of the quesited, is said to be unafflicted by Mercury, lord of the figure's eighth, and by Mars, lord of the eighth counted from the quesited's ascendant. The Moon's separation is called good: from a dexter trine of Jupiter; her next application is a conjunction of the Sun upon the medium-coeli cusp. On these combined grounds Lilly judged that the brother was alive, had suffered no casualty, and was in good health. These are judgments at this point, not yet a reported independent outcome. Because he judged the man alive, he says no further judgment is needed on the remainder of the first question, meaning the conditional soldier-killing question.

House roles: radical first = asking brother; radical third = absent brother's turned first; radical eighth = eighth of the figure; eighth from radical third = radical tenth by inclusive turning, with Mars named as its lord in the source. Keep the explicit source wording and the arithmetic expansion distinct.

## PDF232 / folio198: “When heare of him.”

Venus is lady of the third; Saturn is lord of the ascendant. Lilly says that their signs and degrees show a mutual application by friendly trine. Saturn is ponderous, but retrograde and at more degrees of his sign than Venus; consequently he retrogrades to meet her. This is called a very good argument of very sudden news of the brother.

The cited *Ephemeris of Eichstadius*, November7,1645, is said to place the true Venus-Saturn trine at five in the afternoon on the day of the question. Reduction to the London meridian gives “a little after foure.” This is a source-reported ephemeris timing and meridian reduction, not a calculation recomputed in this reading. No original-ephemeris meridian or exact reduction minutes are supplied here.

Lilly advised the querent to go to carriers from the countries where he knew his brother had been and ask when they had seen him. He said it was probable he would hear that very day, relying, expressly, on the friendly trine meeting of the two significators.

Italic reported endpoint: the querent subsequently confidently affirmed that about four a carrier came casually to where he was and informed him the brother was living and in health. This is a reported querent assertion, relayed by Lilly; the carrier's news, time, and content belong to one dependent news-arrival episode, not three independent cases. It does not say the querent followed the instruction to visit carriers.

## PDF232/198 through PDF233/199: “Where he was.”

Known input: the journey had been westward. Lilly finds the brother's Venus leaving Sagittarius, called northeast, and entering Capricorn, called south; he consequently places the brother in the southeast part of the county to which he went. This is a regional subdivision within the known destination county, not an unconditional southeast bearing from London. He does not give a numeric direction formula.

Venus is described as not far from the ascendant and in the oriental quarter of heaven. Lilly judges the brother no more than one or two days' journey from London. The text supplies no numeric angular-to-distance scale.

Venus is leaving Sagittarius and entering Capricorn, where she has essential dignity by triplicity and term. Lilly therefore judges the man is leaving the country/place in which he had no possession or habitation and returning to his own London house, where he had good property rights. This depends on a change in dignity and the known question context; it should not be turned into a universal assertion that every sign ingress means going home.

Venus is said to lack one degree of leaving Sagittarius; Lilly predicts arrival home in less than a week. Sagittarius is bicorporeal/common, and he says one degree in that sign and the nature of this question could denote a week. Printed Venus28:53 would leave1°07′ to the sign boundary, so the source's “one degree” is qualitative/rounded, not an exact-minute identity.

Reported endpoint: the brother came home the following Tuesday, when the Moon came to the body of Venus; Venus had then entered Capricorn, her own terms, and her diurnal triplicity. The source does not print that Tuesday's calendar date or a clock time. It combines a reported event with a reported astrological circumstance; neither should be counted as a separate new chart.

Saturn and Venus, the two brothers' significators, have an amicable aspect. Lilly says the two brothers always did and still do agree lovingly. This is relationship testimony tied to the existing significators, not a separately requested or independently documented relationship case.

Methodological closing: vary judgment with the significators' positions and the matter proposed; this method may be used for matters belonging to the third house. This contextual instruction limits overgeneralization from the worked example.

## PDF235 / folio201: continuation and hypothetical sibling interpretation

Mars and Saturn in square are said to show division, treason, and mutually thwarted designs among the enemies, so that nothing good would come to them from this report. Lilly concludes that Cambridge was not taken and reports of its taking were lies. This is still his source judgment, not a separate narrated verification.

Explicit hypothetical: **“Had this Question been propounded, Whether the Querent should have Brethren or Sisters?”** The author says to convert the judgment accordingly. It is the same printed chart reused as teaching material.

Scorpio, sign of the third, is fruitful. Cancer, containing its lord, is fruitful. The Moon applies to Venus, who is in a fruitful sign. Reference to printed page89 for the signs classed prolific. These indications could assure the hypothetical querent of both brothers and sisters or plentiful/numerous kin.

More sisters than brothers are indicated because the relevant signs are feminine (reference printed page88), and Mars, lord of the third, is in a feminine sign. “All the signs” must be retained in its local fertility-chain context; the very next clause explicitly calls the Moon's Gemini masculine, so it cannot safely mean every sign anywhere in the entire chart.

Counterbalancing indication of brother(s): Moon, dispositor of Mars, is in masculine Gemini and in a platick sextile with Jupiter. Jupiter is described as masculine, angular, and in a masculine sign and house. This provides the reason for expecting a brother or brethren as well as the female predominance. The sextile is not claimed exact: a later clause says the Moon has lately separated and remains within its orbs.

The certain number is withheld: Lilly says that is too nice a point in art; he intends only general satisfaction and leaves the certain number to divine Providence. Do not turn “numerous kin” into a counted prediction.

Final harmony rule/application: third house not afflicted; no ill aspect between Venus, querent's significatrix, and Mars, third lord; both in signs of the same nature; Moon applying by dexter square to Venus in signs of short ascensions; Moon having lately been, and still being within orbs of, sextile Jupiter. These jointly argue agreement, concord, and unity between this hypothetical querent and kin and the brothers or sisters he should have in future. The source wording “no wayes afflicted, or any ill aspect” is grammatically elliptical; retain the apparent negation scope and do not treat an ill aspect as a positive concord condition. It explicitly treats this qualified square as part of favorable evidence. No numeric orb threshold is supplied here.
