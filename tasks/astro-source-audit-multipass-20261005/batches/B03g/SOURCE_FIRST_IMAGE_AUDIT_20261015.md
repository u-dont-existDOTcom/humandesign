## Historical judgment and uncertainty

The author says he forecast travel westward, a present position a few houses west of the master, return within one or two days, and recovery of goods. He then reports return on the following Friday and the servant’s own claim of staying at Kingston upon Thames. Lilly explicitly qualifies that location report with *which if true*. His westerly and nearby-water interpretations use the claimed Kingston location and the Moon in Cancer.

**Important:** the consulted figure does not show an unambiguous exact consultation date, so a reader cannot calculate the forecast-to-Friday interval. The claimed three-or-four-houses distance is not independently shown to agree with the Kingston narrative; moreover, one concerns location at questioning and the other reported location previously visited, so a direct contradiction is not established. Recovering the servant does not independently establish the assertion of recovered goods. The author’s reported success is **source-internal**, not a blinded, independently documented predictive success.

The source characterizes historical bodily traits, humoral dispositions, astrological factors and servant status; those are the author’s period-bound claims and not validated psychological or health judgments, nor endorsements of treating people as property. Venus/Jupiter or other unobserved planetary configurations have not been added. No complete blind diplomatic transcription, ephemeris parity, validated human outcomes or runtime model changes are claimed.
