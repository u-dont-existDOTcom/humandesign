## First distinctions: querent, goods, and alleged thief (printed330–334)

Lilly begins this theft section with early-modern definitions and mediated authority, including his report of **Haly**. He differentiates the querent/loser (first house and ascendant lord), the supposedly stolen thing (second house/lord and Moon in particular contexts), and the alleged thief (seventh house/lord; sometimes a **peregrine planet angular** or the **hour lord** under a stated precedence). That is a hierarchy of symbolic roles, **not** license to substitute arbitrary planets or identify a person as a thief. The source distinguishes whether a thing went missing accidentally, was taken in jest, has actually been stolen, or is merely suspected to be. In some cases planetary contact suggests recovery even without certainty about theft. [Original PDF364–368; source units R2306–R2377]

This material is unusually conditional. A planet must actually occupy the relevant house or be the designated significator; an **application** cannot silently become separation, and a **conjunction** cannot silently become a sextile or trine. The source mixes reception, transfer of virtue, squares/oppositions, Moon-house lords and the luminaries. A disposition about stolen goods is not an independently observed act or a general definition of criminal conduct.

## Distinct theft, non-theft and future-theft cases (printed334–336)

Printed334 opens *Whether it be stolen or no?*, which is followed by separate headings *That the Goods are stolen*, *Not stolen*, *It will be (or is intended to be stolen)* and *It is Lost or Stolen* on printed335. The rules can conflict or refer to different times. For example, a **peregrine** significator and certain transfers of planetary virtue are used as supposed theft evidence, while other contacts are interpreted as mere loss or negligence. The author gives **future or intended theft** its own conditional heading, not a recorded completed crime. [Original PDF368–370; R2358–R2405]

A fresh image-first reviewer found an easily missed destination: Moon as seventh-house ruler giving virtue to a planet in the **second, eleventh OR FIFTH**; the next printed negative aspect to house cusps has uncertain attachment to those alternatives. Another source clause has a planet in seventh transmitting virtue to a second/eleventh planet and an ambiguous additional **no-sextile/no-trine** condition; its grammatical subject is not clearly stated. The revised source records preserve both issues rather than creating a self-aspect. [PDF369; R2392–R2393; independent_review/OPUS_B_INITIAL.json]

## Alleged age, gender, number of people and human description (printed336–339)

Lilly's unnumbered headings cover *Of the age of the Thief*, a second *The age of the Thiefe*, *Whether the Thiefe be a Man or Woman*, and *If one Thiefe or more*. They combine planetary class, oriental/occidental placement, Moon growing or diminishing in light, degree and house, fixed/movable/common signs, and gendered/significator distinctions. The text is early-modern profiling **without empirical evidence**; it cannot support any claim about a real person's age, gender, guilt or identity. [PDF370–373; R2406–R2463]

The printed p339 six classifications—signs alleged to have many children, few children, diverse forms, barren signs, direct ascensions and oblique ascensions—were initially compressed into one source summary. Six separate original-source classifications were subsequently restored from the overlapping B reader, while the raw C source draft and discarded overlap remain preserved for audit. The chapter includes at least one apparent tension: a fixed-sign/direct-ascension condition points to **one** thief, while another apparently immovable-sign condition points to **more than one**. The source's printed claims are kept distinct rather than silently repaired to agree. [PDF373; OVERLAP_ADJUDICATION.json; READER_OVERLAP_AUDIT.json]

## Clothes, colors, names and printed numerical/planet tables (printed339–342)

The author proposes judgments of a supposed person's **clothing color** from planets, planetary mixtures, signs, degrees and house. He also reproduces named-author color attributions and a historical name-to-planet table, divided into **men's and women's names**. The original PDF374–375 table images are retained as controlling evidence. The present normalized headings and table-level source units **do not constitute a checked diplomatic transcription of every table cell**, including blank companion columns, repeated planet combinations and glyphs; a formula cannot create verified name mappings. No claim is made that names, clothes or genders actually correlate with these placements. [PDF373–375; TABLE_WITNESS_REGISTER.json]

Another image-first reviewer corrected the apparent second planet in a rule relating long names (Richard or William): the printed pair is **Saturn or Venus**, although the Venus glyph is slightly degraded. This is retained as a qualified source reading. [PDF374; R2475; independent_review/OPUS_C_INITIAL.json]

## Familiarity, household roles and seventh-lord house categories (printed342–344)

The headings *Whether the Thief be of the house or not*, *A Stranger or Familiar*, *Another*, and *Rules by the Lord of the seventh house* shift from theft indications to conjectures about whether the imagined culprit is an acquaintance or stranger. Printed343–344 supplies house-by-house categories keyed to **seventh lord in ascendant/fourth, second, third, fourth, fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth and onward**. An overlapping reader duplicated several clauses on PDF377; each is counted only once from its own printed passage. The printed caution **“This must be warily understood”** is kept attached to the potentially accusatory seventh-house example. [PDF376–378; R2487–R2526; OVERLAP_REVIEW_PENDING.md]

Two reviewer corrections preserve unusual source nuance: the second-house paragraph speaks of being **within the power of the loser or of someone of his house**, and says recovery **may be obtained by money**; this is not simply household membership. The eleventh-house paragraph allows a **neighbor** as a possible separate category and says recovery occurs **by such a person OR his means**, without proving that any neighbor, servant or relative committed theft. [PDF377–378; R2511,R2523; independent_review/OPUS_D_INITIAL.json]

## Town, distance, location and direction (printed344–347)

Further original headings ask whether the person is still **in town**, how far owner and alleged thief are apart, *Where the Thief is*, *Another*, and *Toward what part / Which way the Thief is gone*. The source advances more than one distance scheme: some rules rely on significator house and motion, while others count degrees and assign differing units based on fixed/common/movable signs. Separate statements compare angular positions, strength, applying configurations and apparently conflicting directions. These are **early-modern conjectural location methods**, not accurate maps or travel-time predictions. [PDF378–381; R2526–R2571]

On printed342, the author uses **Moon in the ninth, in conjunction, square or opposition** to Saturn or Mars as a supposed return-of-the-thief condition; the first glyph had initially been transcribed as a **sextile**, materially changing the source. The independent source review corrected it to **conjunction** while retaining a further ambiguous retrograde condition. The figure is a historical condition and is not evidence about an actual fugitive or stolen object. [PDF376; R2499; independent_review/OPUS_C_INITIAL.json]

The body and marginalia on printed346 also require separation: **seventh lord in ascendant** is said to indicate the thief returning home before the querent, followed by the author's *probatum est* claim. The printed margin separately says it **sometimes** indicates willingness to bring the goods home. The latter word *sometimes* cannot automatically be made an unconditional qualifier of the body. No independently confirmed instance is supplied. [PDF380; R2551–R2552; independent_review/OPUS_D_INITIAL.json]

## First source-window readbacks: corrected source directions and open glyph (printed331–335)

The opening theft passage was independently re-read from original images in two separate smaller windows. Both first-pass reviews returned **FINDS_ERROR** and found several material changes of meaning. The printed p331 rule naming **LORD OF THE HOUR as ALSO LORD OF SEVENTH** only increases the supposed radicality of judgment; a **separate** later source claim says seventh lord in ascendant may point to querent only under a specific question of fidelity or when querent is the chief object of suspicion. The original source's subsequent complexion/sign rule does **not** require the hour/seventh identity. These are now separate R2319 and R2572 in \`SOURCE_ORDER.json\`, not one merged implied crime. A printed marginal word is **'consentanious'** (consonant with reason), not 'conscientious'; another sentence promises hope of **discovery** of goods, not actual guaranteed **recovery**. [PDF365; R2319,R2572,R2320,R2322,R2327; independent_review/OPUS_A1_INITIAL.json]

Multiple p332–333 worked conditional examples were repaired: the Sun as lord of second is in **TENTH**, not third, and no unprinted Sun/seventh sextile is added. In another example, Venus is in tenth **in sign of tenth**, while Moon **opposes** the seventh lord; a separate 'taken in jest' example also has Moon **opposing** seventh lord, not conjoining it. Another outcome says stolen-in-jest property will **'scant be had again'** (hard to recover), the opposite of a previous 'soon recovered' paraphrase. [PDF366–367; R2339,R2343,R2345,R2347; independent_review/OPUS_A1_INITIAL.json; OPUS_A2_INITIAL.json]

**Important unresolved original glyph:** A1 and A2 read the unusual aspect glyph in the p332 'taken away in jest' clause differently (**opposition vs conjunction** to the second HOUSE). The repaired R2338 preserves BOTH competing original-image readings as uncertainty, rather than making the attractive interpretation an executable rule. The destination is the printed **second HOUSE**, not an inferred second-house lord. More generally, source modalities 'in jest', 'scarcely recovered', 'possibly discovered', 'actually stolen' and 'suspected' are distinct authorial assertions, none empirically authenticated. [PDF366; R2338; A1_A2_RECONCILIATION.json]

Later source copies bear **handwritten** margin numbers 1–3, 5–8, possibly others, a large 7th-house annotation, the printed signature **Kk 3**, and a small letter/caret beside the printed last-line **Planet** on p335. These are catalogued as manuscript/bibliographic witnesses, **not additional numbered Lilly rules**; the owner of the annotations and their relation to the same-volume errata are unknown.
