## Important source interpretations

### Alternative orb columns create flexibility that must be frozen

At printed p.107 / PDF 141, Lilly supplies two orb columns, says he sometimes uses one and sometimes the other as memory permits, and asserts that this produces no error. That assertion is his, **not independent testing**. In the worked example, Venus at Taurus 10° and Saturn at Virgo 18° are 8° away from exact trine. Under the first printed column, their moieties add to **9°**, so the contact is within a platick orb. Under the second column, the moieties add to **8°**, precisely the uncertain **within/equality boundary**. The new reference function requires an explicit source column and returns unresolved for equality. It never chooses retrospectively.

A **partile** aspect and a **platick** aspect remain different: the first is a locally exact degree-and-minute meeting; the second uses the two relevant full orbs' half-sum. Lilly provides no universal probabilistic effect multiplier.

### Aspect polarity differs from actual event completion

At p.106, Lilly describes a square between adversaries as imperfect hostility and an opposition as complete hostility in his illustrative **reconciliation question**. The description does not say every natal square causes hostility.

At printed p.125 / PDF 159 he nevertheless allows a **square to perfect the question** when each principal has dignity in its own degrees and both act from suitable houses; otherwise it does not. He describes a still narrower **opposition perfection**, with reciprocal domicile reception, friendly houses and the Moon separating from the quesited significator then applying to the querent's lord. He says the matter may actually happen while remaining regrettable or costly. His examples distinguish marrying from marital contentment, and recovering a debt from receiving net benefit after litigation. These passages must be held alongside the earlier broad positive reception language, **not resolved by choosing whichever fits a known event**.

### Translation, collection, prohibition and refranation are not equivalent

In XIX, **prohibition** involves an intervening planet reaching an aspect before the intended principals perfect; **refranation** involves reversal before perfection. Neither is just an arbitrary hard aspect at birth. The book distinguishes *translation* (a lighter intermediary leaving one principal and applying to another) from *collection* (both principals cast aspects toward a heavier third planet).

XXI adds decisive conditions to these abbreviated descriptions. Translation requires the source principal to **receive the translating planet by domicile, triplicity or term**, and no other intervening encounter. Collection requires **both principals to receive the collector** in essential dignity; the source explicitly describes their direction of reception. Bare house-dwelling is attributed to earlier authorities but **rejected as sufficient** by Lilly without the Moon transferring the desired matter's light to the Ascendant ruler (printed pp.126–127 / PDF 160–161).

The present reference code deliberately does **not** turn these into a planetary event-sequence solver or make claims about when a particular event will happen.

### Solar visibility has an unresolved internal example

Printed p.113 / PDF 147 defines combustion as within **8°30′** of the Sun in the same sign, yet immediately calls a configuration separated by **10°** combust. It separately gives **17°** for under-the-beams and **17 arcminutes** for cazimi. We preserve both definition and contradiction. The code identifies only the literally attested ten-degree example as conflicting, and leaves exact threshold equality and zone precedence unresolved. It does not silently repair Lilly from modern manuals or infer that the entire 8°30′–10° range is allowed.

### Apparent brightness or house role does not replace a topic-qualified significator

XX identifies the Ascendant and lord as representing the asker and the Moon as a co-significator in questions. Lilly expressly **rejects automatically adding the planet from which the Moon separated** as another querent-significator. XXI asks separately **whether** a matter happens, **by what means**, **when**, and **whether proceeding benefits the querent**. These distinct outcomes must not collapse into an undifferentiated positive match.

For turned houses (printed p.127 / PDF 161), a seventh-house subject's house becomes that subject's first: their property radical eighth, their father radical tenth, and so on. Conversely, a king *asking* the question is still represented by the radical Ascendant, as is a king's own nativity, whereas asking *about* a king uses the tenth. Reference-frame changes and topics cannot silently be interchanged.

## Reference tables and quality limitations

The observed strength table (printed p.115 / PDF 149) contains **41 source rows** across essential/accidental strengths/debilities. It uses multiple **OR alternatives in one row**: being in domicile *or* mutually received by domicile is one listed five-point entry, not two independently accumulable entries. Its positive and negative rows, positional conditions, historical fixed-star references, daylight/sector and visibility requirements, and several mutually overlapping states are **not** promoted to an executable net personality or event score.

The degree and historical body-part tables (printed pp.116–119 / PDF 150–153) preserve damaged glyphs and overwritten printed tokens as explicitly unresolved. These are historical medical/body claims, **not diagnostic facts**. An incomplete non-beholding table remains incomplete rather than being silently expanded to all geometrically absent aspects.
