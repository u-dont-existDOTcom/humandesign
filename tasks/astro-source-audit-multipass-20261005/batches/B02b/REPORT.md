## 1. Lilly explicitly disagrees with the node doctrine he reports
At printed 83/PDF 117, Lilly first reports an ancient doctrine: the Head is good with good planets and evil with evil planets; the Tail does the opposite. He then says his own experience differs: the Head lessens malefic effects and strengthens benefic effects; the Tail strengthens malefic effects.

These are opposing interpretations of the same configuration. They cannot become two votes for the same conclusion. The extraction and lookup helper retain both under explicit doctrine labels.

His Tail-with-benefic discussion is also conditional: a benefic must signify in the question. Obstacles, disputes and apparent hopelessness are not identical to ultimate failure. The failure report is qualified by principal significators being angular AND essentially fortified. That exception does not guarantee smooth or successful completion. The words about doubled/trebled harm are not converted into a numeric multiplier.

A targeted comparison with Holden's Rhetorius chapter 54, printed 38–39, finds the reported ancient pattern in the sixth consideration. Crucially, Holden identifies this passage as supplied from the Latin Book of Hermes after the Greek breaks off. It is retained as that transmission layer, not a claim of unanimous Greek doctrine or independent confirmation. The remainder of Rhetorius is not newly audited here.

## 2. “Well dignified” does not remove difficult characteristics
The seven planets each have separate conditional manners descriptions. Mars's well-dignified branch includes courage and prudent management of his own affairs, but also contentiousness, refusal of subjection and concern with victory. The Moon's favourable branch preserves timidity, prodigality, instability and present-focused concern alongside peace, novelty and learning. Venus's includes sociability and virtue alongside reluctance to labour and easy belief.

Sources: Mars printed 66/PDF 100; Venus73–74/PDF 107–108; Moon81/PDF 115. The underlying qualifiers must be established first: the Jupiter chapter explicitly refers to a person's significator in a question or the Ascendant lord in a nativity, AND well dignified (printed 62–63/PDF 96–97). Mere presence of a planet in a chart does not select its whole portrait.

The helper therefore distinguishes relevant, irrelevant and unknown, with WELL, ILL, MIXED and UNKNOWN conditions. Mixed returns the competing source branches without pretending the text supplies a numerical averaging rule.

## 4. The slow Moon's analogy does not change its astronomical motion
At printed 80/PDF 114, Lilly states that the Moon is always direct but calls a daily motion below 13°10′ equivalent to a retrograde planet. In the same paragraph he gives a mean of13°10′36″.

The reference function uses the exact strict threshold, does not substitute the mean including its extra 36 seconds, and never changes the recorded astronomical direction. No ephemeris was run or replaced.

The same source-reference discipline preserves Mercury's conjunction-based gender condition separately from its general aspect assimilation (printed 77–78/PDF 111–112), and the Sun's figure-based orientality separately from other planets' solar appearances (printed 70/PDF 104).
