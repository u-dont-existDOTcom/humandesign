**The multi-part question and shifts in forecast**: The unknown querent is described as personally invested in Parliament. Rupert as a noble figure is assigned the tenth, Scorpio and Mars, distinct from querent/ascendant. Moon and Jupiter are said to cast sinister and dexter trines to tenth, giving an initial impression of respect and personal protection. Lilly explicitly reports studying the case for **24 hours** before settling on a judgment of **no glorious military honor, future hostility, departure without love/friendship, but survival**. He interprets Mars's detriment and fixed sign as obstinacy. The initially positive popular respect forecast must not be retrospectively converted into the same final no-honor forecast.

**Essex comparison**: Essex is represented by Venus as lord of the house opposite Rupert's tenth, with Saturn terms/reception, Moon light translation and a separating Venus/Mars square. Lilly identifies a preceding conflict as **Edgehill**, already more than a month before the horary date, and claims Essex had an advantage because he held the ground. The battle is a *pre-question event*; one cannot count identifying it as a prospective prediction. His assertion that Rupert would never prevail against Essex and his later assertion that Rupert did not are respectively source forecast and authorial retrospective claimed result. Neither has an independent date-frozen observation here. Independent historians may dispute what constitutes victory at Edgehill.

**Rupert's fate: explicit error**: Lilly predicts that Rupert will come under Parliament's power and that Parliament could determine his fate. On the same original PDF488 he **explicitly acknowledges 'an errour in part'**. He later appeals to the 1646 Oxford siege and the claimed Oatlands departure after surrender as a 'very near truth', while also saying Parliament allowed Rupert to leave and he escaped. The actual escape materially undercuts any literal prediction of continued custody/discretion. The document's retrospective self-repair must not be scored as an independent success.
