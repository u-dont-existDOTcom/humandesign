## 1. Whose words are being read

Lilly's italic introduction says that he has favoured Dariot's method of judgment in sickness and is offering the following material to the reader. The heading therefore establishes an abridgement context; it does not make every following first-person sentence a quotation from Dariot. Explicit marginal attributions and first-person transitions identify some local contributions. Other stretches remain uncertain at sentence level. The extraction records those differences, including reported material from “the Ancients,” rather than assigning a single author to the entire block.

[Original PDF292/printed258, PDF293/printed259, PDF295/printed261, PDF301/printed267; R1550, R1556, R1570, R1571, R1644; `RULES.json`, `UNRESOLVED.json`.]

This batch reads Lilly's 1647 English presentation. It does not claim a comparison with an independently selected original edition of Dariot. The final upper paragraph announces the forthcoming Hermes material and explains Lilly's editorial reasons for including it: variety of teaching, the burden of sickness, the desirability of health, the writer's reported esteem, and its relevance to judgments of the sixth house. Those are Lilly's reported editorial and reception claims; they do not establish ancient authorship or independent clinical corroboration.

## 2. Sickness and outward causes retain their prerequisites

The first sickness rules distinguish the **querent's significator** from a disease significator. The illustrative example puts Mars, described as hot and dry and ruling the ascendant, in cold and moist Cancer. Cadency is a separately stated alternative, with the sixth house emphasized. Another sentence gathers adverse conditions for a diurnal significator below the earth; its mixture of conjunctions and alternatives does not supply unambiguous computational parentheses. The nocturnal extension says to make the necessary corresponding adjustments without enumerating a mechanical inversion.

[Original PDF293/printed259; R1551, R1552, R1553, R1554, R1555; `RULES.json`.]

Lilly then describes his own practice when a question was brought with or without urine. His no-sickness chain begins with an ascendant that is **not** afflicted, followed by adverse lord conditions introduced with “or.” Later alternatives include favourable or unafflicted configurations. The printed syntax leaves the reach of the negative unsettled. The extraction retains the entire chain and its distinct conclusions—no sickness, no following sickness, or a present distemper soon rectified—without adding an unprinted second negative or choosing one executable polarity.

[Original PDF293/printed259, PDF294/printed260; R1556, R1557; `RULES.json`, `UNRESOLVED.json`, `TEXTUAL_CHECKS.json`.]

The inward-cause discussion uses the significator's disposition, sign, house and **good or ill** configuration with malevolent planets. It adds that any planet **may** act malignantly here through such roles as lord of the sixth, eighth or twelfth. This is a context-sensitive role statement. Neither a permanently favourable classification for Jupiter or Venus nor an assumption that only bad aspects matter reproduces all of this wording.

[Original PDF293/printed259; R1558, R1559; `RULES.json`.]

In the outward-cause example, the ascendant lord is strong in essential dignity, swift and in a good house. The subsequent square or opposition of Saturn or Mars retains two exclusions: the afflicting planet is neither the sixth lord nor the Moon's dispositor. Lilly distinguishes being unwell from being perfectly sick, then directs attention to the afflictor's occupied or ruled house. The second-house example additionally requires no inward cause: application concerns current money want, whereas separation concerns a recent loss. The larger harm assigned to opposition belongs to this local example and is not a universal aspect weight.

[Original PDF293/printed259, PDF294/printed260; R1560, R1561, R1562, R1563, R1564, R1565, R1566; `RULES.json`.]

The fifth- and seventh-house examples contain further qualifications. Games require the person's capacity for them; the youth/distemper parenthesis in the fifth-house sentence has uncertain reach. The seventh-house passage gives several alternative sources of discontent and reciprocal husband/wife references. The youth continuation concludes that no sickness follows. These are general or hypothetical interpretations, with a later broad claim about Lilly's customary practice. They are not separately documented patient histories with observed endpoints.

## 3. Disease qualities: exact actors, modifiers and glyphs

The planetary catalogue follows an instruction to erect the figure and select the disease significator. A planet's mere presence is therefore insufficient to transfer every catalogue consequence into a chart. Saturn's tedious diseases, Jupiter's liver and blood descriptions, Mars's fever material, and the following Sun, Venus and Mercury statements retain that local context and their distinct conditions. Historical claims about bloodletting, sweating or other causes remain documentary content; no treatment rule is implemented.

[Original PDF295/printed261, PDF296/printed262, PDF297/printed263; R1571, R1572, R1573, R1582, R1585, R1588, R1589, R1597, R1599, R1602; `RULES.json`.]

The difficult Saturn sentence on printed261 shows **Leo or Scorpio with South Node or North Node**. The two nodal glyphs are identifiable. The reach of “with” remains unsettled: it might qualify Scorpio alone or both sign alternatives. Combustion and violent-star alternatives are also retained. The next poisoning statement is both tentative and conditional on **prior suspicion**. It must not be treated as newly established poisoning from the chart alone.

[Original PDF295/printed261; R1574, R1575; `RULES.json`, `UNRESOLVED.json`, `TEXTUAL_CHECKS.json`.]

The cold/moist account is said to be more assured when Venus or the Moon shares disease signification, while the reach of the following descriptive continuation remains open. The text reads “vitious,” not an editorial substitution of “viscous.” The fixed-sign passage gives **Taurus, Leo and Aquarius as examples** after naming the class. It does not print Scorpio in that list, but “as in” does not establish that Scorpio is excluded from the class.

[Original PDF295/printed261; R1576, R1577, R1578, R1579; `RULES.json`, `UNRESOLVED.json`, `TEXTUAL_CHECKS.json`.]

Elsewhere, melancholy is conditional on Saturn's involvement, while the possessive in “Mars in any of his dignities” remains uncertain. The planet opening printed263 is Mars, continuing the fever sequence.

## 4. Body location and mind/body roles cannot be flattened

The first side rule connects an unfortunate or afflicted sixth lord above the earth with the right and upper parts. A later first-person claim adds a masculine-planet condition. These are separately worded rules; the extra condition should not be silently imported backwards. Another sentence literally says **“superiour and fore-part”** for an alternative that includes the sixth lord below the earth. That reading is retained without changing superior to inferior. The nocturnal-sign continuation and the inheritance of some earlier conditions remain explicit interpretation limits.

[Original PDF297/printed263; R1604, R1605, R1606, R1608; `RULES.json`, `UNRESOLVED.json`, `TEXTUAL_CHECKS.json`.]

The left-side rule has two actors: the disease significator is in a feminine sign and aspects a feminine planet, while that other planet is in a feminine sign **or house**. The relevant house classification is not supplied in the passage. The degree instruction concerns the upper, middle or lower part of **the member**, using few, middle and lower degrees. It does not define numerical bins, decans or a whole-body scale. Its inherited conditions and the numerical meaning of lower remain open.

[Original PDF297/printed263, PDF298/printed264; R1607, R1609; `RULES.json`.]

The mind/body discussion repeatedly changes which role is used. The introductory spirit group contains the ascending sign, Moon and lord of the Sun's house; the member group contains the ascendant lord and Moon's dispositor. One later all-afflicted condition uses the **Sun itself** with ascendant and Moon. Another all-or-two condition uses the **lord of the Sun's house**. A further comparison uses the **dispositor of the ascendant lord**, a second-order role. These are not interchangeable labels. Counting roles also requires distinguishing them from the number of physical planetary bodies they happen to identify.

[Original PDF298/printed264, PDF299/printed265; R1610, R1611, R1612, R1613, R1619; `RULES.json`, `REFERENCE_TABLES.json`, `UNRESOLVED.json`.]

The text reports that many astrologians say Saturn foreshows **or causes** melancholy, preserving an unresolved causal alternative. Its Saturn sequence contains several role, placement and relation branches. The following contrast prints **Jupiter** and says it **never** oppresses the mind but the body. Those words are retained, while the amount of the preceding Saturn condition list intended to transfer is unstated. No modern causal or psychiatric claim is inferred from this historical account.

[Original PDF298/printed264; R1615, R1616, R1617; `RULES.json`, `TEXTUAL_CHECKS.json`, `UNRESOLVED.json`.]

One adverse Mars sentence explicitly lists **sextile, square and opposition**. It is not silently repaired to conjunction, square and opposition. The printed wording about being oppressed in the twelfth also leaves its attachment to the alternatives unresolved. Elsewhere, a malevolent aspect to the ascendant is accompanied by the explicit exclusion **not to the Moon** and by an additional afflicted Sun-sign lord. Comparative statements about degrees being more afflicted than their lords, or the reverse, supply no strength metric, tie rule or numerical aggregation.

[Original PDF299/printed265; R1620, R1621, R1622; `RULES.json`, `TEXTUAL_CHECKS.json`, `UNRESOLVED.json`.]

The remaining mind/body clauses preserve their own combinations rather than harmonizing them into one global classifier: unimpeded rulers can matter alongside afflicted ascendant and Moon; exalted Sun and ascendant lord can coexist with a debilitated Moon dispositor; plural solar-affliction conditions have uncertain distribution; and a later freedom-from-Saturn/Mars phrase has an uncertain subject. “Usually” remains in the Sun-related character account. Venus is not given an extra affliction prerequisite merely because the following Mercury sentence expressly has one.

## 5. Natal, mundane and lunar references remain distinct

The natal callback begins **if the nativity can be procured**. The following danger statement also mentions the eighth sign of the **yearly revolution of the World** falling on natal angles, particularly the ascendant. That reference frame must not be relabelled as the natal or horary eighth house. Aries or Leo mitigates the stated ill omen but does not remove it. The following sentence says **at twelve degrees** there is little danger. It does not say twelve degrees or more, does not say no danger, and does not supply an interpolation between six and twelve. Its phase condition is not restated. These distinctions are documented, without constructing a clinical risk threshold.

## 6. Critical days: a local origin and three angles

The critical-day procedure first asks for the hour at which the sick person took bed, as nearly as it can be obtained. **If that hour cannot be had**, it uses the first time the physician was asked to judge. The Moon's motion is then corrected to the chosen hour. This local two-stage statement is kept separate from the earlier opening procedure on printed243, which also discussed carrying urine and alternatives involving the physician's first access or contact. The later passage does not silently overwrite the earlier one for every question context.

[Original PDF300/printed266, PDF277/printed243; R1633, R1634; `RULES.json`, `RETAINED_SOURCE_EXCERPTS.json`, `REFERENCE_TABLES.json`.]

For **acute, not chronic**, diseases, Lilly identifies changes near the times when the Moon has moved **45°, 90° or 135°** from that first place. This passage does not provide a 180° point, a general sequence of all multiples of 45°, a fixed number of days, or a tolerance for “near.” The helper consequently returns three angular targets only. A corrected starting longitude, applicable illness class and selected origin must be supplied; otherwise the result remains unresolved or not applicable.

## 7. Recovery timing retains discretion and alternative outcomes

The recovery-time question starts **after recovery has already been judged**, and when the querent asks when. Lilly counts degrees between the ascendant lord and the benefic involved in the aspect, considers the houses and signs of both, and joins the quality of the disease to discretion. The exact degree measure—raw distance or a perfected-aspect measure—is not fully specified here. A favourable aspect alone must not be treated as proving recovery.

[Original PDF301/printed267; R1641; `RULES.json`.]

For moveable signs, days are the **ordinary** interpretation; Lilly expresses more certainty when the planets are **both swift and angular**. The stated event is amendment, which need not mean complete cure. For common signs he expressly declines a mechanical choice of months, weeks or days: the illness and what duration is feasible must be considered. His report of the Ancients immediately afterward supplies alternatives, not a single universal scale.

[Original PDF301/printed267; R1642, R1643, R1644; `RULES.json`.]

| Source category | Reported time options | House correspondence |
|---|---|---|
| Moveable sign | Days | Angular |
| Common sign | Weeks or months | Succedent |
| Fixed sign | Months or years | Cadent |

The table gives the Ancients' sign associations and the subsequent house equivalences. The text supplies no priority when the actual sign and house point to different rows. Lilly also asks for the Moon's speed, sign and quality **together with the principal significators**, mixing the considerations. The reference helper therefore displays the sign and house options separately and leaves the selected unit and date empty.

[Original PDF301/printed267, PDF302/printed268; R1644, R1645, R1646; `RULES.json`, `REFERENCE_TABLES.json`, `lilly_dariot_reference.py`.]

A later ingress rule says the ascendant lord leaving the sign occupied at question time and entering an essential dignity is associated with **recovery or sensible improvement**. It does not additionally require the outgoing sign to have been a debility. The numerical sixth-cusp example is different: **28° on the sixth cusp and a common sign** leads Lilly to say the disease will **vary in less than two weeks**. The record preserves the exact condition and strict upper bound. It does not turn 28 into 28-or-more, “vary” into recover, or the hypothetical illustration into a dated case.

[Original PDF302/printed268; R1647, R1648; `RULES.json`, `REFERENCE_TABLES.json`, `UNRESOLVED.json`.]

The earlier timing passage on printed249 also left a choice among months, weeks and days to the disease's nature. That retained callback and the new recovery discussion support keeping the selection context explicit. They do not establish a new combined numerical algorithm. The final promise to give a figure or two refers forward; no such figures occur inside this admitted continuation, so the promise adds neither two charts nor two completed cases to the present inventory.
