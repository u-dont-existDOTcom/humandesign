## Wedding timing, multiple marriages, partner status and conflict

Lilly asks whether a man will marry and adds the Moon's good aspects to Sun/Venus, first–seventh signifier relations, prolific signs and Venus dignities as claims for marriage. Timing is cast as **days with movable signs, months with common signs, years with fixed signs**, counting degrees between applying significators—**only when marriage testimonies are strong and the significators swift**. No calibrated probability, time zone, date or independently authenticated marriage follows. The subsection on number of husbands presents a supposed Arabic count of planets between tenth cusp and Mars, special handling when Mars is in eleventh, other authors' alternative count from Mars to tenth lord, and Lilly's preference for the **seventh lord** plus source common-sign/seventh-house indications. These competing methods must remain separate. [PDF341; PART_B3_341.json]

The author's other unnumbered subsections interpret the region or distance of a partner through seventh lord/sign/quarter, the alleged disposition of a partner through Moon/Venus/Mars/Jupiter, and which spouse is 'more noble' or 'master' using angle, dignity and reception. A later source step evaluates the partner's property via the eighth house, separately the alleged legitimacy of the marriage through afflicted signifiers, and post-marital agreement through first/seventh lord harmonious aspects and Moon/dispositor relations. These incorporate gender hierarchy, nobility categories and legal status as **historical claims rather than contemporary standards or validated descriptions**. [PDF342–343]

The text then gives different potential causes of marital disagreement: afflicted first versus seventh house lords, planets in third/fourth/tenth, hardship involving dowry or relatives, certain Moon afflictions and absence of perfecting applications. The subsequent subsection concerns a **marriage breaking**, the afflicting/mediating planet and which side might marry another first. It quotes an *Arabick Aphorisme* with specific zodiacal degree rules but includes a prominent marginal caution **not to credit it without the consent/concurrence of other significators**. These are not separate corroborated predictions. [PDF343–345]

## Mortality claims and source-specific qualifications

The source asks which spouse will die first and which live longer. It compares first/seventh rulers, which applies first to combustion, whether either ruler is afflicted or near an eighth house, gendered Sun/Venus conditions, and sign modality as an alleged speed distinction. In the following comparison **the eighth house counted from the seventh becomes the radical second** for the seventh-house spouse; this turned-house condition cannot be silently replaced by the radical eighth for both partners. No independently authenticated spouse death or duration is present here. The text supplies **no medical justification for real mortality forecasts**. [PDF345–346]

## Historically prejudicial conduct allegations and their limitations

Several lengthy subsections speculate about chastity, previous relationships, extramarital partners and reputed moral character through Moon, Venus, first/seventh rulers, fixed/movable signs, nodes and malefic planets. The conditions are not uniformly confident. Lilly expressly warns that some judgments are **not always true**, differs from ancient authorities, describes sign combinations as indicating **false rumours/slander** rather than confirmed misconduct, and cautions astrologers to **remain silent rather than cause harm with an unlucky judgment**. He also explicitly recognizes a configuration that he interprets as a person being subject to **force without consent**, rather than voluntary conduct. Omitting these limits would invert or exaggerate the actual source. They provide no evidence about any real person's sexuality, consent, fidelity or prior experience. [PDF346–350]

The source separately distinguishes past lunar *separation* from present *application*, hypothesized household versus distant contacts, aspects with specific planets and whether reception modifies the story. Planetary person-types (e.g., old, wealthy or socially prominent persons) are a traditional stereotype, not proof of the identity, existence or actions of third parties. Even Lilly's reportedly favourable configurations are source testimony only.
