## Contradictory medical source assertions

Lilly says that the alleged illness significators should be in movable signs and favorably aspected by benefic planets with sextile or trine to suggest an easy cure. He contrasts this with significators in fixed signs lacking benefic aspects, which he alleges portend prolonged illness or difficulty ever being cured. He then uses the closeness/exactness of beneficial contact—particularly partile aspect—to grade presumed ease and duration of recovery.

The source assigns three purported recovery channels to Jupiter (money and counsel), Mercury (medicine), and Venus (magic, divine/natural or chance assistance). Those planets must allegedly be strong and beneficially connected to illness significators. Importantly, the author adds a further contrary clause that when disease significators are in their own houses or exaltations, he expects little hope of remedy. No clear prioritization across the conflicting assertions, prospective outcome horizon, exact aspect-orb criteria or observed patient examples is given.

**These are unvalidated seventeenth-century beliefs about illness and cure.** They are not a basis for advising anyone whether a disease is treatable, interpreting their chart clinically, choosing therapy, or delaying medical care. Money, medicine and magic are historic textual categories, not evaluated treatment recommendations.
