## 1. This is a topical character method, not a Sun-sign personality catalogue

III.13 starts from Mercury for reason/intellect and the Moon for the sensory or non-rational portion, with the planets involved in her applications and separations. It then considers their signs, rulers, relevant solar/angle relations and planetary natures (PDF357/printed333).

The following single and paired descriptions presuppose this **kind of domination**. Merely finding two planets in conjunction, or obtaining a high generic chart-dominance score, is not a faithful substitute for first establishing their authority in this particular inquiry (PDF361–365/printed337–341).

The table inventory comprises five governing planets—Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Venus and Mercury—giving five singles and ten distinct pairs. Each has an honourable-position and contrary-position account. The luminaries retain their own referent and assisting roles rather than being silently added as two more single-profile rulers (Robbins note, PDF385/printed361).

**Project implication:** these conditional pair profiles may later be tested as a distinct layer. The existence of their source descriptions does not justify summing all fifteen or treating each adjective as corroboration.

## 2. Dignity is not goodness, and weakness is not the absence of every ability

The general discussion explicitly distinguishes effectiveness from moral character: under the source's conditions, harmful action can be successful and secure, whereas kindness can expose a person to being wronged (PDF363/printed339).

The detail is equally important. Saturn–Mars in honourable positions includes harsh and deceptive attributes while also being adroit, practical and successful. The same paragraph begins with a neither-good-nor-bad expression and later contains intensely negative character language. Both are retained, not reconciled by deleting one (PDF367/printed343).

Conversely, Jupiter–Venus in contrary positions retains trustworthiness, graciousness and approachability despite the adverse descriptions. Jupiter–Mercury in contrary positions retains knowledge, memory, teaching and pure desires amid the unfavourable features (PDF375–377/printed351–353).

**Project implication:** a single positive/negative dignity score would lose the source's distinctions. The new tables preserve both kinds of trait within each placement branch. No conclusion about any actual person's character was drawn.

## 3. Similar vocabulary can conceal different operations

In III.11, bodily temperament concerns the constitutional hot/cold/moist/dry mixture. It cannot simply be reused as a modern personality label (PDF333–341/printed309–317).

A more technical example involves forward motion. The III.11 note explains a daily east-to-west direction in relation to retreating orbital motion; the III.13 note describes advances versus retrogression but expressly points back to the ambiguity. We therefore have not compiled both under the same positive-longitude-speed predicate (PDF337/printed313 and PDF359/printed335).

There is a lexical example too. Robbins explains that the same character-word family can mean good character in one passage and simplicity approaching foolishness in another. That warning must survive later trait coding; a dictionary match is not enough (PDF373/printed349).

The English III.13 sign-mode paragraph says “solstitial.” Its membership scope, in relation to the earlier equinoctial/solstitial distinction, remains marked for reconciliation rather than automatically being relabelled as the four modern cardinal signs (PDF359/printed335).

## 4. A detailed explanatory note must not become a fictitious author rule

The note on honourable positions contains a substantial explanatory checklist, including signs, angularity and proper domains such as decans. It is **Robbins reporting Bouché-Leclercq**, not a new passage in Ptolemy's main text (PDF364–365/printed340–341).

This matters because importing the whole checklist as a directly stated Ptolemaic calculation would silently change the source profile. We preserved the note and its relevance, but did not turn it into a weighted dignity formula. Mixed dignities, tied governors and combinations of more than two rulers remain unresolved by the fifteen-pair lookup alone.

## 5. Mitigation, concealment, cure and public visibility are different claims

III.12 distinguishes pain, bodily appearance, social reproach, treatability, cure, concealment and practical benefit. For example, Jupiter's help may conceal an injury through wealth or status, while Mercury's addition to Venus may yield benefit from the condition itself. Neither claim means that the condition was absent (PDF355–357/printed331–333).

III.14 makes the distinction particularly explicit: one branch is described as incurable but hidden; another as **curable but noticeable**. The page image confirms the latter wording, and the differing textual witness is retained rather than silently substituted (PDF391/printed367).

**Project implication:** these outputs must not be pooled into a generic favourable-health hit. The book's medical and sex-role categories are retained as historical material, not current diagnoses, identity classifications, treatment instructions or forecasts about a person.

There is also a useful check against oversimplification: III.12 gives Mars a conditional relieving role in a speech-impediment configuration, after a specified lunar meeting. It is not an instruction to treat every Mars involvement as equally harmful (PDF349/printed325).
