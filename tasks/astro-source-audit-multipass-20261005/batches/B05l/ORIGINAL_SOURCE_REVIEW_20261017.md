## Core historical logic

The author's generalized fortune judgment starts from the natal figure's planetary strengths and weaknesses. Three or four essential-dignity planets or mutual reception are alternative positive testimonies. A predominance of fall/detriment, unfavorable houses or peregrine status is negative. The Sun and Moon offer separate corroborative or discordant testimony. Mixed conditions lead to uneven fortunes rather than a simple binary conclusion.

**Crucial limitation:** Lilly states that a generally promising nativity may have events that arrive slowly. He says that event dates are derived from directions of the *five Hylegical places* and a significator's approach to promittors already indicated in the radical chart. He does not provide an independently blinded validation, fixed rule for acceptable delay, calibration, or known out-of-sample event-hit rate in this passage. Retroactively selecting different significators or delay windows could explain virtually any person's history, so neither the natal status claim nor the separate timing method is eligible for predictive runtime promotion without precise frozen tests.

A second-page cluster combines peregrine luminaries, afflicted dispositors or benefics, malefics occupying the same quadrant, certain principal significators and generally slow planetary motion as alleged adverse circumstances. The author additionally proposes using occupied malefic houses to explain the origin of misfortune and directions to time it. This is historical astrological attribution, not independently established causal inference.
