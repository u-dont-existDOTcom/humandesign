## Opening marriage mechanics and Alkindus

Lilly distinguishes **the querent's first house and lord, Moon, and Moon's separating planet** from **the desired person's seventh house and lord and Moon's applying planet**. He adds Sun or Venus under historical male/female arrangements, with roles inverted appropriately when a woman herself asks. The opening narrative differentiates the direction of application: first-lord/Moon applying toward seventh is associated with petitions and effort, whereas a subsequent printed p304 passage calls the sought party's assent **'unwillingly'**. That word conflicts with p303's goodwill language; the original volume supplies no p304 erratum. The original reading is retained rather than silently changed to 'willingly'. Hard aspects with **reception** can still perfect slowly, while body conjunction or trine/sextile are given other possibilities. These are source conditions about purported symbolic consent, **not measurements of a person's actual wishes or consent**. [PDF336–338; RULES.json]

The unnumbered **'Aphorismes of Alkindus touching Marriage'** on printed303 are an ancient-author attribution *reported by Lilly*, not a fresh collation of al-Kindī's own text. They begin with an ascendant-lord application to seventh lord **without a stated aspect-type restriction** (and a printed marginal reverse-application alternative). The Moon–Venus rule makes **Venus strong, increasing in motion and dignified**, with the Moon likewise dignified. Venus beholding Sun, Sun dignified in ascendant **AND Sun beholding its own dispositor** form a three-part compound rule. Another passage describes **an existing application with BOTH the applying and applied-to planets cadent** and possibly unseen by their own lords—hope at first, then delay and failure. The angular case separately preserves named Sun, Venus, Moon, both house lords and consent. A new unnumbered heading on the same page opens a more general 'whether it shall take effect' inquiry; it is not a new numbered chapter. [PDF337–338; SOURCE_IDENTITY.json]

The fuller marriage-perfection list includes mutual placement of the first and seventh lords, relevant application between the planets from which/to which Moon separates/applies, Sun–Venus, translation and collection of light, reception, and third-party help. These **alternative routes do not create independent observed marriages**. Lilly also compares who loves more by the first and seventh lord's location. An especially important contradiction occurs when his late chapter says **contrary to the ancients** that first/7th placements imply the opposite party loves more than his earlier passage. This must remain two source statements with their own locators, not be 'repaired' into one timeless rule. [PDF338–339, PDF351; UNRESOLVED.json]

## Mediation, frustration and the house-specific obstacles

A frustrated application is treated as hindrance through the obstructing planet's domicile or house. One source list maps **helpful intermediaries**: the second house to money/dowry, third to sibling, tenth to mother, fifth or eleventh to friend, sixth to relatives or servants. A later list maps **obstacles** differently: second portion/wealth, third kin or journeys, fourth parents or land, fifth children/fertility/sexual concerns, sixth infirmity/servants, seventh third parties/lawsuits, eighth death or estate, ninth religion/journey, tenth sought person's father or asking person's mother/authority, eleventh friends and twelfth concealed hostility or slander. These are **two different conditional functions**, not a universal dictionary of whom an astrological house identifies. [PDF339–341]

The source also distinguishes **trine/sextile** from harder **square or opposition** and repeatedly gives reception a moderating role. **The original printed p305 conjunction glyph in that difficulty list is explicitly DELETED by the same-volume erratum.** The primary source image remains unchanged; the correction is recorded in ERRATA_COLLATION.json. Success, delay, mediation, opposition, perceived affection and the named social categories are conjectures written in 1647, not verified events or evidence of a modern person's relationships.

## Wedding timing, multiple marriages, partner status and conflict

Lilly asks whether a man will marry and adds the Moon's good aspects to Sun/Venus, first–seventh signifier relations, prolific signs and Venus dignities as claims for marriage. Timing is cast as **days with movable signs, months with common signs, years with fixed signs**, counting degrees between applying significators—**only when marriage testimonies are strong and the significators swift**. No calibrated probability, time zone, date or independently authenticated marriage follows. The subsection on number of husbands presents a supposed Arabic count of planets between tenth cusp and Mars, special handling when Mars is in eleventh, other authors' alternative count from Mars to tenth lord, and Lilly's preference for the **seventh lord** plus source common-sign/seventh-house indications. These competing methods must remain separate. [PDF341; PART_B3_341.json]

The author's other unnumbered subsections interpret the region or distance of a partner through seventh lord/sign/quarter, the attributed disposition of a partner through Moon/Venus and **Sun trine/sextile Saturn (wise and painstaking/diligent) versus Sun with Jupiter (honest)**, and which spouse is 'more noble' or 'master' using angle, dignity and reception. A later source step evaluates the partner's property via the eighth house, separately the alleged legitimacy of the marriage through afflicted signifiers, and post-marital agreement through first/seventh lord harmonious aspects and Moon/dispositor relations. These incorporate gender hierarchy, nobility categories and legal status as **historical claims rather than contemporary standards or validated descriptions**. [PDF342–343]

The text then gives different potential causes of marital disagreement: afflicted first versus seventh house lords, planets in third/fourth/tenth, hardship involving dowry or relatives, certain Moon afflictions and absence of perfecting applications. The subsequent subsection concerns a **marriage breaking**, the afflicting/mediating planet and which side might marry another first. It quotes an *Arabick Aphorisme* with an explicitly scoped **17° Sagittarius to the beginning of Capricorn** range, and another referring to the **first twelve degrees of Capricorn**, NOT Virgo; that latter one offers three extreme alternative outcomes without a case observation. The original printed margin warns **not to credit it without the consent/concurrence of other significators**. These are not separate corroborated predictions. [PDF343–345]

## Mortality claims and source-specific qualifications

The source asks which spouse will die first and which live longer. It compares first/seventh rulers, which applies first to combustion, and whether either ruler is afflicted or near the **lord of the eighth house**, gendered Sun/Venus conditions, and sign modality as an alleged speed distinction. In the following comparison **the eighth house counted from the seventh becomes the radical second** for the seventh-house spouse; this turned-house condition cannot be silently replaced by the radical eighth for both partners. No independently authenticated spouse death or duration is present here. The text supplies **no medical justification for real mortality forecasts**. [PDF345–346]

## Historically prejudicial conduct allegations and their limitations

Several lengthy subsections speculate about chastity, previous relationships, extramarital partners and reputed moral character through Moon, Venus, first/seventh rulers, fixed/movable signs, nodes and malefic planets. The conditions are not uniformly confident. Lilly expressly warns that some judgments are **not always true**, differs from ancient authorities, describes sign combinations as indicating **false rumours/slander** rather than confirmed misconduct, and cautions astrologers to **remain silent rather than cause harm with an unlucky judgment**. He also explicitly recognizes a configuration that he interprets as a person being subject to **force without consent**, rather than voluntary conduct. Omitting these limits would invert or exaggerate the actual source. They provide no evidence about any real person's sexuality, consent, fidelity or prior experience. [PDF346–350]

The source separately distinguishes past lunar *separation* from present *application*, hypothesized household versus distant contacts, aspects with specific planets and whether reception modifies the story. Planetary person-types (e.g., old, wealthy or socially prominent persons) are a traditional stereotype, not proof of the identity, existence or actions of third parties. Even Lilly's reportedly favourable configurations are source testimony only.

## Late completion rules, disputed parentage and reconciliation

The late **'If a Marriage shall be perfected or no'** subsection restates the first-house lord/Moon and seventh lord, expressly adding **'if the querent please' and 'we doe suppose a freedome of will in this nature'** to the claimed completion. Nothing in a horoscope substitutes for actual, free consent. It conditionally accepts matching dignities, specified house placements and applying trines or sextiles, **only when no frustration, prohibition, abscission or retrogradation intervenes**; in a contrasting passage, square/opposition **without reception** prevents fulfillment, whereas square **with reception** may work laboriously. In the same context Lilly explicitly says his observation about whether the first or seventh party loves more is **contrary to the ancients**—a disagreement with his own earlier printed rule that this audit refuses to silently harmonize. [PDF351–352]

A separate historical inquiry challenges reputed fatherhood through the eleventh ruler and fifth-house relationships, lunar aspects, reception and adverse Saturn/Mars/Mercury testimony. **The original text explicitly uses the eleventh house as the conception/issue signifier in this paragraph**, despite another fifth-house convention; the extraction does not repair that choice by substituting a modern preferred scheme. The source gives these as **unvalidated, potentially harmful allegations**, explicitly limited to a fallback **'if none of these things be'** before mere BEHOLDING of fifth house/lord and hedged as **'there may be just suspition'**, with original word **'stuprated'** not silently translated to a modern legal category. Nothing substitutes for genetic evidence of actual parentage. [PDF352]

Finally, the author considers a partner who has left a husband or other household and whether the couple reconciles. In this context the seventh house can denote the **absent or banished party regardless of the sex of the querent**. Direct aspects, mediation, Sun/Venus relative positions above/below the earth, Moon increasing/decreasing in light, and occidental/retrograde Venus are used as competing claims concerning rapid versus delayed return, regret or resentment. None determines whether reconciliation is safe, consensual, desired, or likely in real life. Chapter L starts only after the divider on original PDF353. [PDF352–353]

## Two independent first-pass source reviews and corrected units

Separate fresh image-first reviewers examined original PDF336–345 and PDF344–353 respectively, with errata888 in both stages. Their page ranges overlap deliberately but **neither reviewer read the entire 18-page chapter alone**. They reported 38 and 53 issues respectively. The defects included a wrong **'unwillingly' vs 'willingly'** word on printed304, Moon in **tenth rather than seventh**, the erased conjunction symbol on printed305, a false Sun/Mars personality pairing instead of **Sun–Saturn/Sun–Jupiter**, the Capricorn-vs-Virgo Arabic sign mistake, and several missing source **denials**, **non-consent qualifications**, a warning about **slander**, and a **free-will** proviso. The draft also wrongly substituted benevolent translation of light for the *evil* interposing/prohibiting planet in the marriage-hindrance passage. All high-priority issues have source-localized corrective records. The two reviewers' raw diagnostic files, original draft and a machine-readable patch ledger are preserved separately.

The two informed source-window reviews examined changes to the original 187-unit draft and additional recovered clauses, rather than constituting fresh blind replications.
