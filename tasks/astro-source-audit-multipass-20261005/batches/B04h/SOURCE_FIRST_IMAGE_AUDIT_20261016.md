The author makes different kinds of statements: with strong, fortunate, well-aspected eleventh-house lord, a querent can gain the hoped-for thing **or** friends can love/agree if friendship is the subject. Generic favorable routes include a first/eleventh good aspect, reception, light translation, ascendant ruler in eleventh, or eleventh ruler in first. If none qualifies, Lilly checks the Moon; absent suitable lunar connection he says to judge the contrary.

For patronage by a prince, lord, master or nobleman, first/eleventh rulers applying to one another suggest receiving the hoped-for dignity. Trine/sextile forecast ease and rapidity; square/opposition may still forecast attainment, but with struggle and delay, **even with reception**. An angular received eleventh ruler is a different strong favourable rule.

One important graded-outcome distinction derives from the receiving planet of the Moon's disposition: in a common sign **only part** of the desired thing, in a movable sign **only the name, possibility or very little**, and in a fixed sign **the whole and complete result**. If the receiver is unfortunate, the person may acquire the good **and then suffer damage or hurt**. Reception of the lunar receiver can predict more than expected; reception of the ascendant ruler can predict the thing requested. Lilly expressly narrows the latter very broad claim to **feasible and possible things**, excluding claims that impossible goals will be manifested.
