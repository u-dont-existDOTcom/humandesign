# Original 1647 Lilly printed CLX source review — first-edition photo witness

**Scope:** original William Lilly, *Christian Astrology* (1647), Wellcome b30338724, PDF frames 702–711 (printed pages 668–677), and 712–713 contextual seam. This is a documentary audit only. It does not validate astrology or human forecasts.

## Physical source and method

Visual reading of the preserved page photographs located **printed CHAP CLX** after the rule in the lower portion of PDF702/printed668. It starts with an explanation of what mid-heaven directions purport to describe, continues from Saturn through Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury and Moon, adds the eleventh and twelfth house cusps and a rare mid-heaven-to-horoscope case, and ends *above* the rule introducing printed **CHAP CLXI** in the upper/middle of PDF711/printed677. The printed **CHAP CLXII** heading occurs on PDF713 **inside CLXI's following context**, so it must not be mapped as a CLX source clause. One premature CLXII navigation row was explicitly removed before acceptance; its correction is preserved in `repair_b07q_boundary.py`.

Each of the 27 source groups carries source-photo frame(s), its own historic meaning and qualification, and an ordered inverse map to the 258 proposed historical R identifiers. Anchors are *short finding aids*, explicitly **not diplomatic quotations or complete textual transcriptions**. The photographic witness and original image-member SHA256 + bytes are the source of truth when lettering, planetary glyphs or punctuation is disputed.
