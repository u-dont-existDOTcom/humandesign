## Historical source content and important uncertainty

Lilly alleges sight impairments from Sun/Moon, Mars/Saturn, angular aspects, Milky Way degree windows, Pleiades/Praesepe/Coma Berenices/Orion and other historically named nebulous stars. The source proposes blindness, one-eye damage and squint under partly contrary conditions, with Saturn and Mars assigned different alleged causes. The historical star names and zodiac positions require era-correct astronomical/glyph verification, not modern diagnosis.

Hearing and speech passages employ multiple Mercury/Saturn placements, traditional mute signs, favorable-aspect exceptions and first/sixth/twelfth house conditions. Dental passages differentiate tooth loss from pain in various signs and contain exceptions for Capricorn/Aquarius. A much larger historical subsection on *falling-sickness* attributes four alternative causes to earlier astrologers Albubater, Cardanus and Pontanus, then conflates epileptic episodes, so-called madness, impaired intelligence and derogatory character labels. None of these can be interpreted as medically valid neurological or psychiatric outcomes.

The remaining source subheadings propose urinary stone/strangury, gout in later years after purported youth sickness, and physical injury from violent falls or horses, with several opposing/flexible planetary combinations. Lilly also cites his own historical observations of supposedly successful fall predictions, without a denominator, negatives or independently checkable cases.

All these are 1647 source assertions.
