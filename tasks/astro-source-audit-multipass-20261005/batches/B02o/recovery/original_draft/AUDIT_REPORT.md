## A consequential source-date correction

The chart center actually prints **1645, ♃ 8°. Maij, 6:15 P.M., hor: astr:**. The sign ♃ is **Jupiter’s weekday glyph (Thursday)**, not the number **24**. The original chart is captioned **Thursday 8 May 1645 Julian**, equivalent to **18 May 1645 Gregorian**, also Thursday. An earlier B02n handoff erroneously said May24. That mistake is explicitly superseded in CHAPTER_XLV_DATE_CORRECTION.md while the accepted B02n archive is kept immutable. The printed time has no sufficiently established modern timezone or absolute UT conversion; the hor: astr: notation remains unresolved. [PDF320; CHART_INSCRIPTION.json; U02–U04,U20]

## What the author deduced from the chart

**Affected parts.** Lilly gives Scorpio as the ascendant, nearby Chele fixed stars, and no evil planet afflicting the ascendant by its corporeal presence. He therefore examines the sixth, where Saturn is in its fall. Aries signifies the head; Saturn in Aries is assigned the breast; and Mars as Scorpio's ruler in Leo the heart. He says Mars had **recently separated from a dexter square of Saturn and was in Cancer at the time of that square**, which he connects to breast and stomach. Cancer at the earlier aspect is a different time from Mars's present Leo placement, so treating the two signs as contradictory chart data would be an error. The author infers affliction of head, breast, heart and stomach and a melancholic obstruction in breast or stomach. [PDF320–321; R1748–R1754; U05–U09]

**Causes.** Saturn, said to be in its own terms, with the Moon in its house applying to Saturn, is the principal source of melancholy and dry disease. Mars lord ascendant in Saturn's terms, the Moon outside his terms applying square to Sun, and Sun in Mars's terms are instead made to indicate **choler as a secondary cause**. The printed internal page113/244/245 backward references are retained; this isolated section is not an independent re-audit of those earlier catalogues. [PDF321; R1755–R1757]

**Symptoms.** Lilly says that when he came to speak to the doctor he found reports of headache and rumbling, little sleep, silence/melancholy, dry cough, pain and weakness in the chest/heart, a black-yellow complexion, wasting and weariness throughout the joints. The continuation reports difficulty urinating, red gravel and pain, with sign/secret/bladder analogies. These are **one person's symptoms** as recounted by one author, not an independently verified series. Chart construction and disclosure times are not frozen against this encounter; archaic terms do not establish modern diagnoses. [PDF321–322; R1758–R1763; CASE_EVIDENCE.json; U10–U12,U15]

**Remedy and outcome.** Lilly says he knew little physic but recommended the doctor prescribe himself historical gently hot, moist and cordial remedies. He invokes the Moon in the fourth sextile Saturn to argue sickness until death, then says the doctor **died on 14 August following**. The reported death and suggested medication are source documentation, **not** independent clinical outcome confirmation, safe treatment advice, or proof of a prospectively frozen prediction. [PDF322; R1764–R1765; U13–U14,U17,U19]

## Duration argument and evidentiary limitations

For a prolonged illness, the passage invokes Saturn's slow nature, fixed chart angles, both luminaries in fixed signs and square, their terms and angular position, Mars lord of ascendant in a fixed sign and the antiscion of Mars near Sun. These are connected astrological interpretations of **one chart**, not additive independent empirical observations. No complete chart degree reproduction, precise orb, ephemeris parity, defensible disease diagnosis, negative-case controls, or transfer prediction is demonstrated. [PDF322; R1766–R1768; U04,U08,U16]
