## Afflicted body parts — Lilly’s inference

Under **What part of the Body was afflicted**, Lilly gives Scorpio rising and the italic fixed stars **Chelæ** near the ascendant, but says it is not afflicted by an evil planet’s **evil position or presence**. He turns to the sixth, where Saturn in its fall is said to afflict the illness house. He says the afflicted member is sought from that sixth house and cites printed **page244**. Additional associations: **Aries=head** (page245), **Saturn in Aries=breast** (page113), and **Mars, Scorpio’s ruler, in Leo=heart**. Saturn **in** Aries is a sign placement, not a planetary conjunction. Unauthenticated handwritten annotations beside the printed page113 reference do not correct it. [PDF320–321; R1748–R1752; U05,U06,U09,U25]

Mars is then described as recently separated from a **dexter square of Saturn**, with Mars **in Cancer at the time of that earlier square**. The source also gives Mars **in Leo in the chart being judged**. The two signs refer to different moments and must not be conflated. From these factors Lilly infers trouble in head, breast, heart and stomach, and melancholic obstruction in breast or stomach. These are source-internal hypotheses, not independently established medical diagnoses. [PDF321; R1753–R1754; U07]

## Claimed causes, reported symptoms and inferred diagnoses

Under **From what Cause the Sicknesse was**, Lilly calls Saturn the **principal significator**, in Saturn’s own Terms, with the Moon in Saturn’s house applying to it. He assigns melancholy and dry illness **occasioned by melancholic distempers**, possibly in head and breast. He cites **page244** and quotes physicians as knowing **“how to make a right mixture”** and **“what Diseases man may be subject unto in those parts”**. The kind of mixture (medicinal compounding versus a mixture of significations) is not settled by this sentence. That deferral and its source context matter; it is not a contemporary treatment recommendation. [PDF321; R1755–R1756; U08,U09,U14]

Mars the ascendant ruler is also placed in Saturn’s Terms. Lilly next writes of the Moon **“out of his Termes”** applying to a square of Sun, which he puts in Mars’s Terms, and identifies **choler as a secondary cause**. The early-English **out of** may mean outside or from, and **his** is not definitively anchored to Saturn or Mars within the line; an executable negative lunar dignity rule cannot be justified by choosing one interpretation. [PDF321; R1757; U22]

Lilly transitions **“and indeed when I came to speak with him”**, without establishing whether the horoscope was judged before that meeting. The author describes head pain/rumbling, being very silent, **dull** and melancholic, little sleep and dry cough. Only the weakness and pain in the breast and heart are expressly attributed in this paragraph to the doctor’s **complaint**. His complexion was described as between black and yellow, **“as if there was inclination to the Jaundies”**, a hedged inference, not a verified medical diagnosis. He further describes **lingring Consumption**, great weariness throughout the body and in every joint, difficulty urinating, red gravel and regional pain. Scorpio rising and the Moon **in Aquarius** are used for the historical source’s secrets/bladder associations; the airy-sign explanation of bodily weariness is an additional source inference, not a separate observed case. These are all claims concerning one patient. [PDF321–322; R1758–R1763; CASE_EVIDENCE.json; U10–U12,U15]

## Medicine suggestion, retrospective outcome and illness duration

Lilly says he knew little physic and advised the doctor to prescribe himself remedies called gently **hot, moist and cordial**, attempting to prolong life for a while. The next phrase gives the Moon in fourth in sextile with Saturn as an argument for sickness until death; then, **in italics**, Lilly says **“He dyed the fourteenth of August following.”** R1765 preserves an **author’s inference** and a separate **author-reported outcome**, linked to the preceding medical suggestion. There is no independently authenticated patient record or documented prediction freeze before the outcome. These suggested remedies are **not current patient-care advice**. [PDF322; R1764–R1765; CASE_EVIDENCE.json; U13–U14]

A decisive chronology: the heading **Whether the Disease would be long or short?** and Lilly’s following duration analysis are **printed after his report of death**. He first invokes Saturn as the author of the illness, whose slow character indicates **“permanent, or of some continuance”** (page248). The source does not resolve whether this separate Saturn sentence falls within the later **all these** phrase. **Besides**, all angles are said to be fixed. He adds both luminaries in fixed signs, in square, **“out of Angles”** (a phrase of unsettled meaning), in an infortune’s Terms, and Mars as ascendant lord in a fixed sign. **“All these portended the longitude of the Disease”** means the source’s duration rationale, not the patient’s longevity and not multiple statistically independent observations. The antiscion of Mars near Sun appears **after** the preceding **all these** duration statement as an added affliction to the luminary. The phrase **all these** demonstrably includes the immediately preceding fixed-angle/luminary/Mars claims, while its inclusion of Saturn’s earlier statement remains unresolved. Because this discussion appears after the death statement, it does not establish prospective predictive accuracy. [PDF322; R1766–R1768; U16,U19,U23,U24]
