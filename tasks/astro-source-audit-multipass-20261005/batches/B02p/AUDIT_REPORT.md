## What the author attributes to celestial conditions

At the outset, **Virgo rising** is described as especially afflicted by the **bodily presence of Mars**, which the author also partly associates with the eighth-house rulership. For disease location and causes he considers the sixth-house **Aquarius**, the sixth ruler **Saturn in Taurus** (described as fixed, earthy, melancholic and of Virgo's triplicity), and the **Moon near Mars in the ascendant**. The additional glyph afflicting Aquarius is legible as a Dragon-tail form **☋**, with exact ☋/☊ orientation retained as a copy-specific uncertainty. [PDF323 continuing on PDF324; R1773–R1777; U05–U06,U10]

From these testimonies the author infers spleen problems, wind-colic, melancholy bowel obstructions, small fevers, a 'remisse Pulse', head disturbance and restless sleep, with an open '&c.' in the original. The next bracketed claim, **“All which was true”**, is **Lilly's own retrospective corroboration**. This is not an independently documented examination, another patient, or an objective confirmation of the historical horoscope's accuracy. Modern diagnoses are outside the source-audit contract. [PDF324/printed290; R1778–R1781; U12–U13]

## The unusual ten-or-twelve-day account

Lilly says he encouraged the patient to put his affairs in order and make peace with God, stating he could not perceive natural grounds for living longer than **ten or twelve days**. That statement is followed by four rationales: the Sun's asserted perfect square to Saturn, the sixth ruler in fixed signs; Mars physically at the ascendant and substantially associated with the eighth; the Moon near **Cauda Leonis** and afflicted by Mars; and a lunar separation from Mercury's sextile, judged **more properly a square in signs of long ascensions**, followed by virtue transferred to Jupiter as eighth lord. All four are preserved with their named conditions and original-terminology uncertainties; they are not four independent patient outcomes. [PDF324; R1782–R1787; U07–U11]

The text then reports that the patient **died on 28 July following**. July 28 minus July 16 is **12 days by exclusive subtraction**, which matches the upper number only if the earlier warning was timed from the question date; the text does not supply that start moment. This is a real arithmetic correspondence *within the printed account*. Crucially, both the prediction and the outcome appear in a book printed in 1647 describing events in 1645. No independent death record, pre-outcome manuscript, timestamped prediction freeze or denominator of missed cases has been established. It therefore cannot be counted as a validated prospective forecast of exactly 12 days. [PDF323–324; R1783,R1788–R1789; CASE_EVIDENCE.json; U14–U16]

The remaining Mercury/Sun/Saturn and Moon/Sun/cusp descriptions occur **after the death statement**. They are presented as retrospective explanatory transits, not as original pre-frozen targets. The final degrees and aspect calculations have not been reproduced against verified historical ephemerides or an independently implemented house system. [PDF324; R1790–R1792; U17–U18]

## Independent 1647 source-reader corrections

The independent reader examined **both admitted Chapter XLVI original pages**, not five pages. It read both body pages, the original chart wheel and the same-volume errata. The strongest fault in the first draft was the after-death lunar cusp statement. The original PDF324/printed290 says the Moon transited the degree of the **sixth house, viz. 14 of Aquarius**, not a first-house Virgo cusp. The revised R1792 keeps the body reading, with the wheel's approximately **13°32′ Aquarius** label separately preserved; an ordinal fourteenth degree is possible, not proven. The Sun-twelfth-cusp continuation stays an elliptical retrospective clause. [PDF323–324; R1792; CHART_INSCRIPTION.json; U18,U32]

The source page describes the sixth-house Aquarius as afflicted by a node-like **☋** (exact orientation still source-qualified), and separately names the Moon near **Cauda Leonis**. These are not silently equated. The Moon is described as afflicted **by Mars**, not the reverse; a printed adverse influence does not by itself prove an additional square. [PDF323–324; R1775,R1786; U05,U07,U28]

The wheel has provisional house-cusp labels and unresolved Venus/Mercury glyph assignments. Original print spells the clock **7:22 AM:**, not a modernized exact quotation “7:22 A.M.”. The chart's original date is given as Wednesday 16 July 1645; the Julian inference is grounded in its historical English weekday but no printed UTC offset, location or external ephemeris is established. [PDF323; CHART_INSCRIPTION.json]

The Latin vital-principle phrase and **both bodies in fixed signs** in the first prognostic reason have been restored. The fourth reason prints lunar transfer of **“his vertue”**; its referent is most plausibly Mercury but not explicitly resolved. The source says its significators promised no less than death, but supplies no computation for the **ten or twelve days**. Subsequent claimed transits occur in the narrative only **after** the death report. [PDF324; R1784,R1787,R1789–R1792; CASE_EVIDENCE.json; UNRESOLVED.json]

An original heading within the first page reads **“Judgment of the Figure aforesaid.”** The original's handwritten 6th House margin note and ruled annotations are not established as authorial. No horoscope, diagnosis, outcome or calendar result in this chapter has been cross-validated against a real patient's records; the text supplies one retrospective author narrative only.
