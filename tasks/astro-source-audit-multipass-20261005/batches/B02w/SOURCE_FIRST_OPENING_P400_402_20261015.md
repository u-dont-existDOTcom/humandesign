## Chapter LI opening: overcoming an adversary, one paragraph across PDF400→401

Printed heading `CHAP. LI — Of Battle, Warre, or other Contentions`. The question is whether the querent will overcome the adversary. Lilly associates the querent with the ascendant lord, Moon, and the planet from which Moon separated. The *defendant* is associated with seventh house, seventh lord and planet to which Moon applies. Note that the heading and initial querent assignation occur BELOW the horizontal divider on original PDF400, while `fendant` completing `De-fendant` begins original PDF401. This is one continuous paragraph, not two independent methods.

The author compares strength, angular position and aspects of the two sets of significators and gives opposing rules that must be kept separate:

- Evil planets in ASC and fortunate planets in seventh: the adversary overcomes; converse reverses result.
- Seventh lord in ASC: source asserts victory to querent; converse again has different outcome. **Do not conflate this with the previous whole-side benefic/malefic rule**.
- These are authorial historical conditional claims, not validated adversarial outcomes.

## Printed page367: danger and return from war

New heading: `Whether one shall returne safe from Warre, or any dangerous Voyage`. The paragraph considers ascendant lord strength and benevolent company, the seventh lord's quality as a separate potentially adverse factor, Moon's condition and applications, malefics in eighth, and distinct planetary symbolism for dangers or injuries. The opening says strong well-disposed ascendant lord with benefic signifies safety, with a converse; it does **not** warrant collapsing all seventh-house and eighth-house branches into a single condition. The source uses the difficult expression about seventh lord benefic notwithstanding poor ascendant lord and a return with great loss; preserve adverse outcome and mitigation together.

A second heading `If one shall returne safe from Warre` begins a list with more concrete alternatives:
- Ascendant lord and/or good planet in ASC implies historical safe return.
- Sun joined to ascendant lord may mean do not go, because Sun burns it; distinct from a simple beneficial conjunction.
- Seventh lord with benefic and ascendant lord with malefic: impediment, yet not death in that branch.
- Malefic with first lord but benefic in first: severe losses or wounds without death.
- Saturn in first or with first lord: historical advice not to travel, with further incoming Saturn/first-lord distinctions.
- Last printed paragraph crosses PDF401→402 at `with Wood` then `or Stone`; retain the cross-page locator and the separate wound/danger clauses.

## Printed page368: further injury warnings and war outcome

PDF402 completes the previous `or Stone` injury statement and adds further malefic/ascendant/eighth conditions. These include historical threats of injury and death, NOT verified outcomes.

Heading `What will ensue of the Warre` introduces a distinct *two-sided outcome* set, not to be folded into return-safely questions. Its printed first ruler/seventh ruler assignments differ in context:
- First house/first lord = querent; seventh = adversary.
- Good planets in first with malefic in seventh and strong first ruler/weak seventh ruler is said to favor querent; converse set favors adversary.
- Both house lords on querent's side in ASC plus good planets aspecting second cusp: alleged favorable outcome, financial gain, possible reconciliation. **Conjunction of conditions and opponent-lord position matter.**
- Both first/seventh lords in ASC with benefics favoring first and malefics seventh: both lose, querent favored in end.
- Seventh lord in first: actor's fortune; contrary marks converse.
- Separate indications of death of querent or opponent use first/eighth vs second/seventh lord arrangements. Do not treat these historical lethal-outcome allegations as reliable forecasts.

The printed marginal rule assigns some named planetary houses to querent's side and others to quesited's side; retain as a marginal source instruction, not an empirical statement.
