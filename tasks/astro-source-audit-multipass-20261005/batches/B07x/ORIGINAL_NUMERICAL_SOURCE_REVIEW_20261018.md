Source is the **original 1647 edition** of William Lilly's *Christian Astrology*, Wellcome b30338724 (full PDF SHA256 `2cb53e20e122ffc6e47917ba10241f6c49687cd7a4696f3b5db655a2a1aab28b`). ## Three explicitly different historical direction-time conventions

1. **Ptolemy** (printed709 / PDF743) uses **one equatorial degree per year** and **six days for each arcminute**, an implicit 360-day year/30-day month approximation. The natal oblique ascension is printed **312°10′**, Mars terms **324°05′**, Mars opposition **332°02′**. Subtraction yields **11°55′** and **19°52′**. Lilly writes the corresponding times as **11 years 11 months** and **19 years 10 months 12 days**. Both source examples are reproduced by exact sexagesimal arithmetic; they are NOT verified natal event dates.
2. **Antonius Maginus, apparent-solar-motion yearly arc** (PDF744–746) derives **54′** from the two printed solar right ascensions **186°04′** and **186°58′**. The separate annual-rate table on PDF745 says **54′25″** for the **7° Libra** cell, chosen because natal Sun is printed **6°37′ Libra** and Lilly instructs rounding up when minutes exceed 30. This precision difference must not be silently discarded: for the **19°52′** arc, 54′/year gives **22 complete years +4′**, whereas 54′25″/year gives **21 complete years +49′15″**. The fuller 0°–30° by twelve-sign historical table comprises **372 paired minute/second cells (744 numeric fields)**; only the critical 7° Libra cell has been manually audited so far.
3. **Valentine Naibod** (PDF746–749) uses two printed lookup tables: 90 degree rows and 60 minute rows, converting arcs to years/days/hours and carrying with a **365-day calendar year**. Source degree table: 11° = **11 years, 58 days, 21 hours**, 19° = **19 years, 101 days, 17 hours**. Source minute table: 55′ = **339 days, 17 hours**, 52′ = **321 days, 4 hours**. The resulting ages **12 years 33 days 14 hours** and **20 years 57 days 21 hours** reproduce Lilly's worked printed results exactly after 24-hour and 365-day carries. This does not validate the astronomical placements or predict any real event.

## Original Maginus worked-example anomaly — unresolved

PDF746 quotes the annual 54′ measure and its year table: **13 completed years = 11°42′**, **22 years = 19°48′**. To reach the prior target **11°55′**, only **13′** remains; Lilly's paragraph instead uses **42′** to derive about **284 days**. To reach **19°52′**, only **4′** remains; Lilly uses **48′** to derive **324 days**. This is an apparent residual-subtraction inconsistency internal to the printed worked examples, potentially shifting its nominal dates by substantial fractions of a year. It may be a typesetting mistake or historically intended convention not yet reconstructed. This pass establishes the **observable mismatch of explicitly printed numbers**, not a definitive editorial erratum or an empirically corrected prediction.

Do **not** use the 42′ or 48′ passages as independently verified mathematical instructions; do **not** fix their printed figures silently. The frozen witness at `WORKED_DIRECTION_TIME_SOURCE_CASES_V1.json` contains original-number claims and separate reconstructed arithmetic; `lilly_1647_direction_time_mechanics.py` demonstrates Ptolemy, Maginus residuals and Naibod arithmetic with fail-closed units and no natal chart engine. Independent semantic source expertise, table-wide digit collation, and a comparison edition are still required.
