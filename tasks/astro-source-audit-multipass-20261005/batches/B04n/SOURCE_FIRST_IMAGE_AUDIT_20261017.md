The horse-lost-or-stolen worked horoscope begins on PDF501 with its own **11 January 1646, 2:59 PM** square figure, distinct from the preceding general witchcraft chapter. The horse is treated as Mercury ruler twelfth, retrograde and applying toward the Sun (ruler second), with Moon in second and Jupiter/Mars retrograde close to second cusp. Lilly predicts recovery 'within a day or two', 'quickly and unexpectedly', and infers that an alleged thief cannot retain the horse. He derives westward travel from the twelfth sign Gemini, Mercury in Aquarius and other positional testimonies, while explicitly mentioning a contrary Northeast indication from the Moon in Leo. Mercury's retrograde condition is used to argue that the horse will not travel far and will return.

The text explicitly says **the horse returned three days after** and was well, and Lilly says his estimate was **one day sooner** than reality. He then offers a retrospective explanation involving the Sun's lack of dignity and Mercury's partial conjunction, and gives the observed date as **Wednesday or Thursday, January 13 or 14**. That date alternative corresponds to two or three days after the printed January 11 question, preventing a uniquely frozen historical actual time. We preserve both the author-admitted timing miss and the exact-date ambiguity. The favorable node/Moon/retrograde factors and his alleged rider identification are restated afterward, including a self-reported 'very true', but no independent horse owner or witness corroborates any detail.
