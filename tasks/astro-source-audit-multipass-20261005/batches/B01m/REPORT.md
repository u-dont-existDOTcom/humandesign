## 1. The topic’s ruler is selected by a procedure, not by a familiar shortcut

Ptolemy begins by referring back to the length-of-life procedure in III.10. The selected mechanism determines which place is examined: the place of occourse for projection/encounter, or the occident itself for the significator’s descent. Planets occupying the selected places have initial priority. Only if they are absent does the passage turn to the first approaching planets. Other planetary aspects, the signs, and the terms then contribute to the kind of outcome (PDF451–453/printed427–429).

Consequently, an automatic eighth-house-ruler shortcut is not the selection method stated here. Rhetorius’s eighth-house paragraphs and chapter77 offer other routes, including additional Lots and a separately attributed Critodemus term-ruler method (Rhetorius PDF82/printed85 and PDF118–120/printed125–127). These remain different source-specific procedures, not interchangeable implementations of one universal rule.

The new helper preserves the important known-absence distinction: an uncomputed occupancy does not authorize the approaching-planet fallback. It retains multiple/tied supplied carriers and does not pretend to establish the astronomical qualifications itself.

## 2. A benefic label does not override the planet’s specific role

After selection, all five planets have cause descriptions, including Jupiter and Venus. Those lists have topical prerequisites; they are not natal diagnoses. The source also makes an afflicted Jupiter’s testimony a modifier of already adverse Saturn/Mars configurations, adding public or official circumstances in the particular examples (PDF453,457,459–461/printed429,433,435–437).

Our methodological inference is that a system must keep a planet’s role, condition, and inherited context visible. A generic “Jupiter protects” rule cannot simply cancel a qualified example in which Jupiter is explicitly afflicted. Nor does Jupiter’s presence by itself establish any adverse outcome. No known person or event was tested against these passages.

The historical disease vocabulary is retained in the source tables without conversion into modern diagnostic codes. The chapter’s “natural/own” category has both a positive condition and an exclusion: the selected lords are in their own or kindred natural characters, and no qualifying injurious planet overcomes them. The Anonymous commentator’s same-sect-domicile gloss is separately attributed. Failure of that branch does not by itself establish the opposite classification (PDF455/printed431).

## 3. Kind, magnitude, publicity, burial, and location are separate outputs

Ptolemy explicitly assigns affliction to the malefics’ junction, magnitude to luminary testimony, and kind to additional planetary regard and the signs involved. The closing paragraphs separately discuss compounded circumstances, absence of burial, animal/bird consumption, and foreign location (PDF455–457 and461/printed431–433 and437).

The postmortem passage retains its inherited conditions, animal/bird-form qualification, and absence of benefic testimony to the lower meridian or destructive places. It is not a one-factor rule. A benefic relationship defeating that particular compound does not become a claim that the person survives.

Rhetorius provides a useful comparison: one clause says that benefic regard of an ill-situated eighth house or ruler leaves the same kinds of deaths but makes them non-public. A different clause, only benefics aspecting the eighth without Saturn/Mars, describes a peaceful death. The antecedents differ. Mitigation of publicity and alteration of the event are not the same proposition (Rhetorius PDF120/printed127). This comparison is source interpretation, not empirical support.

## 4. Two apparent implementation details are genuine unresolved choices

For the prison example, Robbins’s adopted translation places Saturn in the ascendant opposing a luminary. His note reports that Melanchthon interpreted the rare expression in the opposite sense, in the occident. Both appear in the page image. Selecting the more successful location after observing a life would be a model revision, not a correction of an OCR error (PDF457/printed433).

The concluding combined-malefic clause also says the planets are together and in that state in opposition. The target of that opposition is not made explicit by the English sentence. We preserve the conjunction and the unresolved referent; we do not quietly replace “and” with “or” or assert that two planets are simultaneously in mutual conjunction and mutual opposition (PDF461/printed437).

The source’s “particularly” and “especially” modifiers also remain intensifiers rather than automatic prerequisites. Alternatives retain their disjunctive structure. In the adopted Mars–Venus wording, dying because of women and dying as murderers of women are different actor roles; they cannot be collapsed into an unconditional character or violence claim (PDF459/printed435).

## 5. Source attribution can change the required calculation table

In Rhetorius77’s explicitly alternative Critodemus passage, Holden notes that its term system included the Sun. That note does not supply the complete table, and it does not authorize substituting our already transcribed Egyptian or Ptolemaic tables. The same technical noun can refer to a materially different calculation system (Rhetorius PDF118–119/printed125–126).

The same pages preserve main-text elongation figures and separate editorial comments, including a tentative alternative for Venus. No new numerical threshold was adopted from the tentative note. Rhetorius’s own reuse of material between chapters57 and77 is not counted as independent confirmation. Only the selected available paragraphs were compared; chapter77’s continuation is incomplete in the examined witness, so this is not a complete chapter audit.
