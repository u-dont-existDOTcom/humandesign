## Historical durability hypotheses

The principal condition is a second-house planet with essential dignity. When it is favorably placed, strong and benefic, Lilly alleges undisturbed lifelong property. He then gives three distinct contrary situations: a weak planet may permit continuing resources yet great difficulty and temporary gains followed by roughly compensating loss; a strong malefic may still permit estate to persist with difficulty, but a debilitated malefic is assigned collapse; and moderately strong planets produce intermediate results that the author does not quantify.

When several planets occupy the second house, he ranks the strongest one. When none does, the source explicitly falls back to the lord of the second and the dispositor of the Part of Fortune. These conditions should not be simplified to an automatic judgment from an empty house.

The chapter's event-timing alternatives are unusually broad. Lilly permits directions of significators/Part of Fortune to benefic promittors, second-house ruler, Fortune dispositor, an occupying planet and several aspects, then a *general* fallback using the occupied house quadrant to assign a life period. The author offers youth, manhood (around 25 to 35/40), approaching old age (after 40 before 55), or the latter part of life. He additionally allows predicted lifespan to rescale those periods, planetary oriental/occidental placement to shift them earlier/later, direct/swift motion to hasten events and retrograde motion to delay. The source even proposes greater confidence when the multiple methods agree, without an independent calibration set.

Negative historical conditions include combust second ruler, afflicted Part of Fortune, certain malefic or fixed-star placements, angular-versus-cadent luminary application to preserve or diminish inherited estate, a Saturn-associated avarice stereotype subject to Sun/Moon exception, and a complex combination of malefic aspects, retrograde/cadent/peregrine status and contrary-nature signs. Several symbolic and grammatical referents remain unresolved. None is a real-person financial, legal, or psychological assessment.
