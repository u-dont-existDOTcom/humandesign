## Servant–master judgment

For this 1647 horary question, **first house, first lord and Moon represent the servant**, **tenth house and tenth lord represent the master**, whatever his condition. The author tests joining between the two lords by body or aspect, with and without reception. An exact conjunction **by degree and minute** means quick, easy freedom in the source; separation by a few minutes suggests it is virtually accomplished already. If that first relationship is absent, apply analogous conditions to the **Moon**, and if both servant significators lack the relevant relation with the tenth lord, apply analogous joining/separating conditions to the **Sun**. The source's broad word *conjunction* across body/aspect is preserved rather than harmonised with a narrower later definition. [PDF330; R1853–R1859; U03–U07]

The next question contrasts a **determinate versus not absolute** request, with examples of release from the present master versus ever being free from his power. It lists alternative departure indications: ascendant lord **cadent from an angle without beholding ascendant**, related to a planet **in an angle** or a planet that **beholds ascendant**, or in **third/ninth** (or joined to a planet there). Parallel Moon conditions can substitute. The contrasting denial spans PDF330–331: Moon or first lord in **first, tenth, seventh or fourth**, or joined with a planet there that is **direct**, prevents freedom; **retrograde** gives freedom but slow/difficult. Malefic corporeal conjunction, square or opposition afflicting the angular ascendant lord, or its **entering combustion**, are further negatives. None is a witnessed historical slave release, and none is present-day legal or human-rights guidance. [PDF330–331; R1860–R1870; U04–U08]

## General horary aphorisms 1–16

The first eight demand an acceptable **hour lord–ascendant lord nature or triplicity agreement** (1), warn differently about **early versus late degrees** (2), warn about tenth-house afflicted Saturn/Mars or a node glyph and astrologer's credit (3), reject trivial or unprepared questions (4), give the **Moon special weight as alleged planetary-virtue transmitter** (5), treat Saturn/Mars as symbolically cold/hot, with mutual-reception qualification (6), distinguish Jupiter/Venus application by sextile/trine and essential dignity (7), and give only a general benefic/malefic default (8). [PDF332; R1877–R1884]

Rule (9) says a **void-of-course Moon** is less obstructive in **Cancer, Taurus, Sagittarius or Pisces**; retain all four. **Moon separating** from a planet marks the past (10), **applying** marks the present question's direction under specified good house/aspect/planet conditions (11); application to a planet in fall is an adverse separate condition (12). **Retrograde or first-station** significators show discord (13); malefic significators make bad worse and good incomplete/difficult (14); slow motion delays (15); beneficial aspects mitigate an infortune's evil but rule16 ends with an ambiguous extension concerning fortunes as significators, so no new symmetrical algorithm is invented. [PDF333; R1885–R1892; U16–U17]

## Aphorisms 17–28

The numbered rules retain benefics' **cadency, poor dignity, no ascendant aspect or retrogradation**, with reception as a qualifier (17); contrast reception of malefic and benefic significators (18); describe **peregrine/no dignity** as negative (19); then provide a crucial **Saturn/Mars essential dignity AND angular position** positive exception (20, crossing PDF333–334). A fortune without essential dignity does not wholly perform (21), both fortunes and infortunes weak prompts **deferred judgment** (22), an afflicted question significator combust or in solar opposition cannot perfect (23), joined malefics show poor good or intensified evil (24), and a first lord without dignity and cadent indicates no hope (25). [PDF333–334; R1893–R1901]

Rule (26) specifies **within twelve degrees** of Sun as under the beams and weak, but **within sixteen arcminutes** as **cazimi**, strong. Rule (27) says oriental **Saturn/Jupiter/Mars** suggest earlier resolution, occidental later; **Venus/Mercury reverse that direction**. Rule (28) assigns fixed signs stability, common signs possibility without final conclusion, movable signs sudden resolution either way; historic examples concern house foundations, journeys and moderation. These are not exact tested calendar times, probabilities or safe high-stakes prescriptions. [PDF334; R1902–R1904; U19–U21]

## Aphorisms 29–43

Rule (29) treats contact of first lord/Moon with **Head or Tail of Dragon** as harmful, conditional on house; (30) links pending eclipse position with ascendant or significator degrees; (31) qualifies an impeded Moon with a **waning-Moon life warning** and separate journey and wooing examples. (32) discusses a significator opposite its own house, illustrated by Mercury in Sagittarius/Pisces; (33) traces obstructions through the planet's house lordship/location; (34) distinguishes angular/succedent/cadent positions; (35) treats Moon conjunct Sun/combustion as worse than ordinary malefic aspects; (36) gives a compound negative infortune-aspect status whose exact grammar remains unsettled; (37) requires significators' conjunction **and a congenial sign**; (38) distinguishes interruption by frustration or prohibition **before** perfection; (39) uses a **fortune-like glyph** in a particular house for gain/harm, with glyph identity left source-qualified. The last four clauses (PDF336) say a seventh-house infortune threatens marriage disagreement **unless it is a significator at birth** (40); an afflicted eighth lord **in the eighth** relates to separate woman-death and deceased-person debt claims (41); dignified Jupiter/Venus in house-linked matters suggest benefits, with third-kin, fourth-father/land, fifth-play examples (42); a Dragon-tail-like glyph indicates alleged harm/scandal/slander through that house (43). These are source claims, not independently documented marriage, death or debt predictions. **CHAP. XLIX** begins only **below** the next divider and is not extracted.
