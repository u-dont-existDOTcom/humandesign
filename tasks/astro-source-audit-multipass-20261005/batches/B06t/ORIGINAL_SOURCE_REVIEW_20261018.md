## Source distinctions

Lilly instructs astrologers to judge **both the nature of a planet indicating journeys and the house it occupies**. If a selected travel significator is conjoined with Saturn, occupies Saturn's essential dignities, or Saturn itself signifies journeys, the source offers several *alternatives*: searching for mineral deposits/mines, dealing with an inheritance from a deceased person, handling Saturn-classified commodities, or contacts with older men, magistrates, gentlemen and merchants. These different motives cannot constitute independently replicated successes from one chart. A Jupiter source instead offers religion, honor, law, ecclesiastical preferment, or contacts with clerical/judicial/noble people. Lilly refers other planets to material in his second-house discussion without enumerating all their alternative motives in this chapter.

The **first house** of a travel significator purportedly indicates curiosity or a desire to see unfamiliar countries; the **second** is attributed to trade, buying/selling and potentially enrichment. Subjective intention, embarking on a trip, making a transaction and accumulating wealth are separate endpoints requiring independently measured observations.

The passage attributed to Haly associates the **Lord of the Ninth**, specifically, with placement in eight separate houses: first—travel frequently; second—gain by travel; third—cohabiting with unfamiliar women and changing place; fourth—parents' hidden illness and the native's death on a journey; fifth—children born or present abroad; sixth—benefit involving enslaved persons/servants and illness of cattle during travel; seventh—obtaining a wife described favorably and obediently; eighth—desire to obtain an estate and journeying across seas. **The original excerpt stops after the eighth-house case**. No ninth through twelfth equivalents are supplied, and none should be invented or inferred from later methods. The gender, social hierarchy, medical and mortality claims are seventeenth-century rhetoric only. No real person's safety, family health, intimate behavior or economic status can be concluded from them.
