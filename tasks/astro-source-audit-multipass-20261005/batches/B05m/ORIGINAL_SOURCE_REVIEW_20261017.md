## Source distinctions

- Four groups of wealth indicators: the second-house cusp/ruler and associated dignity; intercepted-sign ruler if present; Part of Fortune and its ruler plus Jupiter; planets occupying the second house. Lilly says closer occupants have stronger apparent signification, but gives no calibrated numerical weight. His geographic/house interval refers to *five degrees before the second cusp* and *within five degrees of the third cusp*. The original wording remains controlling; do not invent exact inequality operators.
- **Ptolemaic attribution**: Lilly claims Ptolemy's Book IV chapter 1 primarily investigates *from whom* or *by what causes* an estate arises, via Part of Fortune sign governors, aspects, elevations, qualitative relationships, favorable planets, and Sun/Moon testimony. This is Lilly's account of Ptolemy, **not yet independently compared against the Ptolemy original**.
- Historical pathways: Saturn allegedly signifies buildings, navigation and agriculture; Jupiter government, trust and religion; Mars military matters; Venus friends or gifts of women; Mercury oratory and commerce. These are authorial vocational stereotypes, not tested financial outcomes.
- The inheritance example requires Saturn governance of Part of Fortune plus qualified Jupiter aspects, angular status and alternatives. The phrase *or she afflicting* is textually/grammatically ambiguous and is not flattened to support a single unconditional inheritance rule.
- Distinct conditions purportedly explain wealth continuing or collapsing, and timing via planetary access to angles and succedent houses. No exact timing thresholds, financial measurement or blind case evaluation are provided.
- Lilly marks the end of his Ptolemy summary with *Thus Ptolomey* and then credits Leovitius and Origanus for later refinements. These are separate historical authorities, not an independent replication.
