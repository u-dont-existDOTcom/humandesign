## Three original 1640 profection examples and printed discrepancy

1. Ascendant 6°37′ Capricorn to Jupiter's antiscia at 8°05′: angular difference **1°28′**. Degree1 gives 12d4h12m; minute28 gives 5d16h22m; printed total **17d20h34m**. Lilly elects to round to **day18**, retrieving **October 6** from the source calendar table.
2. Ascendant 6°37′ to Saturn's dexter trine at 9°02′: difference **2°25′**. Printed degree2 plus minute25 gives **29d10h08m**, associated with **October 17**.
3. Ascendant 6°37′ to Jupiter's terms at 13°00′: the book **correctly subtracts 6°23′** but then **uses the minute37 row instead of minute23**. Degree6 73d1h10m plus minute37 7d12h11m produces the printed **80d13h21m**, associated with **December 7**. Both actual 1647 and 1659 prints show this inconsistent lookup. Using the *source's own minute23 row* would give **77d17h11m**, a difference of **2d20h10m**, before applying the source's rounding convention. The source's date lookup could then differ materially. The printed example is preserved separately from the analytical alternative; this observation does not establish author/compositor error cause or a historically corrected genuine prediction.

No working age-of-nativity ephemeris parity, old-style English calendar/New-Style date conversion, natal solar-hour location, individual event outcome confirmation or blinded predictive success exists for these examples. In particular a convenient interpretation of the printed 1640 calendar date must not substitute for verified 17th-century birth inputs.
