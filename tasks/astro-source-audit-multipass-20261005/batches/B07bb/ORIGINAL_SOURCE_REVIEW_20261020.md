## Parents' historical claims and how to interpret the evidence

The first source paragraph applies the Sun's circle of position at a **40-degree pole elevation** to the father (and, for this example, the mother whose circle is nearly the same). It attributes to Sun and Venus conditions a congenial/personable father, diminished paternal estate, marital harmony, mother's supposed greater affection for the native, and a conjecture that the mother might outlive the father. These are **authorial interpretations about a pre-existing merchant example**. The real parents' independent birth data, family records, observed longevity, financial condition, and outcome confirmations are not supplied here. They cannot count as prospective successes or as verified personality/mortality data.

The second source paragraph attributes to **Ptolemy** a method to derive a **hypothetical parents' chart from a firstborn child's nativity**. This is explicitly different from calculating a parent natal chart from that person's true date, precise time and birthplace. In daytime nativities the Sun signifies the father; marginal material also distinguishes nocturnal father and daytime/nocturnal mother significators. Lilly instructs the reader to erect a scheme under the parent-significator's circle-of-position elevation, use a matching table of houses if available, or add **30° to the Sun's oblique ascension** for the second-house construction before using Regiomontanus-style house poles. His printed example identifies the father's **ascendant as 6°37′ Libra under a pole of 40°**. No full original 1616 sample chart, ephemeris, house tables, and independent Regiomontanus reconstruction has been verified.

The source's attribution to Ptolemy is Lilly's own attribution; this packet does not establish an independent textual match in a specific Ptolemy original edition. The original old astronomical algorithm and its application to the parents' birth chart remain unverified.
