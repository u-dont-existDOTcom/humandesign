## What the source actually says

Lilly lists long-travel indicators from ninth house and ninth ruler, planets occupying ninth, then Moon and Mercury. He separately attributes the addition of Mars to Ptolemy; the original Ptolemy text and the seventeenth-century planet glyph in that line have not independently been compared. This is not independent historical confirmation of an accurate chart method.

He then lists four distinct clusters of questions: **(1)** whether the native travels abroad at all, once or repeatedly, nearly continuously/transmarine or only rarely; **(2)** if travel is established, which world regions are visited relative to the **native country**, including a preferred or repeated destination among multiple regions; **(3)** principal causes of travel; **(4)** travel success and possible life-threatening hazard. These are questions to investigate using natal indicators, **not recorded successful forecasts or fully specified mathematical rules**. The source provides no thresholds for frequency, age window, journey length, distance, compass direction, actual number of travels or probability of danger.

The destination question depends explicitly on travel occurrence, and an observer cannot substitute one answered dimension—e.g., merely taking one trip—for another asserted outcome—e.g., permanent foreign residence or safe travel. Nor can an unspecified dangerousness claim justify a travel safety decision for a real individual.
