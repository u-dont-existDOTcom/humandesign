# Lilly 1647 Book II Chapter LXXII — Archbishop of Canterbury original-source audit

Original witness: William Lilly, *Christian Astrology* (1647), Wellcome b30338724, full original PDF SHA-256 `2cb53e20e122ffc6e47917ba10241f6c49687cd7a4696f3b5db655a2a1aab28b`. The numbered Chapter LXXII spans original PDF453–454 / printed pages 419–420. A **separate** Chapter LXXIII concerning promised wife's portion begins on original PDF455 / printed421; only its heading is retained as navigation. Original page JPEG byte hashes and sizes are frozen in the source manifest.

The figure itself prints **3 December 1644, 2:24 PM**. Lilly selects the ninth-house Capricorn as the Archbishop's derived ascendant, Saturn as representative, and Jupiter as general clergy significator. The source describes the already-public political and imprisonment circumstances. It then interprets Moon separation from Saturn, Moon opposition to Sun, Sun opposition to Mars, and Mars in an airy/human sign as an alleged forecast that the Archbishop would **not be hanged** but would experience another, more noble form of execution within approximately **six or seven weeks, or thereabouts**. The forecast **does not literally specify beheading**.

On original PDF454 Lilly says the Archbishop was beheaded about the tenth of January following. This is an externally corroborated historical event: Oxford Bodleian Archives' *Laudian Collection* catalogue biography dates William Laud's beheading at Tower Hill to **10 January 1645** (https://archives.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/dept/scwmss/wmss/online/1500-1900/laudCLD/laudCLD.html). From 3 December 1644 to 10 January 1645 is **38 elapsed days**, four days short of the strict 42-day lower bound for six full weeks. The original qualifier **or thereabouts** is preserved; no post-hoc specific tolerance or exact timing accuracy is asserted.

Crucially, 1647 text publication follows the 1645 outcome. The source does not supply an independently dated December 1644 prediction manuscript, an original query witness, or a baseline for probable execution after years of imprisonment and 1644 treason proceedings. **Independent historical event authentication is not the same as independent prospective forecast authentication.** This case adds historical text, not a validated predictive result.

Lilly's expressions of respect and grief for Laud, his Oxford benefaction account, the disputed martyr label, and the parliamentary sentencing reference remain attributed to the author, distinct from objective astrological evidence. Personality, conduct and factional statements are period opinions, not independent factual diagnoses.
