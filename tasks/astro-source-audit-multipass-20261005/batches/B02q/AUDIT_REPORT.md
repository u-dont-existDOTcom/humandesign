## What Lilly actually calls a crisis

The chapter defines a *crisis* as the struggle between nature and illness: favourable if nature prevails and dangerous if the disease prevails. It also describes a sudden alteration of a sick person's condition tending toward health or further disease. **Critical, decretory and chrysmal** are presented as overlapping names for days thought to show the disease's course. The source should not be reduced to the idea that a crisis necessarily means a cure or a death; deterioration or further sickness is explicitly within its vocabulary. [PDF324 lower to PDF325 upper; R1794–R1798]

Lilly invokes **Galen**, **Hippocrates** and **Avicenna in Canticis** while discussing differing definitions, including sudden or vehement changes, recovery-versus-death formulations, and an exception for illnesses described as so malignant or poisonous that no fight between nature and disease occurs. These are **Lilly's 1647 attributions**; this packet has not collated the original Greek, Latin or Arabic works. The text also reports an alternative view in which crises may arise from noncelestial, inferior causes. Lilly argues for the Moon's role by discussing the differing days that might otherwise be expected for tertian, quartan and continued fevers. Recording that dispute does not establish the Moon as the medical cause. [PDF325–326; R1802–R1810; U03–U05,U19]

## Noninterchangeable anchors for lunar timing

Lilly prefers the Moon's position at **the actual moment the illness first began**. Only where that instant cannot be obtained does he admit the first time a patient's **urine sample is brought to the physician for recovery advice**, expressly with less certainty; without urine, he names the physician's first conversation **when the sick person requests a judgment and suggested cure**. These are a *hierarchy* of competing event anchors, not three interchangeable favourable measurements or arbitrary later clinic visits. The later mention of the patient's first request for advice must be read in light of that hierarchy. They are historical protocol statements, not informed-consent or medical-service rules for contemporary use. [PDF325–326; R1799–R1801,R1812]

He then takes the Moon's sign and degree at the selected anchor as the **radix**. Its subsequent angular motion is used to identify **indicative** stages, **semiquadrates** (called half-crises), **quadratures** (true crises) and **oppositions** (full crises). On each such occasion the purported result still depends on the Moon's other planetary configurations. The chapter also separately instructs a reader to observe when the Moon passes the **sixth, seventh and eighth house cusps** and what aspects it has then. Neither a single date nor one figure-free angular step supplies all of those conditions. [PDF326–327; R1811,R1813–R1814,R1818,R1824]

## The worked demonstration reuses the previous chart

The chapter explicitly says to **suppose** that the Moon's place in the earlier **16 July 1645** figure represented the actual beginning of an illness. This is **not** a new person's natal chart, independently recorded onset or another verified forecast. In this paragraph the Moon is written as approximately **15°42′ Virgo** and Lilly enters the table at **16° Virgo**, reasoning that 42 minutes almost make a whole degree. That rounding discards 18 arcminutes; the prior chart image contains small, copy-dependent glyph/numeral uncertainties and has not been made equal to a modern ephemeris. [PDF326; R1815–R1816; U11–U12,U31]

In that source demonstration, the first **indicative** point is **8°30′ Libra**; the first **semiquadrate/half crisis** is **1° Scorpio**; the second **indicative** point is **23°30′ Scorpio**; and the first **true quadrature crisis** is **16° Sagittarius**. These agree with lunar angular progressions of 22°30′, 45°, 67°30′ and 90° respectively from the **rounded** 16° Virgo radix. The printed explanation says to continue the process through the remaining sky and assess good or ill depending on the Moon's aspects, also checking the sixth–eighth house cusps. These are **computed angular targets**, not exact dates of observation or demonstrated patient changes. [PDF326–327; R1817–R1824]

## The 30-by-16 original table and its limits

**Original PDF328 / printed294** contains Lilly's full, densely typeset zodiacal table headed *The Table followeth*. Its 30 rows and 16 successive phase columns are retained in the accompanying original image. A **separate, explicitly editorial mathematical reconstruction** provides 480 cells using the visible progression of **22°30′ per successive column**, and one degree per successive row, beginning at **Aries 0°30′**. The recorded cross-checks include the source's Virgo16° → Libra8°30′ → Scorpio1° → Scorpio23°30′ → Sagittarius16° sequence. The mathematical cells are **not claimed to be individual diplomatic transcriptions of all 480 printed entries**; historical numeral misprints or uncertain column/glyph positions have not been eliminated by a formula. The actual original image remains controlling whenever model and print could disagree. [PDF328; TABLE_GEOMETRY.json; R1825–R1826; U13–U15]

The same source states that earlier people used the **seventh, fourteenth and twenty-first days** as critical days, but immediately observes that **the Moon moves at different speeds**. It therefore requires calculation of lunar true motion for a precise day. The table returns zodiacal positions, not calendar timestamps; converting it to actual dates requires ephemeris, location/time definitions and the chosen event anchor. It is methodologically incorrect to hard-code seven days, to divide every synodic month into equal dates, or to claim precise timing from the table alone. [PDF329; R1830–R1831; U16]

## Good/bad indications are conditional, and the remedies are historical claims

Especially at the **first quartile**, Lilly associates the Moon's conjunction or aspects to a fortunate planet with a more favourable crisis—but only with the explicit clause **if the party be ordained for life**. He links that favourable circumstance to the physician's possible restoration by easy medicines. Conversely, the Moon's unlucky aspect to an infortune is described as unfavourable and reason for greater caution. These source claims have nontrivial overlapping conditions, a potentially unfalsifiable survival exception and **no modern patient validation** in this section. They cannot be promoted as clinical death forecasts or care advice. [PDF329 upper; R1827–R1829; U17–U18,U30]

## Four sign families, three purging combinations and planetary faculties

The old sign/temperament mapping names **Moon in fire signs** (Aries, Leo, Sagittarius) as strengthening the *attractive* virtue in phlegmatic people; **earth** (Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn), *retentive* in sanguine; **air** (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius), *digestive* in melancholic; and **water** (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces), *expulsive* in choleric. These four conditional pairings must not be treated as four modern treatment trials. [PDF329 middle; R1832–R1835]

A nearby typographic brace gives historical **purging** instructions when the **Moon is in Cancer, Scorpio or Pisces AND in sextile or trine** to a relevant body: **Jupiter** for melancholy, **Venus** for choler, and **Mars or the Sun** for phlegm. Retaining the common sign/aspect condition, and Mars **or** Sun rather than a fictitious combined planet, is necessary to represent the printed scheme. The script does **not** recommend purging. The separate source correspondence then names **Saturn** with retentive virtue owing to coldness, and pairs **Jupiter** with vegetative/digestive, **Mars** with attractive/irascible, **Sun** with vital/natural, **Venus** with appetitive/concupiscible, **Mercury** with cogitative/imaginative, and **Moon** with expulsive. Those are descriptions of a historical theory, not reliable physiological or psychological associations. [PDF329; R1836–R1845; U20–U21]

The closing elemental-humour table additionally associates **fire with red choler**, **earth with black melancholy**, **air with blood**, and **water with spittle/phlegm**. These rows are separate from the sign/temperament *faculty* rules. Lilly says he intended a longer discussion of sickness but did not add it because **Master Booker** promised to undertake it; no absent additional rules may be fabricated. The continuation on PDF330 ends before the Chapter XLVIII divider. [PDF329–330; R1846–R1851]
