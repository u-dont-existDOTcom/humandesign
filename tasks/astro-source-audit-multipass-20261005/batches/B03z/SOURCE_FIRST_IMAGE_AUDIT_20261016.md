**An auditable chain of source reasoning, not independent evidence:**

1. PDF473: Lilly distinguishes common angles from a **fixed Taurus ninth house**, regards Saturn there as a severe religious-government indicator, and uses Jupiter as a general religion significator. Jupiter is moving from long retrogradation/station toward direct motion and out of exaltation, with the text continuing across the physical 473/474 seam.
2. PDF474: Jupiter's claimed movement through Cancer and Leo, Saturn/Mercury bounds, troublesome fixed stars, and Mars impediment are recorded as **conditional astrological premises**. Venus (ninth lady) is in eighth/Aries detriment, described as needing 21 degrees to reach Taurus; a pair of squares occurs before such stabilization. Lilly specifically limits potentially material effects to conditions involving Saturn's dignity, monastic institutions, or founding a religious order, rather than asserting the squares straightforwardly caused real political events.
3. PDF474: The planet census distinguishes fixed status and essential dignity from Mars/Mercury falls and Venus detriment. The Moon's sequence is **separation from Venus → void of course → square Mars → square Jupiter**. Crucially Lilly calls his judgment *conjectural*, rather than positive or affirmative. Such epistemic restraint cannot be dropped when reading his later confident political language.
4. PDF474–475: Saturn ninth implies (in Lilly's view) an overly harsh Presbytery, public resentment, internal clergy quarrels, and disagreement between junior Mercury-linked members and elder Saturn-linked members. Descriptions of covetousness, rigid temperament, and England's popular disposition are **source judgments**, not independent behavior measurements or modern demographic observations.
5. PDF475: Saturn's asserted poor dignity/support, reception with Moon without aspect, and Mercury tenth **separating** from Saturn anchor an interpretation that gentry and national leadership will retreat from clerical austerity. Lilly predicts soldiers and common folk oppose the system and explicitly says it will not survive **statu quo**; he also speculates that reforms reducing rigidity and greed might change prospects.
6. PDF475–476: Lilly gives a **within three whole years** window after the horary date, but proposes **alternative** pathways: intervention by civil authority or providence; return to earlier religious purity or a more loved arrangement; *or* merely moderating demands. Other forecasted episodes include a period of disorder, soldiers withholding contributions and obedience, magistrate review, opposition among clergy and broad public resistance. Finally he returns to Moon and fourth-house Jupiter testimony and concludes the institution will not persist **in its present form without refinement and amendment**. The **conditional status-quo language is not equivalent to a prediction that all Presbyterian institutions disappear**.
