# First-edition William Lilly Essex westward march — original source audit and independent military history

The first-edition primary witness (*Christian Astrology*, 1647, Wellcome b30338724, original full PDF SHA256 2cb53e20e122ffc6e47917ba10241f6c49687cd7a4696f3b5db655a2a1aab28b) contains an **unnumbered** square horoscope on original PDF507/printed473: *The time of his Excellencies, Robert Earle of Essex, last setting forth into the west*, **Monday 13 May 1644 at 12:29 PM**. It appears after printed Chapter XCVI and **before** the eight-page planetary-hour table beginning PDF508; do not invent a numbered chapter here.

Lilly attributes physical appearance to Aquarius rising and several planetary descriptions of mind. He reports Moon separating from trine Saturn then applying square to Mars, which he identifies with fortune/resources, allies and ninth house journey. He attributes adverse prospects to south node first, Saturn peregrine/fallen in second, Moon in detriment, Fortune disposed by Mercury as enemy signifier, and Jupiter's dexter square to the ascending degree. He says he forecast **little success, much loss, little honor, opposition by powerful figures in London, betrayal and danger of losing all**, while naming no individually authenticated opponent.

He also admits the expedition **initially prospered** and says leading figures ridiculed his forecast. Then he reports bad news reached him September 8: on **September 2**, in his phrasing, 'this worthy man had surrendered all his Ammunition to his Majesty', obtaining quarter for soldiers and other articles; he condemns the Royalist side for alleged mistreatment of terms.

**Independent event-level adjudication (not a forecast verification):** Historic England's registered Battle of Lostwithiel account (official List Entry 1413762, https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1413762) draws on contemporaneous Essex correspondence and both Royalist and Parliamentary accounts. It records that **the Earl of Essex himself escaped by sea early on September 1, 1644**, Major General Skippon negotiated the army's surrender on **September 2**, and parliamentary soldiers relinquished cannon, arms and ammunition in exchange for being allowed to march away. Thus the **army loss** part of Lilly's historical narrative is corroborated, whereas a **literal assertion of Essex personally surrendering** is not. Historic England does not establish that the original 13 May prognostication was authentically deposited or seen prior to September's event, nor does it establish astrological causality or validate the chart. Preserve claims of adverse military outcome and independent verification of event fact separately from any unverified predictive accuracy.
