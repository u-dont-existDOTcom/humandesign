## Important findings

### 1. Selecting the time is part of the method

Lilly first asks for the time illness compelled the person to bed or rest, expressly excluding the first slight feeling of illness. If that time cannot be obtained, he turns to the first carrying of the person’s urine for enquiry. Only after those stages does he give alternatives involving the physician’s first conversation, access or receipt of urine.

Those last alternatives have no stated internal ranking. The reference helper therefore keeps unavailable evidence separate from evidence whose availability is unknown, and preserves competing time candidates. **It does not invent an extra priority rule.** *(XLIV, printed p. 243 / PDF277.)*

### 2. The two lunar-motion thresholds are 36 arcseconds apart

The local duration rule refers to motion below the Moon’s mean. Lilly’s earlier definition gives **13°10′36″ per day**, while a separate analogy to a retrograde planet uses **less than 13°10′00″**.

| Daily motion | Below the stated mean? | Meets the retrograde analogy? |
|---|---|---|
| 13°10′00″ | Yes | No |
| 13°10′18″ | Yes | No |
| 13°10′36″ | No | No |

Both comparisons are strict. They cannot be merged into one condition, and neither makes the Moon physically retrograde. These are controlled arithmetic examples, not reconstructed observations. *(Printed p. 80 / PDF114; XLIV, printed pp. 251–252 / PDF285–286.)*

### 3. A remaining arc does not supply a duration unit

Lilly counts the degrees remaining before a planet leaves its sign, then proposes months, weeks or days according to the sign’s nature and quality. The local passage does not complete that unit-selection rule.

The helper can establish that **29°30′00″ leaves half a degree**. It cannot turn that into half a day, week or month without another justified rule. Missing minutes or seconds stay missing. *(XLIV, printed p. 249 / PDF283.)*

### 4. Prognosis depends on complete qualifications

The records keep recovery, duration, relapse, present danger and death separate. Several details would be lost in a simplified implementation:

- A benefic in the sixth must be well fortified **and not the author of the disease**.
- The adverse Moon–Saturn passage says **“for the most part”** and excepts a disease already **decreasing and leaving the patient**. Decreasing alone does not establish the complete exception.
- When Mars rules the ascendant and is in the sixth, a particular Venus rule extends its relatively favourable judgment even to **square or opposition**.
- Reception may permit recovery while a **very long and grievous illness** remains part of the judgment.

These are historical source statements, not evaluated clinical predictions. *(XLIV, printed pp. 250–257 / PDF284–291.)*
