## S-A01 - Opening purpose, timing hierarchy and observations

Anchor: PDF277 / printed243, from `THAT which I hold convenient to write of Sicknesse, is thus much` through `what house of the Figure that Planet is Lord of`.

1. Lilly introduces what he holds convenient to write about sickness with that opening sentence. It precedes the timing method and is part of the source block, not an omitted decorative opening.
2. First choice: carefully obtain the exact time of the person's first falling sick, meaning the hour as nearly as obtainable. The operative event is expressly **not** the first moment when the patient felt a `smatch` of it. It is the time when the person first became so ill or so extremely oppressed that they were compelled to take bed or repose.
3. Second choice is conditional on the first being unavailable: accept the time when the sick person's urine was first carried to somebody to enquire about the disease. The person asked may be a physician **or not**.
4. Third choice is conditional on no such thing being obtainable: the physician takes the time of their own first speaking with, or access to, the patient, **or** when the urine was first brought to that physician. The text groups these alternatives; it does not rank the three events within this third fallback.
5. A figure is to be erected accordingly and the Moon's place `exactly rectified to the very houre`.
6. To know where the disease is, observe: (a) the ascendant and planets placed there; (b) the sixth house and planets there; (c) the sign and house containing the Moon; (d) how the Moon is affected or afflicted, by which planet, in what house that planet is, and what house of the figure it rules.

The source defines a timing/observation procedure, not a medical examination in present-day terms. `First falling sick`, first symptom, first bed/repose, first urine delivery, and first physician contact must not be collapsed into one undifferentiated timestamp. The opening timing hierarchy is not asserted here to govern a birth chart.

## S-A02 - What part of the body is afflicted

Anchor: PDF277 / printed243, heading `What part of the Body is afflicted, wherein you consider:` and first clause `If the first house be afflicted by the presence of an evill Planet`; continuation PDF278 / printed244 through `predominant and peccant`.

1. The opening condition spans the page turn. If the first house is afflicted by the presence of an evil planet and that planet is retrograde, combust, peregrine, slow in motion, **or** in square or opposition to any planet ruling the fourth, sixth, eighth, or twelfth, the disease is placed in the head **or** in the body part(s) the planet(s) signify in the sign then ascending. The exact coordination is retained; it has not been converted into executable boolean logic.
2. The text refers to a preceding table on **page119**. A crop of the original PNG confirmed the printed reference is `119`; OCR had dropped its first digit. The referenced table itself is outside this assigned reading and was not checked here.
3. Worked example: Cancer ascends with Saturn therein. Lilly says one may judge the person afflicted in the head or with diseases incident to it, since the first house signifies the head and is now afflicted by Saturn's presence.
4. The example also directs judgment of looseness/flux in the belly, an imperfection in the `Reynes or Secrets`, cold raw matter in the stomach, **or** a rotten cough, because Saturn in Cancer signifies those members. These alternatives are stated as part of the example, not modern diagnoses or a claim that all occur together.
5. Lilly says the judgment will be more certain, and he `dare say infallible`, **if** either the ascendant's lord, the Moon, or the sixth's lord occupies a sign and there signifies the very same member as Saturn, **or** if the sixth's sign represents that member. The certainty claim is Lilly's and the corroboration condition must travel with it.
6. He directs the same course in the sixth house: inspect its sign, planets placed there, which member each represents in its occupied sign, from whom the sixth lord last separated, and to whom that lord next applies.
7. Add the Moon's sign, house, separation and application. From those combined observations he permits judgment about the affected body part, the nature/quality of sickness, and the humour that is most predominant and `peccant`.

Source/inference boundary: `evil planet`, `afflicted`, `member`, `same member`, aspect application/separation, and the historical humour vocabulary have not received invented numerical definitions here. The Cancer/Saturn illustration is distinct from the general rule and its strengthened-certainty clause.

## S-A07 - Transition: why judge duration and increase/decrease

Anchor: PDF281 / printed247, paragraph `From what precedes, it's easie to discover` immediately following the Moon list, before `Whether the Disease will be long or short`.

Lilly claims the preceding material makes it easy to discover the member afflicted, disease quality, cause, and original rise. After these are considered, he says it is requisite to be able to acquaint the sick person with the disease's length/shortness and time of `accesse or recesse`. The stated purpose is to comfort the person **if life be ordained**, or make them more penitent and prepared for heaven **if apparent testimonies of death are seen**. Both theological/conditional alternatives belong to the source. The reader has not converted this purpose into modern counselling advice.

## S-A08 - Whether the disease will be long or short: season and planetary source

Anchor: PDF281 / printed247, `Whether the Disease will be long or short.` First seasonal paragraph and the Saturn sentence at the foot, completed by PDF282 / printed248 through `Gouts, &c.`

1. First take account of the season of the year. Sicknesses occurring in winter are **usually** longer and of long continuance; in summer shorter; in spring they are `reputed healthfull`; in autumn **for the most part** mortal and pernicious diseases are stirred up. `Usually`, `reputed`, and `for the most part` must not be removed.
2. Cold and dry diseases proceeding from Saturn, stirred up by him, or of which he is author, are more permanent and long, and **generally** regulated by the Sun. This sentence crosses PDF281-282.
3. Hot and dry diseases procreated from Mars and the Sun's influence are short and determined by the Moon's motion.
4. The ensuing compact list says: Saturn produces chronic infirmities; Jupiter and Sun short; Mars more short, violent and quick; Venus a mean between both; Mercury diverse and inconstant; Moon those that return, such as falling sickness, giddiness, swimming of the head, gouts, etc.
5. The text gives no numerical number of days for these planetary duration classes. It does not specify an executable mixture rule when more than one planet contributes. `A meane betwixt both` leaves its comparison implicit in the surrounding long/short contrast; no precise midpoint is invented here.

## S-A09 - Signs of a long or short sickness: sixth cusp, rulers, strength and exceptions

Anchor: PDF282 / printed248, `Signes of a long or short Sicknesse.` through the foot's `I ever found`. The final predicate is continued at PDF283 / printed249, `it equivalent to a moveable Signe`.

1. Sixth-house sign fixed: expect a long disease. Moveable: short continuance. Common: a mediocrity, neither too long nor short, **but for the most part** an alteration of the disease and return of it again.
2. The last degrees of any sign on the sixth-house cusp indicate that the disease is nearly ended, **or** altering for better **or** worse. No threshold for `last degrees` is supplied in this passage. Fixed signs argue that the humour will not be expelled without much time and difficulty.
3. Sixth lord of evil influence, placed in the sixth: an ill omen / unlucky sign of durable and great sickness. **But if in the like nature a Fortune is there**, disease will soon be cured and is not mortal. The words `in the like nature` are retained; no independent reinterpretation of their reach is supplied.
4. Sixth lord stronger than ascendant lord: disease is **like to** increase; Lilly directs advice for suitable remedies because nature is weaker than disease. This is a recorded historical instruction, not advice from this reader.
5. Sixth lord weaker than ascendant lord: he says one may be assured nature will overcome the disease's malignity without much physician assistance. The strength metric is not defined in this passage, and no equal-strength case is given here.
6. Saturn ruling the sixth and `fixed in the Signe he is in` extremely prolongs the disease; retrograde or slow in motion does the same. The exact expression about being `fixed` is retained rather than choosing a modern formal interpretation.
7. Qualification to Saturn: if he is `in a moveable Sign and in any of his Termes, or swift in motion`, he is not then very unfortunate or greatly prolonging. The printed sentence reads `he is not then much unfortunate, or will he greatly prolong the Disease`; it should be retained with a grammatical ambiguity rather than silently normalized into a strict formula. The AND/OR grouping of moveable sign, own terms, and swiftness is not unambiguously parenthesized by the source.
8. Moveable sign in the sixth, Moon likewise, **and no other impediment appearing**: disease continues only a while. `Likewise` is kept with the source context; the note does not resolve whether it demands the Moon's sign to be moveable alone or adds another shared condition.
9. Common signs make an infirmity continue longer, **except Pisces** when on the sixth cusp: Lilly's first-person claim begins on PDF282 `I ever found` and concludes at PDF283 `it equivalent to a moveable Signe`. Reader B owns that continuation. It must be combined with the antecedent for a complete record; the first-person experiential claim does not become independent verification.
