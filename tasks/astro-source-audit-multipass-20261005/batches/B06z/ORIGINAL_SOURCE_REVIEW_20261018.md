## Source strategy and distinct claim targets

CXLVI asks **how a previously postulated promotion might arise and what its quality will be**, not whether high rank occurs at all. Its first model chooses planet(s) in close or partile aspect to the lights, giving priority to the stronger luminary. Planet and house interpretations combine, with *plurality of testimonies* noted but no reproducible weights or calibration.

Lilly then associates **Saturn, when strong and aspecting the lights**, with inheritance, property, favors and elderly ancestors/frugal people—not simply negative events. **Jupiter and Venus** supposedly confer honor through virtue, honesty, gravity, justice or contacts with respective social groups. **Mars** refers to military expeditions, leadership, victory, acts of valor and recommendations of martial men. **Mercury** refers to wit, judgment, rhetoric, scholarship, merchants, scholars and lawyers. These outcomes differ as motives, occupations, contacts, inherited wealth, achievements and social esteem. Labels such as honest, valorous and virtuous are moral period rhetoric, not verified statements about real people's character.

The cause also depends on the natal **house of the qualifying planet**: first house allegedly implies one's own industry, second wealth, with other houses interpreted using the prior general planet/house method. **When no planet aspects the lights**, the author instructs considering the most powerful planet in tenth; **when tenth is empty**, the lord of tenth and its house. These are conditional, sequential fallbacks, not a pool of independent forecasts.

Lilly also records an alternative proposed by other astrologers: consider the dispositor of the luminary above earth, or the light most closely aspecting the tenth ruler or tenth house. The author supplies no independent human blind comparison to adjudicate the alternatives and no fixed winning score when they conflict.
