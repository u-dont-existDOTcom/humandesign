## Symmetric admission rule

- A person-level **directional claim** must specify enough direction, context and possible disconfirmation to distinguish one behavior from its opposite.
- When the same source supplies **A and not-A as alternatives** without an anticipated context or a probability partition, **neither alternative receives confirmation credit** after the observed outcome is known. An opposite result cannot be saved by a separately available fallback. If the source really forecasts both A-in-one-context and B-in-another, treat this as **one joint prediction**, requiring those contexts and both components to be observed.
- Correctly distinguish *capacity* from habitual frequency, *privacy* from absence of public work, and *strong effort* from unlimited stamina. Those pairs are not logically contradictory; neither substitutes for evidence about the other.
- Coaching questions, hypothetical narratives, “if this applies,” “might,” possible careers, and archetypal strengths/shadows do not constitute affirmative scored predictions unless a specific observable outcome was predeclared.
- Every false directional prediction is retained; do not choose only the successful member of the interpretive menu. Penalize the very large number of opportunities in a long narrative, and do not compare its raw coverage count to a fixed eight-claim list.
- Apply identical rules to the project’s pre-outcome frozen A1–A8 predictions: frozen status establishes provenance, **not predictive specificity**. Explicit null domains are neither failures nor new matches.
