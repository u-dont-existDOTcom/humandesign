# Behavioral patterns — follow-up interview (revised V2)

**Purpose:** Describe your *usual tendencies over time*, rather than what happened on one particular occasion. This is a short supplement to your earlier interview, not a retake of it. There are no right answers. The questions do not reveal an astrological interpretation.

**How to answer:** Think about your adult life, especially the past **2–3 years**. For each question, describe the pattern that occurs **most often** in relevant situations. If different responses are common, say approximately how often each occurs—ordinary words such as *rarely, sometimes, often, almost always* are enough. You may give a rough percentage if it comes naturally, but do not guess exact numbers. Explain which circumstances change your response, and whether the pattern has changed substantially with age. If the relevant situation rarely occurs in your life, simply say so. You do **not** need to supply particular anecdotes unless they clarify an exception or something ambiguous.

## 1. Goals, persistence, and changing course

When you choose a goal that will take weeks or months, and you encounter significant setbacks or progress becomes slower than expected, **what do you usually do?**

- Continue toward the same overall goal, perhaps changing your methods.
- Decide on a substantially different goal.
- Put the goal aside, without replacing it immediately.
- A mixture of these depending on circumstances.

**Clarify if needed:** How frequently does each occur? Does your response depend on whether the goal still feels meaningful, whether slow progress was expected, or whether the effort is sustainable? Has this general tendency changed over time?

## 2. Checking information before advising someone

When somebody asks for your opinion or recommendation about a **factual subject that you don't know well**, and you have enough time to check, **what is your usual approach?**

- Look up information or compare sources before recommending anything.
- Give an initial view from what you already know, making any uncertainty clear.
- Say you don't know, decline to recommend, or refer the person elsewhere.
- Another recurring approach or a strong context-dependent mixture.

**Clarify if needed:** Roughly how often do you independently check rather than answer or defer? What changes this—for example the importance of the decision, the time available, or the person's needs? Distinguish recommendations about facts from emotional support or personal taste.

## 3. Helping others and preserving your own limits

When a friend, colleague, or someone in your community needs **practical help that requires a meaningful amount of your time or energy**, what do you **usually actually do** when you are free to choose?

- Give substantial direct help yourself.
- Offer limited help, advice, or a referral, without taking on the main task.
- Decline or postpone helping.
- Vary considerably with the situation.

**Clarify if needed:** How common is each response? Does it change with closeness, exhaustion, your own priorities, pressure or guilt, or the importance of the need? Please distinguish **wanting to help** from **what you end up doing**.

## 4. Voluntary learning and sustained interests

Across your adult life, when a topic genuinely interests you and you are not required to study it for work or school, **which pattern is most typical?**

- Return repeatedly to the same topic and study it deeply over weeks, months, or longer.
- Go deeply into a topic in an intense burst, then move on to something new.
- Explore many topics relatively briefly without extended study.
- Spend little voluntary time studying, even when interested.
- A mixture that depends strongly on the topic or life period.

**Clarify if needed:** How often have you pursued a subject for several weeks or months by choice? What makes you stay or move on? Has this changed with responsibilities, available time, or age? Learning ability and *choice to study* are different things.

## 5. Initiative and coordination in groups

In groups of peers working toward a shared goal, especially when nobody has been assigned to organize the work, **what role do you tend to take across many different situations?**

- Spontaneously suggest plans, coordinate people, or take the lead.
- Participate actively without wanting to direct others.
- Observe or wait for someone else to coordinate, contributing when asked or when interested.
- Prefer to withdraw from group tasks when possible.
- Different roles depending on the group or situation.

**Clarify if needed:** How often does each role occur? Does it change with familiar versus unfamiliar people, expertise, urgency, respect for the group, or how interested you are? An ability to lead does not necessarily imply that you **prefer** leading.

---

## Optional: changes over time

Of these five patterns, which have remained fairly consistent through adulthood, and which changed significantly? For a changed pattern, what was different before? Do not assume there must be one stable answer across all life periods.

**End.** The interviewer should not repeat a question already adequately answered in your previous interview. Only genuine gaps about frequency, scope, and exceptions need follow-up.
