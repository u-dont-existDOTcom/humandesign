# Three optional follow-ups about your usual behavior

These are intended to clarify **recurring tendencies**, not judge a single recent incident. Much of your earlier interview already addresses the other relevant topics, so there is no need to repeat those questions.

Please consider your adult life, with particular attention to the last **2–3 years**. Describe what is typical and how often exceptions occur. Ordinary terms (*rarely, sometimes, often, almost always*) are fine. Exact percentages and examples are optional. If a kind of situation seldom occurs, say so rather than guessing.

## 1. Staying with a goal after setbacks

When a personally meaningful goal encounters real obstacles or progress is much slower than you hoped, **what do you usually end up doing across different goals**: continuing toward the same aim (perhaps changing methods), choosing a different aim, or setting it aside? About how often do you do each? What circumstances most change your response?

## 2. Checking unfamiliar facts before advising others

When someone asks for **factual advice about something you do not know well**, and you have time to check, what is your usual response: research the subject first, offer a tentative opinion based on what you know, or decline/defer the recommendation? Roughly how often does each happen? Does the seriousness of the decision affect this?

## 3. Taking the lead in groups

When a group of equals is working on something together and nobody is responsible for coordinating it, what role do you **usually prefer or actually take**: organizing the work, participating without leading, observing/waiting until someone needs you, or avoiding the group task? How common is each, and how does this change with familiar versus unfamiliar people or projects you care about?

### Optional clarification

Have these three patterns been fairly stable over adulthood, or have you changed significantly? If you have changed, briefly distinguish your earlier way of behaving from the more recent one.
