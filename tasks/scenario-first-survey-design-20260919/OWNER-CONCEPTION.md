# Owner conception before the external research pass

Captured 2026-09-19. Status: design exploration, not an approved implementation specification.

The owner finds it difficult to name their own life patterns, and finds broad questions about prioritizing oneself or others under-specified and likely to elicit "it depends." They propose representative example scenarios, including childhood and current romantic relationships, as easier entry points. They would accept more questions if each is a simpler, single question rather than several demands bundled together. They ask whether approximate current age is useful. The requested output is brainstorming followed by a handoff packet for a new conversation.

## Exact current request

> ok now i am thinking about a better survey design. currently it's a bit difficult for me to think what my patterns are and it also seems like a lot of times the answers are too obviously "it depends..." ... like would you prioritize yourself or others? ,,,
> what do you think about designing the survey around example scenarios.
> "You're 5 years old again. Your friend calls you and wants to play. You had another thing you wanted to do, but you know they are really bored. What do you tell them?"
> "You're at your current age (btw do we need to have some idea how old they are?), and your romantic partner is feeling anxious a lot. How does it make you feel when you're around them, or when you're at work and they are at home?"
> those are just examples but the point is i think it's easier for people to answer example scenarios (with the idea that these are representative) rather than very general pattern questions. it may mean we need more questions though but i think that's better than asking a bunch of questions in one question that most people won't answer fully or well. let's brainstorm this. then give a handoff packet for new convo

## Initial hypothesis, not a result

Give participants a concrete situation to respond to and let patterns emerge afterward. This may lower the effort of interpreting an abstract trait question, searching for a pattern, and composing an explanation at the same time. It is not yet demonstrated to improve this app's validity, completion, or insight quality. The user's two prompts illustrate a method; they are not locked questionnaire wording or an instruction to reconstruct childhood memories.
