## Three distinct old claims that cannot be pooled

### 1. Historical six-rule AstroHD V1.4 known-case astronomical rarity

`tasks/scenario-owner-recovery-calibration-20260923/ASTROHD-V14-EXACT-CENTURY-RESULT-20260925.json` and `ASTROHD-V15-SURVEY-CAPACITY-AUDIT-20260925.md` describe a selected six-Boolean-astronomical-clause model in which the known owner's recorded instant produced the rare all-positive pattern. **These rules were selected using the known target** from a larger library. It is a potentially useful sparse development model, but the rare pattern does not validate unknown-person birth decoding.

### 2. Independently frozen clean V4.3 survey crosswalk test

`tasks/scenario-owner-recovery-calibration-20260923/V43-SURVEY-CROSSWALK-RESULT-20260923.md` used a frozen neutral translation of earlier survey evidence into 19 V4.3 mappings with zero contradictory mappings. The first-ranked interval was in **2013**, not the recorded 1985 birth. The best correct-local-date interval ranked **872**, while the interval containing the exact recorded birth instant ranked **1,562**. This is a recorded failure of exact birth-time recovery for that particular frozen scorer/crosswalk, not evidence that the answers were meaningless.

### 3. AstroHD V1.5 answer-conditioned five-domain pilot

`reference/research/astrohd_v15_behavioral_bridge.json`, `src/hdmatch/evaluation/astrohd_v15_decoder.py`, and `ASTROHD-V15-SURVEY-CAPACITY-AUDIT-20260925.md` retain the same six astronomical clauses but score only **five signed person-level domains**: sharing understanding; solitude/privacy; romantic connection; persuasion about shared plans; and entering roles through invitation. Only nine original v7 routes can directly support these five domains (G02, F0, G05, G06, R02, G12, G13, R05, G18). **None** of the six current record pairs absent from the old 79 is among those nine direct input routes. Thus the new content supplies no obvious new V1.5 input; if those five confirmed domain codes are unchanged, V1.5 scores and all candidate rankings remain mathematically identical. This is not a new live run of V1.5; the current primary record does not include a freshly participant-confirmed five-domain profile frozen under that model.

The V1.5 audit found 162 distinct six-rule score vectors across its five-domain nominal input space (161 nonabstaining). An answer-conditioned pilot can change scores when domain confirmations change, but five broad categories cannot be asserted to retain the full 79-question information. No out-of-sample birth-decoding validity was established by the live UI or the earlier known-target positive fixture.

## Practical conclusion

The current 84-turn record can be used for **measurement/coding reproducibility checks**, and its few additional answers might modestly refine interpretation of two already-supported reviewer findings. It cannot be called an independent birth match; it mostly reuses the old source. The six-rule AstroHD success and failed V4.3 survey crosswalk are results of **different models**, not inconsistent reruns of the same frozen test.

For a genuinely new test, freeze a richer source-to-target mapping and its scoring/universe before looking at the true chart ranking of untouched participants. Use separately consented birth-data analysis, preserve the old model as a fixed baseline and compare on paired identical participants. Do not alter the stored respondent's current profile after revealing their birth target and call that confirmation.
