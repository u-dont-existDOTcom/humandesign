# Additional natal astrology mapping audit: seven further Life Patterns gaps

**2026-10-18 · Research-only cross-tradition continuation · No new live questions or scored chart mappings**

## Outcome

The earlier chart-domain audit was a bounded check of Ptolemy, Lilly and one Varāhamihira chapter. It found 92 documentary source entries, 46 measured-or-missing human domains and 16 optional survey proposals. It did **not** justify claiming that every important astrology-relevant experience or life event had been inventoried.

This pass checks translated material in Vettius Valens, Dorotheus of Sidon and four more chapters of Varāhamihira's *Brihat Jataka* against the **same actual 79-question V7 scene bank, 25-question TF1 bank, eight EM experience items and 16 optional candidates**. It retains the previous 92/46/16 version unchanged. The new evidence yields **28 additional source-checked passages**, **seven further distinct candidate human-outcome domains** and seven carefully optional question proposals.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Documentary source entries | 92 | 28 | **120** |

Do **not** interpret 120 entries as 120 statistically independent rules. Multiple passages refer to the same topic and a planet-nature list is not a fully evaluated natal horoscope. Survey domain counts are construct-audit judgments, not psychometric estimates of how much the survey reliably measures.

### New domains that the original 46-domain inventory missed

| Priority for a first *optional* participant pilot | Domain | Why the existing questions don't already answer it | Distinct measurement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Actual learning history** | How people resolve a planning puzzle or practice a new skill does not reveal the actual subjects and crafts studied deeply over their lives. | Long-term subjects; formal, self-directed and practical learning; depth and timing. |
| **1** | **Anger persistence, forgiveness and repair** | A baseline mood question or response to an unreliable friend does not measure how long genuine anger persists or whether forgiveness differs from resumed contact and trust. | Typical duration, context dependence, reconciliation and renewed trust separately. |
| **2** | **Contentment versus desire for change** | Having a purpose, wanting recognition, or noticing meaning is not the same as being content in ordinary circumstances. A person can be content and strongly motivated. | **Two axes**: contentment and pursuit of change, plus contextual variation. |
| **2** | **Giving and receiving help** | The shared-meal and competing-commitment hypotheticals mainly ask what someone would do in one prompted situation, not their real pattern over time. | Support *given* and *accepted*; practical versus emotional; context. |
| **3 — sensitive** | **Childhood upbringing and caregiving arrangements** | Home/belonging and parental kinship do not establish actual changes in who raised the person, which household they lived in, or when that changed. | Stable/change/unknown/decline; approximate period; perceived importance if volunteered. |
| **4 — sensitive** | **Everyday privacy and self-disclosure** | Adjusting one's manner of speech, being sociable and trusting a friend do not measure how much of one's inner life one chooses to reveal. | Disclosure by relationship/subject, desire versus actual sharing, changes with trust. |
| **5 — likely *follow-up* rather than new topic** | **Ordinary sensory enjoyment** | Appreciation of art, playfulness, awe and sensory discomfort are not equivalent to how frequently everyday food, music, scent, textures or natural surroundings bring pleasure. | Domains enjoyed, whether sought out, frequency and normal strength. |

The usable, neutral questions and optional follow-ups are in **SEVEN-ADDITIONAL-OPTIONAL-QUESTIONS.md**. For first testing, prioritize the **four lower-burden, nonredundant** constructs (learning history, anger/repair, contentment, giving/receiving). The two private-history subjects require explicit topic selection; sensory enjoyment should normally be a conditional extension of the prior ART or PLAY question. Not a required 7-question supplement.

### What was already covered, and therefore does *not* need another question

The same texts also discuss arts/music, public rank/wealth, legal reasoning, care within a family, religious or divinatory activities, and life setbacks. Many of these are **already represented in the previous 46-domain ledger and its 16 optional candidate questions**, even if not in the live survey. They should refine those items rather than growing the interview indefinitely.

In particular, a Valens ninth-house passage has an explicit translator alternative: **divination/soothsaying versus finance/business**. This is a source ambiguity, *not* independent support for a new occult-ability prediction. Varāhamihira's translated twenty-first chapter records disagreements in traditional interpretation; preserve them rather than choosing the most convenient claimed direction.

### Cross-tradition source evidence and limits

- **Vettius Valens, *Anthologies*, Books I and II:** The preliminary Riley English translation's planetary-nature passages include Moon/nurture and household, Jupiter/beneficence and friendships, Mercury/education and reasoning, Venus/pleasure and music, and Saturn/secrecy. The older Saturn text uses stigmatizing language; legitimate contemporary privacy **must never be equated with deception**. The second-book house texts primarily reinforce public rank and material history already known. We audited three specific files at exact Git SHA (no copyrighted full text copied to humandesign), and counted section/subsection titles across Valens Books I–IX: **252 headings**, not 252 verified natal predicates.
- **Dorotheus, *Carmen Astrologicum*, Books I and II:** The available Pingree English rendering (from a later textual transmission) discusses birth-household/caregiver transitions, parent relationships, reasoning/law as a vocation, and beneficence with joint Jupiter–Venus or Jupiter–Mercury combinations. Some passages treat historical bondage or family loss. These are **not** a reason to probe participants about abuse, orphanhood or trauma.
- **Varāhamihira, *Brihat Jataka*, chapters 16, 17, 18, 21:** The online Sanskrit with secondary English material discusses contentment, generosity, different expressions of anger, forgiveness, family help, pleasure in everyday surroundings, written/math skills and potential childhood separation. Some verses combine unrelated claims (including bodily/medical labels). We record only the appropriate social and experiential topics and flag translation disagreements.

All **28 short textual anchors** were found in the nine retrieved source witnesses, with SHA-256 readback records in **SOURCE-READBACK-v2.json**. These are **documentary/translation checks**, not a claim that the astrological chart conditions actually predict the described traits. The original Greek or Sanskrit critical editions were not independently adjudicated, and chapter headings were not substituted for complete book reading.

## The chart-mapping error still matters

The older CF003 ten-planet target meanings mostly derive from Human Design role categories, **not** a faithful conditional translation of the classical natal rules. This audit reinforces the mismatch: for Valens, Mercury covers actual schooling, reasoning, trade and communication; Jupiter includes practical beneficence; Moon concerns nurture and household; and multiple-planet/house/aspect conditions affect the passage. These cannot be reduced to the current single-planet behavioral label without a separately justified, versioned chart predicate.

Adding seven more respondent questions **does not repair astrological prediction quality** by itself. It repairs the set of experiences we can *ask about*. Any source-specific chart-prediction evaluation must declare calculable natal features, the chosen outcome and comparison baseline **before** looking at an independent person's responses. Neither this audit nor the owner's already-seen answers support a new validated natal finding.

## Survey design next step

1. Keep the existing deployed interview and eight EM questions unchanged. Keep the prior 16 optional research candidates versioned independently.
2. Before asking a new topic, search the person's already submitted interviews for actual *equivalent* life-course evidence. A related scenario is not a reason to mark their experience already covered. Conversely, do not ask again if directly reported.
3. Offer an **optional topic chooser**, not all 23 items. For a first reversible development pilot, offer the four highest-value new topics *alongside*, not ahead of, relevant older options. No more than five optional topics in a session, with explicit **never / not relevant / unsure / prefer not to say**.
4. For subjective feelings, measure recurrence, typical strength and peak separately where relevant. For real events, ask for rough periods only if helpful and with confidence, never compel precise dates or traumatic details.
5. Only if independent participants find a question clear, useful and nonredundant should it become a candidate for the later GPT/Railway upgrade; **do not quietly append it to the existing locked/blind model**.

### Scope still open

This is a bounded additional *source-to-measurement pass*, not an exhaustive historical survey of all Valens, Dorotheus, entire *Brihat Jataka*, subsequent Persian/Arabic astrology, modern astrology, or all still-in-progress Lilly original pages. The Valens translation is preliminary; online Sanskrit passages may contain competing readings. There may be additional uncovered topics. A future systematic all-text scan with exact clause-level chart conditions is a separate larger task once the original book research lane supplies suitable accepted files.
