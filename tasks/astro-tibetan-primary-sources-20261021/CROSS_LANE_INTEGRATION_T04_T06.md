## Implications for Western/Chinese/Jyotish comparison

- Sixty-year profiles use animal-element cycles overlapping Chinese BaZi, but the classical Tibetan narrative emphasizes corresponding vitality elements. Do not count copied or shared traditions as independent successes.
- Year labels say male/female as year polarity, not automatically the native's sex. Preserve separate subject-sex clauses when present.
- The mother-dependent table requires maternal natal trigram or mewa and the child's elemental axes. Three maternal trigrams are not explicitly resolved by the scanned five-column table. Do not infer their mapping until independently sourced.
- Parent-year rotations require the father's birth-year data and are not birth-only predictions.
- Proposed average lifespan ages are source language, not a demographic sample or individually proven dates. The five natal-grade age categories are not mutually exclusive modern age ranges.
- Several entries offer two child counts, and some say 'few' or 'many' rather than a number. A single descendant is not necessarily a single biological child. Missing obstacle counts stay unknown, not zero.
- Source output classes (family, work, wealth, relationship, traits, health) overlap Western Lilly, Indian Jyotish and Chinese Zi Wei, but may use different actual input dependencies. Preserve a rule-family contribution and freeze any mappings to survey items before human data reveal.
- Erlewine's February-fourth solar-year Gua method must not be substituted for the Phugpa or Tsurphu Tibetan lunisolar year.
