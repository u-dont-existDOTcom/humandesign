**Files:** `SOURCE_CANDIDATES_B07.json` contains **24** newly source-located claims; `UNKNOWN_BIRTH_TIME_SOURCE_CROSSWALK_B07.json` groups **10** methodology/lineage comparisons across *Saravali*, Portuguese *Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra* and V. Subramanya Sastri's *Jataka Parijata* notes.

## The new research lane hidden in plain sight

*Sārāvalī* **Chapter 48.1** (PDF129) says a missing birth or conception time can be estimated from a chart cast at the **moment of an inquiry** (a horary/Prashna chart). Chapters **48–51** then provide extensive traits for each of 12 rising signs, **24 Hora half-signs, 36 decanates and 108 Navāṃśas**, totaling **180 nested sign/subdivision description cells**. These cells are related and must **not** be treated as 180 independent predictions. Some paragraphs contain dozens of heterogeneous and mutually qualifying descriptions (body, behaviors, family and wealth), making a flexible matching approach likely to give impressionistic success.

The actual inversions from Prashna to supposed natal data are in **Chapter 52** (PDF140–141):

- **52.1:** first versus second 15-degree half of horary ascendant → natal northern/southern solar course.
- **52.2–3:** planet occupying the horary ascendant, or ascendant-decanate lord if empty, → natal season; author provides **conditional substitution of season rulers** to solve contradictions.
- **52.4–9:** reconstruct natal Jupiter, Sun, Moon and ascendant from horary sign/degree and additional information. One option takes the strongest of horary ascendant/5th/9th; another uses the **body part touched by the querent at the moment of inquiry**.
- **52.10–11:** the querent's **standing, lying, sitting or rising** posture is mapped to first/fourth/seventh/tenth house from horary ascendant and proposed as natal Lagna.
- **52.12:** parent's assigned personal **name syllable lengths** and a physical **shadow-length measurement** are used to derive supposed natal lunar mansion.
- **52.13–15:** alternative multiplication by sign/planet factors, separate divisors for season, day and time; adjustment of **plus/minus nine** is permitted when output is "anomalous".
- **52.16:** text **acknowledges ambiguity in the presented earlier schools** and recommends professional judgement. This is a source-origin uncertainty flag rather than an excuse to replace an unsuccessful result with a more flattering solution.
- **52.17–21:** a separate horary **Moon-based** inversion family with alternate correction counts.

These are **not** simply unknown-birth-time versions of a forward natal chart. They consume *different types of data*: question moment and location; approximate age; name and language; physical shadow; posture or touched body part. If those inputs are observed in the person and then reused to score a person's personality/experience, the test is no longer blind forward prediction. Repeatability at different question times must be measured.

## A second book describes strikingly parallel calculations

The supplied Portuguese *Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra* section **"Horário Desconhecido," lines 5492–5506** also answers what to do if year, season, lunar day, star, ascendant or birth time is unknown:

- **5494–5498:** unknown quantities are derived from horary ascendant and its D12/Hora/decanate divisions; planet-based seasonal rulers correspond closely to those of Saravali 52.
- **5498:** when solar course and derived season contradict, a defined exchange of planetary rulers is allowed, also seen in Saravali.
- **5502:** because Jupiter returns approximately every twelve years, the person’s **approximate age** is explicitly used to choose between candidate years. The method therefore does *not* infer everything from celestial geometry alone.
- **5504–5506:** another solar-longitude difference and relative sunrise calculation is said to yield *iṣṭakāla* (elapsed time).

The substantive overlap is documented in `UNKNOWN_BIRTH_TIME_SOURCE_CROSSWALK_B07.json`. This is **textual parallelism**, not two independent sets of successful birth-date recoveries. The Portuguese material was rendered from English and does not provide ancient original Sanskrit/verse numbers in these paragraphs.

## *Jātaka Pārijāta*: a third source layer with modern editor contamination risk

`Jātaka Pārijāta` **Adhyaya II, sloka23**, scanned printed pp36–37 / PDF454–455, sets out the rulers of the six Indian seasons by planetary decanates. The **Sastri note on PDF455** then describes using the ascendant-occupying planet or decanate lord to decide an unknown person's birth season, paralleling the other traditions.

But Sastri also adds **his own illustrative Western-style progression/aspect example** when explaining seasons (PDF455–456). That belongs to the **editorial notes**, not the printed Sanskrit sloka or proof that Vaidyanatha explicitly taught the same progression system. The family crosswalk records this contamination risk.

## Consequences for the project's empirical testing

1. **Separate two test questions:** natal chart → concealed human responses; and time-of-inquiry chart plus permitted live observations → concealed birth parameters. The second cannot be used to pad the first system's score.
2. Require a **fixed question-time protocol**; letting an astrologer pick when to ask may introduce outcome selection.
3. **Freeze the exact method** for each result and pre-register which variable it predicts: season, lunar star, rising sign, approximate year, or minutes. Unknown/unresolved predictions remain unknown.
4. Preserve each formula's counterexamples, correction attempts and **post-hoc freedoms** (±9, alternate rising/Moon methods, season exchange); require all candidate predictions to be counted when evaluating performance.
5. Baselines must use realistic season/sign/day-length distribution and the participant's supplied approximate age, not an artificially weak uniform null when latitude, calendar and age narrow the candidate universe.
6. **Privacy:** actual birth records are the answer key and must remain outside the source-reader's file corpus until evaluation; no name/posture/live-observation artifacts in the public research branch.

## Why this matters beyond the immediate books

We now know the source tradition explicitly proposes ways to cope with imprecise or absent natal data, but those methods consume additional participant/horary information. A researcher cannot claim full Jyotish predictive power without cataloguing these, and cannot claim a blind birth-record recovery if the birth truth was used to select among them.

**B07 adds 24 source-only notes, bringing this reader from 129 to 153.** The next source frontier is broader non-spiritual chapter coverage and original-language verification of the historic methods. Do not merge into the other reader branch or modify the live questionnaire/model without a distinct approved experimental freeze.
