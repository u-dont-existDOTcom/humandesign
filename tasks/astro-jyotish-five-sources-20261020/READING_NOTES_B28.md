## Why the opening provenance matters

**Chapter 1.2–5, physical PDF5** explicitly says the author Kalyāṇavarman expands Varāhamihira's condensed *Bṛhat Jātaka* on sign subdivisions, Raja Yoga, longevity, periods, etc., and selects teachings attributed to Yavanas. This directly supports a textual borrowing/dependency interpretation: a corresponding formula in *Sārāvalī*, *Jātaka Pārijāta* or a modern compilation cannot automatically be treated as three independently successful human predictions.

**Chapter 2.1–5, PDF5** uses *horā* in different contexts: etymologically `ahorātra` with first/last parts removed, a sign's half, Lagna, and broad natal divination doctrine. A program that equates the Sanskrit word "horā" everywhere with one D2 half-sign operation would misread this source.

## Severe childhood claims are not medical evidence, and the text supplies its own counterrules

**Chapter 10, PDF22–26**, distinguishes supposed predetermined, accidental and yoga-derived dangers (10.1–3). Many historical passages confidently assign specific fatal outcomes and dates to children and relatives, none independently validated here and none clinically actionable. The translator itself records alternate ten- versus sixteen-year claims for the *same Rahu configuration* in 10.14–15. A later **10.77–82, PDF25**, switches to a chart cast **when illness begins**, not simply a natal chart; those are distinct inputs, impossible to score as natal-forward predictions without an independent recorded onset time. Other instructions mention birth-time meteor/wind omens not captured by ephemeris alone. The **"Notes" on Marana Pada after 10.104, PDF26**, distinguish a simple eighth-house interpretation from the projected eighth-house pada; this editor note cannot settle ancient source semantics.

**Chapter 11, PDF26–27**, immediately follows with Moon-related cancellation formulas: lunar fullness/brightness, own/exalted/friendly varga, benefic aspects and strength are said to suppress prior adverse conditions. The explanatory metaphors invoking historical medical mixtures do **not** constitute validated health remedies. If a traditional method were ever tested under ethics review, it would have to freeze both the Chapter 10 adverse premises and Chapter 11 positive exception/priority rules before seeing cases—not reserve cancellations only for apparent missed forecasts.

## The solar yoga chapter has clear computational antecedents and broad outcomes

**Chapter 14.1, PDF30:** Veśī=other-than-Moon planet in 2nd from Sun; Vāsī=in 12th; Ubhayacārī=qualifying bodies in **both** 2nd and 12th. The explanatory translation excludes lunar nodes. **14.2–9, PDF30–31** then assigns different results depending on which of Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter or Saturn causes the Sunapha-like solar yoga; 14.10 requires source-specific strength, rāśi and aṃśa before declaring outcomes. This provides actual computable features but the descriptive lists contain many common/vague social and character terms. No natal scoring, statistical validation or physical feasibility enumeration was performed.
