Compared the actual user's 150-page English Saravali PDF **p110** with the independently accessible Sanskrit printing catalogued as **Kalyanavarman, Pandurang Javji 1928**, scanned/reprinted with later front matter by Jain Quantum: [Sanskrit printed page174](https://jainqq.org/explore/009887/174), [page175](https://jainqq.org/explore/009887/175), [book transcript](https://jainqq.org/booktext/Saravali/009887). **Two concrete inconsistencies affect any proposed questionnaire:**

- English PDF p110 bilious-dream paragraph (labelled Ch38.18–19) says the person dreams of **red lotus, eunuch, blood and lightning**. The printed Sanskrit Ch37.25 (scan175) instead writes `रक्ताब्जषण्ड` = a grove/cluster of red lotuses, supported by the second Sanskrit transcription; the separately translated word 'eunuch' is a likely erroneous split of `ṣaṇḍa` from the red-lotus compound. Do **not** add 'dreamed of eunuchs' as a classical prediction without source-critical resolution.
- English PDF p110 phlegmatic-dream paragraph (Ch38.20–21) says **coral stones**. Printed Sanskrit Ch37.27 (scan175) reads `मुक्ताफलप्रकर` = heaps/groups of **pearls**, also present in the online Sanskrit text and independent modern English rendering. Coral vs pearls is a concrete materially different outcome. Both variants remain documented; never silently recode the uploaded text.

There is also **chapter/verse renumbering and rearrangement**: original scanned Sanskrit heading explicitly labels Chapter **37**, which includes the five-great-person and natural-constituent material together. Its **37.11** corresponds to uploaded English **38.5** (five elements); Sanskrit **37.22–23** = English **38.16–17** (wind/flying), **37.24–25** = English **38.18–19** (pitta gold/fire), **37.26–27** = English **38.20–21** (kapha water/pearls). ## Independent domain knowledge: an Ayurveda dream theory predates this analysis

Two narrative/literature reviews identify **almost exactly the same triad in Ayurvedic prakriti theory**, without relying on a natal chart's planetary-strength selector:

2. M. Preethishree and P. The article cites multiple named Ayurvedic sources.

These articles **compile/interpret historical claims**. They do not establish that a person with a given Ayurvedic type actually dreams more frequently of those objects, and do not show that planets predict the dreams. The similarity **does not prove direct textual borrowing** by Saravali, because chronology/lineage and original passages across Sanskrit manuscripts have not been collated. It does show an existing **non-astrological explanatory tradition** that a rigorous astrology comparison should control for.

## Consequences for source-based experimental selection

A meaningful *conditional* comparative experiment would measure three different sources of information:

- **Planetary natal chart→temperament assignment→predeclared dream categories**, with independent astronomy, exact formula and tie handling.
- **Non-astrological Ayurvedic prakriti descriptions→dream categories**, using a frozen questionnaire classifier rather than treating such categories as scientifically proven body types.
- **Empirical baselines for dream motifs and recall**, taking into account age, cultural priming, sleep duration, repetition and participant description, using prospectively collected dream diaries.

Use blind preregistered prediction hashes, person-level splits, matched decoys and joint proper scoring. Even a positive dream-association comparison cannot be called evidence of astrology until chart-derived predictions outperform both ordinary nonastrological baselines and a same-person comparison blinded to chart labels. **Do not start this live experiment or change the survey** based on a source-audit packet.

The domain itself is legitimate: *Saravali* speaks about literal dream content, while the other source books also distinguish dreams in weak planetary dashas. But textual duplicates, alternate interpretations and the shared Ayurveda motif must not multiply a handful of observed dreams into spurious positive astrological confirmations.
