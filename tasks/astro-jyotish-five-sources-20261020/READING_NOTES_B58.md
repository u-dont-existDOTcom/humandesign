## Positive and negative outcomes: distinct observable claims

- **29.8–15 (eleventh from ārūḍha):** The text describes gains with planetary conjunctions/aspects at the eleventh, morally characterized **means** of acquisition, mixed influences, status/rulership when all planets aspect it (v11), and gain without obstacles when eleventh receives aspects but **twelfth does not** (v12). Vv13–15 add strength, argalā and favorable conjunction conditions: they do not authorize granting credit for mere presence of one planet without its specified clauses. Richness, lawful/unlawful acquisition, happiness, and political position are four different claims and must not be retrospectively merged.
- **29.16–21 (twelfth from ārūḍha):** Separates amounts of spending from claimed *causes* and from the actor/source of spending—e.g., royal/government attribution v18, relatives or quarrels v19, self-resources v20, and siblings v21. These are historical assertions, **not** evidence of real wrongdoing or an acceptable basis for financial decisions. V17 explicitly preserves **mixed** conditions; a mixture must never be counted simultaneously as an independent good and bad success.
- **29.23–26 (seventh from ārūḍha):** Includes historical claims of illness/aging from nodes, and wealth/good reputation under selected planetary circumstances; health-related predictions are archival only. Verse25 specifically allows **one**, two or three of Moon/Venus/Jupiter rather than always requiring all three; verse26 still requires exalted placement. Common and precise conditions must be distinguished.
- **29.28–36:** Strong benefics in second, financial consequences involving Dāra-pada A7 and A1, relationship friendship versus enmity in seventh house versus 6th/8th/12th, and separately political status. A7 in a trika cannot be relabeled a positive relationship forecast because a different passage describes status via trines. Sociopolitical status and relationship quality are different targets, and sign-based A1/A7 depend on B57's **unresolved source version**.

## Three explicit transfer mechanisms, plus one extension

A reader who treats every line in this chapter as a distinct freely activatable rule can manufacture apparent predictive successes without learning anything from people. The Sanskrit itself contains transfer instructions, not four fresh independent sources:

1. **29.22: twelfth-to-eleventh** — planetary combinations described for expenditure from the 12th become the basis for gain when *that configuration is in the eleventh*. This is a transfer of condition, **not** two simultaneous hits for one planet in the 12th.
2. **29.27: seventh-to-second** — combinations described in the 7th relative to ārūḍha are to be considered in the **second** relative to it. Does not prove two independent outcomes for same chart. Health or harmful allegations remain excluded in both positions.
3. **29.29: ārūḍha-to-kārakāṃśa with an explicit exception** — the source applies earlier ārūḍha combinations to kārakāṃśa **excluding relatives** (`bāndhava-varjitāḥ`). Ignoring this clause would make spurious family predictions. Kārakāṃśa itself requires exact Atmakāraka and Navāṃśa scheme not established for production.
4. **29.35: A1/A7 to other padas** — extends favorable/unfavorable relations to other padas such as children. The derived source family should retain ancestry/provenance and never count as independent confirmation merely because the same traditional source repeated an inference with a new relative.

The negative/positive relationship conditions in **29.34** divide the 12 relative house possibilities into **positive** {1,4,5,7,9,10}, **negative** {6,8,12} and **unspecified** {2,3,11}, when using standard whole-sign kendras/trines/trikas. However **29.36** separately includes 3rd/11th as *rulership* configurations, **not** friendship in 29.34. Those two claims have different target types; the apparent missing 3/11 classifications in 29.34 are **not** automatically remedied by 29.36. Further detailed grammatical collations of 29.31–36 remain open; the modern site's paraphrase alone is insufficient for a source-canonical forecast.
