## The key source-critical separation

- Ch 24.62 (sixth lord in second) uses **paradeśī**, describing life away from one's familiar land/region; **not** citizenship, a legal boundary, or indefinite residence. B42-001 retains other conjunct descriptions (happiness, oratory).
- Ch 24.70 (sixth lord in tenth) uses **videśe ca sukhī**, being happy in a foreign place. It does **not** explicitly require permanent emigration. B42-002 remains a separate happiness-context claim.
- Ch 24.126 (eleventh lord in sixth) uses **pravāsī**, one living away from home, together with hostile circumstances/illness. The negative context remains attached; B42-003 cannot become a positive blanket 'foreign success' rule.
- Ch 11.11 assigns **pravāsa** (absence/foreign residence) to tenth-house signification alongside career, honor, father and debt. It is a domain map, not a deterministic natal event for every tenth-house connection.

These Sanskrit terms and contextual conditions **cannot all be mapped to an automatic international migration/citizenship endpoint**. The contemporary 'BIG 3 LF' citizenship/infallible-soon interpretation is an attributable modern commentator extension, **not** the BPHS primary text. See the precise source in the JSON; do not import that interpretation as classical consensus.

## Mandatory source qualifiers otherwise lost

- Ch 24.145 explicitly directs considering each house lord's **strength and weakness**. The VedicPupil page currently displays only the second hemistich for v145, while siva.sh prints both; this is a **web transcription completeness discrepancy**, not an established Sanskrit recension difference.
- Ch 24.146 tells the reader to reconcile **two sign-lordships** and describes cancellation of equally opposing results.
- Ch 24.147–148 additionally gives full, half and quarter **effect strengths**. These are symbolic traditional assertions, **not** measured hit rates, calibrated probabilities or a license to invent prediction weights.

## A new period-specific counterexample family

- Ch 53.61 describes positive **house and land gains** for a favorably placed Venus sub-period of the Moon major period.
- Ch 53.62 changes the configuration (Venus in 6th/8th/12th relative to major-period lord and/or associated with malefics) and includes distress from **foreign residence**. The displayed English translates the situation as 'deportation'; the Sanskrit compound **videśa-vāsa-duḥkha** does not itself specify a government-ordered deportation.
