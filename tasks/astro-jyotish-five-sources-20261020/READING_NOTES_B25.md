## Critical finding 1: many apparent 'independent' source rules are known borrowings

Sastri's printed introductory notes explicitly credit **Bṛhat Jātaka** for:
- Adhyāya **III**: **46 of 80** verses, listing verse ranges (PDF494 / printed96).
- Adhyāya **IV**: verses **17–18, 32–39, 99** (PDF571 / printed173).
- Adhyāya **V**: verses **7, 9, 11 and 17–21** (PDF631 / printed233).
- Adhyāya **VI**: verses **48,49,50,52,99,100** (PDF61 / printed319).

These are **Sastri's documentary attributions**, not proof that we have critically collated manuscripts of both works or observed independent predictions. Agreement with an earlier *Bṛhat Jātaka* formulation must carry a **dependency edge**, not add a second unit of predictive support. Other Sastri chapter endnotes import *Karaka-bharaṇa* timing rules **after Jātaka IX closes** (PDF390), which must be labeled editor-added.

## Finding 2: nonhuman births, horary and childbirth-location claims require extra inputs

III.1, PDF494–495, describes an animal/plant life-class prediction based on Moon's **D12 (dvādaśāṃśa)** and strong malefics, not the D9 normally used for natal subdivisions. Sastri **explicitly warns** the early chapter's term `aṃśa` means **D12 rather than D9** and describes broader horary/lost-animal/cultivation uses. The latter are **the commentator's additional use cases**, not all direct wording of the original first verse.

III.66 (PDF550/printed162) distinguishes house of father, distant birth location, outbuilding and house of maternal grandfather based on strongest birth Lagna rāśi versus its navāṃśa, with a vargottama exception. That is **observable birth location** only when certified natal place/family setting can be independently checked; no permission to infer private family structure.

IV.1 (PDF571/printed173) says **children's lifespans cannot reliably be determined until age twelve**, even though the next verses include specific child and parental mortality claims. The source itself acknowledges a severe accuracy boundary. IV.17 (PDF578–579) gives symbolic planet rulers of successive gestational months and historical prenatal-development assumptions; **not current embryology or medical recommendations**.

## Finding 3: no single ‘canonical longevity formula’

V.1 (PDF631/printed233) lists **eight** different named `āyurdaya` approaches. V.2 allocates fixed natural lifetimes of **20, 1, 2, 9, 18, 20, 50** years to Sun–Saturn respectively (sum 120) under one natural-period convention. Other methods combine elapsed longitude/planetary strength differently. This does not imply any real person's longevity is predictable, and source precedence among eight methods remains underdetermined.

## Finding 4: explicit inverse yogas to positive rulership claims

VI.1 (PDF61/printed319) opens with four families that **undermine Rāja Yogas**: `Bhaṅga`, `Reka`, `Preṣya` and `Kemadruma`, associated by the translated text with loss of kingship, poverty or dependence. Future models must include **the cancellation logic**, not merely the abundant royal-yoga promises. The social labels and moral characterizations are historical, not valid diagnoses.

VII.1 (PDF114/printed372) gives extraordinary political-power configurations. Sastri's **Notes** explicitly attach expanded definitions from other texts, rather than silently showing them as part of the original verse. VII.137 (PDF200) supplies *Chāmara Yoga* with a broad **philosopher OR ruler** outcome, whose looseness is a pre-test specificity issue. It has no held-out human success record.

VIII.9 (PDF245) assigns varied life narratives to different three-body conjunctions, overlapping the *Sārāvalī* conjunction catalog whose textual errors we corrected in B15. VIII.115 (PDF323) then specifies **D9/dignity-strength** modifiers and reports a variant in a Sastri note, so an unconditional match on planets in one sign is not a complete source-faithful prediction.

## Finding 5: time almanac as separate chart inputs, not independent personality evidence

IX.1 (PDF325) announces **Gulika** and effects of birth-year, month, weekday and hour. IX.10–30 (PDF331–341) links descriptions to the traditional **60-year saṃvatsara** cycle—not simple Gregorian birth years. IX.31–39 (PDF346–350) describes lunar month, fortnight, tithi and weekday; these variables can be highly correlated and produce overlapping trait bundles. A frozen **calendrical astronomy engine** is needed to compute the right labels for the original epoch/timezone. Counting each label's vaguely fitting adjective as independent prediction would inflate scores.

The closing **Sastri Notes**, PDF390/printed648, quote another text `Karaka-bharaṇa` for **when** period-specific year/month/tithi/weekday results occur. Those statements are not automatically original Vaidyanātha Adhyāya IX rules.
