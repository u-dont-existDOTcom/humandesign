## Relocation is not a single generic personality label

**XI.23, physical PDF729/printed719:** the condition uses the **lord of the twelfth sign from the sign occupied by Lagna lord**. It purports to predict going to another country if this lord is inimical/debilitated/weak, and **settling there permanently** if that lord receives a favorable Venus aspect. The same line qualifies the nature/size of destination (small township under solar obscuration, prosperous municipality under source-specified strength). Place of residence, duration/permanence and destination size are three separable externally observable endpoints. Do not accept any international holiday as confirming permanent emigration.

**XI.24, same page:** favorably located 12th-from-Lagna-lord with kendra/trikona benefic conditions is linked to an agreeable foreign sojourn; certain other relative positions and hostility reverse the interpretation toward **remaining in one's home country**. This is not two independent forecasts when the same person matches both positive and negative optional clauses. The stronger condition and exceptions must be frozen together.

**XI.25, physical PDF729–730/printed719–720:** **movable ascendant, movable lagna-lord placement and movable-sign aspects** are associated with wealth in a foreign country, whereas analogous fixed signs signify wealth in native country. Sastri adds a note alleging parallel text in another work; the title is unclear in this scan, so no attribution is invented. A source model must distinguish (a) actual migration, (b) which country is financially productive, and (c) having strong assets at all.

**XVII.82–83, PDF1004/printed994:** a *Kālacakra Daśā* period under specified movable sign and D9/ruler conditions is claimed to trigger foreign relocation **during that period**, and the next verse links the foreign stay to the **length of that period**. If only one of two movable-sign requirements holds, the text says relative strength determines the result. These are substantially more precise claims than lifetime travel preference, but their trial requires a verifiable original daśā calendar, destination/departure dates, fixed effect-duration error windows, and explicit conflict resolution. No such prospective run occurred.

## Wealth, literal debt and treasure are different outcomes

- **XI.61, PDF742–743:** weakness of the second lord and an unaspected-by-benefics Lagna implies loss of wealth.
- **XI.62, PDF743:** particular Lagna/second/eleventh lord arrangements supposedly indicate *finding treasure* or valuable property. Source footnote records a different reading. Rare unexpected acquisition differs from ordinary income, home purchase or inheritance.
- **XI.64, PDF743:** an obscured/debilitated second lord occupying a malefic **D60** division is associated with being burdened by debt. D60 requires precision and documented birth-time uncertainty; financial liabilities versus income require separate ground truth, not a broad 'financial hardship' proxy.
- **XIV.68, PDF890/printed880:** four or five favorable bodies in ninth, with ninth lord and multiple dignity/varga requirements, are alleged to grant high-status prosperity. The verse explicitly differentiates *fortune in native country* under some varga statuses from *fortune abroad* otherwise. This is a long, conjunctively rare formula with an opportunity confound, not a universal ninth-house predictor.

These examples establish **what the historical books claim to predict**, not whether astrology does predict it. Study design can preregister migration timing, domestic/foreign assets, residence permanence and liability events separately; the source should not be allowed to score any career success, any journey, or any inheritance as equivalent confirmations.

## Remaining gates

The sources may share older copied verses, precise D9/D60 and movable/fixed whole-sign calculations must be defined, and cultural/social determinants of migration are large. A future test must precommit predictions before receiving people's histories, use outcome-wise baselines (age, nationality, opportunity, education, finances), count negative counterparts and abstentions, and never access participant answers during rule selection.
