## Confirmed agreements and material differences

**Primary offsets agree** between Rath Lesson 17 and BPHS 31.3: from the reference sign or planet, support comes from 2nd, 4th, 11th (opposition in 12th, 10th, 3rd respectively). Both include a 5th vs 9th supplementary pair. Neither book has independently predicted a modern person's outcome in this batch.

**The 3rd-house malefic clause is *not* a simple universally frozen rule.** Rath PDF361–364 describes a special third-house argalā from malefics, then declares it unobstructed; the Portuguese BPHS chapter says specifically **three or more** malefics for *viparīta argalā*. The independently presented Venkateśa commentary on the short Jaimini sutra offers two readings: a malefic third-house influence triggers a form of argalā, but **one or two** malefics can be obstructed by planets in the eleventh, whereas **many** are unobstructed. The visible BPHS Sanskrit verse 31.4 (`tṛtīye vyādhikāḥ pāpāḥ`) **does not explicitly contain a written numeral three**. Its exact grammatical force requires an edition-aware Sanskrit adjudication: do not attribute the integer threshold to the ancient verse merely because a later English/Portuguese summary reports it. Rath and Venkateśa also disagree as to whether the special-third obstruction can ever apply. Synthetic 1–2-malefic cases separate the versions sharply.

**Node reversal is source-specific.** Rath cites *Jaimini* `viparītaṃ ketoḥ` (PDF370–371), explicitly naming **Ketu**; his algorithm reverses Ketu's counting, not automatically Rāhu's. Kerr's BPHS Portuguese renders reversed counting for **both** Rāhu and Ketu. The electronic Sanskrit BPHS 31.6 has `tamograhabhavā ... vyatyayāj`, a shadow-planet expression not explicit individual Rahu/Ketu proper names. It supports a different interpretive scope but is not an independently checked manuscript reading. Running a Rahu case under both methods yields different whole-sign positions.

**Ties and quarters cannot be guessed.** Rath PDF363 says equal planetary counts can cause *bandhana* (stagnation) and elaborates extra strength/benefic rules. BPHS 31.4 merely makes **weaker or fewer** obstructors ineffective and does not explicitly confer a universal equal-count prediction. Rath PDF369–370 reads BPHS31.10's opposing 7°30′ quarters through a **first/seventh-house axis** illustration; Kerr's translation calls it a general quarter rule for obstructing signs. Applicability to only the 1st/7th versus every ordinary argalā pair remains unresolved. The arithmetic pairings are still testable without choosing a doctrinal winner.

**Rath's eighth-house supplementary argalā is not explicitly in the compared sutra.** PDF366 includes eighth-to-sixth as another secondary pairing in his commentary; Jaimini's `prāgvat trikoṇe` and BPHS31.6's fifth vs ninth do not themselves name this additional 8th/6th calculation. Preserve as Rath's editorial method until stronger verse evidence appears.

**Sūtra numbering varies.** Rath's special-third appears at 1.1.5; Sūryanārāyaṇa Rao's version calls the same short text sutra 1.1.6. Numbering of further examples is offset locally, but do not propagate an unverified global -1 or +1 rule; align actual Devanāgarī incipits.
