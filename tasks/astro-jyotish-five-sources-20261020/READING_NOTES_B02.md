## What the comparisons establish

A transcription of *Saravali* chapter 20 slokas 2–19 (English compilation PDF pp40–41) produces **47 listed conjunction clauses, only 37 unique unordered sets of planets**. Nine distinct planet sets occur more than once, for a total of ten redundant clause occurrences. Across all 37 unique sets, there are 88 strict subset→superset pairs. These are **structural counts of source-language clauses**, not prediction-success rates or independent corroborations.

Especially important: planets Sun+Moon+Mars+Mercury+Jupiter occur at *Saravali* XX.3, XX.8 and XX.17. The English rendering calls the corresponding person a mendicant, honored sage and sorrowful/unsuccessful ascetic respectively. An exact same five-planet set therefore has multiple narrative outcomes within the same chapter. These may encode context, text transmission or genuine ambiguity. A programmer cannot resolve them by using the owner's known spiritual biography as a tiebreaker. See `SARAVALI_CH20_CONJUNCTION_AUDIT_V1.json`.

For a second comparison, Jataka Parijata **Adhyaya XV slokas 26–39** (scan PDF pp918–922, printed908–912) were aligned by consecutive thematic structure with *Saravali* **XX slokas 6–19** (PDF pp41–42). Of 37 transcribed Jataka clauses, **28 share the exact same normalized planet set** within the aligned corresponding verse. **Nine do not**. Seven corresponding verse pairs have identical sets (Jataka XV.27, 28, 32, 33, 34, 35, 38). This reveals a shared textual tradition and meaningful variations, **not** two independent sets of successful predictions. See `JP_SARAVALI_CONJUNCTION_CROSSWALK_V1.json`.

Notable nonidentities in the supplied English texts:

- Jataka XV.26 differs from Saravali XX.6 in its first conjunction group: Jataka has Sun+Moon+Mars+Jupiter+Saturn, while Saravali has Mars+Mercury+Jupiter+Saturn. The other two groups in this pair match.
- Jataka XV.29 includes one group with Venus where Saravali XX.9 repeats the Saturn-containing six-planet group twice in different word order.
- Jataka XV.36 lists **five** distinct groups against Saravali XX.16's **four**; three matched groups are shared, two extra Jataka groups and one Saravali-only group require Sanskrit manuscript comparison.
- Jataka XV.37's third group has **Saturn**, where Saravali XX.17's third group has **Moon** (all other members being Sun/Mars/Mercury/Jupiter). Both traditions contain favorable and unfavorable distinctions, so this is a potential consequential textual difference.
- Jataka XV.39 and Saravali XX.19 share **zero exact sets** in their respective two-clause formulations. Under a flexible "subset" reading they may overlap, but treating a five-planet subset as equal to a six-planet conjunction would be a new modeling assumption.

These comparisons use **English renditions and the supplied scans**, not verified original Sanskrit editions. The Jataka original opening expressly names Saravali as a source for abridgment (I.3, PDF p8). We must not report 28 matches as evidence of physical causation or statistically independent verification.

## Three distinct targets often conflated as 'spirituality'

The page-image checks (rendered directly from supplied Jataka Parijata PDF pp917, 918, 923) confirm three materially separate text claims:

1. **Founding a philosophical system** (Jataka XV.23, original print907/PDF917): a highly specific externally observable leadership claim, conditioned on Jupiter ninth house and Saturn aspecting Jupiter/Moon/ascendant with Raja Yoga. A separate clause predicts joining a holy order **before** becoming a ruler. The chronology is part of the claim.
2. **Emancipation** (Jataka XV.25, original print908/PDF918): a metaphysical outcome attributed to a tenth-house-lord configuration. This should not be scored as demonstrably achieved simply from a biographical narrative or self-ascribed awakening. One can score a preregistered statement or behavior if explicitly declared as such, but that is **not identical** to confirming emancipation itself.
3. **Observable ascetic life** (same Jataka XV.25): a *different* four-planets-in-tenth conjunction rule. This can potentially be assessed as actual residence/practice/renunciation with dates, provided culture and ambiguous orders are operationally defined and controls for base rates are specified.

Other historical distinctions: *Saravali* XX.20–21 and *Jataka* XV.42 describe failure to enter or remain in an order under qualifications such as combustion/defeat; *Jataka* XV.41 alleges apostasy with certain nodes/amsa conditions. These are **negative predictions to be retained**, not to discard after observing a spiritual narrative. Attribution is restricted to the historical texts; none is a present-day clinical or moral diagnosis.

## Consequences for a reproducible astrology model

- Use a **dependency graph** so verbatim or formula-family duplicates add zero independent confirmatory weight. A source majority of three texts is not a real-human outcome.
- Define what exact "together" or conjunction means before calculating charts: same sign? same house? within degree threshold? It materially changes prevalence.
- Keep each conjunction's predicted target narrow: initiation vs desire/abandonment vs forest asceticism vs ruler-then-renunciate vs philosophical school founding.
- Treat a combination's overlap with a larger combination as a model conflict/interaction needing fixed precedence, not permission to count both as separate hits.
- Predeclare conflict resolution for Saravali XX.3/8/17 and for cross-translation JP/Saravali variants **before** inspecting test participants. Freeze variants as genuinely different proposed models if no source-critical resolution exists.
- Do not use ancient spiritual or medical outcome assertions as medical advice or factual moral attributions to real people.
- No source in this limited B01–B02 reading has yet supplied a **specific, testable original formula** for everyday surprise, awe, synchronicities, sudden realization frequency, or feeling guided by a higher power. Do not invent it merely to fill questionnaire coverage.
