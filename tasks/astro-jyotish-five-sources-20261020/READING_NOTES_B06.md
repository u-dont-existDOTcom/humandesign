# JYOT B06 — Dream content and subjective versus outward dasha outcomes (21 October 2026)

**Scope:** 16 source-only candidates from the original five uploads, with *Sārāvalī* PDF60/110/118 and *Sarvārtha Cintāmaṇi* PDF59/368 rendered and visually inspected, plus *Jātaka Pārijāta* PDF716. Supporting source locators and publication-status flags are in `SOURCE_CANDIDATES_B06.json`; explicitly excluded search hits in `TERMINOLOGY_TRIAGE_B06.json`. **Zero independent human forecasts; zero rule promotions.**

## Finding 1: The source texts claim not only meditation and initiation, but *specific dream motifs*

`Sārāvalī` Chapter **38**, especially slokas **5** (PDF109) and **16–21** (PDF110), links elemental and humoral disposition to dream content:

- The five-element classification is associated with the relative strength of Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Mars and Mercury, with mixed strength leading to mixed temperament (**38.5**).
- Wind-dominant subjects are said to dream of **flying in the sky** (**38.16–17**).
- Bile/fiery temperament is said to correspond to dream imagery including **gold, sunlight, forest fire, red flowers, lightning, blood** (**38.18–19**).
- Phlegmatic/watery temperament is linked to dreams involving **sea, rivers, lakes, snow and aquatic objects** (**38.20–21**).

These are recognizably **sleep-dream phenomenology claims**, not rhetorical uses of *dream*. The exact translated PDF110 text and headings have been visually checked; the source of the English-only compilation and original Sanskrit remain uncollated. The classification procedure lacks an unambiguous implementation for several equally strong planets. That uncertainty cannot be resolved by examining a person's dream responses.

**Potential narrow study design (NOT a live protocol change):** independently compute/freeze the elemental/temperament type and exact dream-category predictions before access to outcomes, then compare 30-day prospective dream journals using predeclared coding for flight, fire/gold/lightning, water/snow, with matched decoy charts and a dream frequency/recall baseline. Absence of dream recall must remain *unobserved*, not proof of nonoccurrence. Treat all motifs within a category as a correlated family, not six independent confirmations.

## Finding 2: A different rule predicts *where* weak daśā effects manifest

`Sārāvalī` **41.74**, PDF118, says a weak planet's daśā produces its good effects in **dreams and thoughts** rather than actual external circumstances. **41.75**, same page, allocates first/middle/last part of a period by planetary sign's rising classification. Original PDF page 118 was visually checked and shows the chapter boundary **41→42** just below this pair.

An externally negative outcome plus a positive dream is not independently corroborated by that verse unless its predictive target (inner experience rather than outward event) was frozen **before** responses. Otherwise the rule offers an exceptionally flexible retrospective "rescue" interpretation. A claim about dream content from Chapter 38 cannot automatically be treated as independent support for Chapter 41's "dreams and thoughts" period rule.

## Finding 3: Nightmare conditions also occur in multiple texts

- `Sarvārtha Cintāmaṇi` **Chapter II**, tenth-house daśā/bhukti subsection verse **33**, PDF59/printed56: malefic subperiod of the tenth lord is linked with **bad dreams and major worry** along with other harmful outcomes. The PDF59 source shows original Sanskrit alongside J.N. Bhasin's English.
- Same book **Chapter XVI**, Venus-in-trine main period/malefic subperiod around v**8**, PDF368/printed365: **bad dreams** plus sorrow and social downfall. PDF368 source image checked. Translation/verse boundary still needs specialist collation.
- Supplied Portuguese `BPHS` text **line4499**: Saturn 8th or 12th from the main daśā lord is associated with **bad dreams** alongside family losses and mental distress. It is only a second-hand translated witness, not independently copied original Sanskrit.

These are conditional **nightmare phenomena with timed astrological triggers**, not independent observed accuracy and not a license to derive psychiatric or medical conclusions.

## Finding 4: Sources include *simpler* meditative practice signatures

`Sārāvalī` **26.17–18**, PDF60, associates **Mercury in Sagittarius** with practicing abstract meditation, writing, teaching and scholarly interests. This is far less conjunctively rare than the many 4–6-planet renunciation yogas already indexed, though it also has broad bundled outcomes and needs a fair base-rate and specificity analysis.

The supplied Portuguese `BPHS` text **line2264** similarly attributes **meditation/religious practice** to a **tenth-house Gulika** condition (different computation and baseline).

The supplied `BPHS` **line3612**, among Mercury's `Sabha` `avastha` outcomes, speaks of **complete enlightenment** when Mercury is also exalted. This is another source assertion about a metaphysical state, **not empirical evidence**. The word *Samadhi* appearing elsewhere in the same Portuguese paragraph is awkwardly attached to **neck/throat disease**, not a prediction of absorption. Counting that hit as supporting meditation would be a source-interpretation error.

## Finding 5: The text also contains afterlife/past-life predictions — different evidence class

`Jātaka Pārijāta` **Adhyaya V-A, slokas11 and15**, PDF714–716, uses specific decanates and stronger luminary rules to assign supposed **prior-birth realms** or a **postmortem destination**. Jupiter and Pisces conditions are said to indicate eventual release/bliss. Sastri's **PDF716 'NOTES'** distinguish `mokṣa` from heaven-like states, but that distinction is the translator's commentary and not all the verse's wording.

Past-life/postmortem ontology must **not** be graded as independently verified factual human outcomes, regardless of whether a participant endorses reincarnation or spiritual liberation. At most one can record a person's beliefs or descriptions without implying their literal cosmology is validated.

## Finding 6: An additional non-chart input can override otherwise positive royal yoga

`Sārāvalī` **39.11**, PDF111, includes meteors, extreme environmental disturbances or earthquakes at the time of birth as alleged cancellations of Raja Yogas. That is an **observational birth-environment omen**, generally not reconstructible from date/time/place alone. It is in scope for faithful historical documentation, but an automated natal chart parser cannot claim to implement the **full text** from astronomical coordinates alone. It must either exclude this extra-input class transparently or collect independent contemporaneous event records; it cannot assume an omen did or did not occur.

## Bounded negative lexical findings

`TERMINOLOGY_TRIAGE_B06.json` stores search terms and **14 explicit nonpredictive or commentary-only hits** to avoid mistaking keywords for verse evidence. Examples:

- **"visions"** as a substring of **"divisions"**, including texts about zodiacal subdivisions.
- **"synchronicity"-looking forms** in Sanjay Rath (PDF176 calendars synchronized, PDF236 limbs and brain coordination): *not* cosmic coincidences.
- Rath **PDF36** uses *revelation* while explaining grammar and knowledge, not birth-derived propensity for sudden insight; his dream accounts on PDF93/97 are religious biography, not forward natal test cases.
- `Sārāvalī` PDF110 mentions *mokṣa* only as an explanatory contrast with the three worldly ends; that particular word match is not a mokṣa-producing combination.
- `Sarvārtha Cintāmaṇi` PDFs33–34/244 are **Bhasin translator commentary** discussing Samādhi, unlike chapter VII's original Sanskrit verse-based rules already indexed.
- The phrase *No wonder* in `Sarvārtha Cintāmaṇi` PDF175 is rhetorical, not an instrument for evaluating the person's day-to-day sense of wonder.

This targeted English/PDF and Portuguese lexical screen is **not an exhaustive proof that traditional astrology lacks source formulas for awe, sudden realization or meaningful synchronicity**. Original Sanskrit may use differently translated or OCR-missed vocabulary. No specific classical formula for those three constructs was verified in this bounded screen.

## Evidence hierarchy and continuation

1. Preserve exactly what a printed ancient-language verse, a translated interpretation, and a modern commentary each asserts.
2. Preserve the *domain*: literal dream imagery, premonition belief, meditative practice, reported meditative absorption, postmortem assertions and waking-life unexpected events are not equivalent.
3. Demand source-credible computation and blind outcomes before any physical-causation/predictive-success claim; these are documentary annotations only.
4. Next reader frontier: continue systematic domain inventory across other chapters, especially planetary subperiod sequence and nonstandard astronomical/birth-event inputs; independently collate source-language of dream and subtle-state passages before freezing dream model variants.
