## The source itself contains a one-year threshold discrepancy

- Bhasin *Sarvārtha Cintāmaṇi*, Chapter X **verses2–3, physical PDF240**, says short life is below **32**, medium is **32 to 70**, long above 70 and the highest designation beyond 100.
- Chapter XII **verse1, physical PDF260**, instead announces medium life as **33 to 70**.
- The difference may reflect inclusive ordinal counting, an edition/translation problem or a substantive difference. It is not resolved merely because a canonical model expects a neat tripartition. Store both exact statements and an explicit **32-versus-33 unresolved boundary**, not a secretly adjusted threshold.

The same Chapter X **verse1** reports that some earlier authorities considered yoga rules adequate while others insisted planetary daśā periods were indispensable. The text is therefore **not one uncontested longevity calculator**.

## Explicit counterexamples and authorial limits

- Chapter X **verse4, PDF240**, says age below twelve makes life-span assessment uncertain/unreliable. Yet neighboring chapters enumerate very specific infant or young-person death outcomes. It is a source-internal calibration/conflict condition; no child-specific predictions should be issued.
- Chapter XI **vv1–10, PDF256–257**, enumerates supposed mitigating conjunctions and strength/aspects, including Jupiter in Lagna and strong benefic Moon.
- Chapter XI **v15, PDF258**, invokes rising Agastya and Seven Rishis as a cancellation condition—**an environmental/visible-sky prerequisite**, not exclusively calculable from seven natal planetary signs.
- Chapter XII **v26, PDF264**, explicitly calls yogas **innumerable** and says the work has selected traditional ones. This limits completeness claims.
- Chapter XII **vv32–33, PDF265**, quotes Yavanācārya and others treating political power or prosperity as implying long life, then contrasts Varāhamihira treating fortune and longevity separately. The author further points out **poor people can live long**, rejecting a naive monotonic wealth-to-lifespan relation.
- Chapter XII **vv65–76, physical PDF270–271**, offers life spans of **2,000, 10,000, 60,000 years, or a Yuga/Brahmā period** under special horoscopes. These are obviously not literal normal human biological lifespans and cannot be promoted as realistic modern longevity predictions. Whether some numbers are rhetorical/metaphysical would require an original Sanskrit comparison, not an assistant's assumption.
- A **Bhasin Translator's Note** after Chapter XII v18, **PDF263**, mixes *sunset* as anchor of the first planetary Hora with an example that counts *sunrise*. The notes are commentator source layer, and the reference epoch must not be silently repaired.

The chapter's claims are retained for textual/computational completeness only. No personal death, injury, clinical risk, or life expectancy should be inferred from them.

## What Rath's Jaimini teaching does—and does not—say

- **Lesson VII, PDF131:** modern philosophical exposition treats Lagna as attachment to identity, purpose, parents and soul. This is not a fully specified astronomical feature-to-question mapping.
- **Lesson VIII, PDF157:** Rath explicitly presents a sexagesimal/Yuga timing computation as his own work, claiming unprecedented year-level precision. This is **modern research speculation**, not the literal Jaimini Sutra, and cannot establish a chronology validated by historical observation.
- **Lesson XI, Sutra1.1.1, PDF235:** very short Sanskrit introduction to *Upadeśa* accompanies Rath's further claim that its syllables secretly invoke Viṣṇu. That is **Rath's creative hermeneutics**, not an unambiguous word-for-word translation.
- **Lesson XII, Sutra1.1.33, PDF243:** Rath teaches the Kāṭapayādi phoneme-numeral system as encoding references to houses, signs and Varnada across the entire corpus. The scope of that reading and any alternative decoding schools must be frozen before implementing a rule parser.

Neither a philosophical lesson, a birth-chart anecdote nor a textual conjecture can be counted as a successful blind forecast.
