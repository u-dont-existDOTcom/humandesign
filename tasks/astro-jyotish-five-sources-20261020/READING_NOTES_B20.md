## 1. The texts assign **observable timing and level of financial responsibility**, not just “wealthy”

*Sarvārtha Cintāmaṇi* Bhasin English:
- **Ch II verses114–116, PDF75** distinguish **ages 16, 20 and 30** as claimed onsets of prosperity, conditioned on different ascendant and navāṃśa-lord arrangements. To test this one must establish earnings/assets at dated life intervals; a subjective lifelong “wealthy” answer is not an adequate target.
- **Ch III verse1, PDF76**, includes education, speech, right eye and **dependents supported** among second-house domains. Bhasin's adjacent **TRANSLATOR’S NOTE** asserts *his* preference for formal education under second house rather than fourth/fifth. Original author listing education among second-house topics does not establish that exclusive interpretive school.
- **Ch III verses20–24, PDF80** lists livelihood difficulty, supporting 20 dependents, having authority over 300 or 1,000 people and protecting many, under different combinations. Responsibility for a workforce and one’s actual dependents must be distinct outcomes; social opportunity/scale and historical social hierarchy are obvious confounders.
- **Ch IV verses54–56, PDF115** attributes house acquisition and ease of obtaining property to fourth-house lords and dignity.
- **Ch VIII eleventh house vv2–10, PDF212–213**, asserts income magnitude and income-source direction, including explicit adverse cases. **Ch VIII twelfth vv1–7, PDF214–215** labels spending as legitimate/illegitimate with moral assumptions unsuitable as factual profiling.
- **Ch XV Venus period vv25–30, PDF320** assigns welfare, generosity and becoming known through writing to specified Venus-period placements. Known outcomes cannot be used to select favorable verse variants.

These are **documented assertions**, not evidence astrology accurately predicts assets, career or philanthropy.

## 2. Specialized study is a *separate domain* from intelligence or mysticism

*Jātaka Pārijāta*, Adhyāya XI vv81–86 **PDF750–751**, distinguishes mathematician, student of argumentation, grammarian, ontology student and expert in six traditional sciences, each under **different** astronomical/dignity conditions. The discipline chosen, degree of skill, and actual vocation must be coded separately; “intelligent” is a poor proxy. The same book, XI vv55–56 **PDF741**, offers different source-of-wealth family pathways (brother versus mother).

A useful source-critical boundary occurs in **XI.88, PDF751**: translator Sastri says the verse's **latter half is absent in all manuscripts available to him**. This authorial/transmission gap has *no authorized completion* from general astrological lore. `SOURCE_CANDIDATES_B20.json` marks it as incomplete.

Other distinctive *Jātaka* rules:
- **XVII.14–16, PDF985**, applies Deha/Jiva role in Kālacakra Daśā, with mixed benefic/malefic outcomes; the adjacent “Parāśara adds” statement is source attribution, not direct Vaidyanātha.
- **XVIII.6, PDF1017**, requires an **ascendant at the moment a daśā begins** in addition to the original natal chart, and gives a seven-varga condition. Sastri's NOTES after v6 explicitly say the next **five verses are taken from Śrīpati Paddhati**—textual agreement between editions is not independent evidence.
- **XVIII.22–23, PDF1025**, physically rendered: worship of a planet’s associated deity, religious austerities linked to ninth lord, rites to tenth lord, and manifestation of temperament types at period onset. Faith/ritual membership is distinguishable from externally verified realization.

## 3. Rath's *Jaimini* course includes both ancient sutras and *his own implementations*

- **Lesson 9 PDF190–204:** Sanskrit name-letter `varga` counts are based on **phonetic spelling and vowel units**. This is not deducible from birth information alone and is incompatible with casually substituting a foreign-language name spelling.
- **Lesson 13 Sutra 1.1.2 and commentary, PDF257–258:** `rāśi dṛṣṭi` sign-aspects depend on movable/fixed/dual types and excluded adjacent signs. Do not conflate sign-aspects with the planetary `graha dṛṣṭi` in Parāśara or `Sārāvalī`.
- **Lesson 14 Sutra1.1.25 and commentary, PDF271–280:** odd/even zodiacal/anti-zodiacal daśā counts and special dual-lord exceptions. Rath invokes lineage to justify importing rules from Parāśara—**an interpretive lineage claim**, not proof the literal Jaimini sutra already contains each exception.
- **Lesson 14 PDF282:** special year-unit conversions for elephants/humans and other nonhuman categories; animal-life methods require independent species units and cannot count as human chart successes.
- **Lesson 19 Sutra1.1.35 PDF391:** the tiny sutra invokes established `Horā` teaching. Rath's explanatory chapter extends that into a lengthy list of other chart methods. Keep literal sutra separate from Rath's inventory.

## 4. Parāśara gives a specific three-reference timing system with an explicit fallback

The second-hand Portuguese `Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra`, **Sudarśana Cakra section74 lines5406–5432**, describes concentric natal **Lagna, Moon, Sun** rings with one-year main periods for twelve houses and monthly subperiods. But **line5426** says when two/three of Lagna, Moon and Sun coincide, use the ordinary natal chart instead. **Line5432** cross-checks results with Aṣṭakavarga and escalates them when both sources agree; if discordant it says compare relative strengths. This is a *source-derived conditional dependency*, not two independent empirical confirmations. Its 12-year ring cycle and ordinary Vimsottari/Yogini daśā cannot be pooled without frozen versioning.

The Portuguese elemental paragraph **lines5454–56** links five planet strengths to bodily temperaments, paralleling `Sārāvalī`’s elemental/dream sections and an older Ayurveda conceptual tradition. The historical similarity should be documented without claiming either ancient model has proven objective medical or dream-prediction validity.
