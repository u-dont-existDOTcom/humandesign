# Shared-model, full-survey development proposal

Date: 2026-09-25. Status: PROPOSAL in response to the owner's request for a way forward. No second participant data have been supplied, no joint fit has been run, and no survey, scorer or deployment is changed by this document.

## Owner question and outcome

The owner proposes having another person complete the same full survey, then finding rules that work for both people. This is the recommended next development step, with one essential interpretation: learn one shared answer-conditioned model, not two separately fitted natal signatures or person-specific switches.

Preserve the owner outcome: source-grounded birth-date/time recovery from measured behavior; cross-rulebook composition allowed; equal-vote and lineage-aware variants; no invented continuous astrology weights; authorized target-aware development distinct from inference and independent testing. Do not replace this outcome with independent success of every astrological tradition, validation-only work, or a reduced questionnaire whose sufficiency has not been demonstrated.

## 1. Collect comparable full behavioral evidence

Use the existing full scenario bank and its admission/coding protocol, not the nine-prompt five-domain pilot. The bank is a menu rather than a quota: preserve matched-scenario requirements, conditional follow-ups, skips, privacy choices and natural stopping. Comparable measurement does not require irrelevant questions or an identical number of turns.

Reuse the owner's existing private full transcript. Do not ask the owner to repeat it. Apply the same versioned neutral coding specification to both records. Where questions changed after the original live pilot, preserve actual evidence scope rather than pretending an old answer answered a repaired question. Keep conditions, contrasts, explicit counterevidence and missingness. Record which measured distinctions have admitted chart interpretations and which remain unmapped; never claim the whole interview is scored when most of it is discarded.

Use one consenting new adult with a reasonably documented birth date, time, time uncertainty and birthplace. Keep birth information in a separate evaluation record. Interviewing and neutral coding do not receive charts, actual dates, desired answers or rankings. Freeze the new person's reviewed behavioral record before revealing birth information to the fitting/evaluation stage. No diagnosis or sensitive-attribute inference is needed.

## 2. Preserve a before-refit baseline, then fit both people

Once the second record is frozen, record the existing frozen six-rule pilot's result on that person as a limited baseline. This uses only that pilot's supported subset of the evidence and is not a test of full-survey capacity. Failure of this baseline does not veto the owner's proposed joint-development step. Preserve both its success and its failure without reinterpreting them post hoc.

Next fit a single shared model to both full records and both known targets. Both people are development cases at this stage. Reuse the broader source-rule inventory as a starting resource, not a guarantee of adequate source coverage. Preserve all six historical selected rules as a baseline; do not force their retention or impose a six-rule ceiling on the generalized model.

The unit admitted to a general decoder is an explicit chart-condition -> behavioral-prediction -> evidence-comparison record, with historical source and modern interpretation separately identified. A generic claim of planetary strength is not automatically a precise behavioral prediction. State any operational bridge as a hypothesis. Missing a positive condition does not establish the opposite personality. Freeze each candidate bridge version before its scoring run; a revised bridge is a declared model revision applied consistently to both people.

Select rule membership mechanically with equal votes; compare raw and lineage-deduplicated stacking as requested. The same rule meanings, coding, aggregation and selected subset apply to everyone. Different people may satisfy different rules and have different answer-dependent scores. A rule does not have to be true of both charts or individually recover either birth; the shared ensemble must explain the different evidence under the same semantics.

Forbidden substitutes: separate custom subset for each person; person identity/name as a branch; inference using known birth time to choose rules; profile-specific definitions invented after target reveal; custom sign flips to rescue one person; new test restrictions introduced solely because a candidate fails. Learning a shared model can still overfit two cases, so joint fit is development evidence only.

## 3. Optimize actual recovery for both, not an average proxy

Proposed selection objective: among shared source-grounded subsets that satisfy the declared date/time recovery criteria separately for each person, choose the smallest; then assess margins and robustness. A gain for one person does not compensate for failure on the other. During diagnosis retain a per-person vector of rank, ties, surviving dates and time-window width, rather than merely an aggregate score or semantic-fit improvement.

Start with 100 candidate instants per person, then 1,000, 10,000 and the century when the smaller direct challenges are met. Include seeded broad decoys, wrong dates near the target, same-date time alternatives, and persistent discovered false matches from earlier stages. Birthplace is held at each person's documented location. Do not use age/demographic clues to shrink the declared century after failures. Report birth-date and within-date time recovery separately, with an honest predeclared treatment of birth-record uncertainty.

Evaluate each previously frozen candidate on the larger panel before any refit. Version and preserve refits. After revealing a panel, it is development evidence for later versions, not untouched validation. A tie is not unique recovery. Report infeasibility or insufficient representation instead of forcing a nominal success or inserting target-proximity tie-breaks. Run the selected unchanged shared candidate over both full declared universes, not only over the first person's already discovered winning minutes.

## 4. Check that behavior actually carries the distinction

A cheap diagnostic exchanges the two frozen behavioral profiles while holding each location and candidate chart set fixed. Inspect whether the correct profile gives its actual chart a more favorable comparison than the mismatched profile. This prevents birthplace or an effectively constant score from being mistaken for a behavioral decoder. With two records this is a diagnostic, not a well-powered permutation significance test. Similar measured profiles may be legitimately indistinguishable; that is a capacity result to report.

A separate bounded false-target experiment addresses why the original six-rule result looks striking: assign randomly sampled alternative target instants and rerun the entire recorded subset-selection procedure with the same library, search budget, hard-negative generation, stopping rules and subsequent challenge. Compare how often similarly small models isolate those false targets. Do not merely score false dates under the already selected real-target model. This estimates flexibility of the recorded search, not every undocumented historical modeling choice; do not turn it into a formal p-value without specifying a justified null and adequate sampling. It is useful supporting work, not a prerequisite that stalls collecting the next full survey.

## 5. Expand development without losing honest forward evidence

A model that fits both people is a two-person development model. The next participant can receive a first frozen-model prediction before being added to training. Preserve that result. If a new version is then trained on all three, retain the earlier failure/success and use a later unseen participant for the next first-look test. Do not require a large recruited cohort before making the immediate two-person development improvement, and do not claim general validation from either a two-person fit or one subsequent success.

As a larger cohort becomes available, split by person: all answers, charts, decoys and fitting choices for a held-out person remain outside fitting. Splitting millions of decoy dates for the same two people does not create a large independent participant sample. Existing-work basis: Cawley & Talbot (2010), https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html; Ojala & Garriga (2010), https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/ojala10a.html. These support the separation of model selection from performance evidence and the use of properly specified randomized controls; they do not validate astrology or determine this protocol's numerical thresholds.
