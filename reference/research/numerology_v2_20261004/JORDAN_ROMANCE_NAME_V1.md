## J02. Notation, alphabet, reduction and compound policy [pp.7–8,63–68,75]

Let `r(n)` repeatedly add decimal digits until a single digit; `r(0)=0` is only a computational empty-sum marker, not an invented tenth personality type. Alphabet: **1 AJS; 2 BKT; 3 CLU; 4 DMV; 5 ENW; 6 FOX; 7 GPY; 8 HQZ; 9 IR**. K=2 and V=4, including transit durations. Numeric values are not Cheiro's values or a K=11/V=22 arithmetic table.

For component `s`, let `S(s)=sum(letter values)`. Full-name number is `r(sum(r(S(s)) for s in birth-name components))`. Store each raw component total, each reduction chain, and the sum of component roots. **Never add all raw letters straight through and use that as the final name's two-digit modifier**: p.75 explicitly distinguishes the methods. The root may agree while the compound differs.

**All totals, including 11 and 22, are reduced to 2 and 4**; pp.64–66 explicitly reject leaving them standing alone. Preserve 11/2 and 22/4 as spiritually significant background, not arithmetic stopping rules. Eleven is a spiritual-messenger configuration, twenty-two a spiritual-master-in-form configuration. No 33/44 master-number arithmetic is supplied. Compound meaning follows the generating route: the root dominates, with constituent numbers modifying it (p.272). There is no complete Cheiro-style 10–52 or Javane–Bunker 1–78 catalogue here.

Sixteen/seven and nineteen/one receive explicit karmic treatment (pp.66–68): 16 upheaval/awakening through truth and knowledge; 19 self-reliance/courage with tests. Both can have constructive outcomes. Do not import 13/14 as universally defined karmic debts; this book gives 14/5 an Essence interpretation but does not classify it that way. Eight has its own effort/judgment/tests discussion, not a rule that every 8 is calamity. Sixteen's lightning-struck Tarot tower is a symbolic reference, not a complete Tarot calculation system.

## J03. Name authority and input normalization [pp.73–76,88–89,320–323]

Use the full name actually given at birth, however briefly used, with every name component. First/given name describes personal disposition, middle name latent resources, surname ancestral/family/vocational background. Later nicknames, signatures, married, professional or other names are channels of expression, **not replacements of the birth pattern**. A confirmation name is not retroactively part of the birth name.

For adoption, preserve the original if known and separately study the adopted name; p.88 permits concurrent comparison. When the original is genuinely unknown, Jordan allows the adopted name as the available reading rather than inventing an original. This is a labelled source-unknown fallback, not proof the original has changed. A birth name used only hours can still govern the natal chart. Initials should be expanded to their actual names; Jordan declines an initials-only name reading when expansions are unavailable (p.89), while date material remains usable.

Use foreign birth spelling as originally given (pp.76,323); children named after a family's spelling change have their own actual birth spelling. The book supplies only a 26-letter Latin table: non-Latin transliteration and diacritics remain unresolved. No explicit rule for apostrophes, hyphens, title prefixes/suffixes or other punctuation was found in the whole supplied text. Nonletter removal may be a transparent implementation convention for arithmetic, but **hyphen tokenization and title membership cannot be silently certified as Jordan's rule**. Preserve token arrays and alternative joined/separate hyphen compounds when relevant. A first-vowel or first-letter feature is mentioned at pp.298–299 but no separate elaborate phonetic table or extra formula is supplied; do not import Campbell's first-vowel planets or Decoz's Cornerstone/Capstone stack.
