## 1. Technical foundations

### 1.1 Motion, coordinates, and the actual target

Primary motion is the apparent east-to-west daily rotation of the sky caused by terrestrial rotation. In the frozen-natal-coordinate approximation, a body's declination stays constant and it travels around a diurnal circle parallel to the celestial equator. Secondary planetary motion changes its astronomical coordinates during the few postnatal hours being mapped symbolically to years. The latter is a separate correction, especially important for the Moon. [Ch. 1, pp. 1–2; Ch. 3, pp. 33–44; Ch. 7, pp. 82–84.]

Ecliptic longitude λ and latitude β describe a direction relative to the ecliptic. Right ascension α and declination δ describe the same direction relative to the equator. Longitude difference is not, in general, the Earth's required rotation. Equal differences in λ can produce unequal directional arcs because of obliquity, declination, latitude, and the chosen positional relationship. [Ch. 1, pp. 3–5; Ch. 3, pp. 37–44; Appendix I §§1a–1b, pp. 147–150.]

The geocentric horizon is not the equator. The MC is an ecliptic-meridian intersection, not the zenith. A bodily planet rises when it intersects the eastern half of the horizon, not necessarily where the ecliptic ascendant degree rises. Thus a direction to the Ascendant can mean contact with an entire horizon semicircle. An interplanetary direction generally means equal temporal proportion or intersection with a chosen position circle—not occupation of the identical three-dimensional point. [Ch. 3, pp. 34–39; Ch. 4, pp. 48–59.]

### 1.2 Quantities and conventions

Use a geocentric, geometric, frozen-coordinate baseline unless a configuration explicitly changes it. All angles below are degrees; right ascension in hours must first be multiplied by 15. Positive geographic latitude φ is north; positive declination is north. Let θ be RAMC, numerically local sidereal time in degrees. Define signed hour angle H = θ − α, increasing with forward primary motion.

| Quantity | Meaning and relation | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| α, δ | Equatorial longitude and latitude of the body or constructed point | Both change when nonzero β is retained |
| RAMC θ | Right ascension on the upper meridian | Sets the natal rotational phase; not MC longitude |
| Meridian distance MD | Angular distance in right ascension from the relevant meridian | Must retain upper/lower and east/west quadrant information |
| Ascensional difference AD | In the declared signed convention, asin(tan φ tan δ) | Measures the departure of rising/setting from an equatorial quarter-turn |
| Diurnal semi-arc DSA | 90° + AD, upper culmination to rising or setting | Depends on the individual point's δ |
| Nocturnal semi-arc NSA | 90° − AD, lower culmination to rising or setting | DSA + NSA = 180° |
| Oblique ascension OA | α − AD | Appropriate eastern-horizon rotational coordinate |
| Oblique descension OD | α + AD | Corresponding western-horizon coordinate |
| Pole of a point | Axis/elevation associated with its chosen position circle | Different circle definitions produce different poles |
| Temporal/horary hour | One twelfth of the body's day or night arc, hence DSA/6 or NSA/6 in rotational degrees | Its clock duration varies; it is not automatically sixty civil minutes |

These relations are an independently stated signed-coordinate formulation of Appendix I §§1–4, pp. 147–157, with temporal-hour terminology checked in the Glossary, p. 165. The book also gives verbal southern-hemisphere sign instructions. Software must not combine a signed φ with an additional unexamined sign reversal. Southern cases require explicit validation against geometric horizon identities.

If |tan φ tan δ| > 1, the point has no ordinary rise/set crossing: a circumpolar domain problem, not a number to force into asin. The text recognizes the difficulty at high latitude but does not supply a fully general polar-direction framework. [Ch. 3, around p. 40.] Tiny floating-point excursions around ±1 may be rounded within a documented tolerance; genuine domain failures must be returned as such.

### 1.3 Roles and vocabulary

The **significator** denotes the life domain or recipient of a direction; the **promissor** contributes the active event quality. These interpretive roles are not interchangeable with fixed/moving computational roles. In traditional direct motion, the promissor moves to a fixed significator relationship. In traditional converse, the significator moves toward the promissor, but the physical primary motion remains east-to-west. Older language often describes the significator as being directed even when it is computationally fixed. [Ch. 1, pp. 5–7, reference 9 pp. 9–10.]

A modern reversed-time or reversed-rotation converse is a different algorithm. Do not implement traditional converse merely by negating a direct arc: swap the role of the moving and fixed points and reconstruct the relevant aspect relationship. In the traditional account summarized at Ch. 5 p. 71, an aspect is a promissor: moving in direct motion and fixed in traditional converse. This constrains which role swaps are historically admissible. A fixed horizon or meridian cannot physically move like a planet; directing its natal zodiacal degree as a point is an additional construction. Rapt parallels, with both bodies moving, are a stated exception to the simple fixed/moving description. [Ch. 1, reference 5 p. 9; Ch. 8, pp. 92–101.]

“Zodiacal” and “mundane” are also overloaded. A β = 0 zodiacal aspect point is specified by longitude. A bodily conjunction/opposition can retain planetary latitude. Placidean mundane aspects instead use normalized semi-arc position. Calling every latitude-inclusive calculation “in mundo” erases this distinction: the equivalence only works for conjunction/opposition, not the remaining aspects. [Ch. 4, pp. 51–55; Ch. 5, pp. 65–70; Ch. 8, pp. 89–92.]

### 1.4 What can be directed

Planets, angles, aspects, fixed stars where admitted, bounds/terms endpoints, lots, and other chosen points are not one homogeneous target class. A planet has actual β and secondary motion; an ordinary zodiacal bound endpoint has β = 0; an angle may be a circle or its natal ecliptic degree; traditional Fortune is an ecliptic construction, whereas Placidean mundane Fortune is artificial and has a special declination/semi-arc definition. Intermediate house cusps introduce their own circle/curve choices. [Ch. 1, pp. 5–7; Ch. 4, pp. 55–59; Ch. 8, pp. 94–101; Appendix I §5, pp. 158–160.]

### 2.1 Latitude and aspect variants

For a zero-latitude zodiacal aspect, form λ ± ψ on the ecliptic, then convert that new point to α and δ. Do not add ψ to right ascension unless intentionally implementing the distinct right-ascensional-aspect innovation. [Ch. 5, pp. 65–70; Ch. 8, pp. 88–90.]

Gansten describes Bianchini's inclined-circle treatment, including reversed latitude at opposition, zero latitude at squares, and half-latitude prescriptions for sextiles/trines with differing signs. Morin instead refers to an orbit's preceding or following maximum latitude, selected according to the direction of latitude change. These are not “include latitude” switches with identical outputs. The exact relationship between a geometrically defined inclined great circle and fractional-latitude rules needs checking; half the maximum is not automatically an exact spherical identity. Morin's required orbital information also exceeds the instantaneous natal β. Gansten identifies tables attributed through ar-Rijal to a figure probably al-Hamdani as a possible earlier correction, but explicitly says he has not seen those tables. That possibility supplies neither a confirmed priority date nor an implementable algorithm. [Ch. 5, pp. 67–70 and note 5, p. 72.]

Morin's reported position also admits projected contacts as meaningful while assigning greater potency to the body. A directional effect may span the interval between projected and bodily contacts, in either order, with suitable returns required. A faithful research model should therefore distinguish this duration hypothesis from choosing one contact and discarding the other. [Ch. 4, note 3, p. 62; Holden 1994:42f as cited there.]

A hybrid keeping the fixed body's latitude but projecting the moving body onto the ecliptic is mentioned in Appendix I §4b. Keep it distinct from both-bodily and both-projected models; the inspected attribution is insufficient to assign it universally to a named school.

## 3. Independently worded implementation specifications

These are specifications derived from the inspected geometry, not transcriptions of the appendix. A documented algorithm is not yet production software. Branch handling and polar exclusions are part of its identity.

### 3.1 Common input/output contract

Inputs: birth epoch and time scale; geographic position; obliquity model; astronomical coordinates and their reference frame/equinox; zodiac definition; fixed/moving roles; target/aspect construction; geometry family; physical direction of time; secondary-motion, parallax, refraction and disc-contact flags; arc-to-age key; calendar convention. Do not mix apparent and mean coordinates, or topocentric and geocentric coordinates, silently.

Output each contact with the configuration, angular arc in degrees, age convention, selected root/quadrant, intermediate quantities and domain/precision warnings. Store natal-time uncertainty separately from numeric solver tolerance.

An independent robust conversion, equivalent to rotation of coordinate axes, is:

~~~
x = cos(β) cos(λ)
y = cos(β) sin(λ) cos(ε) − sin(β) sin(ε)
z = cos(β) sin(λ) sin(ε) + sin(β) cos(ε)
α = atan2(y, x) modulo 360°
δ = asin(z)
~~~

Trigonometric arguments here are degrees; an implementation must convert for its math library. Longitude in this rotation must use the equinox origin consistent with the equatorial frame: a sidereal astrological longitude needs its declared zero-point conversion first. This is a coordinate-consistency requirement, not a choice of preferred zodiac. The atan2 formulation avoids the quadrant ambiguity of a one-argument inverse tangent. The appendix supplies a separate sine check on RA and warns about latitude/quadrant ambiguity near equinoxes. [Appendix I §§1a–1b, pp. 147–150.]

### 3.2 Meridian and horizon contacts

For forward frozen-coordinate motion:

~~~
MC arc = (α_moving − θ_birth) modulo 360°
IC arc = (α_moving − θ_birth − 180°) modulo 360°
Asc arc = (OA_moving − θ_birth − 90°) modulo 360°
Desc arc = (OD_moving − θ_birth + 90°) modulo 360°
~~~

Select upper/lower culmination and east/west crossing explicitly. Modulo gives the next contact within a whole rotation; it does not authorize a second biological forecast cycle. Apply the preregistered age/arc range afterward. [Appendix I §§2–3, pp. 150–152.]

### 3.3 Proportional semi-arcs: a phase formulation

The appendix works with meridian distances and quadrant-dependent subtraction. The following independent reformulation makes quadrant changes explicit. It assumes frozen coordinates and ordinary rising/setting. Define H = (θ − α) modulo 360°, a = DSA and b = NSA. Let u measure normalized diurnal position, increasing with primary motion:

~~~
if 0 ≤ H ≤ a:          u = 90 H/a
if a < H ≤ 180:        u = 90 + 90(H−a)/b
if 180 < H ≤ 360−a:    u = 180 + 90(H−180)/b
if 360−a < H < 360:    u = 270 + 90(H−360+a)/a
~~~

Thus MC = 0°, Desc = 90°, IC = 180°, Asc = 270°. These are normalized positional units, not ecliptic degrees. Determine u_fixed at birth. Invert the same piecewise map using the moving point's own a and b:

~~~
if 0 ≤ u ≤ 90:        H_target = u a/90
if 90 < u ≤ 180:      H_target = a + (u−90)b/90
if 180 < u ≤ 270:     H_target = 180 + (u−180)b/90
if 270 < u < 360:     H_target = 360−a + (u−270)a/90
arc = (α_moving + H_target − θ_birth) modulo 360°
~~~

For conjunction, use u = u_fixed. For a declared mundane aspect ψ, use the chosen u_fixed ± ψ branch. For traditional converse, change fixed/moving roles and construct the appropriate aspect before calculating. A β = 0 *zodiacal* aspect is instead constructed in longitude before this positional calculation. [Ch. 4, pp. 55–59; Ch. 8, pp. 89–92; Appendix I §4a, pp. 152–154.]

This phase formulation is an analytical reconstruction, not a claim that Gansten prints this pseudocode. It was checked for endpoint continuity, inversion, and the numerical case below; a comprehensive implementation still needs the test grid in §8.

### 3.4 Regiomontanian position circle

On the northern, eastern-above-horizon branch used in example 1, let m be positive upper meridian distance of the fixed point. Appendix I §4c gives the following auxiliary route:

~~~
X = atan(tan δ_fixed / cos m)
Y = φ − X
Z = atan(cos Y / (tan m cos X))
p = asin(sin φ cos Z)
AD_fixed,p = asin(tan p tan δ_fixed)
AD_moving,p = asin(tan p tan δ_moving)
arc = (α_moving − AD_moving,p) − (α_fixed − AD_fixed,p)
~~~

All p-dependent ascensions use the **same fixed point's pole**, not separate poles for the two points. West branches use the corresponding oblique-descension construction. The one-argument atan forms are not a license to ignore quadrant reconstruction, singularities, or southern branches. [Appendix I §4c, pp. 156–157; Fig. 17, p. 58.]

An independently derived geometric specification avoids some auxiliary-angle singularities: represent the fixed point S and the local north horizon point N as unit vectors; their cross product is normal to the position-circle plane. In the equatorial Cartesian frame, S = (cos δ_S cos α_S, cos δ_S sin α_S, sin δ_S) and N = (−sin φ cos θ, −sin φ sin θ, cos φ). Rotate the moving vector westward by candidate arc A around the polar axis, replacing α_M by α_M − A, and solve (N × S) · M(A) = 0. Select the intended position **semicircle** and first forward crossing; the plane also contains the antipodal semicircle. A zero cross product is a degeneracy requiring a declared limit or explicit failure. This plane identity was checked for example 1, not for a complete global engine.

### 3.5 Placidean under-the-pole approximation

For the same northern eastern-above branch:

~~~
r = MD_fixed / DSA_fixed
AD_at_pole = r AD_fixed
p = atan(sin AD_at_pole / tan δ_fixed)
arc = OA_moving,p − OA_fixed,p
~~~

Use the matching nocturnal/upper/lower and east/west definitions on other branches, rather than reusing positive upper MD everywhere. The δ_fixed = 0 expression is formally 0/0; its branch limit must be established rather than evaluated literally. The equatorial limit derived from this expression is atan(r tan φ), but that extension still needs independent branch testing. [Ch. 8, pp. 94–95; Appendix I §4b, pp. 154–156.]

This procedure approximates the semi-arc position curve by a great circle. Agreement for some data does not make it identical to §3.3.

## 4. Arc-to-age conversion and time

Let A be a directional arc in degrees and y an age in model-years.

| Key | Age rule | Required assumption/data |
|---|---|---|
| Ptolemaic conventional key | y = A | Symbolic 1° per year; not a claim that Ptolemy invented it |
| Naibod mean-solar key | y = A/k, with declared k near 360°/365.25 | Mean solar movement used as a constant; historical rounded DMS and exact rational versions must be separate parameterizations |
| Brahe birth-date key | Compute true solar RA advance between the local true midnights enclosing birth; use this constant k_birth; y = A/k_birth | Solar ephemeris plus a precise definition of local true midnight |
| Placidean true-solar key | Find elapsed postnatal days d such that unwrapped α_Sun(t_birth + d) − α_Sun(t_birth) = A; set y = d | Variable accumulated solar RA, not simply the first day's rate |

[Ch. 6, pp. 73–79.] These are independent of direction geometry. Using Placidus's time key does not imply using every Placidean aspect or Fortune construction.

The exact rational diagnostic k = 360/365.25 gives 0.9856262834°/year, or approximately 0°59′08.2546″/year. Different printed rounded constants must not silently be treated as identical to it. The diurnal-directions discussion uses a related daily increment convention and should retain its own literal parameter. [Ch. 10, pp. 143–144.]

Two additional key questions appear in Ch. 6 notes 1–2, p. 80. Morin attributes a Naibod-like view to Cardan, while Kolev supplies a rate said to derive from 360/365; Gansten points out that the stated arithmetic does not yield the printed seconds, and neither gives an explicit Cardan passage. Keep this disputed attribution separate from an executable exact-rational 360/365 variant. Likewise, Brahe's birth-date key is sometimes named after Kepler, but Gansten locates it in Brahe's *De Stella Nova* (1573). The different proposal Morin attributes to Kepler amounts, in Gansten's reading, to solar-arc directions, a separate technique rather than another interchangeable primary-direction key.

To calculate physical postnatal time from an equatorial arc, distinguish sidereal rotation from civil clock time. The opening four-minutes-per-degree rule is approximate. An actual ephemeris implementation requires UT/TT conventions, Earth-rotation/sidereal-time model, longitude and historical civil-time conversion. Calendar age also needs a declared rule: an anniversary fraction and 365.25-day model-years do not always give the same calendar date. Neither synthetic example below claims an ephemeris-derived date.

Gansten discusses indications developing over periods, not universally exact-day events; later-life examples separate fixed keys more strongly. Birth-time rectification, multiple keys and post-hoc window changes can all manufacture apparent precision. [Ch. 6, pp. 73–79.]

### Reproducibility kernel

The following original Python fragment reproduces the displayed central outputs without an ephemeris or publisher chart data:

~~~python
import math as m
def s(x): return m.sin(m.radians(x))
def c(x): return m.cos(m.radians(x))
def t(x): return m.tan(m.radians(x))
def ai(x): return m.degrees(m.asin(x))
def at(x): return m.degrees(m.atan(x))
def ad(delta, pole):
    q = t(delta)*t(pole)
    if abs(q) > 1: raise ValueError("no ordinary rise/set")
    return ai(q)

phi, theta, aS, dS, aM, dM = 45, 0, 50, 15, 80, -10
ads, adm = ad(dS, phi), ad(dM, phi)
r = (aS-theta)/(90+ads)
semi = aM-theta-r*(90+adm)
pp = at(s(r*ads)/t(dS))
X = at(t(dS)/c(aS-theta))
Y = phi-X
Z = at(c(Y)/(t(aS-theta)*c(X)))
pr = ai(s(phi)*c(Z))
def pole_arc(p):
    return (aM-ad(dM,p))-(aS-ad(dS,p))
print(semi, pole_arc(pp), pole_arc(pr))
assert abs((aM-semi)/(90+adm)-r) < 1e-12

for beta in (0, 5):
    lam, eps = 120, 23.44
    x = c(beta)*c(lam)
    y = c(beta)*s(lam)*c(eps)-s(beta)*s(eps)
    z = c(beta)*s(lam)*s(eps)+s(beta)*c(eps)
    alpha = m.degrees(m.atan2(y,x)) % 360
    delta = ai(z)
    arc = alpha-ad(delta,phi)-90
    h_sine = s(phi)*s(delta)+c(phi)*c(delta)*c(arc-alpha)
    assert abs(h_sine) < 1e-12
    print(beta, alpha, delta, arc, arc*365.25/360)
~~~

This deliberately restricted kernel is not a global direction engine: it omits branch search, historical astronomical inputs, motion corrections and calendar dates.

## 6. Prediction, interpretation, and combination rules

### 6.1 What the book intends directions to describe

Gansten presents the natal chart as setting the possible event domain and directions as timing its unfolding. The same moving planet therefore does not carry a fixed event label across all charts. His opening pair of lunar examples contrasts career advancement with a relationship crisis through natal rulership and affliction. Those anonymous retrospective examples do not provide a test set. [Ch. 1, pp. 2–6, Figs. 1–2.]

Lilly's significator taxonomy, quoted by Gansten, encompasses the following broad domains. This table is a selective paraphrase, not a complete reproduction of Lilly's list:

| Requested domain | Source-grounded interpretive route | Empirical operationalization needed |
|---|---|---|
| Major life changes | Ascendant/body and natal house determinations; prominent angular or luminary contacts | Define event severity and observation window |
| Relationships/marriage | Moon/Venus and seventh-house rulership/condition | Distinguish marriage registration, union formation, separation and subjective distress |
| Career/status/wealth | MC/Sun; Fortune for resources; Jupiter and natal rulership | Separate promotion, income, office and reputation |
| Family/children | Relevant natal houses and parental/ancestral or Jupiter significations | Define births, bereavements and household changes independently |
| Travel/relocation | Moon/Mercury and natal house context | A journey is not automatically a change of residence |
| Spiritual/intellectual development | Jupiter's religious and Mercury's scholarly/intellectual domains | Subjective growth is weakly falsifiable unless a prospective measurable endpoint is specified |
| Illness/danger/longevity | Ascendant/luminary bodily significations; historical hyleg and malefic rules | Historical doctrine only; no personal mortality forecasting |

[Ch. 1, pp. 5–6; Ch. 5, pp. 65–70; Ch. 9, pp. 106–127.]

Computing a contact does not decide its event domain, positive/negative valence, strength, duration or whether an event happens at all. Natal rulership, house position, dignity, natal aspects and the selection of allowed significators are interpretive parameters. Gansten favors admitting more significators than the restricted five associated with Ptolemy and does not treat aspect labels alone as sufficient. [Ch. 1, pp. 5–7; Ch. 5, pp. 65–70.]

### 6.2 Longevity as historical material

The hyleg is a selected life-signifying point, not merely another name for the Ascendant. Gansten's reconstruction of Ptolemy uses sect, eligible places, luminaries, dignity conditions and fallback rules, with manuscript/interpretive complications. The reported eligible places are the tenth, first, eleventh, seventh and ninth; the eighth is not an interchangeable addition. This is not a complete selector: sect, strength and dignity exceptions still matter. Eastern and western procedures differ; the latter includes setting and adjustments expressed in temporal hours. Medieval alchocoden procedures add disposing rulers and planetary-year arithmetic, which are not simply directional arcs. Morin rejects the hyleg framework and works through natal significations; Placidus and Worsdale introduce further selection and aspect differences. [Ch. 9, pp. 106–124.]

Do not merge these into a universal “traditional lifespan algorithm.” Nor should illness or death in an illustrative historical chart be called validation. The Gustavus Adolphus comparison includes retrospective rectification and conflicting treatments of Jupiter; such flexibility is itself a methodological concern. Gansten's personal preferences—including wider converse use and skepticism about some hyleg rules—are not a recovered historical consensus. [Ch. 9, pp. 111–124.]

For this project, retain longevity methods in a historical-method registry. Do not issue individual death dates or present them as medically meaningful. Any future archival statistical analysis would require separate ethical design, fixed eligibility rules and no retrospective chart adjustment.

### 6.3 Other forecasting layers

Annual profection in the discussed continuous form advances 30° of zodiacal longitude per year, including fractional progress during the year. That is not the same as a whole-sign annual step at birthdays. Lilly uses profections to reinforce or qualify a directional indication. Morin rejects profections in favor of returns. [Ch. 10, pp. 134–139.]

Solar/lunar returns add choices about zodiac, precession and birthplace versus current residence. Gansten regards them less favorably than profections on his experience, not on a blinded comparative experiment. Placidus's day-for-year secondary directions and his differently named synodic-month-for-year progressions must remain distinct from primary directions and from each other. In their original supplementary role, Gansten says these techniques concern aspects to the Sun and Moon; that is narrower than a general modern progressed chart. Troinski's later tropical-month-for-year “tertiary directions” are another variant, with a different month definition. Gansten reports limited or no personal experience with parts of this package. [Ch. 10, pp. 138–140, summary p. 145; Ch. 2, note 32, p. 31.]

Transits can be referenced to natal, profected or directional positions, with different authors assigning different roles. Gansten reads Ptolemy's hierarchy as Saturn/natal, Jupiter/annual, Moon/daily and the other planets/monthly; the whole-sign interpretation is presented as a probable reading, not a demonstrated unique algorithm. Placidus's ingresses and Kühr's emphasis on transits to directed places are separate proposals. A moving point's directional longitude may require numerical inversion of the direction engine, not addition of the arc to natal longitude. [Ch. 10, pp. 140–143.]

Diurnal/short directions and directions within return charts create additional timing layers. Kühr's combination of under-pole geometry, Naibod, reverse converse and modern planets should not be labeled simply “Placidus.” Gansten does not endorse a universal guarantee of matching transits and has not experimentally established the additional return-direction layers. [Ch. 10, pp. 142–144.]

## 8. Research integration and discriminating tests

### 8.1 What the Western source map should add

Add a versioned method record per historically attested package, with independent fields for geometry, projected/bodily coordinates, aspect provider, direct/converse semantics, allowed significators, points/lots, key, astronomical corrections, house system, zodiac, interpretation and auxiliary techniques. Keep source attribution, reconstructed formulas and outcome studies in different tables linked by IDs. The Preface, pp. vii–viii, explicitly specifies K. S. Krishnamurti's ayanāṃśa and Alcabitius houses for Gansten's charts except those taken from older authors; Ch. 9 p. 112 also discusses his preference. For the same physical bodies and contact definition, consistent coordinate relabeling alone does not change the rotation arc. Zodiacal absolute targets, house rulerships, dignities and interpretation still require their own declared conventions.

Do not use “Ptolemaic,” “Placidean,” “Alcabitius” or “traditional” as executable specifications. In the broader Western/Hellenistic/Jyotish/Chinese comparison, match measurable prediction tasks and evaluation rules, not merely similarly translated technique names. Gansten notes in Ch. 1 reference 1 p. 9 that directions do not appear to have entered India with other early Hellenistic material; this is his historical claim, not proof that no analogous Indian technique exists. Chinese methods are not established by this book.

### 8.2 Implement separately

First priority: common spherical-coordinate layer; exact proportional semi-arcs; Regiomontanian position circles; Placidean under-pole approximation; independent zodiacal and mundane aspect providers; explicit direct/traditional-converse/reverse-converse modes; interchangeable key objects. Then add historically sourced sign-rising-time interpolation, bounds periods, parallels, artificial Fortune and motion corrections as distinct modules. Defer incomplete crepuscular and orbital-latitude variants until their missing definitions are resolved.

### 8.3 Reproducibility suite

Promote the two synthetic examples, with inputs and tolerances, into tests before fitting any real biography. The phase map passed 135 same-agent inversion/continuity checks across five geographic latitudes, three declinations and representative quadrant/endpoints; this is not independent global-engine certification. Add MC/IC/horizon limits; northern/southern reflection; λ near 0°/90°/180°/270°; δ = 0 limits; both hemispheres and all four quadrants; circumpolar rejection; nonzero β; direct/converse role reversal; phase inversion; antipodal position-circle root rejection; high-latitude sensitivity; and exact versus rounded keys.

For example 1 require arcs within 10⁻⁸ degrees of the printed unrounded computations. For example 2 require both equatorial conversion and altitude residual tests. Also compare an independently written Cartesian engine and a separately maintained historical-direction implementation, documenting differences instead of tuning toward a desired event.

The book's historical worked examples can later become provenance/reproduction tests **after** their complete input data, table versions and rectification status are checked. Anonymous cases with withheld data cannot serve as independently reproducible benchmarks.

### 8.4 Most discriminating empirical designs

| Test | Frozen competing models | Outcome and safeguards |
|---|---|---|
| Off-angle geometry test | Semi-arcs vs Regiomontanian vs under-pole, identical points/aspects/key | Use charts selected for large predicted separation, without seeing outcomes; documentary birth times; compare event-window scores on held-out people |
| Latitude test | β = 0 vs bodily β, same horizon/angle contact and key | Include cases with large predicted arc separation, especially Moon/high β; preregister both affected domains and timing windows |
| Late-life key test | 1°/year vs Naibod vs Brahe vs true-solar accumulated RA | Later ages amplify divergence; no key selection per biography; stratify birth season and age |
| Converse test | Direct only; plus traditional converse; plus reverse-time converse | Penalize additional forecast opportunities; identical interpretation vocabulary and exposure time |
| Aspect/model-complexity test | Restricted zodiacal aspects vs mundane aspects/parallels/additional modern aspects | Compare out-of-sample improvement after accounting for the larger number of candidate hits |
| Auxiliary-layer test | Directions alone vs fixed profection/return/transit conjunction rule | Nested models with predefined trigger hierarchy; no post-hoc “confirmation” substitution |
| Interpretation test | Planet-nature-only vs natal-house/rulership-informed domain classifier | Blindly code dated marriage, promotion, relocation, childbirth and documented illness onset separately; score timing and domain, including misses and false positives |

Predict a limited, externally dated endpoint rather than “an important year.” A workable first protocol could require a fixed forecast set of marriage registrations and occupational promotions within ±6 or ±12 months, with one window chosen before analysis. Use all eligible person-years, not only celebrated event dates. Specify how overlaps, repeated events and absent events are scored.

Use event-time baselines, shuffled birth times and appropriately matched controls; control for age-dependent event incidence and documentary selection. Split by person, not by event, so one biography cannot leak into training and test data. Rectification using the tested event invalidates a blinded timing test. Freeze permitted natal interpretation in advance and report configurations that fail, not only the best-fitting variant.

### 8.5 Remaining mathematical and source uncertainty

Highest-priority unresolved items are southern/quadrant conventions; the Appendix I subtraction-label issue; exact orbital-latitude aspect constructions; circumpolar domains; moving intermediate cusps; historical mixed-ascension terminology; Fortune's special kinematics; and consistent secondary-motion semi-arcs. These are computational/historical questions, not evidence either for or against predictive efficacy.

The companion open-questions file records the closed book-reading gaps and the remaining primary-source, numerical and empirical checks. The separate morphed-Fortune and temporal-month constructions, the Sahl polarity issue, and the new textual alternatives should be added to the source map before any model-selection exercise. No existing repository or source audit was modified, and no empirical comparison was performed.
