## Persuasion: capacity is distinct from preferred use

The person has a strong demonstrated capacity to persuade effectively when deliberately choosing to do so. They can identify framing, value, leverage, and the presentation most likely to move a particular audience.

This capacity is not normally exercised maximally in every interaction.

- Deliberate persuasion may feel manipulative or ethically intrusive when unnecessary.
- In private one-to-one communication, the person often prefers to state what they regard as true and allow the other person to decide rather than optimize every phrase for conversion.
- In public writing, guides, articles, and advocacy intended to change understanding or behavior, deliberate persuasive framing is used substantially more often.
- The tendency to restrain persuasion is therefore a use/preference variable, not evidence that the capacity is absent.

This is a clarification of the V3.2 statement, not a reversal.

Behavioral confidence: 0.90.
Revision class: R1 contextual sharpening.
Independent confirmation eligible: no for this clarification.

## Energy: engaging work and overload are not opposites

The established energy description remains unchanged in substance.

- Meaningful, self-chosen, highly engaging work can sustain or renew activity at ordinary-to-high workloads for substantial periods.
- The person can also produce very large concentrated bursts of output.
- Sufficiently extreme, prolonged, or repeated overwork can produce disproportionate depletion and require genuine stopping, retreat, sleep, or recovery.
- Therefore `engaging work can sustain me` and `enormous/prolonged bursts can deplete me` are simultaneously true at different intensity/duration ranges.

A questionnaire that forces those statements into mutually exclusive alternatives is malformed for this target and must not be used to claim a behavioral revision.

Behavioral confidence: 0.90.
Revision class: R0 measurement correction / no substantive target change.
Independent confirmation eligible: not applicable.

## Immediate somatic signals: behavior first, HD label second

The person does not claim to know what a Human Design `Spleen` feels like phenomenologically and should not be required to identify a sensation with an HD term.

The behavioral observation remains:

- quick bodily impressions do occur;
- they can be subtle, easy to miss, or difficult to interpret;
- important decisions can sometimes produce an immediate sense of safety/danger, correctness/misalignment, or attraction/aversion before extended reasoning;
- the impression may not repeat in exactly the same form;
- the person is uncertain which rapid bodily impressions, if any, correspond to the HD construct called Splenic Authority.

The uncertainty concerns the symbolic attribution, not the existence of rapid somatic impressions.

Behavioral confidence for the phenomenology: 0.80.
Confidence that the person can self-label a signal as `Splenic`: 0.00; this label is not a scored behavioral observation.
Revision class: R2 construct/label disambiguation.
Independent confirmation eligible: no for this clarification.

## Invitation/recognition is domain-sensitive

Recognition/invitation sensitivity remains strong for interpersonal entry, romance, communal roles, coordination with other people, and externally conferred positions.

It is not a rule that the person waits for an invitation before initiating their own research, writing, experiments, businesses, software, or other self-authored projects.

The target must distinguish external interpersonal entry from self-initiated project creation.

Behavioral confidence: 0.85.
Revision class: R1 contextual sharpening.
Independent confirmation eligible: no.

## Questionnaire development audit

The 18-item concealed-direction questionnaire collected on 2026-08-22 is retained as a measurement-development exercise, not as a clean independent validation set.

Reasons:

1. most constructs had already been elicited in V3.2/V3.5;
2. several answer options incorrectly treated coexisting/context-dependent behaviors as mutually exclusive;
3. Q1 selected decisions partly by later outcome even though decision phenomenology and long-term outcome are distinct constructs;
4. Q16 incorrectly opposed engaging-work sustainability to depletion under extreme bursts;
5. autobiographical prompts such as `last N important opportunities` would introduce unstable retrospective selection and must not be substituted as fake precision.

The questionnaire may clarify wording where it reproduces existing evidence, but it receives zero confirmatory status as a test of the NetInformation-first rule.
