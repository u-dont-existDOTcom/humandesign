## V3.5 discriminator refinements

### Caring / nourishment is selective, not a generalized propulsion

The person can care intensely and take substantial responsibility, but noticing that something could be supported or nourished does not by itself create a broad, automatic drive to intervene.

The impulse to care is strongly conditional on context, especially:

- existing personal investment;
- whether the person or project genuinely matters;
- whether the user feels uniquely suited, specifically needed, or has accepted responsibility;
- whether intervention has meaningful leverage.

A detected need can be ignored comfortably when those conditions are absent. The ability to nurture therefore must not be conflated with nourishment/care as an independent universal motivational driver.

Revision class: R2 construct disambiguation.
Selection risk: moderate because candidate differences were already visible.
Behavioral confidence: 0.75.
Independent confirmation eligible: no.

### Recurrent-question process is selective to consequential mysteries

The person does not report a generalized tendency for every unresolved idea to cycle repeatedly. However, certain important existential, structural, technical, or personally consequential mysteries can recur over months or years after periods of being set aside.

The pattern is best described as:

- the problem may be put down for substantial periods;
- it can later return to attention without external prompting;
- repeated engagement continues until a formulation or degree of understanding becomes internally satisfying;
- many ordinary questions do not behave this way.

This is especially strong for existential and reality-level questions and was more affectively urgent in childhood.

Revision class: R1 contextual sharpening.
Selection risk: moderate.
Behavioral confidence: 0.75.
Independent confirmation eligible: no.

### Developmental relationship to mystery

A major developmental pattern concerns learning to tolerate unresolved important puzzles and mysteries.

In childhood, a consequential problem or contradiction could become extremely difficult to release. The user recalls becoming highly distressed by errors in mathematics materials when forced to engage with something believed wrong. Important puzzles could feel emotionally urgent.

In adulthood, the person remains deeply interested in difficult mysteries but has developed much greater capacity to leave even bizarre or unresolved questions unanswered for long periods without the same distress. The attraction to mystery remains; the compulsive need for immediate closure has reduced.

Revision class: R1 contextual sharpening with developmental evidence.
Selection risk: moderate.
Behavioral confidence: 0.75.
Independent confirmation eligible: no.

### Ambition, advancement, and skill-development clarification

The person did pursue high performance in chess, programming, and school, including attempts to sharpen skills and earn excellent grades. These behaviors should not be interpreted automatically as status competition or hierarchical/material ambition.

The reported motive was primarily:

- learning;
- doing one's best;
- competence;
- solving the activity's intrinsic problem;
- maximizing development in an interesting domain.

Competitive settings may be enjoyed, but dominance, prestige, rank, and advancement are not established as stable independent motives.

Strategic alliance/resource awareness and enterprise competence remain real capacities, and may be developmentally important, but they should be scored separately from a generalized drive to rise materially or socially.

Revision class: R2 construct disambiguation.
Selection risk: low-to-moderate because this clarification includes candidate-discordant evidence as well as candidate-concordant evidence.
Behavioral confidence: 0.75.
Independent confirmation eligible: no.

## V3.5 scoring-relevant rules

1. Remove or downweight any mapping that treats generic caring capacity as evidence for a broad, internally generated nourishment drive.
2. Preserve selective caregiving as a real behavior but require context-specific predicates.
3. Positive evidence for recurrent-question processes must be restricted to consequential mysteries and repeated return after periods of disengagement; ordinary curiosity is insufficient.
4. Developmental questions involving mystery tolerance should be modeled separately from static trait questions.
5. Skill pursuit must not be treated as material/status ambition unless independent evidence establishes that motive.
6. Competitive activities such as chess do not, by themselves, establish dominance motivation.
7. Unknown/depends answers remain unscored rather than forced into one side.
8. Any numeric weights assigned after these answers were observed are exploratory/descriptive unless frozen before a future rerun.
9. All accepted V3.5 refinements require a full-universe rerun rather than finalist-only rescoring.
10. Preserve V3.4 and earlier results in the audit trail.
