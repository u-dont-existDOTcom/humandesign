# 26 — Retrospective Relationship Profile Model

Status: descriptive development model only. It is designed to fit and organize known relationship histories, not to claim prospective predictive validity.

## Existing-work scan

No single established relationship model cleanly spans all of the development targets that proved important in the three-pair audit: sexual/romantic passion, confiding/understanding, emotional conflict/ambivalence, and engulfment/dependent closeness.

The strongest established candidates were:

- Sternberg Triangular Theory of Love: intimacy, passion, commitment. Useful but too coarse for engulfment/conflict and for separating friendship-like love from possessive love.
- Olson Circumplex/FACES: cohesion/enmeshment, flexibility, communication. Strong for engulfment/autonomy and relationship-system structure but weak for erotic/romantic passion.
- PREPARE/ENRICH typologies: broad multidomain couple assessment including sexual relationship, communication, conflict, finances, leisure, family/friends, roles, etc. Empirically useful but global typologies are too coarse for the current target distinctions.
- Davis–Todd Relationship Rating Form (RRF): viability, intimacy, care, passion, satisfaction, commitment, conflict/ambivalence. Its Passion construct includes fascination, exclusiveness, and sexual intimacy; Intimacy includes confiding and understanding.
- Lee/Hendrick Love Attitudes Scale: Eros (romantic/passionate), Storge (friendship/companionate), Mania (possessive/dependent), plus Ludus, Pragma, and Agape.

Davis & Latty-Mann (1987) explicitly studied the Lee/Hendrick love styles against Davis/Todd relationship-quality dimensions, so combining these is an adaptation of directly linked established work rather than an arbitrary new taxonomy.

Decision: use **RRF + Love-Style overlay** as the best retrospective descriptive model. Treat it as a profile, not a scalar score.

## Core profile axes

### RRF axes

- `passion`: fascination, exclusiveness/specialness, sexual intimacy;
- `intimacy`: confiding, understanding, feeling known;
- `care`: assistance, advocacy, willingness to give/support;
- `viability`: acceptance/tolerance, respect, trust;
- `satisfaction`: enjoyment/success/reciprocity/esteem at the relationship level;
- `commitment`: intention/expectation of maintaining the relationship;
- `conflict_ambivalence`: conflict plus mixed/contradictory feelings.

### Love-style overlay

- `eros`: erotic/romantic attraction and passionate love;
- `storge`: friendship-like/companionate affection;
- `mania`: possessive/dependent/uncertainty-driven love; use only as an imperfect analogue of engulfment, not as a clinical label;
- `agape`: self-giving/care-oriented love;
- `ludus`: low-involvement/game-playing style;
- `pragma`: practical/criteria-based partner selection.

## Why this fits the current development cases better

The current three cases require at least three distinct processes that the prior AstroHD comparison collapsed:

1. **Erotic/romantic intensity** — captured by RRF Passion + Eros.
2. **Understanding/communication intimacy** — captured by RRF Intimacy, especially confiding and understanding.
3. **Engulfing/possessive intensity** — approximated by Mania and kept separate from Eros/Passion.
4. **Emotional ease versus friction** — captured primarily by Conflict/Ambivalence and must remain separate from Intimacy.

This allows a relationship to be simultaneously very high in Eros/Passion and Mania/engulfment but low in emotional ease; another can be high in Intimacy/Storge while lower in Eros; an early relationship can be low in known Intimacy because disclosure is sparse while remaining unknown on Passion.

## Three-pair retrospective fit

The development-case mapping remains pseudonymous in repository artifacts.

### Pair 1 — Erotic–manic/high-conflict profile

Observed pattern supports:

- `passion`: very high;
- `eros`: very high;
- `mania`: highest of the three as a descriptive analogue of engulfment/dependent closeness;
- `conflict_ambivalence`: highest of the three based on lowest emotional ease;
- `intimacy`: lower than Pair 2 on confiding/understanding/communication quality, but not necessarily globally low;
- `care`, `viability`, `commitment`, `satisfaction`: require separate historical coding rather than inference from passion.

This profile explains how the same relationship can be the strongest erotic/romantic bond and also the most engulfing and emotionally difficult.

### Pair 2 — Companionate/intimacy-dominant profile

Observed pattern supports:

- `intimacy`: clearly highest of the three on communication/intellectual understanding;
- `storge`: high as a friendship/companionate descriptor;
- `passion`/`eros`: below Pair 1;
- `mania`: middle of the three by reported engulfment;
- `conflict_ambivalence`: middle of the three by emotional ease;
- `care`, `viability`, `commitment`, `satisfaction`: require separate coding.

This profile explains a relationship that can contain unusually strong understanding/companionship while being less intensely erotic/romantic than Pair 1.

### Pair 3 — Early low-information/autonomous profile

Observed pattern currently supports:

- `intimacy`: low or unresolved **at present** because confiding/understanding is limited by opacity and intermittent communication;
- `mania`: lowest so far by reported engulfment, with low confidence because exposure is early;
- `conflict_ambivalence`: low so far in outward interaction, corresponding to highest outward emotional ease;
- `passion`/`eros`: unresolved due insufficient mature evidence;
- `storge`: unresolved;
- `care`, `viability`, `commitment`, `satisfaction`: unresolved.

Strong declarations of love followed by days of silence should not be forced into either high commitment or avoidant attachment. The model records sparse disclosure/low current intimacy separately from expressed affection.

## Relation to Acevedo–Aron romantic-love distinction

Acevedo & Aron (2009) distinguish:

- romantic love: intensity, engagement, and sexual chemistry without obsession;
- passionate/obsessive love: romantic love plus obsessive/uncertain features;
- companionate love: calmer friendship-type attachment.

That distinction provides a useful high-level retrospective shorthand for the first two development cases:

- Pair 1 resembles **high romantic/passionate love with a larger obsessive/engulfing component**;
- Pair 2 resembles **more companionate/intimacy-centered love with lower erotic intensity**.

This is descriptive resemblance only, not a diagnosis or a claim that either partner individually has a fixed love style.

## Why not collapse to one compatibility score

Two relationships can trade off:

- Pair 1: stronger sex/romance, greater engulfment/friction;
- Pair 2: stronger understanding/communication, lower erotic intensity;
- Pair 3: lower current engulfment/easier outward emotional field but insufficient evidence on mature passion/intimacy.

A global score would hide the exact distinctions that falsified the prior AstroHD ranking.

## Prospective use

This model can organize retrospective histories now, but it must not be treated as validated prediction merely because it fits them.

For future cases:

1. collect RRF-style and love-style observations independently of birth-derived predictions;
2. freeze R-HD/R-WA/R-AstroHD predictions before relationship outcomes are revealed;
3. test whether symbolic models predict the separate profile axes;
4. preserve failed domain predictions;
5. do not refit the symbolic meanings on these same three development relationships and report the fit as validation.
