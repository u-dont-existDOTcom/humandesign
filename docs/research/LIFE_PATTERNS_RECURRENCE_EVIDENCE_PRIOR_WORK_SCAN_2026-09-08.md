## Independent conception preserved before scan

The owner correction was preserved first in `state/LIFE-PATTERNS-RECURRENCE-EVIDENCE-CORRECTION-2026-09-08.md`:

- a generalized behavioral statement such as `I always X` is recurrence self-report evidence;
- a confirming concrete incident elicited because the participant already asserted the pattern is conditionally selected and is not an independent frequency observation;
- useful follow-up should target scope/denominator, exceptions, exception frequency, temporal/context boundaries, or another uncertainty that can change the inference;
- concrete incidents should be requested only when they add discriminating information or arise from a sampling design that makes them frequency-informative.

This snapshot predates the external scan below and is not rewritten by it.

## External existing-work scan

A bounded scholarly scan was run on the underlying methodological problem rather than the project terminology.

### Behavioral-frequency response strategies

**Conrad, Brown & Cashman (1998), “Strategies for estimating behavioural frequency in survey interviews,” _Memory_, DOI 10.1080/741942603.**

Respondents use different strategies for behavioral-frequency questions depending on event regularity, similarity, and frequency: episode enumeration, rate estimation, or conversion of a general impression into a number. This supports keeping the *response process and evidence basis* visible rather than assuming every frequency answer is an episode count.

### Memory/context in frequency judgment

**Menon & Yorkston (1999), “The Use of Memory and Contextual Cues in the Formation of Behavioral Frequency Judgments.”**

Behavioral frequency judgments can be constructed from memory and contextual cues rather than literal enumeration. This supports preserving ordinary-language recurrence self-report as a distinct evidence form while not silently upgrading it into a verified opportunity-level rate.

### Probing can help or harm depending on design

**Foddy (1995), “Probing: A dangerous practice in social surveys?”, _Quality & Quantity_, DOI 10.1007/BF01107985.**

Probes do not automatically improve data and can create avoidable interviewer effects; response frameworks and key terms should be defined so probes are systematic and purposeful rather than ad hoc. This is consistent with an information-gain gate rather than ritual probing.

**Priede, Jokinen, Ruuskanen & Farrall (2014), “Which probes are most useful when undertaking cognitive interviews,” _International Journal of Social Research Methodology_, DOI 10.1080/13645579.2013.799795.**

Anticipated, emergent, and conditional probes were especially useful in their cognitive-interviewing study. This supports conditional follow-up tied to actual ambiguity/boundary conditions rather than mechanically demanding one probe form for every answer.

### Cognitive interviewing as interpretation validation

**Scott et al. (2021), “The devil is in the detail: reflections on the value and application of cognitive interviewing to strengthen quantitative surveys in global health,” _Health Policy and Planning_, DOI 10.1093/heapol/czab048.**

Cognitive interviewing tests how respondents understand questions and formulate answers, and supports iterative revision where researcher intent and respondent interpretation diverge. This is directly relevant to checking what participants mean by quantifiers such as `always`, `usually`, or `sometimes` rather than imposing a literal formal interpretation.

**Campanelli et al. (2016), “Cognitive interviewing as tool for enhancing the accuracy of the interpretation of quantitative findings,” _Quality & Quantity_, DOI 10.1007/s11135-015-0188-y.**

Cognitive follow-up can reveal response processes that are invisible in aggregate response distributions. This supports preserving how a recurrence judgment was formed rather than collapsing all recurrence evidence into a single count-like field.

### Conversational clarification

**West et al. (2018), “Can conversational interviewing improve survey response quality without increasing interviewer effects,” _Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series A_, DOI 10.1111/rssa.12255.**

Conversational clarification can reduce response bias for complex questions without the feared magnitude of interviewer effects in that study. This supports bounded clarification of scope and meaning when needed, while preserving neutral/non-leading constraints.

### Interview reports versus behavior

**Jerolmack & Khan (2014), “Talk Is Cheap: Ethnography and the Attitudinal Fallacy,” _Sociological Methods & Research_, DOI 10.1177/0049124114523396.**

The paper warns against treating verbal accounts as equivalent to independently observed situated behavior. For this project that means direct behavioral self-report should remain evidence **of reported recurrence**, not be mislabeled as external verification of actual opportunity-level frequency.

## Existing-work map

### Already supported by prior work

- behavioral-frequency answers need not arise from episode enumeration;
- interviewer probes can alter data quality and should be purposeful/systematic;
- clarification of respondent interpretation is a legitimate measurement-development function;
- verbal self-report and independently observed behavior are distinct evidence classes.

### Partially solved / needs project adaptation

The literature does not supply a drop-in Life Patterns representation for combining:

- generalized recurrence self-report;
- explicit exceptions and boundary conditions;
- concrete episode evidence;
- exact-source provenance;
- sampled/prospective opportunity evidence;
- later theory-blind coding and target-model isolation.

### Novel remainder for this project

A versioned recurrence evidence contract that preserves those evidence roles separately and prevents self-selected confirming anecdotes from being counted as independent frequency evidence while still retaining direct recurrence self-report.

## Disposition

**COMPOSE / ADAPT.**

Reuse survey-memory and cognitive-interviewing principles for response-process awareness and purposeful probing; preserve the project’s existing exact-source/blinding/provenance architecture; add a project-specific recurrence evidence contract as the unresolved remainder.

## Strongest baseline for the revision

The revised method must beat the current episode-centric rule on the following simple baseline criterion:

> Given `I always X`, can the method preserve that direct recurrence report, identify its scope and possible exceptions, avoid counting a prompted confirming anecdote as independent frequency evidence, and distinguish self-reported recurrence from sampled/externally observed frequency?

If it cannot, added schema complexity is not justified.

## Scientific boundary

This scan concerns **measurement of reported behavioral recurrence**. It does not establish that a participant’s self-reported frequency is objectively accurate, and it does not authorize using target-model agreement to tune the policy.
