## Purpose

This protocol defines the **conventional human-human benchmark route** for testing whether independent theory-blind human coders can reproducibly recover the reconciled codebook's episode boundaries, prerequisites, substantive values, missingness, sequence, context, and derived summaries from first-person autobiographical evidence.

It remains scientifically valid when the project chooses a full human benchmark. It is not required merely to begin development, and it is not the only route to validation-candidate authority. The alternative LLM-primary/human-calibrated route is specified separately.

The protocol is a measurement-development/validation procedure. It does not test Human Design, AstroHD, astrology, any birth-derived model, participant benefit, or construct validity.

## Non-negotiable blind

Before all first-pass coding and development adjudication are frozen, coders, trainers, adjudicators, and anyone making substantive codebook revisions must not receive:

- participant birth data;
- chart outputs;
- Human Design/AstroHD mappings or predictions;
- astrology target-model mappings or predictions;
- candidate-model scores, rankings, residuals, or error patterns;
- any statement about which neutral observables would favor or disfavor a target model.

The pilot corpus may contain ordinary autobiographical content, including spontaneous participant references to astrology/HD if those occurred in the original source. Such references are retained as source provenance and flagged; they are not converted into target-model information for the coder.

## Frozen inputs before coding

Freeze and hash, before the first reliability pass:

1. exact reconciled codebook candidate;
2. exact coding-procedure instructions;
3. exact pilot corpus manifest;
4. exact episode/source evidence presented to coders;
5. training examples and answer key;
6. coder training receipts;
7. annotation exchange schema/tool version;
8. prespecified reliability outputs and disagreement taxonomy.

No first-pass codebook wording may change while coding is underway. Any necessary clarification discovered during coding is logged and deferred to the post-pilot revision phase unless the run is explicitly abandoned and restarted under a new frozen procedure.

## Pilot corpus

### Development-only evidence

Use only development evidence. Do not use the later confirmatory validation corpus for codebook repair.

The corpus should include ordinary participant-approved behavioral episodes where available plus deliberately selected **development boundary cases** needed to exercise the unresolved U1–U14 distinctions in the reconciled candidate.

### Selection rules

Corpus selection may use only theory-neutral criteria:

- episode length/detail;
- presence of observable prerequisites;
- non-action ambiguity;
- multi-step sequences;
- candidate overlap/confusion pairs named in the reconciled codebook;
- rarity of a prerequisite;
- diversity of contexts/life periods/language/modality;
- duplicate/retelling detection;
- data quality.

Selection must not use birth data, model outputs, model-fit information, or whether an episode appears favorable to any target theory.

### Training cases versus reliability cases

Keep two sets separate:

- **training/adjudication examples:** may be synthetic or development episodes and may be discussed with coders;
- **reliability set:** coded independently without discussion until both first-pass outputs are frozen.

Training cases must never be counted in the first-pass reliability estimates.

### Operational starting target for this route

If the project deliberately chooses the conventional human-human route, use two independent human coders and a corpus large enough to exercise every common observable and each high-priority confusion pair multiple times. As an operational planning target, roughly **60–100 independently codable episodes** is reasonable for an initial development pass, rather than forcing equal counts per observable.

This target applies only to this route. It is **not** a prerequisite for the LLM-primary/human-calibrated development workflow.

Sparse prerequisites such as recognized possible-loss exposure, recognized error, interpersonal repair, or explicit rule/procedure response should be reported as sparse. Do **not** weaken prerequisites to fill cells.

If a sparse-prerequisite extension batch is needed, select it using only the frozen prerequisite definitions and corpus metadata, and report the extension separately so prevalence-enriched reliability is not mistaken for natural prevalence.

This is a development sampling target, not a universal psychometric threshold.

## Coder training

Each human coder receives:

- the exact frozen candidate codebook;
- the coding procedure;
- theory-neutral training examples;
- explicit instruction that abstention is legitimate;
- explicit instruction that narrator-stated causes are not objective causes;
- explicit instruction to preserve sequences and counterevidence;
- explicit instruction to apply the four-part non-action gate;
- explicit instruction not to infer target theories or personality types.

Training should deliberately cover the highest-risk boundary distinctions:

- R03 vs R04;
- R05 option-set vs resolution facets;
- R07 vs R08;
- R07 vs R09;
- R10 competition types;
- R11 immediate vs later goal response;
- R13 active checking vs passive feedback;
- R15 private endpoint vs interpersonal commitment;
- R16 vs R17;
- R18 vs R19;
- R20 vs R21;
- IE vs NA;
- qualifying non-action vs unknown/nonmentioned behavior;
- temporal precedence vs narrator-explicit influence.

A coder training receipt records coder ID, training version/hash, completion time, and blind attestations. It does not claim competence merely because training was completed.

## Independent first pass

1. Coders segment episodes before assigning substantive codes.
2. Coders work independently and may not see each other's labels, notes, or uncertainty judgments.
3. Each coder records prerequisite/applicability before substantive value.
4. Non-action is coded only after awareness, opportunity, feasibility, and established non-action are each satisfied.
5. Multi-step behavior is recorded in order rather than collapsed to the final action.
6. Missingness and uncertainty flags remain explicit.
7. Context modifiers are extracted separately from substantive values.
8. Narrator-explicit influence and temporal precedence are separate fields.
9. Counterepisodes are identified independently.
10. First-pass outputs are frozen before any coder discussion or adjudication.

## Reliability outputs

Do not reduce the pilot to one coefficient.

Report separately:

### Episode segmentation

- exact boundary agreement;
- duplicate/retelling identity agreement;
- disagreements by segmentation error category.

### Observable applicability/prerequisites

For every primary observable:

- number of opportunities assessed;
- applicable / not-applicable / insufficient distribution;
- raw agreement;
- confusion matrix;
- chance-corrected agreement where estimable;
- abstention/missingness rates.

### Substantive values

For each observable/facet with adequate data:

- double-coded N;
- class distribution/prevalence;
- raw agreement;
- Krippendorff-style nominal agreement statistic where estimable;
- Gwet-style agreement coefficient where estimable;
- full confusion matrix;
- `other specified` rate;
- disagreement categories.

Do not interpret an apparently high coefficient without the class distribution and raw confusion pattern.

### Sequence coding

Report separately:

- agreement that multiple steps exist;
- agreement on the set of coded steps;
- exact order agreement;
- common sequence-collapse errors.

### Missingness / non-action

Report separately for:

- awareness;
- opportunity;
- feasibility;
- defined window / established non-action;
- IE vs NA;
- each missingness flag.

### Context and derived summaries

Report separately:

- context-modifier agreement;
- narrator-explicit influence vs temporal precedence;
- counterepisode identification;
- recurrence determination;
- context split vs mixed;
- repeated-condition summary;
- temporal-change summary.

Derived summaries are evaluated only after primary episode coding has been frozen.

### Coder burden

Record:

- coding time per episode;
- number of observables considered/applicable;
- forced-decision complaints;
- clarification requests;
- skipped fields;
- `other specified` usage;
- free-text notes on ambiguous boundaries.

Coder burden is a measurement-development signal, not a reason to delete constructs solely for convenience.

## Disagreement taxonomy

Every disagreement should be classifiable, where possible, into one or more of:

- episode-boundary disagreement;
- prerequisite disagreement;
- awareness/opportunity/feasibility disagreement;
- IE-vs-NA disagreement;
- competing observable applicability;
- substantive value confusion;
- sequence/order disagreement;
- narrator-causal-link disagreement;
- context-modifier disagreement;
- duplicate/retelling disagreement;
- unclear codebook wording;
- missing source evidence;
- coder oversight;
- other specified.

The taxonomy itself may be revised only in a new version after the first pass is frozen.

## Adjudication

Reliability is calculated on **pre-adjudication** independent outputs.

After those outputs are frozen:

1. coders may review disagreements;
2. if consensus is reached, preserve both original codes and the consensus result;
3. unresolved cases may be sent to a third theory-blind adjudicator if available;
4. adjudication reason and affected codebook language are logged;
5. adjudicated labels may form a development reference set, but must never replace or erase the original independent labels.

Adjudication rate is itself reported.

## Revision rules after pilot

A v2 revision may be justified by blind development evidence such as:

- persistently indistinguishable neighboring codes;
- high prerequisite disagreement;
- ambiguous wording producing recurrent errors;
- excessive `other specified` use;
- repeated sequence collapse;
- systematic IE/NA confusion;
- unreliable context distinctions;
- empirically empty or extremely sparse constructs;
- coder burden that produces demonstrable omissions/errors.

Substantive v2 changes must occur in a theory-blind revision context. The exact v1 reconciled candidate, pilot manifest, raw coder outputs, reliability report, adjudications, and revision rationale remain preserved.

No revision may be made because a change improves correspondence with Human Design, AstroHD, astrology, another candidate model, or any model-fit result.

## Validation-candidate promotion under the conventional route

If this conventional human-human route is chosen, promotion requires, at minimum:

- exact content/provenance freeze;
- independent theory-blind replication/reconciliation requirement satisfied;
- completed blind human double-coding;
- reliability report bound to the exact ontology/coding procedure/corpus;
- documented handling of unreliable or ambiguous distinctions;
- a final theory-blind content review/revision decision;
- final validation-candidate version frozen after development and before any target-model scoring.

The project does not predeclare a universal numeric coefficient cutoff. Promotion decisions must inspect raw agreement, prevalence, confusion matrices, abstention, error categories, and the consequences of disagreement, and must be made before target-model results exist.

Alternative validation routes are governed by `LIFE_PATTERNS_LLM_PRIMARY_CODING_PROTOCOL.md` and the machine-checkable theory-blind authority contracts. LLM self-consistency alone is never sufficient for validation.
