## 1. A significant independent modern ethnographic qualification

**Olivier Le Guen and Lorena Ildefonsa Pool Balam (2012)**, *No Metaphorical Timeline in Gesture and Cognition Among Yucatec Mayas*, Frontiers in Psychology 3, [DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00271](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00271), report Yucatec Maya-language fieldwork in **Kopchen and Chemax**. In *Cultural Background and Forms of Time-Keeping Among Yucatec Mayas*, they contrast contemporary Gregorian, agricultural and religious calendar use with continued 260-day sacred counts among **some other** Maya communities. Their assertion that this count is not used among modern Yucatec Mayas cites **Villa Rojas 1945**. It is contextual background in a paper on language and gesture, **not a census of every Yucatec community or a 2026 field check of natal daykeepers**.

The colonial Yucatec books compared in [B07](MAYA_B07_ORIGINAL_YUCATEC_NATAL_PREDICTIONS_SOURCE_AUDIT_20261022.md) explicitly describe **people born on Chuen** as craft makers. Existence of the historical **natal text** is genuinely established, but **an uninterrupted 260-day natal daykeeping tradition in contemporary Yucatec communities is NOT established**. This affects external/cultural identity of a test, not its ability to falsify a declared historical-reconstruction algorithm.

## 2. Additional historic birth-hour uncertainty

In his scholarly blog post [*What Time of Day Does the Ancient Maya Calendar Commence?*](https://ajchich1.blogspot.com/2012/12/what-time-of-day-does-ancient-maya.html), **Carl D. Callaway (2012)** compares epigraphic interpretations of Puuc-style paired dates: Mathews/MacLeod favor sacred-day **sunset** versus solar Haab/Long Count **dawn** alternatives, while Stuart describes a different **dawn** sacred-turnover interpretation. These are competing explanations of **Classic inscriptions**, not recorded modern Yucatec midwives assigning birth signs. A lone 2026 blog comment suggesting modern noon-to-noon reckoning lacks adequate primary documentation here and is not implemented as a rule.

Likewise, the earlier Guatemala Ministry 2016 sacred-day sunset / solar dawn source concerns Guatemalan traditional/historical contexts, not a universally mandated Yucatec birth-clock convention. Never silently generalize from ancient glyphs or living K'iche' to contemporary Yucatán natal practice.

## 4. The closest testable historical claim and content-validity choice

The original colonial **Kaua, Chan Cah and Códice Pérez** natal witness comparison favors **Chuen/CHUWEN** artisan, weaving, woodworking, maker behavior as the source-faithful single sign. Men weaving vocabulary is variable; Caban carpentry appears only in the longer Kaua wording, while short variants emphasize trade/travel. The modern investigator-invented three-sign artisan class is exploratory and cannot rescue failure of Chuen alone.

B07 exposed a study-design error: the draft question counted only hobby crafts and excluded paid occupations, while the historical source describes carpenters and weavers. The new [B08 study draft](MAYA_B08_CHUWEN_HISTORIC_RECONSTRUCTION_PILOT_V1.json) includes **paid AND unpaid tangible object-making** over 12 months, excludes generic cleaning/cooking, separates paid/voluntary activities and collects craft opportunity, occupation, region, education and language without pretending these are independent natal successes. The English and French item versions remain **UNFROZEN**; a competent Yucatec translation has **not** been made. Modern craft-hours are an operational proxy, **not a proven faithful translation** of colonial person descriptions.

A primary one-sided contrast (ordinal craft-hours mean difference, Chuen vs pooled other 19 day signs) and a prespecified chart-label permutation/nonastrology baseline are candidate choices **only**, not a final statistical preregistration. The model, seed/decoys, question versions, missingness, thresholds, birth policy and analysis must be hashed/frozen **before** any new participant answers. No existing known development person's chart/profile is an untouched test.

A random sample of **N=60** gives ~**3 Chuen birthdays** on average, and under uniform independent dates there is a ~**4.6%** chance of zero Chuen births. Such a cohort is a **feasibility/process pilot**, not decisive evidence for or against ordinary-sized effects. Prior Gaussian approximate power planning for a hypothetical standardized difference d=0.3, alpha=.05, 80% power and 5% positives suggested **about 1,836 unscreened people**; this is not a measured effect or a commitment to recruit that number.
