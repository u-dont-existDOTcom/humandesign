# Content-validity field review — Chuen source-derived craft measurement (B10)

**23 October 2026 · READY TO USE FOR COGNITIVE REVIEW, NOT AN APPROVED STUDY OR FROZEN HUMAN PREDICTION**

## What this review is actually assessing

The underlying **colonial Yucatec Kaua** birth-day statement associates people born on **Chuen** with skilled carpentry, weaving, and making things. **Thompson (1950), p.80**, independently reproduces an earlier Barrera Vásquez rendering, but this and Bolles derive from the **same Kaua historical lineage**. The words support a **historical meaning**. They provide zero independent human outcome results.

The exact open scientific question for a modern behavioural test is which single **non-stigmatizing observable proxy** most closely translates that historical association. Historical "artisan" may refer to *trained skill/profession*, *doing crafts*, or *auspicious ritual arts* depending on the source. We must not equate them without review.

Established measurement work should be reused rather than redefining "creative personality": the [ILO ISCO-08](https://isco.ilo.org/en/isco-08/) codes jobs by *tasks* and explicitly includes craft workers; the [BLS American Time Use Survey](https://www.bls.gov/tus/lexicons.htm) provides category **120309: leisure arts/crafts**, but **NOT** the paid artisan work referenced in the source. The [COSMIN 2018 methodology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5891557/) distinguishes **relevance**, **comprehensiveness** and **comprehensibility** of an instrument. We apply those three general design concepts *by adaptation*, not claim official COSMIN certification.

## Candidate constructs before source-aware outcome freeze

| ID | Proposed measure | Why it matches source | Key vulnerability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** — preferred for source fidelity | Skilled crafts as an actual current/recent work or apprenticeship role, independently coded with a **narrow subset** of ISCO-08 task codes | Directly tests "carpenter / weaver / maker" as an occupation/learned trade | Excludes some skilled unpaid artisans, depends on employment opportunity and local title coding |
| **C2** | How often the respondent physically made or repaired a tangible object in the previous **four weeks**, paid **or** unpaid | Measurable repeated material-making behavior, less remote recall | Misses irregular/seasonal practice; not a direct measure of competence |
| **C3** — former B09 primary draft | Recall total craft hours over **12 months**, including paid and unpaid | Captures lifetime role exposure in one familiar response format | Major retrospective error/paid work dominance; source does not specify hours |
| **C4** — checking measure | Seven-day activity log separating work, unpaid handicraft and routine chores | Short recall, can audit C2/C3 comprehension and classification | Time window too brief to define a lifetime artisan role; participation may be zero for a true craft worker |

The recommended **draft default** is C1 for literal historical fidelity, C2 as an important sensitivity measure, and C4 for methodological/coding validation. This is *not a preregistered decision*, and it does **not** make occupation causally attributable to a birth sign. A pre-outcome content-validity reviewer may instead recommend C2 as the single primary if C1 discriminates opportunity rather than skill; record the reason **before collecting any natal comparison outcomes**.

No birth date or Maya day is collected during this content review. Ask each reviewer and cognitive tester **only** about interpretation and measurement, not their actual astrological classification.

## Cognitive interview instructions for 3–6 adult development testers, if available

This small range is a **practical development suggestion**, not a recognized statistical power threshold or a COSMIN requirement. Their answers cannot later count as untouched natal prediction validations.

1. Ask the participant to **think aloud**, without mentioning Chuen or forecasting any personal qualities: “What counts as making something with your hands?” and “What would you exclude?”
2. Present C1 and C2 items in neutral wording. Have them explain in their own words what a paid artisan, an unpaid family repair, casual sewing, cooking, industrial machinery monitoring, and digital art would count as. **Do not correct or coach first.**
3. Test the reference period: distinguish **last four weeks** from **a typical month**, and current/previous roles from lifetime interest. Note how a seasonal craft worker would answer.
4. Ask whether the response options are feasible across languages (English/French) and contexts (informal apprenticeship, self-employment, subsistence weaving, occasional repairs). Ask about privacy discomfort, confusion and burden.
5. The reviewer records each misunderstanding as an item-level, anonymous issue. No birth information, civil identity, chart labels, or actual Chuen status is necessary.
6. Decide which **one** outcome has adequate relevance, coverage and comprehensibility *before* any real scored participant is enrolled. Preselect exclusion/unknown rules and statistical unit.

A two-part summary—**what participants thought the question meant** and **which source predicate it would test**—is more important than a consensus checkbox. Redesigns based on these *development-only* discussions are permissible and must be versioned; redesigns after prospectively scored participants are prohibited for the same cohort.

## Work and leisure questionnaire — neutral unscored candidate text

**Neutral work-task elicitation**: “Describe the main things you did in your work, self-employment, unpaid apprenticeship or regular subsistence production in the last 12 months. Which physical things did you make or repair, if any?” The occupation code is assigned *from actual tasks*, preferably by two blinded coders, not by a claimant labeling themselves an artisan. Examples in the **proposed narrow ISCO core**: **7115** carpenters; **7314** potters; **7317** wood/basketry handicraft; **7318** textile/leather handicraft; **7522** cabinetmakers; **7531** tailors/dressmakers; **7533** sewing/embroidery work. This subset is **our** source-specific selection of recognized occupation codes, not an officially endorsed ILO definition of Maya natal Chuen.

**Neutral recent making activity**: “In the last 4 weeks, on how many separate occasions have you used your hands and tools to make, weave, sew, carve, construct or materially repair an object—including paid and unpaid work, but not cooking, cleaning, or monitoring automatic machines?” Responses: zero, one, two/three, four to seven, eight or more, unable to estimate, prefer not. Ensure a separate item for **craft practice during other parts of the year** to flag seasonality without cherry-picking the main result.

**Optional short diary**: log day, approximate minutes, type of object/task and paid/voluntary/subsistence category over seven consecutive days. BLS ATUS hobby-category code **120309** applies only where craft is **leisure**; the **paid** task belongs in its work activities, not that leisure bucket. Photography, social-media production and general digital design are not automatically tangible Chuen-making tasks.

## Reviewer rubric (completed before any predictive enrollment)

| Dimension | Reviewer records | Decision meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Historical source fidelity | Which exact Chuen words does the measure reflect? Which additional meanings were inferred? | Does not conflate legend, auspicious-day ritual and **natal** statement |
| Relevance | Does the primary item capture **carpenter/weaver/maker role or practice**, not moral virtue or generic creativity? | Reject irrelevant or overbroad outcomes |
| Comprehensiveness | Are unpaid, informal, paid and culturally local skilled arts included? Are noncraft chores excluded? | Identify missing relevant classes and examples |
| Comprehensibility | Could English/French participants accurately interpret timeframe, activities, response bins and unclear cases? | Revise before prospective freeze |
| Recall and reliability | Can adults estimate yearly hours? Does four-week seasonality distort? Can task coding be blinded? | Prefer established occupation/diary methods where fitting |
| Equivalence | Is one proposed outcome **one** predeclared contrast with a falsifier? What would count as a negative? | Prevent secondary outcomes rescuing a failed primary |
