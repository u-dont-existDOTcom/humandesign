## Witness 1: treatment of profession / quality of action

- **Location:** Book II, ch. 19 “Concerning the Quality of Action,” **printed pp. 66–67**, PDF p39 L/R.
- **Hephaistio's Ptolemaic-style opening (p66):** select an indicator of the quality of activity from (a) the star closest to the Sun already visible in morning appearance, (b) star upon the Midheaven, especially if receiving a Moon application; consider dominance if both indicators differ; where no planet meets them use a house ruler. The translated source itself has an editorially inserted negative in a conditional sentence at **Schmidt fn.172**, therefore there is a **TEXTUAL_VARIANT / EDITORIAL_REPAIR** gate at this step.
- **Hephaistio's distinct Dorothean account (p67):** order the angles/points as Midheaven → subterranean pivot (IC) → Horoskopos (Ascendant) → Lot of Fortune, explicitly omitting the Descendant. Where the Mars/Venus/Mercury trio does not occupy relevant points, examine the Moon's application and then the Sun relationship; Hephaistio interjects a personal objection to literal Sun application and prefers a visible morning star; the sixth and second signs follow as later fallback contexts. Outcome *type of work* is assigned by the character of Mars, Venus, Mercury, their zodiacal placements and configurations with other planets. This is a **separable, algorithmic candidate**, not one flat generic “profession promise.”
- **Source dependence:** Schmidt's **fn.173 on printed p67** claims this Dorotheus treatment of action does *not survive* in the transmitted Arabic version. Do not elevate this to a proved original Dorotheus reading until compared to the owned `Carmen Astrologicum` edition and Greek citation history. Hephaistio is a witness **about** Dorotheus, not Dorotheus' autograph.
- **Observed action:** verified printed page location, segmented both source text and editorial annotation; **no chart outcomes computed**, no human events inspected and no scoring claim frozen.

## Witness 2: four dignities vs later decan as fifth dignity

- **Location:** Book II ch. 26 “Concerning the Division of the Times,” **printed p79**, PDF p45 R, text plus Schmidt **fn.199**.
- Hephaistio's translated passage enumerates a hierarchy of **domicile, exaltation, trigon (triplicity) and bounds** for selecting a planetary governor. Schmidt's note states that his comparator Arabic Dorotheus / David Pingree translation, Book III ch. 2, adds **decanate**, absent here.
- Schmidt **infers** this fifth dignity was an Arabic-period addition. **This is a translator hypothesis**, not established ancient chronology. Preserve variants `HEL_4` and `DOR_AR_5` as independent readings until exact sources are side-by-side and a normative historical conclusion is adequately justified.

## Additional chapter inventory for reading order

Book II has **36 chapters**; the inspected two-up translated table of contents appears on PDF pp3–4. Source-first order for measuring person-specific predictions:
1. ch. 19 profession (printed p66 onward); ch. 17–18 acquisition and rank, then ch. 21–24 marriage, children, friends, foreign travel and ch.25 death (historical-source only, no high-stakes practical recommendations);
2. ch.26–29 directional/annual/month/day/10-year-nine-month time allocation; ch.30–36 planetary time-lord delineation, to compare to Valens and owned *Persian Nativities IV*;
3. ch.2–3 conception/rectification only after reproducible calculation settings are documented; ch.4–16 parental, natal physical and psychological topics with sensitivity and text-transmission checks.

The main four-versus-five discrepancy relates to time rulers/dignities, not directly to the career selection rule. Do not combine them into one favorable explanatory claim.
