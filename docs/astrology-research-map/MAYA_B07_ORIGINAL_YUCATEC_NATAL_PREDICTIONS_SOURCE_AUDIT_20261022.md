## 2. Provenance: what the parallel variant pages actually show

Bolles constructs **one edited Yucatec passage** from multiple surviving pieces. His original/source-parallel PDF **pp.38–41** prints colonial spellings in separate columns and an edited amalgamation, making provenance auditable. The sources include:

- **Kaua** manuscript loci pp.11–12, 14 and 21–22 — **three passages of one broader manuscript source**, not three statistically independent people or communities.
- **Chan Cah** pp.1–4 — a distinct witness with passages and linguistic variation; it does **not** unambiguously contain every identical attribute.
- **Codex Pérez** pp.94–95 — a compilation whose textual editorial interventions and copying errors Bolles himself discusses.
- **Ixil** p.17b — secondary context only for the first two days, **not** an extra artisan-source replicate.

The matched English PDF renders edited meanings on pp.21–24. Three relevant and comparatively less-stigmatizing birth-day cases were audited **against the *parallel source columns*, not only the polished English version**:

|---|---|---|
| **Chuen / CHUWEN** | Woodworking, weaving, production/making | Specific craft/making wording in Kaua's long pp.11–12 and its p.21–22 variant; related general maker/weaver words in Kaua p.14 and Pérez; Chan Cah text related but not fully independently resolved. Source fidelity: **best** of these three |
| **Men** | Weaving and other maker/speech activities | Longer Kaua passage explicitly describes craft/weaving; shorter variants have overlapping but not identical vocabulary. Possible scribal or translator differences weaken universal wording |

The composite is **historical literary testimony, not an independent demographic sample**. Five columns of manuscript words do not mean five separate real-world observations of craftspeople; the overlapping three Kaua columns are closely dependent. The document's bracketed **world directions** are often supplied by the editor by comparison with *Madrid* and **Fejérváry-Mayer**, not necessarily stated in the same colonial birth-sign witness. Neither should be counted as another original natal predicate.

### Necessary linguistic guardrail

The historical Yucatec `Kan` maps to the converter's **K'AN** and corresponds to Highland K'iche' **K'AT**, *not* to Highland `KAN` (which instead maps to Yucatec Chicchan). Likewise Yucatec `CHUWEN` corresponds to K'iche' `B'ATZ'`; and Yucatec `KAB'AN` corresponds to Highland `NO'J`. A common transliteration string without the tradition-labelled crosswalk is **not** a source-accurate birth chart.

## 3. A falsifiable candidate with visible limits

The best-supported source-local hypothesis here is **Chuen-born respondents show greater involvement in physical object-making/crafting than the other birth-day groups**. This is an **investigator's operationalization** of a historical natal statement. Its strength has *not been measured*. The three-sign composite `Chuen + Men + Caban` is a **new exploratory grouping**, not one found explicitly in the colonial text, and cannot be used to rescue an unsuccessful Chuen-only result.

[Pre-registration *DRAFT* and candidate questions](MAYA_B07_ARTISAN_BLIND_STUDY_DRAFT_V1.json) cover chart assumptions, decoy day shifts, other astrology and nonastrology controls, hidden predictions, self-report domains, opportunity constraints, and missing answers. **Nothing is frozen or running.**

**Critical content-validity problem detected:** the initial prototype question excludes paid/required work, **but the source predicts roles/occupations (woodworker, weaver, maker)**. A strict leisure-only measure could miss source-compatible paid crafts. Before freezing, choose an outcome that covers **paid AND voluntary unpaid** craft, separate their contributions, exclude generic chores by a source-aligned rule, and explicitly pre-review translation into the survey languages; record age, physical opportunity, materials access, vocational training, income/workload, and country/region as potential confounds. Do not silently retrofit the questionnaire after seeing answers.

With a roughly uniform 1/20 occurrence of a single sign, a modest `N=60` process pilot contains only ~3 Chuen birthdays *on average* — unsuitable for claiming predictive success or meaningful absence of effect. A rough unbalanced two-group Gaussian calculation at `d=0.3`, two-sided `alpha=.05`, 80% power and zero attrition estimates **1,836** unscreened people; ordinal outcomes and culture-specific date uncertainty may require more. A designed enriched/blinded Chuen/control cohort may need fewer completed respondents but still costs screening and requires source-based birth-date and hour policy verification.

Predeclared matched alternatives and nonastrology base rates must be calculated **before** seeing scored responses; `GMT584285`, sunset variants, shifted birthdays and merged “artisan” labels cannot be selected after seeing which yields favorable results. Participants already involved in development cannot become untouched validation cases by recoding.
