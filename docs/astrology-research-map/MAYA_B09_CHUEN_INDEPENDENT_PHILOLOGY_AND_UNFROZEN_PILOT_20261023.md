## 1. A genuinely earlier *translation* of the same colonial Yucatec source

The independently published linguistic source needed after B07/B08 has now been examined **at the photographed original page**, not just in a later summary:

**J. Eric S. Thompson**, *Maya Hieroglyphic Writing* (1950), chapter 3 *The Cycle of 260 Days*, **printed p.80 / chapter PDF p.15**, [source chapter facsimile](https://www.mesoweb.org/publications/Thompson/Thompson1950-Chapter3.pdf), downloaded research-only PDF SHA-256 `690f412479ceb1971e2f741a5d51ee6bdff4918687af8877efad40f63ace4e23`. Thompson explains that the colonial Motul dictionary uses `ah chuen` for a skilled artisan and related trade compounds for metalsmiths, potters and ironworkers. He quotes an **earlier translation by Barrera Vásquez** of the *first Kaua day-sign list* describing Chuen with **carpentry, weaving, and mastery of arts**. This is important: Barrera's rendering long predates David Bolles's later facing Yucatec/English edited amalgamation, and it independently corroborates the substantive *occupational vocabulary*.

But the source lineage is **the same Kaua textual tradition**. Two interpreters reading a shared passage are not two independent sampled birth groups, and Thompson also mixes **(a)** mythological Chuen/B'atz' monkey brothers skilled in arts, **(b)** a day patron of crafts, **(c)** Yucatec Chuen auspices, and **(d)** an *Aztec Ozomatli* birth-day arts ascription. None is an observation that modern Chuen-born participants craft more.

## 3. A more usable participant-blind question package, still not frozen

[B09 source-aligned questionnaire and consent **DRAFT**](MAYA_B09_CHUEN_SOURCE_ALIGNED_BLIND_QUESTIONNAIRE_DRAFT.json) now contains one primary behavioral item and separately marked supporting questions. The one proposed scored question covers **total paid and unpaid hours during the preceding 12 months actually making, building, decorating or repairing tangible objects** (weaving, sewing, woodworking, pottery, metalwork and similar). It **excludes ordinary household chores**, uses six prelisted ordered hour bands (including zero), and has distinct “cannot estimate” and “prefer not” options that do not score as zero.

Separate, **non-primary** measures record paid-work share, recurring paid craft occupation, training or apprenticeship, and materials/time/opportunity. Age/region/language/education are potential controls; previous awareness of Maya birth signs is gathered **after** behavioral responses but before response freeze to avoid priming the target. The consent draft says the historical predictive model is **not scientifically established** and does not represent a verified current community practice. English and French wording are draft-only; a Yucatec-language instrument has not been translated. **No one has consented, enrolled or answered these questions.**

The B08 model proposed a single reconstructed chart model: GMT584283 + declared Gregorian civil birth date, with Chuen vs the other 19 signs; the 584285/day-shift alternatives may be reported as sensitivity **only**. That does not purport to identify today's Yucatec sacred-day rollover. Source-specific hour/skill/profession constructs must not be swapped based on whether the outcome agrees.

## 4. Concrete multiple-calendar-selection false-positive diagnostic

We added a **research-only exact label-permutation diagnostic**, [code](maya_b09_synthetic_null_demo.py) with [fixed-seed saved results](MAYA_B09_SYNTHETIC_NULL_MULTI_POLICY_DEMO_V1.json), not a replacement for the HD source-to-human forward-blind scorer:

- **384 synthetic datasets**, each **60 fictitious observations** attached to 60 consecutive Gregorian dates; craft-hour ordinal scores drawn independently and uniformly from integers 0 through 5. There is *deliberately no real birth-day effect*.
- Four distinct, previously declared 584283/584285 × date-only/+1-day schemes, each producing **three Chuen-positive synthetic dates**. The four positive groups are **mutually disjoint** in this artificial cohort.
- Exact one-sided upper-tail null probabilities are computed by all fixed-size `k`-subset score sums (dynamic programming, preserving tied response values), not by sampling actual people's birthdays.
- At nominal `p ≤ 0.05`, using the **one locked primary model** yielded **20/384 (5.2%)** false alarms. Selecting whichever of the **four alternative calendar mappings** gave the smallest p-value yielded **55/384 (14.3%)**. The latter is a seed-specific Monte Carlo illustration of multiple comparison bias, **not** a universal familywise error rate; it has sampling uncertainty and ignores confounding/heterogeneous birth dates.

This is useful because the earlier B08 arithmetic exposed **4/20** potentially Chuen-positive dates versus only 1/20 under a *single* model; the new demonstration explicitly shows how *post-response policy selection* can inflate apparently significant results under a known null. **No real astrological effects, human predictions, birth records or respondent questionnaires have been touched.**
