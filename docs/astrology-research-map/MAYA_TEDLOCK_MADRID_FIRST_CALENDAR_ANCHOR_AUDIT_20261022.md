## Interpretation corrections from actual reading

**A continuous count need not have a privileged first day.** Tedlock pp. 94–97 critiques older researchers' habit of starting all day lists at Imox/Imix or at B'atz and reports Momostenango specialists using current day, yearbearer or most recent day 1. The Zunil Komon Tohil/Smithsonian p. 1 description of *B'atz' ceremonies opening a new 260-day ritual cycle* is not automatically a contradiction: ritual observance and a mathematically permanent epoch are different questions. Keep both community statements.

**Numerals and uses are not interchangeable.** In Tedlock pp. 107–108, day numbers carry strength, ritual and shrine connotations. Specific *day-as-answer-to-a-divination* examples on pp. 109–110 also depend on the question and coefficient, and must not be mistaken for an automatic personality horoscope. No invented 13-tone personality multipliers may be added to the Zunil day-name claims.

**The cross-community E comparison is not corroboration.** The living daykeepers of **Zunil** directly call those born on E good travelers (Smithsonian *The Meaning of the Days*, p. 1). Tedlock's **Momostenango** E birth passage, pp. 117–118, instead concerns culturally gendered priest-shaman/midwife roles and life fortune; its *road* imagery in ritual/divination does not independently establish a modern elective-travel disposition. Moreover, the English phrase **good travelers** might mean frequency, competence, enjoyment, or favorable outcomes. Do not pick whichever reading best fits the survey.

**There is a source-local, less ambiguous natal candidate.** Tedlock p. 122 describes Noj birth-day thinking as creative and innovative and refers to problem-solving ability. That is a direct Momostenango birth-disposition attribution; it may be translated into an objectively scored creative-problem-solving task *as a draft*, without treating reported daykeeping career as a universal outcome. Its historical source basis is stronger than assuming Momostenango validates the Zunil E travel trait.

**A birth-time policy is still outstanding.** Tedlock p. 61 discusses ceremonial activity starting at dawn, p. 101 describes a solar yearbearer entering at midnight, and p. 203 discusses ambiguity for dreams across midnight. These are distinct contexts, **not** a verified rule that every baby's sacred day turns over at sunrise, midnight or sunset. Date-only results must remain date-only.

## The Madrid book is useful, but not as a substitute natal text

Vail and Aveni, ch. 5, printed pp. 131–137, distinguish the 260-day tzolk'in, 365-day haab', Calendar Round and Long Count and outline Maya ritual/divinatory almanacs. Gabrielle Vail, ch. 8, pp. 215–223, argues some Madrid almanacs track haab'/52-year ceremonies and yearbearer activities, including Madrid M. 34–37. These readings improve **calendar source classification and historical method**, not an individual birth prediction.

John M. D. Pohl's ch. 12, pp. 385–387, mentions auspicious marriage calculations and **Borgia 58–60** prognostics in comparison with Mixtec and Nahua practices. That is **not** a marriage fate rule recovered from the Maya Madrid Codex. Put the source lead in a separate Central Mexican tradition lane and count historical transmission/dependency before claiming cross-cultural replication.

## One proposed falsifiable rule — not yet preregistered

**Provisional hypothesis `MAYA-MOMO-NOJ-DIVERGENT-THINKING-DRAFT-01`:** among independently birth-dated consenting adults from the appropriate K'iche' practice context, participants calculated as born on Noj (pooled over numbers 1–13) will perform **better on a preselected and blindly scored novel-problem-solving task** than matched non-Noj participants. This predicts a **directional, observable contrast**, rather than fitting either positive or negative personality words after seeing responses.

To make this an admissible test, first fix the birth-date locality/day-boundary and continuity policy; the task prompts, translation, scoring rubric and independent masked graders; one primary score; eligibility, enrollment and power; non-astrology baseline (at minimum age, schooling and language), and matched calendar-shift/permutation decoys that retain seasonal birth structure. Treat the original source's broad *creative thinking* language versus a brief laboratory creativity task as a **construct-validity concern** needing external review. Keep the Komon Tohil E/generosity/travel possibility secondary and unscored, not a second free look.

**Predeclare the falsifier:** if Noj does not exceed the controls on the locked metric, or the effect disappears against the locked non-astrology comparator, report that explicitly as an unsuccessful test. No rescuing predictions with number tone, gendered occupations, an alternative GMT correlation, missing cultural traditions, or another questionnaire after outcomes arrive.

At present the specification is **a bounded, reversible preregistration draft**: no questionnaire/model freeze, no participant results, no sample-size calculation, no scored rule. Known owners' and friends' earlier surveys are *development data*, not an untouched cohort.
