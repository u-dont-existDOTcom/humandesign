# C18 — P.Oxy. 4277 degree-index reconciliation and chronology

**24 October 2026.** Historical-source research only. No human predictive validation or scoring changes. Continues C17 without reacquiring the Oxford original papyrus.

The new source comparison is Cristian Tolsa, “New” Zodiacal Divisions in P.Oxy. LXI 4277, *ZPE* 203 (2017), pp. 192–198, especially the method table on p196 and degree-fraction analysis on p197. The article was read in a publicly accessible textual repost. This remains a **secondary scholarly interpretation**, not independent redecipherment of three papyrus planetary names from original ink.

## Tolsa's printed p196 method table

In order Mercury / Midheaven / Lower Midheaven, the **papyrus-reported labels** are Venus / Mercury / Jupiter.

The author's comparison gives these values:

- Paulus chapter 32 (trigonal): Jupiter / Jupiter / Mercury (**0/3**).
- Olympiodorus, chapter 34: Mars / Jupiter / Mercury (**0/3**).
- Valens IV.26 (sign and planetary cycle): Mercury / Mars / Saturn (**0/3**).
- Tolsa's post-hoc mixed reconstruction: Venus / Jupiter / Mercury (**1/3**).

These are source-fidelity counts, not human forecasts.

## New degree-index reconciliation

C04/C05's by-sign Chaldean calculator treats longitude 6°22′ as **ordinal seventh degree**, and 27°08′ as **ordinal twenty-eighth degree**. It computes, respectively, **Moon / Sun / Jupiter**, matching **1/3** of the same documented ruler labels.

Its prior **completed-degree-as-ordinal diagnostic** treats those longitudes as the sixth and twenty-seventh degrees and computes **Mercury / Mars / Saturn**. These **match Tolsa's published Valens IV.26 column in all three numerical cases** (3/3 *method-to-method* parity), but match **0/3 papyrus-reported labels**.

Tolsa explicitly adopts discarding the fractional degree in this comparison. Thus a discrepancy between the C05 and Tolsa Valens columns is accounted for by **degree indexing** on all three examples, not evidence that their underlying seven-planet rotations differ. This is **limited three-case parity**, not proof that the complete Paulus ch5 and Valens IV.26 methods coincide in every sign or manuscript.

## Chronology: earlier horoscope cannot copy a later treatise

Tolsa dates **P.Oxy. LXI 4277** approximately to the **late second or early third century CE**, using handwriting; Greenbaum's scholarly reference places composition of **Paulus's Eisagogika in 378 CE**. On these accepted dates the papyrus **predates the surviving authored treatise**. It can be compared with a subsequently recorded technique, but it is not evidence that this older scribe followed Paulus's *written book*. An older shared predecessor is possible but unproven.

This restricts historical source attribution without changing the earlier **1/3** calculation result. The high-resolution papyrus photographs were authenticated and examined in C17, but individual monomoiria glyphs have **not** been independently collated against Jones 1999 vol. II pp421–427; preserve uncertainty. No source variant was selected from known lives or biographies.
