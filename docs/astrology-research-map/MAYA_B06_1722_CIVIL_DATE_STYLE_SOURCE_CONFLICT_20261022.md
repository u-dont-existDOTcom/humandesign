# B06 — 1722 K’iche’ source: Julian-vs-Gregorian label ambiguity

22 October 2026 · **Historical civil-date source criticism, not a natal model**

## New source caveat found after the complete 52-group collation

The [Newberry Library catalogue of colonial linguistic texts](https://www.newberry.org/uploads/files/Modern%20Editions%20of%20Colonial%20Spanish%20Sources%20-%20Linguistics.pdf), printed/PDF **p.26**, describes the 1722 K’iche’ calendar in Berendt's **1877** copy as listing Quiché day/month names with **“Julian calendar equivalents in Spanish.”** The FAMSI/UPenn catalogue appears to use related historical archive metadata; do not count their descriptions as multiple independent confirmations.

By contrast, **Alejandro Garay and Igor Xoyón, *Armonización de los calendarios mayas* (Guatemala Ministry of Culture, 2016)**, label their photograph of this same 1722 K’iche’ copy as an alignment to the **Gregorian calendar**, and their corresponding date table uses GMT correlation **584283**. A published example is **3 May 1722 = 9 Kej**. The authors' date matches the earlier B04 photo transcription, but that is a later scholarly *date-conversion interpretation*, not proof of what European calendar style the original scribe intended. FAMSI original manuscript [folio 1](https://www.famsi.org/research/mltdp/item58/ms_coll_700_item58_wk1_body0001.html) visibly pairs **Mayo 3** with **9 Queh**, but does not itself display a clear Julian/Gregorian heading in the inspected table.

The [publisher-provided 2009 book introduction](https://upcolorado.com/media/acfupload/9780870819969.pdf) explains historical calendrical names and concepts, but the **complete edited 1722 transcription and calendar-correlation appendix** are not available in the inspected free 18-page excerpt. This investigation has therefore **not resolved** the divergence between bibliographic terminology and retrospective Gregorian treatment.

## Fixed diagnostic comparison — no retrospective policy optimization

For the relevant **1722–1723** dates, the same day/month/year in the Julian calendar is **11 actual days later** than that label in the Gregorian calendar. This is a historical European civil-date interpretation issue, not a GMT astronomical correlation variant.

| Manuscript's written civil label | If the label is Gregorian | If the label is Julian |
|---|---|---|
| **3 May 1722** — photographed **9 Queh** | Gregorian 3 May = **9 Kej** | Gregorian 14 May = **7 Tijax** |
| 30 photographed rows (B04, 1722–23) | **30/30** match GMT584283 arithmetic | **0/30** match GMT584283 arithmetic |

The 30 rows come from **one** surviving copied manuscript; the sample is not 30 independent sources. Under the fixed reference calculation, the Gregorian reading reproduces the manuscript's handwritten numbered names while the Julian interpretation does not. **This strongly describes which *computational convention* underlies the prior parity, but does not independently settle historical calendar terminology.** A date-normalization decision, copyist practice, archival cataloguing shorthand, or regional historical count could account for part of the discrepancy; none has yet been verified.

## What remains valid versus what becomes conditional

**Still confirmed on the physical 1877 manuscript copy:** 30 visually read Spanish-labelled dates paired with 260-day numbered names; the all-52 photographed five-day group sequence and **260 unique coefficient/name combinations**; K’iche’/colonial orthography crosswalk from the 2009 publisher introduction. Those manuscript observations are invariant to European calendar convention.

**Requalified:** B04/B05's “30 Gregorian dates matched” claim must be read as **assuming the written Spanish month/day/year is interpreted as a Gregorian label**. The government table and the manuscript's day labels agree under that stated model; they are not calendar-style-independent proof that the calendar was labelled Gregorian in 1722. The 2016 author and earlier fieldwork testimony are not an independent universal birth-hour policy.

**Not inferred:** a corrected historical epoch, an 11-day shift of every living K’iche’ birth sign, 1722 “50+” personality or event predictors, or an ability to improve any person's horoscope by selecting a favorable variant.
