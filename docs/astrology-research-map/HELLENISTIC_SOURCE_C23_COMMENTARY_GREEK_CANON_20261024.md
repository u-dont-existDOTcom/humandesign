## Material new primary-source discovery

Located and inspected a **fourteen-century (1301–1400) original Greek handwritten canon** in the Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana **Plut. 28.14**, catalogued as containing the *In Apotelesmatica Pauli Alexandrini* tradition attributed to **Heliodorus astrologus** (composite commentary, not securely attributable in each paragraph to the historical Heliodorus or Olympiodorus).

Official source portal: `https://cdm21059.contentdm.oclc.org/iiif/plutei:566076/manifest.json`. Biblissima catalogues the manuscript, physical date, and full digital surrogate. The catalogue associates the commentary with **fols. 177–212**, which were actually searched: **72 numbered source-page thumbnails** covering digital canvases **366–437** were fetched and examined for figure/section location.

- **Greek original diagram:** handwritten **folio 204v**, digital **canvas 421**, original image `https://cdm21059.contentdm.oclc.org/iiif/2/plutei:565809/full/full/0/default.jpg` — HTTP 200, **2536×2572 px**, **741,471 bytes**, SHA-256 `52a1b8b4f8d67514c815a56178d54c1d45e03a991a09b48417f599ff3336128e`.
- **Immediately preceding explanatory prose:** folio **204r**, canvas **420**, original source SHA-256 `c450625b85b0b655999b700b32c03ef590d33585c3f5b5789e4082f1dd8b78d0`.
- **Following Greek explanatory discussion:** folio **205r**, canvas **422**, original source SHA-256 `3b92483a923a7dfa8754ea79d67caf8967ab75dc6e010dac8acc345857474bd5`.
- The **red rubric** over the chart is visually normalized approximately as *kanonion trigōnikōn monomoir[i]ōn*: **canon of the triplicity monomoiriai**. This is manuscript lettering, **not a modern title invented from chart appearance**. Exact abbreviation expansion and punctuation should be independently checked by a Greek palaeographer.
- The figure groups **all twelve signs in four triplicity columns**, with **30 numbered degree positions** per column, arranged two degree lines per horizontal ruled band. At each degree, there appear **two consecutive abbreviated planetary-glyph positions**, plausibly **day and night**. The reading of those paired positions as day versus night is **provisional**, inferred from the pattern, first ruler pairs and adjoining text; the day/night abbreviations have not been independently transcribed and philologically certified.

This source is **not** the already-photographed Paulus diagram on Florence **Plut. 28.7 f168v** (C14), nor the Munich and Vatican copies of that Paulus table (C13/C15). It is **different manuscript material belonging to the attributed commentary tradition**. No physical images or substantial source text were copied into Git; only short notes, source locators, hashes and narrowly bounded planetary labels.
