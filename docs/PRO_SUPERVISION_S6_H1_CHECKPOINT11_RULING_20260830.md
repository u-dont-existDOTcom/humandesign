**How should prior exposure affect eligibility to serve as an H1 chart-blind construct-content

### E1 — Broad zero-exposure exclusion

Automatically exclude anyone with any prior HD or AstroHD exposure, astrology exposure, relevant
repository or conversation exposure, AstroHD mapping exposure, individualized HD or chart
feedback, or relevant prior contact with Joel or his project.

- Benefit: strongest and simplest anti-contamination firewall.
- Cost: may exclude otherwise usable authors for incidental zodiac familiarity, generic astrology
  knowledge, or unrelated acquaintance with Joel, making recruitment feasibility poor.

### E2 — Project-specific exclusion with peripheral-exposure adjudication

Automatically exclude exposure to HD or AstroHD theory; this repository or its conversations;
mapping hypotheses or results; candidate birth times or chart outputs; individualized HD/AstroHD
feedback; or owner-provided examples or explanations capable of seeding constructs. Record generic
astrology familiarity and unrelated owner familiarity without making either an automatic
exclusion. Require a separately governed chart-blind eligibility adjudication before content
authorship.

- Benefit: preserves the essential AstroHD firewall without excluding incidental background
  familiarity automatically.
- Cost: requires an explicit adjudication protocol and more governance than a universal rule.

### E3 — Predeclared graded-exposure policy

Define exposure levels by source, depth, recency, and likely semantic influence. Permit only
predeclared low-risk levels, with independent adjudication and sensitivity tracking.

- Benefit: most flexible and potentially easiest for recruitment.
- Cost: highest discretion and circularity risk; classification could become outcome-driven unless
  frozen before considering any author.

Pro recommends **E2** because it protects independent construct development from AstroHD influence
without treating ordinary cultural awareness as automatically disqualifying.
